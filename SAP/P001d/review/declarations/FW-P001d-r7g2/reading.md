# Blind reading

Written before opening `claims.json` or `record.json`. The five Challenge
declarations, all declarations listed in their `closure.json` entries, and the
relevant Mathlib definitions were read. `scratch/Inspect.lean` also prints every
generated auxiliary declaration in those closures. No Solution proof is used.

## Shared expansion of Mathlib notation

All scalar values below are real. A `Fin q` index ranges from 0 through q-1.
Finite matrices use ordinary finite sums for multiplication, transpose and
matrix-vector multiplication. A vector dot product is the sum of coordinate
products. `IsUnit G.det` for a real square matrix means `det G != 0`.
Mathlib's matrix inverse is `Ring.inverse(det G)` times the adjugate; it is the
ordinary inverse when the determinant is nonzero and is the zero matrix when
the determinant is zero.

For a measure P, E[U] means the real Bochner integral of U against P.
Cov(U,V) means E[(U-E[U])(V-E[V])]. Var(U) means
`(integral_nonnegative ||U-E[U]||_e^2 dP).toReal`. With the stated second
moments these are the ordinary covariance and variance. `MemLp U 2 P`
means U is almost everywhere strongly measurable and its extended L2 norm is
finite: `(integral_nonnegative ||U||_e^2 dP)^(1/2) < infinity`.

The expanded meaning of the IID assumption on a sequence Z_i is: each Z_i is
measurable, the sigma algebras generated by the Z_i are mutually independent
under P, and each Z_i is almost everywhere measurable and has the same
pushforward measure as Z_0. Mutual independence means that for every finite
set of distinct indices and measurable events in their generated sigma
algebras, the measure of the intersection is the product of the event measures.

The expanded convergence-in-probability conclusion used below means: for every
real delta > 0 the real sequence
`(P {w | delta <= dist(Z_n(w), c)}).toReal` tends to 0 as n tends to infinity.
For real variables the distance is absolute difference. Convergence is
Mathlib `Filter.Tendsto` from `atTop` to the real neighborhood filter of 0.

Bochner integration assigns 0 to a nonintegrable function (including a
function without the required almost everywhere measurability). `toReal`
assigns 0 to infinity. Real division by 0 is 0. Each section checks whether
these totalizations can occur. No definition used by these five statements
uses a real logarithm, a partial-function default, a real infimum/supremum or
liminf/limsup. L2 norms use an extended nonnegative real power at the fixed
positive exponent 2 and root 1/2, not a real power at a negative base.

## SAP.P001d.T63

Source: `SAP/P001d/Challenge/Part1.lean:53-55`;
statement: `SAP/P001d/Defs/Part2.lean:54-65`.

Quantifier order: for every type Omega with a measurable space, every measure
P on Omega with total mass 1, every natural m > 0, and every sequence of
random vectors X_i : Omega -> (Fin m -> R), assume the expanded IID condition
above and assume each coordinate X_0,j has finite second moment. Define, in
ordinary finite sums,

* A_i(w) = (sum over j in Fin m of X_i(w)_j) / m;
* M_l(w) = (sum over i = 0,...,l-1 and j in Fin m of X_i(w)_j) / (l*m).

The conclusion is the conjunction of all the following:

1. The scalar variables A_i are measurable, mutually independent and have
   identical pushforward distributions.
2. For every natural l > 0, the functions M_l and
   `(sum over i = 0,...,l-1 of A_i)/l` are equal, and E[M_l] = E[A_0].
3. E[A_0] = `(sum over j of E[X_0,j])/m`.
4. For every natural l > 0,
   Var(M_l) = Var(A_0)/l =
   `(sum over j,k of Cov(X_0,j,X_0,k))/(l*m^2)`.
5. For every delta > 0,
   `P {|M_(n+1)-E[A_0]| >= delta}` tends to 0 as n tends to infinity.

The expectation in items 2, 3 and 5 is over the same P. The weak law is for
fixed m and an increasing number of independent clusters, with no
independence between coordinates within a cluster.

The SAP dependencies `clusterAverage`, `clusterMean`, `sampleMean`, `IID`,
`CP`, and `prob` have all been expanded above. The remaining closure entry
`T62Statement._proof_2` is just a proof that the numeral 2 is at least two,
used for the L2 exponent; it contains no statistical assumption.

Junk-value audit: m and every finite-sample l in a conclusion are positive;
the convergence sequence uses n+1, so no zero-size mean is evaluated there.
The general mean definitions would give 0 at m=0 or l=0, but those cases are
excluded here. IID transfers the coordinate second moments at cluster 0 to
every cluster. Finite sums of these coordinates have finite second moments;
their means, variances and covariance products are integrable. Thus the
Bochner and infinite-variance defaults do not affect the theorem. P is a
probability measure and all deviation events are measurable, so `prob` never
converts infinity to 0. No natural subtraction is used. Natural-to-real casts
of m, l and n+1 are exact. The index type Fin m is nonempty; Omega cannot be
empty when it carries a probability measure. There is no sqrt in this target.

The docstring asserts IID cluster averages and the mean, variance,
covariance-sum and weak-law formulas, matching this blind mathematical
reading. It does not explicitly repeat the second-moment assumption.

## SAP.P001d.T64

Source: `SAP/P001d/Challenge/Part1.lean:57-67`;
statement: `SAP/P001d/Defs/WholeW3.lean:38-99`.

The statement has two independently universally quantified conjuncts.

First conjunct, in order: for every measurable Omega, probability measure P,
natural m > 0, sequence X_i : Omega -> (Fin m -> R) satisfying IID, and with
every coordinate X_i,j in L2(P), for every natural l > 0 and reals s,rho,
assume 0 <= rho <= 1, Var(X_i,j)=s for all i,j, and, for every i and every
distinct j,k, both sqrt(Var(X_i,j)) and sqrt(Var(X_i,k)) are positive and

`Cov(X_i,j,X_i,k)/(sqrt(Var(X_i,j))*sqrt(Var(X_i,k))) = rho`.

The conclusion is:

1. s >= 0, and Cov(X_i,j,X_i,j)=s for every i,j.
2. For each i and distinct j,k, covariance equals the correlation quotient
   multiplied by the two standard deviations, and equals rho*s.
3. For each i,
   `sum over j,k Cov(X_i,j,X_i,k) = m*s + m*(m-1)*rho*s`.
4. For each i, the variance of the pooled mean
   `M_l = (sum over h<l,j X_h,j)/(l*m)` equals the covariance sum in item 3
   divided by l*m^2.
5. Consequently that variance equals
   `(m*s + m*(m-1)*rho*s)/(l*m^2)` and also
   `s/(l*m)*(1+rho*(m-1))`.
6. If rho=0, it is s/(l*m), both with real multiplication and with the exact
   natural product l*m cast to R. If rho=1, it is s/l.

Here m-1 is subtraction in R after casting m. For m=1, all distinct-pair
requirements are empty; s=0 is allowed and rho has no effect on the formula.
For m>=2 those pair requirements imply s>0. No coordinate mean equality is
assumed within a cluster.

Second conjunct, in order: for all natural l,m with l>0 and m>0 and every
fixed observed array x : Fin l -> Fin m -> R, put

* D_l = the finite set of functions a : Fin l -> Fin l;
* nu = the product over the l output blocks of the uniform probability
  measure on Fin l, so every a in D_l has mass (1/l)^l;
* x^a_(i,j) = x_(a(i),j);
* for any real estimator T on the array space, t_x(a)=T(x^a).

The conclusion is that nu is a probability measure, its block-label
coordinates are mutually independent and each takes every label j with
probability 1/l, each full label assignment has probability (1/l)^l, the
number of observation slots |Fin l x Fin m| is l*m, and x^a and t_x have
exactly the displayed values. Further, for every natural B, the law on
`Fin B -> D_l` is the B-fold product of nu, is a probability measure, and
its block assignments as well as their transformed estimates t_x(a_r) are
mutually independent. For every T, t_x has finite second moment under nu,
and the defined bootstrap standard error is sqrt(Var_nu(t_x)). Variance here
means the expanded integral of the centered squared estimate with x fixed.
This asserts the conditional resampling standard deviation; it does not
assert convergence of a finite B sample standard deviation.

The dependencies `wholeCorrelation`, `clusterMean`, `IID`, `prob`,
`wholeBlockLaw`, `wholeBlockReplicateLaw`, `blockResample`,
`wholeBlockEstimate`, and `wholeBlockSE` have all been expanded. The generated
`T64WholeStatement._proof_1` establishes that the L2 exponent is at least
two; `_proof_2` constructs `NeZero l` from l>0; and
`wholeBlockLaw._proof_1` supplies nonemptiness of Fin l from that instance.
None introduces an additional premise.

Junk-value audit: the covariance/variance integrals in the first conjunct
are finite by L2. All real divisors are positive in their asserted uses.
The correlation quotient is only used for distinct pairs with positive
standard deviations. A singleton zero-variance cluster never requires a
correlation calculation; thus the possible 0/0 default of the global
definition is unused. Square roots in use have nonnegative arguments.
The finite resampling measures have nonempty finite supports because l>0.
Every t_x is measurable and integrable with every finite moment on that
finite discrete space, even if T is not measurable on the full array
space. Consequently neither a nonintegrable-integral default nor infinite
variance is involved. B=0 gives the singleton empty-tuple product probability
measure and empty independence conditions, a legitimate empty experiment.
No empty population of observations occurs. Probabilities are at most one.
The subtraction m-1 is real, not truncated natural subtraction; casts and
slot counts are exact. The docstrings match these formulas and explicitly
describe the positive-pair guard and the independent repetitions.

## SAP.P001d.T65

Source: `SAP/P001d/Challenge/Part1.lean:69-79`;
statement: `SAP/P001d/Defs/WholeW3.lean:101-131`.

For every natural n,K, assume n>0; then for every real n by (K+1) matrix X
and response vector Y of length n, assume X_i,0=1 for every observation i.
For any coefficient vector b define

* e(b)_i = Y_i - sum over j in Fin(K+1) of X_i,j*b_j;
* Q(b) = sum over i in Fin n of e(b)_i^2;
* G = X^T X;
* beta = G^(-1) X^T Y (this expression is used in the invertible branch).

The full conjunction of conclusions is:

1. For every b and i,
   `e(b)_i = Y_i - (b_0 + sum over k in Fin K of b_(k+1)*X_(i,k+1))`.
2. For every b, Q(b)=e(b) dot e(b).
3. For every b, `(for every c, Q(b)/n <= Q(c)/n)` iff
   `(for every c, Q(b) <= Q(c))`.
4. There exists b such that Q(b)<=Q(c) for every c.
5. If det G != 0, for every b,
   Q(beta)<=Q(b) and `(Q(b)=Q(beta) iff b=beta)`, and
   G*beta=X^T Y.
6. For every b, if Q(b)<=Q(c) for every c, put e=e(b). Then
   X^T e=0, G*b=X^T Y, `(sum e_i)/n=0`, and for every k in Fin(K+1),
   `(sum e_i*X_i,k)/n=0` and
   `(sum e_i*X_i,k)/n - ((sum e_i)/n)*((sum X_i,k)/n)=0`.

These are deterministic statements about every realized data matrix. There
is no random-sample assumption. The existence and every-minimizer clauses
cover singular Gram matrices as well. There is no assertion that the
inverse formula selects a minimizer for a singular matrix.

Both SAP dependencies `olsRSS` and `olsCoef` have been expanded as Q and
beta. `T65WholeStatement._proof_1` merely proves K+1 is nonzero so that its
zeroth coordinate can be named.

Junk-value audit: real division uses n>0 throughout. A singular matrix
inverse is globally totalized to zero by `olsCoef`, but the only
conclusions using this coefficient formula have det G != 0 as a premise.
The singular-design conclusions quantify over actual minimizers instead.
K=0 is a valid intercept-only design: the scalar nonintercept sum is empty,
but the full coefficient space still has one coordinate. n=0 is excluded.
There is no natural subtraction; k.succ indexes the actual coordinate k+1.
All sums contain finite real numbers. No integral, toReal, square root,
infinite limit, or other partial operation occurs. The Challenge and
statement docstrings accurately enumerate these deterministic conclusions.

## SAP.P001d.T66

Source: `SAP/P001d/Challenge/Part1.lean:81-89`;
statement: `SAP/P001d/Defs/WholeW1.lean:18-50`.

Quantifier order: for all naturals n,K with n>=2, every real n by (K+1)
matrix X and response Y in R^n, assume its zeroth column is identically 1
and det(X^T X) != 0. Let G=X^T X,
beta=G^(-1) X^T Y, e=Y-X*beta, and let

* s2 = `n/(n-1) * ((sum e_i^2)/n - ((sum e_i)/n)^2)`;
* A_j,k = `(sum X_i,j*X_i,k)/n`;
* B_j,k = `(sum e_i^2*X_i,j*X_i,k)/n`;
* R = `G^(-1) * (X^T diag(e_i^2) X) * G^(-1)`;
* C = `s2 * G^(-1)` (entrywise scalar multiplication).

The conclusion is:

1. For every b, beta minimizes Q(b)=sum (Y_i-(X*b)_i)^2 and equality
   Q(b)=Q(beta) holds exactly when b=beta.
2. `(sum e_i)/n=0`.
3. s2=`(sum e_i^2)/(n-1)` and s2>=0.
4. A=`(1/n)*G` and B=`(1/n)*(X^T diag(e_i^2) X)`.
5. `(1/n)*(A^(-1) B A^(-1))=R` and `(s2/n)*A^(-1)=C`.
6. For every coefficient index k in Fin(K+1), R_k,k>=0, C_k,k>=0,
   and the standard errors defined as square roots of diagonal entries of
   the two normalized matrices in item 5 are sqrt(R_k,k) and sqrt(C_k,k).

The last formulas use a zero-based Fin index k. Its ordinal matrix row
and column in one-based notation are k.val+1; they do not access the next
coefficient. This is a deterministic identity between covariance estimates,
not a distributional assertion about the actual covariance of beta.

The definitions `olsCoef`, `olsRSS`, `wholeEmpiricalVariance`, `robustCov`,
`classicalCov`, and `coefficientSE` are precisely the formulas above.
`T66WholeStatement._proof_1` proves the intercept index exists (K+1 != 0).

Junk-value audit: n>=2 makes n and real n-1 positive. The subtraction in
the empirical variance is real subtraction, not Nat subtraction. G is
invertible by hypothesis and A=G/n is invertible as well; no zero-matrix
inverse default enters. Both covariance diagonals are proved nonnegative
before being used as square-root arguments. The global sqrt definition
would return 0 on a negative argument, but that case does not occur here.
K=0 allows an intercept-only regression and no full coefficient index type
is empty. All arithmetic is on finite real sums. There is no integration,
toReal, or asymptotic operation. The docstring's term 'unbiased empirical
variance' names the displayed n/(n-1) sample-variance formula; the theorem
itself makes no expectation/unbiasedness assertion.

## SAP.P001d.T67

Source: `SAP/P001d/Challenge/Part1.lean:91-96`;
statement: `SAP/P001d/Defs/Part3.lean:63-73`.

Quantifier order: for every measurable Omega, probability measure P, natural
K, and X : Omega -> (Fin K -> R), assume X is measurable. Then for every
epsilon : Omega -> R, assume epsilon has finite second moment and the
following event-integral specification of conditional mean zero:

* the constant-zero map on R^K is measurable;
* epsilon and the constant-zero function on Omega are Bochner integrable;
* for every Borel set D of R^K,
  `integral over X^(-1)(D) of epsilon dP = integral over X^(-1)(D) of 0 dP`.

Then for every real sigma2>=0 assume the event-integral specification of
constant conditional centered second moment:

* the constant-sigma2 map on R^K is measurable;
* `(epsilon-0)^2` and the constant-sigma2 function on Omega are integrable;
* for every Borel D,
  `integral over X^(-1)(D) of (epsilon-0)^2 dP =
   integral over X^(-1)(D) of sigma2 dP`.

Finally assume for every j,k in Fin K that X_j*X_k and epsilon^2*X_j*X_k
are integrable. Define real matrices

* G_j,k = E[X_j*X_k];
* H_j,k = E[epsilon^2*X_j*X_k].

Assume det G != 0. The conjunction concluded is:

1. The constant-sigma2 map is measurable, epsilon^2 and the constant-sigma2
   function on Omega are integrable, and for every Borel D,
   `integral over X^(-1)(D) of epsilon^2 dP =
    integral over X^(-1)(D) of sigma2 dP`.
   This is the same conditional-second-moment assertion as the preceding
   centered-moment assumption after simplifying epsilon-0.
2. H=sigma2*G.
3. G^(-1)*H*G^(-1)=sigma2*G^(-1).

Every SAP dependency is thereby unfolded: `IsCEF` means exactly the
measurability, integrability and event identities listed above, and
`populationGram` and `populationMeat` mean G and H. The generated
`T67Statement._proof_1` proves 2 is at least two for the L2 exponent.
Measurability of X makes its inverse images measurable and makes these
event conditions an ordinary version of conditional expectation given X.
There is no outcome Y, specified BLP coefficient, OLS sample, sample size,
sampling sequence, asymptotic normality or covariance approximation.

Junk-value audit: all entry integrals have explicit integrability premises.
The conditional specifications require integrability rather than relying
on a zero-valued integral of an undefined quantity. Event restrictions of
those integrable functions remain integrable. G is invertible by hypothesis,
so its inverse has the usual meaning. K=0 is allowed: the two matrices are
0 by 0, their determinant is 1, and the matrix conclusions have no entries;
the scalar conditional assumptions/conclusion remain. This legitimate
zero-dimensional instance does not eliminate the positive-dimensional
instances also quantified by the theorem. sigma2=0 is allowed, compatible
with an almost surely zero epsilon. There is no division, sqrt, natural
subtraction, or limit. The docstring discloses omission of asymptotic and
linearity-equivalence assertions and describes the population identities.
