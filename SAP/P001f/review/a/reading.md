# Blind mathematical reading

This file was written from `SAP/P001f/Defs*.lean`, the five Defs parts, the Challenge files, and the relevant Mathlib definitions, before opening `claims.json` or `record.json`. The theorem docstrings are not treated as additional hypotheses or conclusions. There are eighteen theorem stubs. Each asserts the proposition with the corresponding `T…Statement` name.

## Conventions and fully expanded shared notions

All random-variable expectations below are Mathlib real Bochner integrals with respect to the displayed measure. A probability measure has total mass one. An unqualified event probability below means the real value of its extended-nonnegative measure. On probability spaces this conversion loses nothing. Mathlib defines a nonintegrable Bochner integral to be zero; statements below are read with their actual moment hypotheses, rather than silently assuming every displayed expression is integrable. `Integrable` means almost everywhere strongly measurable with finite integral of the absolute value. `MemLp … 2` means almost everywhere strongly measurable with finite second moment. Almost everywhere equality and predicates are relative to the indicated measure.

For a measurable covariate X into a measurable space E, write **CM(μ,X,Y,m)** for this exact predicate: (1) m:E→R is measurable; (2) Y is μ-integrable; (3) m∘X is μ-integrable; and (4) for every measurable A⊆E,

    ∫_{X⁻¹(A)} Y dμ = ∫_{X⁻¹(A)} m(X) dμ.

The SAP predicate itself does not assume X measurable. Each theorem's explicit measurability assumptions are recorded below, including the exception T107. This predicate specifies a conditional-mean version whenever X is measurable. Its integrals over a set use the restricted measure, not a normalized conditional measure.

Write **CI(μ,X;Y,Z)** for the following exact eventwise property, with X explicitly measurable: for every measurable A in Y's codomain and every measurable B in Z's codomain,

    E[1_{Y∈A,Z∈B} | σ(X)] = E[1_{Y∈A} | σ(X)] E[1_{Z∈B} | σ(X)]  μ-a.e.

The exceptional null set may depend on A and B. Here σ(X) is the pullback (`MeasurableSpace.comap`) of the specified sigma algebra on E. Conditional expectation is Mathlib's conditional expectation. In its definition it defaults to zero for an invalid sub-sigma algebra, failure of sigma-finiteness of the trimmed measure, or a nonintegrable argument. For measurable X on the finite measures used here, and bounded measurable indicators, these defaults do not apply.

**IID(μ,Z)** for Z indexed by all natural numbers means: every Z_i is measurable; the sigma algebras generated by the Z_i are mutually independent (every finite intersection has product probability); and for every i the pushforward law of Z_i is the law of Z_0. Mathlib `IdentDistrib` also requires almost everywhere measurability, already implied here. This is an infinite sequence hypothesis, even when a conclusion uses only its first n entries.

Write **Avg_n Z** = (Σ_{i=0}^{n−1} Z_i)/n. At n=0 Lean's real division gives zero; every occurrence in the theorem conclusions is guarded by n>0. Real division by zero generally gives zero in Lean. `eventMean(μ,X,Y,x)` is ∫_{X=x}Y dμ / P(X=x); it is not a regular conditional expectation at a zero-probability point.

**Density(ν,f)** means f is almost everywhere Lebesgue measurable, nonnegative at every point, Lebesgue-integrable, and ν is Lebesgue measure with density `ENNReal.ofReal(f)`. Thus f is an actual density, not merely a function informally called a density. On R^K and its product with R, volume is the standard finite-product Lebesgue measure. The density need not be continuous, and its chosen point values enter assertions of the form f(x)>0.

For finite weights w and arrays on Fin n, define

* M_w(Y)=Σ_i w_iY_i;
* P_w(A)=Σ_i 1_{A(i)}w_i;
* M_w(Y|X=x)=Σ_{X_i=x}w_iY_i / P_w(X=x).

Finite conditional independence of Y and Z given X means: for every x,y,z, if P_w(X=x)>0, then

    P_w(X=x,Y=y,Z=z) P_w(X=x)
      = P_w(X=x,Y=y) P_w(X=x,Z=z).

This definition does not itself impose nonnegative weights or normalization; T99 supplies those separately.

The elementary shared functions are: C(y,r)=yr−99(1−r); O(y0,y1,d)=y1 d+y0(1−d); L(y,d,a,b)=yd+a(1−d)−y(1−d)−bd; U(y,d,a,b)=yd+b(1−d)−y(1−d)−ad. The logistic function is p_{a,b}(x)=exp(a+bx)/(1+exp(a+bx)). These denominators are always positive.

`variance(Z,μ)` is the real conversion of the extended integral of |Z−E Z|². All uses of it below have second moments. Standard deviation is its nonnegative real square root. A derivative is an ordinary two-sided real derivative. Mathlib `deriv` is zero if a derivative does not exist; `DifferentiableAt` asserts existence and `HasDerivAt g a 0` asserts that the derivative is a. Write

    ∂_k m(x) = deriv at t=0 of m(x with coordinate k replaced by x_k+t).

`samplePDA(X,mhat,n,ω,k)` is, by definition, ∂_k(mhat_n(ω))(Avg_n X(ω)), with the average taken separately in each coordinate. A polynomial's `natDegree` is its usual degree except that the zero polynomial has natural degree zero. Natural subtraction below occurs only in T110 as n−k, where k≤n follows from the count definition. A real `tsum` is the infinite-sum operator; the assertions using it either explicitly assume summability or conclude it.

## T96

This is a closed conjunction, with no data hypotheses. There are three cells with covariates x=(-1,0,1), sizes N=(6,6,6), treated counts r=(3,1,5), treated-outcome sums st=(12,3,20), and control-outcome sums sc=(9,10,3). Define

    ell(a,b)=Σ_{j=0}² [r_j log p_{a,b}(x_j)+(N_j−r_j)log(1−p_{a,b}(x_j))].

It asserts all of the following, in conjunction:

1. For every real a,b, ell(a,b)≤ell(0,log 2), and equality holds iff a=0 and b=log 2.
2. For each of the three cells, 0<r_j<N_j, and its fitted probability at this maximizer is respectively 1/3,1/2,2/3.
3. The fitted-score IPW estimate Σ_j[st_j/p_j−sc_j/(1−p_j)]/18 is 59/36. The poststratified estimate Σ_j N_j[st_j/r_j−sc_j/(N_j−r_j)]/18 is 1. These values differ.
4. The observed treated fractions are respectively 1/2,1/6,5/6.
5. The three count-minus-fitted-count residuals equal 1,−2,1; 1−2+1=0 and −1+0+1=0.
6. The fully numeric IPW expression with treated cell means (4,3,4), control means (3,2,3), counts (3,1,5) and (3,5,1), and fitted propensities (1/3,1/2,2/3), divided by 18, equals 59/36. The numeric weighted cell-mean difference (6/18)(4−3)+(6/18)(3−2)+(6/18)(4−3) equals 1; the values differ.
7. Their gap is 23/36 and is nonzero.

## T97

For every finite type E with decidable equality, and every natural n, assuming n>0, for every three real arrays nt,nc,q on E, assuming (in order) nt_x>0 for every x, nc_x>0 for every x, and 0<q_x<1 for every x, the following equivalence holds:

    [for every pair of arbitrary real arrays st,sc on E,
       Σ_x(st_x/q_x−sc_x/(1−q_x))/n
       = Σ_x(nt_x+nc_x)(st_x/nt_x−sc_x/nc_x)/n]
      iff [for every x, q_x=nt_x/(nt_x+nc_x)].

There is no assumption that n is the sum of the arm masses, that masses are integers, or that E is nonempty. The outcome arrays are universally quantified inside the left side of the equivalence; they are not fixed observed outcomes. The empty E case is a true empty equivalence, but the assertion also covers nonempty E.

## T98

For every measurable space Ω, measurable space E, probability μ, and sequences X_i:Ω→E and D_i:Ω→R: assume IID of the pairs (X_i,D_i). Next quantify a function p:E→R and assume CM(μ,X_0,D_0,p). Assume D_i(ω)∈{0,1} for every i and every ω (pointwise, not just almost everywhere). Assume there exists one real ε>0 such that for every i and every ω,

    ε<p(X_i(ω))<1−ε.

Then the following are all true:

* CM(μ,X_0,D_0/p(X_0),1) and CM(μ,X_0,(1−D_0)/(1−p(X_0)),1).
* E[D_0/p(X_0)]=E[(1−D_0)/(1−p(X_0))]=1.
* For every n>0, E[Avg_n(D_i/p(X_i))]=E[Avg_n((1−D_i)/(1−p(X_i)))]=1.
* E[1/p(X_0)]>1 and E[1/(1−p(X_0))]>1.

The constant-one conditional means include integrability. There is no outcome variable, fitted propensity, or convergence conclusion here.

## T99

This is a closed assertion on Fin 2 with weights w=(3/4,1/4), treatment D=(0,1), constant covariate X=0, and constant pair of potential outcomes (0,1). It asserts, in order:

* all weights are nonnegative and sum to one;
* P_w(D=0)=3/4 and P_w(D=1)=1/4;
* finite conditional independence of (0,1) and D given X;
* M_w(D|X=0)=1/4;
* 1/8<1/4<1−1/8 and 1/4<3/8<1;
* M_w((1/4)(1−0))=1/4;
* M_w(1|D=1)=1;
* M_w((1/4)(1−0))≠M_w(1−0|D=1);
* M_w((1/4)·1)/M_w(1/4)=M_w(1|D=1).

Thus the two conditional-event denominators and the normalization denominator are positive in this displayed finite law.

## T100

For every measurable Ω,E, probability μ, measurable X:Ω→E (its measurability proof is an explicit parameter), and real Y0,Y1,D: assume Y0,Y1,D measurable; Y1−Y0 integrable; D∈{0,1} μ-a.e.; and 0<P(D=1)<1. Then quantify p:E→R and assume CM(μ,X,D,p), followed by CI(μ,X;(Y0,Y1),D). Set

    P=P(D=1), W=E[p(X)(Y1−Y0)], A=∫_{D=1}(Y1−Y0)dμ/P.

The conclusion is the conjunction

    E[p(X)]=P,  0<P,
    W=E[D(Y1−Y0)],  W=P A,
    A=W/E[p(X)],
    [W=A iff P=1 or A=0].

There is no separate integrability assumption on Y0 or Y1 individually, nor an overlap assumption. Since P<1 is a premise, the P=1 alternative in the final equivalence is unavailable within this theorem's hypotheses.

## T101

For every type Ω and every pair of subsets A,B of Ω,

    A\B={s:s∈A and s∉B}, and A\B=A∩Bᶜ,

where the complement is in Ω. No finiteness, probability, nonemptiness, or other hypothesis is present.

## T102

This asserts a negation, followed by a universal plug-in identity.

The negated proposition is the following full universal assertion, in this quantifier order: for every measurable Ω, probability μ, natural K, measurable X:Ω→R^K and measurable Y:Ω→R, assuming each X-coordinate and Y integrable; for every f:R^K→R and q:R^K×R→R satisfying Density(law(X),f) and Density(law(X,Y),q); for every m0,m1:R^K→R satisfying CM(μ,X,Y,m0) and CM(μ,X,Y,m1); for every k∈Fin K, assuming that for every x with f(x)>0 each of the two one-variable functions t↦mi(x with coordinate k changed to x_k+t) is differentiable at zero; put xbar_j=E[X_j]. There exists a real a such that both these one-variable functions based at xbar have derivative a at zero. T102 says this universal assertion is false. It does not supply a named counterexample in the theorem statement. Since k is quantified over Fin K, K=0 contributes no failure witness.

The second conjunct says: for every type Ω, natural K, arbitrary arrays X_i:Ω→R^K and mhat_n:Ω→(R^K→R), and natural n>0, for every ω and k∈Fin K, if the coordinate function of mhat_n(ω) at the coordinatewise sample mean is differentiable at zero, then

    samplePDA(X,mhat,n,ω,k)=∂_k(mhat_n(ω))(Avg_n X(ω)).

This is the defining identity for samplePDA. There are no measurability, distribution, independence, or consistency hypotheses or conclusions in this second conjunct.

## T103

Define phi(m,s,y)=exp(−(y−m)²/(2s²))/(s sqrt(2pi)); the lognormal density is phi(m,s,log y)/y for y>0 and zero otherwise; the exponential density is r exp(−ry) for y≥0 and zero otherwise. A parameter θ consists of seven real entries. It is valid if

    θ0≥0, θ1≥0, θ0+θ1≤1, θ3>0, θ5>0, θ6>0.

Its mixture density at y is θ0 phi(θ2,θ3,y)+θ1 lognormal(θ4,θ5,y)+(1−θ0−θ1) exponential(θ6,y), and the sample likelihood is the product over the sample entries.

T103 negates this statement: for every natural n>0 and every real sample y indexed by Fin n with every y_i>0, there exists a valid θ such that for every valid θ′ its likelihood is at most that of θ. Equivalently there is at least one nonempty all-positive sample with no valid global maximizing parameter. There is no asymptotic, uniqueness, or estimator-consistency statement.

## T104

This negates the following assertion: for every pair of sequences t,c:N→R with each t_k,c_k≥0, both sequences summable, both totals strictly positive, and their totals summing to one,

    {k:t_k>0}={k:c_k>0}
      iff there exists ε>0 such that for every k with t_k+c_k>0,
        ε<t_k/(t_k+c_k)<1−ε.

Here t,c are unnormalized joint arm/covariate masses, not conditional covariate laws. At cells with zero total mass no propensity inequality is imposed. The statement asserts that a universally valid equivalence fails; it does not explicitly state which direction fails or a specific witness.

## T106

For every measurable Ω and probability μ, assert the conjunction of two universally quantified components:

1. For every μ-integrable real X,Y, then every real a,b,c,

       E[aX+bY+c]=a E X+b E Y+c.

2. For every real X with finite second moment, then every real a,c, all of the following hold:

       Var(X)=E[X²]−(E X)²;
       Var(X+c)=Var(X);
       Var(aX)=a² Var(X);
       sqrt Var(X+c)=sqrt Var(X);
       sqrt Var(aX)=|a| sqrt Var(X).

   Additionally, if Var(X)>0, then for every ε>0,

       P(ε sqrt Var(X) ≤ |X−E X|) ≤ 1/ε².

There is no explicit pointwise measurability hypothesis on X,Y beyond the almost everywhere measurability included in the moment predicates.

## T107

For every measurable Ω,E, probability μ, and functions X:Ω→E,Y:Ω→R, assume Y integrable. Then for every m:E→R satisfying CM(μ,X,Y,m),

    E Y=E[m(X)].

In particular CM includes its own copy of Y's integrability, m's measurability, m(X)'s integrability, and the restricted-integral identities. X is not explicitly required to be measurable. Taking A=E in CM already supplies the displayed conclusion, independently of that omission. This is only a total-mean equality.

## T108

For every measurable Ω, probability μ, and real sequence X_i:Ω→R, assume IID(μ,X), then assume X_0 integrable. For every natural n>0,

    E[Avg_n X]=E X_0,

and if X_0 has finite second moment, both

    Var(Avg_n X)=Var(X_0)/n

and, if Var(X_0)>0, for every real ε>0,

    P(ε≤|Avg_n X−E X_0|) ≤ Var(X_0)/(ε² n).

The iid assumption concerns all natural-number observations; no limit assertion appears.

## T109

The first conjunct says: for all real a,b with a≤b, every f:R→R continuous on [a,b], and every ε>0, there exists a real polynomial p such that at every x∈[a,b], |f(x)−p(x)|<ε.

The second conjunct is this full population least-squares assertion. For every measurable Ω, probability μ, and real X,Y, assume X measurable and Y square-integrable. Next take arbitrary real a,b. Assume X∈[a,b] μ-a.e. Assume either

* there exists a density f of law(X) whose pointwise positive set is exactly [a,b], or
* the actual range of X is countable and, for every real x, P(X=x)>0 iff x∈[a,b].

Next quantify m:R→R and assume CM(μ,X,Y,m), followed by continuity of m on [a,b]. The conclusion is the conjunction:

1. There exists a family of real polynomials p_J, J∈N, such that each has natural degree ≤J and, for every J and every real polynomial q of natural degree ≤J,

       E[(Y−p_J(X))²] ≤ E[(Y−q(X))²].

2. For every family p_J with these degree and minimization properties, for every ε>0 there exists J such that for every J′≥J,

       E[(m(X)−p_J′(X))²] < ε.

The existence family is not used as the quantifier binding the second part: convergence is for every minimizing family. The second part does not require a≤b explicitly; its distribution hypotheses imply a nonempty interval. The discrete alternative forces every point of a real interval to be an atom, so any satisfiable discrete example has a=b. There is no uniqueness assertion, no uniform convergence assertion for the minimizing family, and no finite-sample estimation assertion.

## T110

For every natural n>0 and every binary array Y:Fin n→Bool, let k be the number of true entries and a=k/n. The first conjunct asserts:

* 0≤a≤1;
* a=Σ_i 1_{Y_i=true}/n;
* for every t∈[0,1], (1−t)^(n−k)t^k≤(1−a)^(n−k)a^k, with equality iff t=a.

The second conjunct states the same maximum and equality characterization for the specific function (1−t)t² at t=2/3, for every t∈[0,1]. Endpoint samples are included; powers with exponent zero use 0^0=1, as appropriate for an empty product of Bernoulli factors.

## T111

This is a conjunction of three universal population assertions and one finite computation.

**MCAR.** For every measurable Ω, probability μ, real Y,R, assume (in order): Y measurable; R measurable; Y integrable; R∈{0,1} μ-a.e.; P(R=0)>0; P(R=1)>0; independence of Y and R. Conclude

    E Y = ∫_{R=1}C(Y,R)dμ/P(R=1).

**Discrete MAR.** For every measurable Ω and countable measurable E with measurable singletons, probability μ, measurable X:Ω→E, and real Y,R, assume: Y,R measurable; Y integrable; R binary μ-a.e.; both response events have positive probability. Next quantify p:E→R, assume CM(μ,X,R,p), then assume there exists ε>0 with ε<p(X) μ-a.e., and assume CI(μ,X;Y,R). Next quantify m:E→R and assume CM(μ restricted to {R=1},X,C(Y,R),m), followed by μ-integrability of m(X). Conclude CM(μ,X,Y,m), summability of x↦m(x)P(X=x), and

    E Y=Σ'_x m(x)P(X=x).

**Vector MAR.** For every measurable Ω, natural K, probability μ, measurable X:Ω→R^K, and real Y,R, assume: Y,R measurable; Y integrable; R binary μ-a.e.; both response events have positive probability. Next quantify p,m:R^K→R, assume CM(μ,X,R,p), existence of ε>0 with ε<p(X) μ-a.e., CI(μ,X;Y,R), CM(μ restricted to {R=1},X,C(Y,R),m), and μ-integrability of m(X). Conclude CM(μ,X,Y,m) and E Y=E[m(X)]. No density of X is assumed.

**Finite sample.** Take censored observations ys=(1,−99,1,0,1,−99), indicators r=(1,0,1,1,1,0), covariates x=(0,0,0,0,1,1) in Fin 2. Define respondent average Σ_i ys_i r_i / Σ_i r_i. Define poststratified average as the sum over both covariate cells of the cell respondent average, times the cell sample count divided by six. The asserted values are respectively 3/4 and 7/9. There are four respondents in total and three and one in the respective cells, so no displayed sample denominator is zero.

## T112

For every measurable Ω, natural K, probability μ, measurable X:Ω→R^K, and real Y,R, assume: Y and R measurable; R binary μ-a.e.; P(R=0)>0; P(R=1)>0. Next quantify p:R^K→R and a proof of CM(μ,X,R,p); assume CI(μ,X;Y,R). Next take any real ε>0, assume ε<p(X) μ-a.e. Then assert:

    CI(μ,p(X);R,X),
    CI(μ,p(X);Y,R),
    CM(μ,p(X),R,id),
    there exists η>0 with η<id(p(X)) μ-a.e.

The conditioning covariate in all three predicates is now the scalar p∘X, whose measurability follows from the hypotheses. No integrability of Y is required, since conditional independence here involves bounded indicator functions rather than Y itself. The identity CM includes integrability of R and p(X).

## T113

For every measurable Ω, natural K, probability μ, measurable X:Ω→R^K, and real Y,R, assume Y,R measurable, Y integrable, R binary μ-a.e., and both response events of positive probability. Next quantify p:R^K→R and assume CM(μ,X,R,p), existence of ε>0 with ε<p(X) μ-a.e., and CI(μ,X;Y,R). Next quantify m,q:R^K→R and assume, in order, m measurable, q measurable, m(X) integrable, and the disjunction

    [CM(μ restricted to {R=1},X,C(Y,R),m)
       and there exists η>0 with η<q(X)<1−η μ-a.e.]
      or [q(X)=p(X) μ-a.e.].

Conclude

    E Y=E[m(X)+R(C(Y,R)−m(X))/q(X)].

The regression-integrability premise applies in both branches. The second branch does not require an upper bound away from one for p or q; its positive lower bound follows from q(X)=p(X) almost everywhere. All correctness and overlap premises in this statement concern versions up to μ-null sets.

## T114

The first conjunct is this universal sharp-bound statement. For every measurable Ω, probability μ, and real Y0,Y1,D, assume the three functions measurable. Next quantify real a,b and assume Y0,Y1∈[a,b] μ-a.e., D binary μ-a.e., P(D=0)>0, and P(D=1)>0. Put Y=O(Y0,Y1,D), P_d=P(D=d), μ_d=∫_{D=d}Y dμ/P_d, and

    L=μ_1 P_1+aP_0−(μ_0 P_0+bP_1),
    U=μ_1 P_1+bP_0−(μ_0 P_0+aP_1).

Then E[Y1−Y0]∈[L,U], U−L=b−a, and for every real c∈[L,U] there exist measurable real Z0,Z1 on the same Ω such that:

* Z0,Z1∈[a,b] μ-a.e.;
* the pushforward joint law of (O(Z0,Z1,D),D) is the original joint law of (Y,D);
* E[Z1−Z0]=c.

The alternatives are constructed on the same probability space and use the original D. Integrability is not explicit, but follows from the measurable almost everywhere bounded outcomes. There is no conditional-ignorability assumption.

The second conjunct is a closed finite calculation, with y=(1,1,1,0,1,0), d=(1,0,1,1,1,0), a=0,b=1: Σ_{i∈Fin 6}L(y_i,d_i,0,1)/6=−2/6 and Σ_i U(y_i,d_i,0,1)/6=4/6.
