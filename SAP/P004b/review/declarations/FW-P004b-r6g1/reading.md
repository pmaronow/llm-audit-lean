## Shared expansion into mathematics

For an integer N ≥ 0, an assignment is a function z : {0,…,N−1} → {false,true}; a circle table is a real-valued function R(i,z). Write z_i for its 0/1 real indicator. Given real numbers p_i, let w_p(z) = ∏_i (p_i if z_i=1, otherwise 1−p_i). E_p X = ∑_z w_p(z)X(z), P_p(A) = ∑_z w_p(z)1_A(z), Cov_p(X,Y) = E_p(XY)−E_p X E_p Y, and Var_p X = Cov_p(X,X). These are finite algebraic sums. They are probabilities/expectations under independent Bernoulli coordinates whenever 0 ≤ p_i ≤ 1, as is the case in every probabilistic application here.

Let q_i(a) = p_i for a=true and 1−p_i otherwise. Define μ_i(a)=E_p[1_{z_i=a}R(i,z)]/q_i(a), τ_i=μ_i(true)−μ_i(false), τ=N⁻¹∑_i τ_i, and μ̄_a=N⁻¹∑_i μ_i(a). The HT statistic is H(z)=N⁻¹∑_i[z_iR(i,z)/p_i−(1−z_i)R(i,z)/(1−p_i)]. The observed arm count n_a(z)=∑_i1_{z_i=a} is real-valued, the arm mean is M_a(z)=∑_i1_{z_i=a}R(i,z)/n_a(z), and J(z)=M_true(z)−M_false(z) is the Hájek statistic. For a deterministic function g(i,a), the augmented statistic is A_g(z)=N⁻¹∑_i[g(i,true)−g(i,false)+z_i(R(i,z)−g(i,true))/p_i−(1−z_i)(R(i,z)−g(i,false))/(1−p_i)]. Its population-arm-centered member L is obtained by setting g(i,a)=μ̄_a. The corrected displayed formula F is τ+N⁻¹∑_i[z_i(R(i,z)−μ̄_true)/p_i−(1−z_i)(R(i,z)−μ̄_false)/(1−p_i)]. A residual contrast is c_g(i,z)=z_i(R(i,z)−g(i,true))/p_i−(1−z_i)(R(i,z)−g(i,false))/(1−p_i).

Say I_R(i,j) when i=j or when there are two assignments equal on every coordinate except possibly j on which R(i,·) differs. Say S_R(i,j) when there exists l with I_R(i,l) and I_R(j,l). In particular these definitions force self-interference even for constant outcomes. For a real function D(i,j), the radius-r ball is the finite set {j : D(i,j)≤r}. Locality with cutoff h means ∀i,j, h<D(i,j) implies ¬S_R(i,j).

A spatial population consists of a metric and measurable space X, N specified nonempty subsets A_i⊆X, a measure ζ, outcomes Y(x,z)∈ℝ, and an arbitrary function B taking a real d to a set of real distances. Set d_i(x)=infDist(x,A_i), and D(i,j)=inf{dist(x,y):x∈A_i,y∈A_j}. Define C_i(d)={x:d_i(x)∈B(d)} and R_d(i,z)=(∫_{C_i(d)}Y(x,z)dζ)/(ζ(C_i(d))).toReal. Well-definedness at d means, for every i, C_i(d) is measurable, 0<ζ(C_i(d))<∞, and Y(·,z) is integrable on it for every z. Finite source range b means that for every l,x,z,z′, if z,z′ agree outside l and b<d_l(x), then Y(x,z)=Y(x,z′).

In the plane E=EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2), a polygon is a finite union (possibly zero terms) of convex hulls of triples of points. Let Q(a,b,c,e)={x∈E:a≤x_0≤b and c≤x_1≤e}, and let (a,b) denote the Euclidean point obtained from that coordinate vector. Plane separation is the same infimum of pairwise Euclidean distances just defined. Plane volume is Mathlib Lebesgue measure; the use of EuclideanSpace fixes the Euclidean metric.

A population sequence permits its metric/measurable space to vary with N. The spatial local conditions mean the conjunction of: (i) ∃b>0, eventually in N, ∀x,z, |Y_N(x,z)|<b; (ii) ∃h:ℝ→ℝ with h(d)≥0 for d≥0, such that for each d≥0, eventually in N, R_{N,d} is local at cutoff h(d); (iii) ∃c:ℝ→ℝ such that for each r≥0, eventually in N, every radius-r ball for D_N has cardinality at most c(r). The eventual threshold can depend on d or r; there is no uniformity in these arguments and no requirement that set separation satisfy the triangle inequality.

For varying N and Bernoulli parameters, convergence in probability to zero means ∀ε>0, P(|U_N|>ε)→0. U_N=O_p(r_N) means ∀ε>0 ∃C>0, eventually P(|U_N|>C|r_N|)<ε. Φ(x) is the real value of the standard Gaussian probability of (−∞,x]; a normal limit means ∀x∈ℝ, P(U_N≤x)→Φ(x). Nondegeneracy of an estimator variance sequence V_N means ∃v>0, eventually v≤N V_N.

A closed dependency graph B_i is required to contain i, to be symmetric, and to give independence of entire subvectors on any two finite vertex sets I,J having no cross-edge. In the general measure version this is Mathlib `IndepFun` for the maps ω↦(V_i(ω))_{i∈I} and ω↦(V_j(ω))_{j∈J}. In the finite Bernoulli version it is the explicit identity P(A(V_I)∧C(V_J))=P(A(V_I))P(C(V_J)) for all predicates A,C on those finite vectors. The latter is full subvector independence, not merely pairwise independence.
