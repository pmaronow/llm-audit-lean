## Shared objects and Mathlib meanings

All scalar arithmetic is in the real numbers unless a measure value is explicitly in `[0,∞]`. `Bool` has two elements, written 0/1 below; `bit` sends them to the real numbers 0/1. `Fin n` indexes exactly n observations. All sums and products over these types are finite. A binary policy is a pair `(a,b)` of real numbers, prescribing treatment probabilities a at type 0 and b at type 1. Its feasible set B is `[0,1]²`. An admissible policy on a measurable space C is a measurable function C→R with values everywhere in `[0,1]`.

`IsBest(P,f,p)` means `p∈P` and `f(p')≤f(p)` for every `p'∈P`. `bestValue(P,f)` is the real conditional supremum of `{f(p):p∈P}`; `regret(P,V,p)` is this supremum minus V(p). The definitions themselves do not require nonemptiness or boundedness, so any interpretation as an optimum needs those checks. The finite feasible sets of policy values below are bounded, and the main general statement assumes an actual welfare maximizer.

For a treatment vector T, k=Σ bit(T_i). `wATO(T,Y)` is `Σ Y_i bit(T_i)/k` if k>0 and is 0 otherwise. `wATT(h,X,T,Y)` applies this average to `Y_i-h(X_i)`. `wWeightedTT(h,g,X,T,Y)` is `Σ (Y_i-h(X_i))bit(T_i)g(X_i)`; `wTT` sets g=1. There is deliberately no division by n in TT. `positiveRule(m)(x)=1` exactly when m(x)>0. `printedAverageRule(m)(x)=1` exactly when m(x)>0 and `m(z)≤m(x)` for every z, and is 0 otherwise. `emptyFactor(n,q)=Σ_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-q)^j`.

`coinMass(q,t)` is q for t=1 and 1-q for t=0. `twoATO(p,m,(a,b))` sums `wATO` over two independent type draws of mass `coinMass(p,·)` and independent conditional treatment draws with probabilities a,b, using outcome `m(type)` in the reward. `finiteIIDAverage(n,p,m,π)` is the analogous n-observation sum over every type and treatment vector. These expressions are probability expectations on feasible probabilities, and otherwise just their displayed finite real sums. `oneTT(y,h,π)` is the one-observation TT sum with fair type, treatment probability π(type), treated outcome y(type), and estimator `h(type)(query)`. `finiteIIDTT(n,y,π)` is the n-observation product sum with fair types, the same treatment mechanism, treated outcome y, and estimator identically zero. `twoTTConstant` is its n=2, y=1 instance. `binaryWelfare(p,τ,(a,b))=(1-p)aτ(0)+pbτ(1)`.

`outcomeP1(0)=0`, `outcomeP1(1)=1`; `effectP1(β,0)=1`, `effectP1(β,1)=1-β`; `rewardP1(p,π)=twoATO(p,outcomeP1,π)`.

An `Experiment Ω C n` includes n>0, a measure P on C, real functions μ0,μ1, a policy-indexed measure Lπ on Ω, covariates C_i:Ω→C, potential outcomes Y0_i,Y1_i, treatment indicators Tπ_i:Ω→Bool, and estimators hπ(ω,c). Its effect is τ=μ1-μ0. Its observed outcome is Y1_i when Tπ_i=1 and Y0_i otherwise. Its welfare Vπ is the Lπ-integral of observed outcome minus Y0 for observation i=0. Its `meanWelfare` is ∫π(c)τ(c)dP, but V is **defined** using the first sampled observation, not by this latter integral. Expected ATO/ATT/TT/weighted TT are the corresponding Lπ-integrals of sample rewards. `normalizedTT` is expected TT divided by n.

`Standing E` is the conjunction of all the following, in declaration order:

1. P is a probability measure.
2. μ0 and μ1 are measurable and P-integrable.
3. For every admissible π, Lπ is a probability measure.
4. For every admissible π and every i, C_i,Y0_i,Y1_i,Tπ_i are measurable.
5. For every admissible π and every i, Y0_i,Y1_i and `ω↦hπ(ω,C_i(ω))` are Lπ-integrable.
6. For every admissible π and i, C_i under Lπ has distribution P.
7. For every pair of admissible π,π' and i, the joint distribution of `(C_i,Y0_i,Y1_i)` is the same under Lπ and Lπ'.
8. For each admissible π, the n vectors `(C_i,Y0_i,Y1_i,Tπ_i)` are mutually independent and identically distributed under Lπ.
9. For each admissible π and i, the conditional expectations of Y0_i,Y1_i given the sigma algebra generated by C_i equal μ0(C_i),μ1(C_i), respectively, almost surely.
10. For each admissible π and i, the conditional expectation of bit(Tπ_i) given C_i equals π(C_i), almost surely.
11. For every admissible π, i, and measurable set D⊆R², the conditional expectation of `bit(Tπ_i)1_D(Y0_i,Y1_i)` given C_i equals π(C_i) times the conditional expectation of `1_D(Y0_i,Y1_i)` given C_i, almost surely.

`PointwiseUnbiased(E,Pset)` says: for every π∈Pset and then every fixed c∈C, `ω↦hπ(ω,c)` is Lπ-integrable and its integral equals μ0(c). It imposes no sample or treatment independence on h and no conditional unbiasedness at a random query.

Mathlib `IsProbabilityMeasure μ` means μ(universe)=1. `Kernel X U` is a measurable measure-valued function; `IsMarkovKernel κ` means κ(x) is a probability measure for **every** x. `PX.compProd κ` is the joint measure whose measurable-set mass is ∫κ(x)({u:(x,u)∈A})dPX. `IdentDistrib` requires almost-everywhere measurability of both functions and equality of their pushforward measures (Mathlib/Probability/IdentDistrib.lean:71). `IndepFun` and `iIndepFun` express independence of their generated sigma algebras (Probability/Independence/Basic.lean:136,144). Conditional expectations are Mathlib `condExp` (MeasureTheory/Function/ConditionalExpectation/Basic.lean:102), which default to zero if the sub-sigma-algebra, sigma-finiteness, or integrability prerequisites fail. Standing supplies these prerequisites in its probabilistic uses. `Integrable` means almost-everywhere strong measurability and finite absolute integral (MeasureTheory/Function/L1Space/Integrable.lean:59). Real Bochner integrals default to zero for nonintegrable functions (MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean:158). Set integrals integrate against restricted measures. `eventMean(μ,A,f)=(∫_A f dμ)/(μ(A)).toReal`. `toReal` sends infinite measure to zero as well as preserving finite masses; division by zero in R is zero. These conventions must be checked rather than interpreted as statistical means automatically. `withDensity` gives A mass ∫_A density dμ (MeasureTheory/Measure/WithDensity.lean:39); `ENNReal.ofReal r` clips negative r to zero. `Tendsto f atTop atTop` means f tends to +∞ as its real argument tends to +∞; replacing the first filter with `atBot` means the argument tends to -∞.
