# Blind reading of the Lean statements

Saved before opening `claims.json` or `record.json`. This reading uses the Defs and Challenge sources and the Mathlib definitions they invoke. Names T16, etc. below mean `SAP.P006b.T16`, etc. All scalar arithmetic below is real arithmetic unless an index or sample size is explicitly natural.

## Shared definitions and conventions

A policy on a measurable space C is **admissible** exactly when it is measurable and takes values in [0,1] at every point. Write b(t)=1 for the Boolean true and 0 for false. For n observations, N(T)=sum_i b(T_i). The sample ATO reward is sum_i Y_i b(T_i)/N(T) when N(T)>0 and is **zero** otherwise. ATT applies that same reward to Y_i-hat_mu(X_i). Weighted TT is sum_i (Y_i-hat_mu(X_i)) b(T_i) g(X_i); TT sets g=1. In particular TT is a total, not an average.

`IsBest(P,f,a)` means a belongs to P and, for every a' belonging to P, f(a')<=f(a). It asserts attainment, not uniqueness. `bestValue(P,f)` is the real supremum of {f(a):a in P}; regret(P,V,a) is that supremum minus V(a). These use the conditionally complete order on the reals; an empty or unbounded set is not automatically a legitimate finite optimum. Any use as an ordinary supremum needs nonemptiness and an upper bound. A supplied `IsBest` gives both. Finite binary-policy objectives used here are bounded on the policy square.

The positive rule of m is 1_{m(x)>0}. The **printed average rule** is instead 1 exactly at x for which m(x)>0 AND m(z)<=m(x) for every z in C. This uses all points, including null points, and does not treat positive values below the global maximum.

An `Experiment` E consists of a positive integer n; a measure P_C; functions mu0,mu1:C->R; for every function pi:C->R, a measure P_pi on a common measurable sample space Omega; random variables X_i,Y0_i,Y1_i (independent of the policy argument as functions); treatment maps T_pi,i; and an estimator hat_mu_pi(omega,c). The outcome Y_pi,i equals Y1_i if T_pi,i is true and Y0_i otherwise. Set tau=mu1-mu0. Its welfare V(pi) is E_{P_pi}[Y_pi,0-Y0_0], using the first unit. `meanWelfare` is integral pi*tau dP_C. Expected ATO, ATT and TT mean integrals of the corresponding sample rewards under P_pi. Normalized TT is expected TT divided by n.

`Standing(E)` is the following conjunction, and contains no estimator unbiasedness or independence condition:

1. P_C is a probability measure. mu0 and mu1 are measurable and absolutely integrable under P_C.
2. For EVERY admissible pi, P_pi is a probability measure. For EVERY unit i, X_i,Y0_i,Y1_i,T_pi,i are measurable; Y0_i,Y1_i and omega->hat_mu_pi(omega,X_i(omega)) are absolutely integrable under P_pi.
3. For every such pi and i, the law of X_i is P_C. For every pair of admissible pi,pi' and each i, the joint law of (X_i,Y0_i,Y1_i) agrees under P_pi and P_pi'.
4. For every admissible pi the vectors (X_i,Y0_i,Y1_i,T_pi,i) are mutually independent over i, and any two of these vectors have the same distribution under P_pi.
5. For every admissible pi and i, conditional on the sigma algebra generated by X_i, E[Y0_i|X_i]=mu0(X_i), E[Y1_i|X_i]=mu1(X_i), and E[b(T_pi,i)|X_i]=pi(X_i), almost surely under P_pi.
6. For every such pi,i and EVERY measurable B subset of R^2, E[b(T_pi,i) 1_B(Y0_i,Y1_i)|X_i] equals pi(X_i) E[1_B(Y0_i,Y1_i)|X_i], almost surely under P_pi. The null set may depend on B,pi,i.

`PointwiseUnbiased(E,P)` says: for EVERY pi in P, then EVERY fixed c in C, omega->hat_mu_pi(omega,c) is absolutely integrable under P_pi and its mean is mu0(c). This does not say that the estimator is conditionally unbiased at the random argument X_i, or independent of treatments or data.

For public X, private U and a probability kernel kappa from X to U, define

* q_pi(x)=integral (1-pi(x,u)) dkappa_x(u).
* B_pi(x)=integral mu0(x,u)(1-pi(x,u)) dkappa_x(u) / q_pi(x), the selected baseline.
* Q_pi,x has density ofReal((1-pi(x,u))/q_pi(x)) relative to kappa_x.
* D(x,u)=sup_v |mu0(x,u)-mu0(x,v)|, the maximum deviation.

For finite types i in Fin K, put M(p,pi)=sum_i p_i pi_i and A(p,m,pi)=sum_i p_i m_i pi_i. The finite selected baseline is A(p,m,1-pi)/M(p,1-pi). `leaveOnlyType(j)` is zero at j and one elsewhere; `treatOnlyType(j)` is one at j and zero elsewhere.

Binary policies are arbitrary pairs (a,b) of reals as a type, acting with probability a at false and b at true. The feasible set P is [0,1]^2. For a Boolean t, c(q,t) equals q when t=true and 1-q otherwise. Binary welfare is W_p(tau,(a,b))=(1-p)a tau(false)+p b tau(true).

`twoATO(p,m,pi)` sums, over all x0,x1,t0,t1 in Bool, the product c(p,x0)c(p,x1)c(pi(x0),t0)c(pi(x1),t1) times the two-unit sample ATO of (m(x0),m(x1)). `finiteIIDAverage(n,p,m,pi)` is the corresponding sum over all Boolean covariate and treatment vectors of length n, with product masses. These are genuine expectations when p and pi are feasible, and are merely real algebraic sums otherwise. `emptyFactor(n,q)` is sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-q)^j.

`oneTT(y1,est,pi)` sums over x,t in Bool the mass (1/2)c(pi(x),t) times the one-unit TT with covariate x, treated outcome y1(x), and estimator z->est(x,z). `twoTTConstant(pi)` is the two-unit TT finite expectation for independent fair binary covariates, treated outcomes identically 1, and zero estimator. `finiteIIDTT(n,y1,pi)` sums over length-n vectors of pairs (x,t), with product masses (1/2)c(pi(x),t), the TT with zero estimator and outcomes y1(x).

Set m1(false)=0,m1(true)=1; e_beta(false)=1,e_beta(true)=1-beta; R_p(pi)=twoATO(p,m1,pi).

Logistic sigma(z)=1/(1+exp(-z)). A logistic fit in dimension d consists of real beta0,beta2 and vectors beta1,beta3 indexed by Fin d. Its predictor is L(x,t)=beta0+sum_j beta1_j x_j+beta2 b(t)+sum_j beta3_j x_j b(t). The signed outcome s(y) is 1 at true and -1 at false. The signed Bernoulli mean at p is sum_y c(p,y)s(y).

For a finite covariate sample X_i and real values m_i, G_x={i:X_i=x}; empiricalMean(x)=sum_{i in G_x}m_i / |G_x|; empiricalGamma=(1/n)sum_i |empiricalMean(X_i)-m_i|. Equality on the covariate type is decidable. Empty group means, when evaluated, are zero by real division by zero; all bound assertions below explicitly require a nonempty group.

For a measure mu, eventMean(mu,A,f) is (integral_A f dmu)/(mu(A)).toReal. All displayed integrals are Mathlib Bochner integrals: `Integrable` includes almost-everywhere strong measurability and finite integral of the norm; a non-integrable function has integral zero by definition. Conditional expectation likewise defaults to zero if its conditioning sigma algebra is not a subalgebra, the trimmed measure is not sigma finite, or the function is not integrable. The Standing assumptions make the relevant conditional expectations ordinary conditional expectations. `IdentDistrib` includes almost-everywhere measurability and equality of pushforward measures. `IndepFun` and `iIndepFun` mean independence of the generated sigma algebras, the latter for all finite subfamilies. A Markov kernel is a measurable measure-valued map with probability measure at every x. P_X.compProd(kappa) is the joint measure whose set masses are integral kappa_x({u:(x,u) in A}) dP_X. `withDensity` integrates its nonnegative extended-real density over measurable sets. `ofReal(r)` truncates negative r to zero; `toReal(infinity)=0`. Real division by zero is zero. The positivity and probability hypotheses must be checked wherever these conventions could matter. Limits to atTop/atBot below are ordinary divergence to positive/negative infinity, not limsup assertions.

## T16

This is a conjunction of three independently universally quantified statements.

**Main statement.** For every measurable Omega,X,U with U nonempty; every n and E:Experiment(Omega,X times U,n); then Standing(E); every probability measure P_X; every Markov kernel kappa with P_C=P_X.compProd(kappa); every policy set P whose members are admissible; assume, in this order:

* For every pi in P, q_pi(x)>0 for P_X-almost every x.
* For P_X-almost every x, u->mu0(x,u) is integrable under kappa_x.
* For every x there EXIST u_min,u_max such that for EVERY u, mu0(x,u_min)<=mu0(x,u)<=mu0(x,u_max).
* For every c=(x,u) there EXISTS v such that for EVERY w, |mu0(c)-mu0(x,w)|<=|mu0(c)-mu0(x,v)|.
* For every pi in P, EVERY omega and EVERY c, hat_mu_pi(omega,c)=B_pi(c.public).
* Then take any real gamma, with D integrable under P_C and integral D dP_C<=gamma.
* Then take any functions b,s:(X times U)->R with b best for expected TT on P and s best for V on P.

The conclusion is the following conjunction. For every pi in P, for P_X-almost every x, Q_pi,x is a probability measure, B_pi(x)=integral mu0(x,u)dQ_pi,x(u), inf_u mu0(x,u)<=B_pi(x)<=sup_u mu0(x,u), and for EVERY u, |B_pi(x)-mu0(x,u)|<=D(x,u). For every pi in P, expectedTT(pi)/n=integral pi(c)(mu1(c)-B_pi(c.public))dP_C, and

|expectedTT(pi)/n-V(pi)| <= integral |B_pi(c.public)-mu0(c)|dP_C <= integral D dP_C <= gamma.

It separately asserts the intermediate bound integral |B_pi-mu0|<=gamma and the final bound |expectedTT(pi)/n-V(pi)|<=gamma. Finally regret(P,V,b)=V(s)-V(b) and regret(P,V,b)<=2 gamma. The existence of b,s is a premise and in particular forces P nonempty.

**Pointwise sufficient condition.** For every pair of measurable spaces X,U, every Markov kernel kappa, and every admissible pi with pi(c)<1 for EVERY c: q_pi(x)>0 for EVERY x; and for EVERY function b0:X->R and EVERY x, selecting the baseline mu0(x,u)=b0(x) gives B_pi(x)=b0(x). No measurability or boundedness assumption on b0 is imposed, because each fixed-x fiber is constant.

**Full-information identity.** Independently, for every measurable X,U, Markov kernel kappa, arbitrary functions p,b0:X->R and x in X, if p(x)<1, then B_pi(x)=b0(x) when pi(x,u)=p(x) and mu0(x,u)=b0(x). Here p need not be measurable, nonnegative or globally below 1; the particular fibers are constant.

## T17

For EVERY natural K, EVERY p,m:Fin K->R, with p_i>0 for every i and sum_i p_i=1, then EVERY j in Fin K: the untreated mass for leaveOnlyType(j) is p_j, and its finite selected baseline is m_j. If there EXISTS i different from j, then the untreated mass for treatOnlyType(j) is positive, and its baseline equals (sum_{i!=j}p_i m_i)/(sum_{i!=j}p_i).

An additional closed example asserts that with three equal masses and m=(0,1,3), treatOnlyType(0) gives selected baseline 2 and leaveOnlyType(0) gives 0, and 2!=0. No random-outcome conditional-mean identity is part of this theorem.

## T20

## T21

For EVERY natural d, EVERY logistic-fit parameter tuple beta and EVERY x:Fin d->R: for every Boolean t, the signed Bernoulli mean at sigma(L(x,t)) is 2 sigma(L(x,t))-1; the treated minus untreated signed means equal 2(sigma(L(x,true))-sigma(L(x,false))); and for EVERY real z, 2 sigma(z)-1 != sigma(z). ## T22

For EVERY type C with decidable equality, natural n, covariate vector X, value vector m, then n>0: for EVERY x with G_x nonempty, (i) for EVERY pair of real bounds l,h, if l<=m_i<=h for EVERY i in G_x, then l<=empiricalMean(x)<=h; and (ii) sum_{i in G_x}(m_i-empiricalMean(x))=0. ## T23

There are two fixed worlds w=false,true. Let prevalence v(false)=1/2,v(true)=3/4. Given y0, the untreated observation probability r(w,y0) is 1/2 in the true world, and is 3/4 for y0=true and 1/4 for y0=false in the false world. The mass on (y0,t,r) is c(v(w),y0)*(1/2) times 1_{r=true} if t=true, and times c(r(w,y0),r) if t=false. Thus the treated outcome is always true and observed; the untreated outcome is y0. The observed record is (t,r,some(if t then true else y0)) if r=true and (t,r,none) otherwise.

The theorem asserts all these joint masses nonnegative for EVERY w,y0,t,r; total mass 1 in EVERY world; summing out r gives c(v(w),y0)/2 for EVERY w,y0,t; in each world P(t=false)=1/2 and P(t=false,r=true)=1/4; and for EVERY possible record, its summed mass is the same in both worlds. Population means of b(y0) are respectively 1/2 and 3/4; the observed-untreated conditional mean is 3/4 in each world; its difference from the population mean in the false world is 1/4. Further conjuncts give four record masses in the false world: (false,false,none):1/4; (false,true,some false):1/16; (false,true,some true):3/16; (true,true,some true):1/2. For every world the untreated observation rate is (1/4)/(1/2)=1/2.

## T24

For EVERY measurable Omega, probability measure mu, real random variables Y0,Y1 and Boolean random variables T,R, assume in order that all four are measurable, Y0,Y1 are integrable, (Y0,Y1) is independent of T, mu(T=false).toReal>0, and mu(T=false,R=true).toReal>0. Define A={T=false}, A1={T=false,R=true}, A0={T=false,R=false}, observed Y=if T then Y1 else Y0, and q=mu(A1).toReal/mu(A).toReal. Then:

1. IF eventMean(A1,Y0)=eventMean(A,Y0), THEN integral Y0 dmu=eventMean(A1,Y).
2. IF q<1, THEN integral Y0 dmu=q eventMean(A1,Y0)+(1-q)eventMean(A0,Y0), AND [eventMean(A1,Y0)=integral Y0 dmu IFF eventMean(A1,Y0)=eventMean(A0,Y0)].
3. IF Y0 and R are independent under the conditional probability measure (mu(A))^{-1} times mu restricted to A, THEN eventMean(A1,Y0)=eventMean(A,Y0).

This is one arbitrary supplied probability law; it does not itself construct conditional laws indexed by covariates. The q<1 assumption ensures the denominator for A0 is positive whenever that mean is used in the mixture/equivalence.

## T26

The **negation of one joint universal assertion**: it is NOT the case that, for EVERY measurable Omega,C with C countable and measurable singletons, every n and every Experiment E, Standing(E) and PointwiseUnbiased(E,{admissible policies}) imply BOTH that the printed average rule of mu1 is best for expected ATO on all admissible policies AND that the printed average rule of tau is best for expected ATT on all admissible policies.

Equivalently, some experiment on those types satisfies Standing and the unbiasedness condition, but at least one of those two maximizing assertions fails. It does not assert that both fail, or identify a witness inside this theorem's statement.

## T30

A closed conjunction: (1,1) is best on P for the one-unit fair-binary TT reward with treated outcome constantly 1 and estimator zero; it is also best for the analogous two-unit reward. Their best values are respectively 1 and 2 and the first is strictly smaller. It additionally asserts for EVERY Boolean x the tautology 1<=1, and again 1<=1. There is no general quantification over pairs of agents, effects, sample sizes or reward laws, and no separate named ranking predicate.
