## Common objects and exact conventions

Indices of an n-vector are 0,...,n-1 (`Fin n`). A graph is a symmetric
Boolean n-by-n array with false diagonal: a finite simple undirected graph.
Write A for its real 0/1 adjacency matrix and deg_A(i) for its number of
neighbors. Matrix `1` is the identity, so `oracleMatrix A` is I+A.
`Compatible R d A` means R is a subgraph of A and deg_A(i)<=d_i for every i;
the nonnegative integer d_i need not itself be at most n-1. Put
d'_i=min(d_i,n-1). `ProgramFeasible` says the same in real adjacency
coordinates: sum_j A_ij<=d_i and R_ij<=A_ij. `Maximizes R d f A` means
A is compatible and, for every compatible B, f(B)<=f(A).

For a real vector x put bar(x)=sum_i x_i/n, z_i=x_i-bar(x),
S(x)=sum_i z_i^2, s^2(x)=S(x)/n, and q(M,x)=sum_ij M_ij x_i x_j.
Then the definitions are exactly

* V1(A,x)=[S(x)+q(A,z)]/n^2;
* V2(A,x)=s^2(x)/n [1+sum_ij A_ij/n];
* V2'(d,x)=s^2(x)/n [1+sum_i d'_i/n];
* V1R(A,x)=S(x)/[n(n-1)]+q(A,z)/n^2;
* V2R(A,x)=S(x)/[n(n-1)] [1+sum_ij A_ij/n];
* V2'R(d,x)=S(x)/[n(n-1)] [1+sum_i d'_i/n];
* VR(A,r)=q(I+A,r)/n^2 (there is no additional centering here).

A `Model n` is an arbitrary probability measure P on R^n, deterministic
real weights theta_i, two graphs G,R, and integer caps d_i. Its probability
field asserts P(R^n)=1; it does not by itself assert dependency, moments,
valid caps, or n>0. Let Y_i(y)=y_i, X_i=theta_i Y_i,
B=bar(X)=betaHat, beta=E B, mu_i=E X_i, delta_i=mu_i-beta,
Sigma_ij=E[(X_i-mu_i)(X_j-mu_j)]. Here E is the Mathlib Bochner integral
against P. `var m f` is E[(f-Ef)^2], not a separately assumed finite
variance. `covOf m f g` is E[fg]-Ef Eg, whereas Mathlib covariance is
E[(f-Ef)(g-Eg)]; these agree on the moment domains stated in T17.
All these integrals are total Lean functions, which return zero for a
nonintegrable integrand. Moment/boundedness assumptions must therefore be
checked where they are used, rather than inferred from the notation E.
`probability m E` is `(P E).toReal`: the real value of the measure's outer
measure on E. Probability finiteness prevents infinity-to-zero here.

`Dependency m` asserts that for every pair of disjoint finite vertex sets
S,T with no G edge from S to T, the whole coordinate subvectors Y_S and
Y_T are independent. This uses Mathlib `IndepFun`, independence of the
sigma algebras generated by the two maps; it is stronger than just
pairwise nonedge independence. `Homoskedastic m` means all Sigma_ii are
equal. Set sigma^2=meanVariance=sum_i Sigma_ii/n,
spread=sum_i delta_i^2/n, bias=q(I+G,delta)/n, and
remainder(y)=s^2(X(y))-sigma^2-spread. Define rho_ij=0 if sigma^2=0 and
Sigma_ij/sigma^2 otherwise, and
V2*(m,y)=s^2(X(y))/n [1+sum_ij G_ij rho_ij/n].

A `Family` has sizes n_k>0 tending to infinity, and an unrelated model
P_k,theta_k,G_k,R_k,d_k at each k. `Nested` adds monotone sizes and, for
each k, the existence of an embedding of G_k as an induced subgraph of
G_(k+1). It imposes no coupling or marginal-law consistency across k.
`Scaling F c1 c2 c3 c4` is the conjunction of Nested, c1,c2,c3,c4>0,
dependency at every k, almost surely |X_ki-beta_k|<=c1 simultaneously for
all i at each k, deg_Gk(i)<=c2, n_k Var(B_k)->c3, and
|theta_ki|<c4 for every k,i. `Valid F` asserts compatibility of G_k with
R_k,d_k at every k. Write t_k=n_k Var(B_k), O_k(y)=n_k V1(G_k,X_k(y)).

`InProbability F T a` means: for every real epsilon>0,
P_k(|T_k-a_k|>epsilon)->0. It allows a moving deterministic target a_k.
`Conservative F V` means for every epsilon>0,
P_k(n_k[Var(B_k)-V_k]>epsilon)->0. `Tight F T` means for every epsilon>0
there exist B>0 and k0 such that for all k>=k0,
P_k(|T_k|>B)<=epsilon. All limits are ordinary sequence limits, expressed
by Mathlib `Tendsto ... atTop (nhds ...)`.

For a deterministic n-by-p design D, `MeanModel m D b` requires each
Y_i integrable and E Y_i=(Db)_i. Define
bHat(D,y)=(D^T D)^(-1)D^T y and
Theta(D)=n (D^T D)^(-1)D^T.
Mathlib matrix inverse is adjugate times the ring inverse of the
determinant, zero for singular real matrices. T16 explicitly excludes
singularity. True residuals are theta_i[Y_i-(Db)_i]; fitted residuals
are rHat_i=theta_i[Y_i-(D bHat)_i]. Euclidean norm `l2` is
sqrt(sum_i x_i^2). Only fitted residuals occur in the theorems below.

Phi(t) is the cdf of Mathlib `gaussianReal 0 1`, the real normal law
with mean zero and variance one. Its cdf is P((infinity negative,t]).
q_alpha=inf{t:1-alpha/2<=Phi(t)} (`sInf` on R).
Coverage is P(|B-beta|<=q_alpha sqrt(max(V(y),0))). Only 0<alpha<1 is
used, so the quantile set has a finite real infimum. `Real.sqrt` is the
nonnegative real square root (zero on nonpositive arguments).

Upper-edge variables are indexed by unordered pairs represented as i<j.
`fromUpper` reflects Boolean variables across the diagonal, with zero
diagonal; `toUpper` restricts a graph to i<j.
U(a,w)=sum_(i<j) a_ij w_ij, interpreting Boolean a as 0/1.
udeg(a,i) counts selected upper edges incident to i.
`RelaxedFeasible R d a` for real edge variables means 0<=a_e<=1,
a_e=1 on R, and sum of incident a_e<=d_i for every i.
`upperSum f` is sum_(i<j) f(i,j).
The `nonzeroRule(x)` selects exactly those edges with z_i z_j != 0,
regardless of their signs or of R,d.

## Explicit laws used below

`uniformLaw f` is the pushforward of the uniform PMF on a nonempty
finite input type, converted to a measure. Thus all inputs are equally
likely, and uniform function inputs make their coordinates independent.
`sign(b)` is +1 for true and -1 for false.

`signModel(n)` has independent fair signs Y_i, theta_i=1, empty G=R,
and all d_i=0. `signFamily` uses n=k+1. `threePointModel(n)` instead
has independent uniforms on {0,1,2}, with the same graphs, caps and
weights; `threePointFamily` also uses n=k+1.

`pathModel(t,K)` consists of K independent blocks of three vertices,
each block (t+U,-2t+3U/5+4V/5,t+V), with independent fair signs U,V.
Its G=R is the disjoint union of paths 0--1--2, weights are one, and
caps are the corresponding path degrees.

`starModel(upper,K)` has K independent four-vertex blocks. Within a
block, leaves are independent Bernoulli draws made by three independent
uniform integers in {0,1,2,3}. For upper=false their success probabilities
are (1/4,1/4,1/4); for upper=true they are (3/4,1/4,1/2).
Given the leaves with count N, the center is 1 iff an independent uniform
integer v in {0,...,47} is below 33+4N (false) or 18+4N (true).
The G=R of each block is the three-leaf star centered at 0; caps are
(3,1,1,1); weights are one. `starFamily upper` uses K=k+1, n=4K.
Block/local indices are floor(i/q), i mod q; `blockGraph` puts the same
given q-vertex graph on each block and no edges across blocks.

`constantWitness` has n=4, Y=(1,1,1,1) surely, theta=(2,3,4,5),
G=R empty and caps zero. `edge12` is edge 0--1 on three vertices;
`edge23` is edge 1--2. `completeGraph(n)` has every off-diagonal edge.

## T17

For every n>0, every Model n, and every i,j,k,l in Fin n (repetitions
allowed), define r_a=X_a-B and
Q_ab=E[X_a X_b]-E[X_a B]-E[X_b B]+E[B^2].
Let v=(i,j,k,l), Y_a=Y_(v_a), theta_a=theta_(v_a). There are five
separate conditional conclusions, with no dependency assumption:

1. Suppose B^2 and, for each of the pairs (i,j),(k,l), X_a X_b,
   X_a B,X_b B are integrable. Then E[r_i r_j]=Q_ij and
   E[r_k r_l]=Q_kl.
2. In addition to those weighted pair assumptions, suppose for every
   subset S of the four *positions*, the monomial
   (product_(a in S) X_(v_a)) B^(4-|S|) is integrable. Then
   covOf(r_i r_j,r_k r_l) equals Mathlib covariance, equals
   E[r_i r_j r_k r_l]-Q_ij Q_kl, and equals F-Q_ij Q_kl, where
   F=E[X_i X_j X_k X_l]
   -E[B(X_i X_j X_k+X_i X_j X_l+X_i X_k X_l+X_j X_k X_l)]
   +E[B^2(X_i X_j+X_i X_k+X_i X_l+X_j X_k+X_j X_l+X_k X_l)]
   -E[B^3(X_i+X_j+X_k+X_l)]+E[B^4].
3. Separately, suppose every nonempty positional-subset product
   product_(a in S) X_(v_a) is integrable. For every constant b in R,
   covOf((X_i-b)(X_j-b),(X_k-b)(X_l-b)) equals
   covOf(X_i X_j,X_k X_l)
   -b[covOf(X_i X_j,X_k)+covOf(X_i X_j,X_l)
       +covOf(X_i,X_k X_l)+covOf(X_j,X_k X_l)]
   +b^2[covOf(X_i,X_k)+covOf(X_i,X_l)
         +covOf(X_j,X_k)+covOf(X_j,X_l)].
4. Separately suppose B^2 and, for both designated pairs, Y_a Y_b,
   Y_a B,Y_b B are integrable. Then each Q_ab and E[r_a r_b] equals
   P_ab=theta_a theta_b E[Y_a Y_b]-theta_a E[Y_a B]
   -theta_b E[Y_b B]+E[B^2].
5. In addition to those raw pair assumptions suppose every
   (product_(a in S) Y_a) B^(4-|S|), for S a positional subset,
   is integrable. Then covOf(r_i r_j,r_k r_l) equals Mathlib covariance;
   E[r_i r_j r_k r_l] equals the 16-term expansion H obtained by
   expanding product_(a=0,...,3)(theta_a Y_a-B) inside expectation,
   with coefficient (-1)^(4-|S|) product_(a in S) theta_a on each
   E[(product_(a in S)Y_a) B^(4-|S|)]; and the covariance equals
   H-P_ij P_kl.

Every subset has size <=4, so the natural exponent 4-|S| here is
ordinary nonnegative subtraction. These branches do not assume all
their moment domains simultaneously.
