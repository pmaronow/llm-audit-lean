## Shared notation and underlying definitions

On a finite set A, write E_p Z = sum_a p(a)Z(a), P_p(E) = sum_{a:E(a)} p(a), and Var_p Z = E_p[(Z-E_p Z)^2]. A probability vector means every p(a) >= 0 and sum p = 1. For X:A -> B, the cell mean is E_p[1_{X=x}Z]/P_p(X=x), and the cell probability of D=d is P_p(X=x,D=d)/P_p(X=x). These definitions themselves allow zero denominators (Lean real division by zero gives zero); the substantive displayed cells below have positive masses. The residual weight is w(a)=(D(a)-E_p[D|X=X(a)])^2, and the effective vector is q(a)=p(a)w(a)/E_p w. ConditionalIndependent(p,X,U,D) means, for every x, u, d in their respective codomains, P(X=x,U=u,D=d)P(X=x)=P(X=x,U=u)P(X=x,D=d). On finite A this is the usual conditional independence on positive-mass cells, and imposes a trivial equality on null cells.

The effect contrast at two real doses d,d' is (Y(a,d')-Y(a,d))/(d'-d). Every contrast used here has d=0,d'=1. The IPW variable is DY/e-(1-D)Y/(1-e). Population coefficients are (E_p[W'W])^{-1}E_p[W'Y]. Sample leastSquares is (W'W)^{-1}W'Y. These are Mathlib square matrix inverses, which are zero for singular real matrices. Determinant assertions below exclude that convention at the actual uses. Fitted residual is D-V omega.

For n independent finite draws the mass of a:Fin n -> A is product_i p(a_i). The empirical mean is sum_i Z(a_i)/n, including a defined zero-denominator value at n=0. All finite-sample moment and tail bounds below require n>=1; limits as n -> infinity do not depend on n=0.

Write sigma(eta)=1/(1+exp(-eta)), c(p)=log(p/(1-p)), and T1(f,c,a)=f(c)+f'(c)(a-c). Mathlib deriv is zero at points where differentiation fails; the differentiability hypotheses/results used below give its genuine meaning. Define ell_m(n,k,eta)=k log(m(eta))+(n-k)log(1-m(eta)), h_m(eta)=m'(eta)/(m(eta)(1-m(eta))). For a design W, counts n,k, and parameter theta, L_m(theta)=sum_i ell_m(n_i,k_i,(W theta)_i), residual equation R_m(theta)=W'(k-n m(W theta)), and the defined score S_m(theta)=W'[h_m(W theta)(k-n m(W theta))]. Logs are total real logs in Lean; admissible means in (0,1) are essential and are present at the points to which score/optimization assertions apply. Binomial combinatorial constants are omitted from L.

Phi and phi are Mathlib's cdf and real density of gaussianReal 0 1, where the second parameter is variance, so these are the standard normal cdf and density. q_N=inf{a:3/4<=Phi(a)}. h_P(eta)=phi(eta)/(Phi(eta)(1-Phi(eta))). The scalar Taylor polynomial sigma_N(c,a) includes terms of degrees 0 through N, using taylorWithinEval on the whole real line.

For a probability measure P and measurable countable-valued covariate X, P_x=P(X=x)^{-1} P restricted to {X=x}, with inverse and scaling in extended nonnegative reals. Write mu_x=integral D dP_x, v_x=integral (D-mu_x)^2 dP_x, pi_x(d)=P_x(D=d).toReal, w(s)=(D(s)-mu_{X(s)})^2, V=integral w dP, and Q=(ofReal V)^{-1} P.withDensity(ofReal w). Assertions below using these as conditional laws assume P(X=x)>0 and finite second moment. Bochner integrals default to zero for nonintegrable functions, but the L2 assumptions and explicit integrability guards exclude that use in these clauses.

For general measurable X write G=sigma(X), m=P[D|G], w=(D-m)^2, and v=P[w|G]. Mathlib conditional expectation is an a.e. representative, defined as zero if integrability, the sub-sigma-algebra relation, or sigma-finiteness fails. Here measurability of X, a probability measure, and D in L2 give the usual conditional expectation and residual integrability. Equality of representatives is distinguished below from a.e. equality.

Mathlib TendstoInMeasure on a probability measure is convergence in probability, expressed by measures of the sets with distance >= every positive threshold tending to zero; it does not itself require measurability of the functions. iIndepFun is joint independence, not merely pairwise independence. CondIndepFun is conditional independence of the sigma-algebras generated by its arguments. Big O at a real neighborhood means an eventual absolute-value bound by a fixed finite constant. HasFPowerSeriesAt is equality with a convergent complex multilinear power series in some positive-radius neighborhood. Its radius is the norm convergence radius; in one complex dimension this is the ordinary scalar power-series radius.
