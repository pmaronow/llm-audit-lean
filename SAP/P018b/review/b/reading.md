## Mathematical dictionary and conventions

* `Vec k` is the real vector space of functions on {0,...,k-1}; `Mat k` is the
  space of k by k real matrices. Dot products are finite sums. `outer x y` is
  xy^T, with (a,b) entry x_a y_b. `sandwich P M` means PMP, without a transpose
  on its last factor. P is assumed symmetric only where explicitly stated.
* A dyad on N units is a two-element subset of {0,...,N-1}. Thus there are no
  dyads for N<2. Its first and second units are its minimum and maximum.
  `neighbors d` consists of all OTHER dyads sharing a unit with d. `overlap d f`
  means nonempty intersection, INCLUDING d=f.
* For finite unit and edge types V,E, let s_d be a two-element subset of V,
  with d -> s_d injective whenever that is hypothesized. E need not contain
  every possible pair, and the statements allow empty finite types. For
  record counts T_d in the nonnegative integers and q_dt in R^k, write

      b_d = sum_t q_dt,
      A = sum_{d,f: s_d intersects s_f} b_d b_f^T,
      D = sum_d b_d b_d^T,
      I = sum_{d,t} q_dt q_dt^T,
      u_i = sum_{d: i in s_d} b_d,
      F_i = sum_{d: i not in s_d,t} q_dt q_dt^T,
      C_i = u_i u_i^T + F_i,
      F_i^ord = sum_{r in {0,1}} F_i = 2 F_i,
      C_i^ord = u_i u_i^T + F_i^ord,
      H_i = sum_{d != f: i in s_d and i in s_f} b_d b_f^T,
      J_i = sum_{d: i in s_d} b_d b_d^T.

  These are respectively `meat`, `dyadMeat`, `individualMeat`, `unitScore`,
  `nonincidentMeat`, `clusterMeat`, `orderedNonincidentMeat`,
  `orderedClusterMeat`, `crossUnitMeat`, and `incidentDiagonalMeat`.
  All double sums are ordered sums. For meat/unitScore/dyadMeat used with
  arbitrary one-record scores q_d, substitute q_d for b_d.
* For finite observations X_d,Y_d, Ghat=sum_d X_d X_d^T,
  betahat=Ghat^{-1} sum_d X_d Y_d, ehat_d=Y_d-X_d^T betahat,
  and Vhat=Ghat^{-1}[sum_{d,f: s_d intersects s_f}
  X_d ehat_d ehat_f X_f^T]Ghat^{-1}. These are `sampleGram`, `sampleOLS`,
  `fittedResidual`, and `sampleVariance`.
* Matrix inverse is Mathlib's nonsingular inverse, equal over R to
  det(G)^{-1} adj(G); it is the zero matrix if det(G)=0. It is not a
  pseudoinverse. Real division is total, so division by zero gives zero.
  `Dreal N` is the REAL expression N(N-1)/2, not natural truncated subtraction.
* `meanVec` and `meanMat` integrate each coordinate. `covMat(f,g)` has entries
  integral (f_a-E f_a)(g_b-E g_b). `varMat(f)=covMat(f,f)`.
  Mathlib Bochner integrals are zero for nonintegrable functions. The distinct
  scalar `ProbabilityTheory.variance` is the nonnegative extended integral of
  |f-E f|^2 followed by `toReal`, which sends infinity to zero. Extended
  integrals in T19 retain infinity, rather than applying `toReal`.
* Positive semidefinite means symmetric with nonnegative quadratic form on
  all real vectors, and positive definite means symmetric with strictly
  positive quadratic form on every nonzero vector. Mathlib uses finitely
  supported test vectors; here the coordinate type is finite, so these are
  the usual notions.
* `inProbability mu f` means: for EVERY real epsilon>0,
  mu_N{omega: epsilon <= ||f_N(omega)||}.toReal tends to zero as natural N
  tends to infinity. It does not itself require source probability measures
  or measurability of f. Both are supplied separately where specified.
  Without set measurability the measure is its outer-measure extension.
  Vector norms are the finite-coordinate maximum norm; matrix norms in the
  enabled elementwise scope are the maximum absolute entry norm. Thus the
  finite-dimensional convergence statements have their usual topology.
* `weakLimit mu f nu` means (1) f_N is a.e. measurable for every N,
  (2) nu is a probability measure, and (3) for EVERY bounded continuous real
  function g, E_mu_N[g(f_N)] tends to integral g dnu. Source probabilities
  are hypotheses of its uses here. This is weak convergence of the laws.
* `centeredNormalLaw nu V` requires V positive semidefinite, nu a probability
  measure, and, for EVERY c in R^k, the image of nu under x -> c^T x to equal
  the real Gaussian with mean zero and variance max(c^T V c,0).
  Positive semidefiniteness makes that maximum redundant. Variance zero is
  a point mass at zero. `IsGaussian` means every continuous linear image is
  a one-dimensional real Gaussian, with its corresponding mean/variance.
* `MemLp f 2 mu` includes a.e. strong measurability and a finite L2 seminorm;
  for real coordinates it is the usual square-integrability condition.
  `IndepFun` is independence of the sigma algebras generated by the two maps.

## Shared one-record probability model

A `Kernel U k` supplies a measurable space U, a measure mu, and functions
X:U x U -> R^k, Y:U x U -> R. Units U_0,...,U_{N-1} have product law mu^N
(`unitSampleLaw`); an unordered dyad {i,j}, i<j, has X_ij=X(U_i,U_j) and
Y_ij=Y(U_i,U_j) (`design`, `response`). Define under two independent units

    G = E[X X^T], beta = G^{-1} E[X Y],
    e(u,v) = Y(u,v)-X(u,v)^T beta,
    z(u,v) = X(u,v)e(u,v), S_N = sum_{i<j} z(U_i,U_j),
    h(u) = integral z(u,v) dmu(v), Gamma=E[h(U)h(U)^T],
    L = 4 G^{-1} Gamma G^{-1},
    W_N = N / (N(N-1)/2)^2 * G^{-1} Var(S_N) G^{-1}.

These expand `populationGram`, `populationBeta`, `populationResidual`,
`populationScore`, `scoreSum`, `projection`, `projectionGamma`,
`limitingTarget`, and `trueTarget`. `epsilon` is e evaluated on a sampled
dyad, not the fitted residual. `coefficientError` is betahat-beta.

`Standing K` is the conjunction, in order, of:

1. mu is a probability measure.
2. X and Y are measurable as functions on U x U.
3. For EVERY u,v, both X(u,v)=X(v,u) and Y(u,v)=Y(v,u).
4. There EXISTS C>=0 such that, for mu x mu almost every (u,v),
   ||X(u,v)||<=C AND |Y(u,v)|<=C.
5. There EXISTS an index j<k such that for EVERY u,v, X(u,v)_j=1.
6. det(G) is nonzero.

`SampleRank K` means there EXISTS a deterministic natural N0 such that for
EVERY N>=N0, Ghat is nonsingular for mu^N almost every sample. It does not
assert nonsingularity on every sample.

`Proposition1Extra K` is the conjunction, in order, of:

1. There EXISTS C>=0 such that for EVERY natural N, for mu^N almost every
   sample, for EVERY dyad d, |ehat_d|<=C.
2. For EVERY a,b<k, G_ab != 0.
3. For EVERY a,b<k, E[X(U_0,U_1)_a X(U_0,U_2)_b] != 0 under mu^3.
4. For ALL SUFFICIENTLY LARGE N, both E[ehat_d^2]!=0 for EVERY dyad d,
   and E[ehat_d ehat_f]!=0 for EVERY d and EVERY f in neighbors(d).

Let F_X be the sigma algebra generated by the WHOLE design map of a sample.
`conditionalResidualMean K d` is E[e_d|F_X]. `conditionalResidualCov K d f`
is E[(e_d-E[e_d|F_X])(e_f-E[e_f|F_X])|F_X]. Write

    Q_df = E[Cov(e_d,e_f|F_X) X_d X_f^T],
    Sigma_print(N) = sum_{d,f: d overlaps f} Q_df.

These expand `conditionalTerm` and `printedSigma`. Conditional expectation
is Mathlib `condExp`: it is zero when the sigma algebra is not a sub-sigma
algebra, its trimmed measure is not sigma finite, or its input is not
integrable. Under Standing, design is measurable, the measure is a
probability, and the inputs here have finite moments; those fallback cases
are excluded. The covariance centers by conditional residual means.
