## Shared definitions, expanded

A design consists of finite types U (population) and Ω (sample outcomes), real
weights w(ω) ≥ 0 with Σω w(ω)=1, and arbitrary Boolean selection indicators
I_k(ω)∈{0,1}. Sample size need not be fixed; zero-weight outcomes remain in Ω.
Write E_D f=Σω w(ω)f(ω), p_k=E_D I_k, p_kl=E_D(I_k I_l), and
Δ_kl=p_kl−p_k p_l. `Positive D` means ∀k∈U, p_k>0. The design variance is
Var_D f=E_D[(f−E_D f)²]. The code does not posit nonemptiness of U; Ω is forced
nonempty by its weight total. All population sums below are finite, and pair
sums are over **ordered** pairs.

For y:U→ℝ, let t̂(ω)=Σk I_k(ω)y_k/p_k, and μ̂=t̂/|U|. Let
Z={(k,l):k≠l and p_kl=0}. The estimators and deterministic quantities are

* H_y(ω)=Σkl with p_kl>0 of I_k I_l(Δ_kl/p_kl)(y_k/p_k)(y_l/p_l), with
  summand zero when p_kl≤0.
* A_y=Σ_(k,l)∈Z y_k y_l.
* A*_(y,a,b)=Σ_Z (|y_k|^a_kl/a_kl+|y_l|^b_kl/b_kl).
* B_(y,a,b)(ω)=Σ_Z [I_k |y_k|^a_kl/(a_kl p_k)+I_l |y_l|^b_kl/(b_kl p_l)].
* C_(y,a,b)=H_y+B_(y,a,b).
* B2_y(ω)=Σ_Z [I_k y_k²/(2p_k)+I_l y_l²/(2p_l)], C2_y=H_y+B2_y.
* A2_y=Σ_Z(y_k²+y_l²)/2 (defined but not directly used in a stub).
* W_(y,m)(ω)=Σk I_k[y_k/(p_k/m)−t̂(ω)]²/[|U|² m(m−1)], where m is a
  natural number but m−1 in this denominator is **real** subtraction.

Real powers of absolute values are Mathlib `Real.rpow`. Its nonnegative-base
meaning is the usual real power, with 0^0=1 and 0^r=0 for r≠0. Real
`a.HolderConjugate b` means a>0, b>0, and a⁻¹+b⁻¹=1; in particular a,b>1.
This was checked in `Mathlib/Basic/Real/ConjExponents.lean:46–59` and
`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Pow/Real.lean:30–50`. The finite-design definitions
themselves are total even at zero denominators (Lean real division by zero is
zero); theorems' explicit positivity conditions must determine when ordinary
fractions are being asserted.

For a measurable space Ξ and a measure μ, E_μ f means the real Bochner integral
∫f dμ. `Integrable f μ` includes almost-everywhere strong measurability and
finite absolute integral. Mathlib assigns integral zero to a nonintegrable
function; `NoiseMoments μ ε v` explicitly requires, for every k, integrable ε_k
and E ε_k=0, and, for every ordered k,l, integrable ε_k ε_l with expectation
v if k=l and 0 otherwise. When used by a theorem, μ is a probability measure.
Thus these hypotheses assert ordinary finite first/second moments and
uncorrelated errors, not independence or Gaussianity.

`GaussianNoise μ ε v` requires every ε_k to be μ-almost-everywhere measurable
with pushforward law N(0,max(v,0)), and the whole family to be mutually
independent (`iIndepFun`, independence of the sigma-algebras generated by the
coordinate functions). All uses in the Gaussian clauses have v>0, so the
`Real.toNNReal` truncation has no effect. Mathlib `gaussianReal` takes a
**variance** parameter. `chiSquaredLaw d` is the pushforward of the finite
product of d standard N(0,1) laws by x↦Σ_j x_j²; d=0 gives a point mass at
zero. Pushforwards here are ordinary ones because of measurability; Mathlib's
arbitrary-Dirac convention for nonmeasurable pushforwards does not apply to
these noise laws. The existential chi-square variables in T19 are constrained
by both their laws and an a.e. identity, without a separate explicit
measurability conjunct.

Measure probabilities are written `(μ event).toReal`. `ENNReal.toReal` would
send infinity to zero, but events under a probability measure always have
finite outer measure. A.e. equality means equality outside a μ-null set.
`Module.finrank` is ordinary finite vector-space dimension on the finite
real-coordinate spaces used here. `Tendsto ... atTop (𝓝 L)` is the ordinary
limit as a natural index tends to infinity; `𝓝[<] 1` is approach to 1 from
below. `StrictMonoOn f (Ico 0 1)` means f(x)<f(y) whenever 0≤x<y<1.

The Mathlib definitions of integrals, integrability, independent functions,
Gaussian laws, products, pushforwards, real/nonnegative coercions, finite
dimension, and filters were followed in their source files. No limit superior
or infinite population sum appears in these stubs.

## Two-stratum definitions

For an integer M>0, U={0,…,M−1}×{0,1}, Ω={0,…,M−1}², each sample has
probability 1/M², and ω=(i,j) selects (i,0) and (j,1). Thus the two stratum
draws are independent and uniform. For arbitrary real α let the two means
be m_0=α,m_1=−α. The defined variance of the two means is their sample
variance, Σ_s(m_s−(m_0+m_1)/2)²/(2−1). Generated populations are
y_(i,s)(ξ)=m_s+ε_(i,s)(ξ). The collapsed estimator is
CS_y(i,j)=(y_(i,0)−y_(j,1))²/4. The stratum variance s_s² is
(Σ_i y_(i,s)²)/M−[(Σ_i y_(i,s))/M]², using divisor M, not M−1.

The functions called heterogeneity and pair-correlation formula are
q(α)=α²/(α²+1/2) and c(α)=α²/(α²+1). The fixed M=5 population uses the same
deviation vector d=(3/2,−3/2,1/2,−1/2,0) in each stratum:
y_(i,s)=m_s+d_i. For a finite design, Cov_D(f,g)=E_D(fg)−E_D f E_D g and
Corr_D(f,g)=Cov_D(f,g)/sqrt(Var_D f Var_D g).

### The periodic design and component definitions

Take natural r>0 and m≥2; let G={0,…,r−1}×{0,1} be q=2r phases. The original
population is the disjoint union of m units per phase, size N=mq. A sample
chooses one phase g, with probability p_g=1/(6r)=1/(3q) if g's second
coordinate is zero (low) and p_g=5/(6r)=5/(3q) otherwise (high), and selects
all its m units. Each low unit has x_k=2 and each high unit x_k=10.
`liftDesign` literally copies the phase selection to every unit of that phase.
For a population z, define Y_g=Σ_j z_(g,j), Q_g=Σ_j z_(g,j)²,
Q=Σ_k z_k². V(z),H(z),C(z),W(z) mean, respectively, Var_D μ̂_z,
E_D(H_z/N²), E_D(C2_z/N²), E_D W_(z,m).

Let R(q)=5(q−1)/(9q−5), c(q)=6q/(9q−5), and
T(z)=C(z)−(1+R(q))H(z). All their denominators are positive at q=2r.
Raw model outcomes are βx_k+ε_k; z_k is the raw value minus its unweighted
finite-population mean.

A `PhaseDecomposition r m z` consists of four populations w,s,l,h with:
Σ_j w_(g,j)=0 for every phase g; s_k=b(x_k−6) for some real b; l is constant

zero; h obeys the analogous high-phase conditions; and z=w+s+l+h pointwise.

are the within-phase-zero-sum space, span(x−6), the centered low-supported
