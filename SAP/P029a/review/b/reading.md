## Common objects and Mathlib semantics

A potential response is `(x,d0,d1,y0,y1)`, with covariate in an arbitrary type X,
binary potential receipts, and real potential outcomes. Write tau=y1-y0,
C={d0=0,d1=1}, and F={d0=1,d1=0}. Receipt under z is Dz=dz and observed outcome
is y1 if Dz=1 and y0 otherwise. Thus exclusion/consistency are built into the
observed-outcome function; they are not separately tested.

For a finite type L, `IsFiniteLaw p` means every p_i is nonnegative and sum p_i=1.
Write P(A)=sum_{i:A(i)}p_i, E(v)=sum p_i v_i, and
E(v|A)=sum p_i 1_A(i)v_i/P(A). Cov(u,v)=E(uv)-E(u)E(v), Var(v)=Cov(v,v), and
Corr(u,v)=Cov(u,v)/sqrt(Var(u)Var(v)). Finite independence means
P(U=a,V=b)=P(U=a)P(V=b) for **every** a,b in the respective codomains;
conditional independence means the analogous conditional cell equality for
every x,a,b with P(X=x)>0. These are ordinary independence notions on the
finite supports, even if their codomains are not finite types.

The finite compliance score is c(x)=P(C,X=x)/P(X=x). Finite latent mean equality
means E(tau|X=x)=E(tau|C,X=x) for every x with P(X=x)>0. Finite receipt mean
equality at x is the pair E(yd|D=1,X=x)=E(yd|D=0,X=x), d=0,1. This is a pair
of mean equalities, not a definition of independence of potential outcomes.

The finite IV bundle means: p is a probability law; 0<P(Z=1)<1;
Z is independent of the **joint tuple** (X,d0,d1,y0,y1); P(F)=0; and
E(D|Z=1)-E(D|Z=0) != 0. Its Wald functional for arbitrary observation weights w
is the ratio of the difference between E(wY|Z=z)/E(w|Z=z), z=1,0, to the same
difference with D replacing Y. A finite arm difference is just one such
normalized difference before taking the Wald ratio.

For measures, P(A) is `(mu A).toReal`, and E(v|A) is the Bochner integral of v
over A divided by P(A). Each universal population assertion here explicitly
requires mu to be a probability measure. The population IV bundle additionally
requires the five response coordinate functions and Z to be measurable and y0,
y1 integrable, then imposes the same joint independence, assignment positivity,
zero defier probability and nonzero first stage. Population c(x),
tau_C(x)=E(tau|C,X=x), latent/receipt mean equalities and weighted Wald are the
corresponding event-integral formulas. Cov(u,v)=integral(uv)-integral(u)integral(v).

Relevant Mathlib conventions checked at source:

* `IndepFun`/`iIndepFun` are independence of the sigma-algebras generated by the
  functions (Probability/Independence/Basic.lean:136,144 and its kernel definitions).
* `Integrable` is a.e. strong measurability plus finite norm integral;
  `MemLp f 2 mu` gives the usual a.e. measurable square-integrability here
  (MeasureTheory/Function/L1Space/Integrable.lean and LpSeminorm/Defs.lean:152).
* A nonintegrable Bochner integral is defined to be zero
  (MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic.lean:158,204). On a probability measure
  event probabilities are finite; ENNReal.toReal maps infinity to zero otherwise.
* Real division is total and a/0=0. Accordingly a conditional mean on a null
  event is zero by definition. Positivity must be established separately when
  interpreting it as an ordinary conditional mean. The definition of latent
  equality only guards P(X=x), not P(C,X=x).
* `Real.log 0=0` and log is a total real function; on positive arguments it is
  the ordinary logarithm. The separately defined `extendedNegativeLog x` is
  +infinity if x=0 and the finite real value -log x otherwise. It is not itself
  restricted to nonnegative x; the assertions using it below have nonnegative
  products. EReal sums have the usual finite-value/+infinity interpretation here.
* `ProbabilityTheory.cond volume (-1,1)` is volume restricted to (-1,1), scaled
  by the inverse of that interval's mass: the ordinary uniform probability law.
* Matrix multiplication, transpose and mulVec are finite-sum operations. Square
  matrix inverse is Mathlib's nonsingular inverse, zero for a nonunit determinant;
  the asserted determinant/unit conditions below exclude that fallback.
* `StrictConcaveOn` includes convexity of the domain and strict Jensen inequality
  for two distinct domain points and positive coefficients summing to one.
  `HasDerivAt` is the ordinary real derivative at the specified point.
* Bernoulli contribution is p^d(1-p)^(1-d) with **natural** exponents; 0^0=1.
  No truncated subtraction occurs in real effect/count formulas. Binomial
  exponents n-k use natural subtraction with k ranging from 0 through n.
