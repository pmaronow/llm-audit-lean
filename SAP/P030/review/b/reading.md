## Common mathematical vocabulary, fully expanded

Indices in Lean are zero based: `Fin N` is the set of integers from 0 through N−1. Below, examples describe units by their one-based labels where explicitly indicated. An assignment is a function z from `Fin N` to {false,true}; its real treatment indicator Z_i is 0 or 1. An assignment set Ω is a finite set, with duplicates removed. A fixed indicator S is another Boolean function. Write F={i:S_i=true}, and

    Ω'(Ω,S,a) = {z∈Ω : for every i∈F, z_i=a_i}.

A potential-outcome schedule Y assigns a real Y_i(z) to every unit and every binary assignment, including assignments outside Ω. The predicates are support-relative:

* `NoEffects Ω Y`: for every i, every z∈Ω, and every z'∈Ω, Y_i(z)=Y_i(z').
* `NoInterference Ω Y`: for every i, every z∈Ω, and every z'∈Ω, if z_i=z'_i, then Y_i(z)=Y_i(z').
* `FixedOutcomeNull Ω' S Y`: for every i with S_i=true, every z∈Ω', and every z'∈Ω', Y_i(z)=Y_i(z').

Let y_F(z) be the vector (Y_i(z)) indexed by the subtype F. Given any function g:(F→ℝ)→(assignments→ℝ), write

    t(z)=g(y_F(z),z),    R(obs,z)=g(y_F(obs),z).

`statistic` is t; `referenceStatistic` is R. The second argument to g is allowed to vary even when the outcome vector is fixed.

A `FiniteLaw A` consists of a finite set K⊆A and a real mass function m on A, with m(z)≥0 for z∈K and Σ_{z∈K}m(z)=1. Values outside K are unrestricted and unused. For any predicate P,

    P_μ(P)=Σ_{z∈K, P(z)}m(z),
    L(x)=P_μ(f(z)≤x),    U(x)=P_μ(x≤f(z)).

Both tails include ties. For a finite Ω, `uniformProbability Ω P` is |{z∈Ω:P(z)}|/|Ω|, with cardinalities cast to real numbers. `uniformLaw Ω h` uses mass 1/|Ω| and requires a proof h that Ω is nonempty. `uniformMean Ω X` is Σ_{z∈Ω}X(z)/|Ω|. Real division in Lean is totalized (a/0=0); whether it is used is part of the audit, not an implicit assumption here.

The printed rejection rule is p<α/2 OR 1−α/2<p. The corrected rejection rule is p_L<α/2 OR p_U<α/2. The conventional two-tail number is p_2=min(1,2 min(p_L,p_U)).

For a real vector a indexed by `Fin n`, its midrank and centered midrank are

    r_a(k)=#{l:a_l<a_k}+(#{l:a_l=a_k}+1)/2,
    q_a(k)=r_a(k)−(n+1)/2.

Every index is counted, so equal values retain their multiplicity. Set C(a,b)=Σ_k q_a(k)q_b(k), V(a)=Σ_k q_a(k)^2. `spearman a b` is ρ(a,b)=C(a,b)/sqrt(V(a)V(b)). `rankCov m a b` is C(a,b)/m. The separately defined printed formula is

    printedRho_m(a,b)=(C(a,b)/m)/((V(a)/m)(V(b)/m)).

`Nonconstant a` means that there exist indices i,j with a_i≠a_j. Mathlib's real square root is the nonnegative square root for nonnegative inputs and is 0 for negative inputs. These formulas are total functions; there is no implicit nonzero-denominator hypothesis.

Given positions x, S, z, the treated variant set is V_z={j:S_j=false and z_j=true}. `nearestDistance x S z i` is min_{j∈V_z}|x_i−x_j| if V_z is nonempty, and 0 otherwise. Its `Finset.inf'` is a genuine minimum on a nonempty finite set.

The remaining shared definitions are expanded where used below. The underlying Mathlib operations are ordinary finite-set filtering, image with duplicates removed, deletion, cardinality, and finite sums; universal finite sums range over every index. `Disjoint` for finite sets means empty intersection. `IsGreatest E x` means x∈E and every member of E is ≤x. `Filter.Tendsto f l₁ l₂` means that every l₂-neighborhood has l₁-large preimage. In particular `nhdsWithin 0 (Set.Ioi 0)` is approach to 0 through positive real numbers, and `nhds A` is the ordinary neighborhood filter of A. Natural subtraction is truncated; integer and real subtraction are ordinary subtraction.

Mathlib definitions consulted: `Data/Finset/{Defs,Filter,Image,Erase,Card}.lean`; `Data/Fintype/Defs.lean`; `Algebra/BigOperators/Group/Finset/Defs.lean`; `Data/Finset/Lattice/Fold.lean` (infimum generated by order duality from `sup'`); `Analysis/Real/Sqrt.lean`; `Order/Bounds/Defs.lean` (`IsGreatest` generated from `IsLeast`); `Order/Disjoint.lean`; `Order/Filter/Defs.lean`; `Topology/Defs/Filter.lean`.

## Shared concrete experiments and assertions

### Five-unit experiment

In one-based unit labels, x=(0,4,8,−1,−2), F={1,2,3}, and Y(z)=(0,1,2,0,0) for every assignment z. For a=0,1, z^a treats only unit a+4. Ω₅={z⁰,z¹}. Fixed-unit outcomes are y₅=(0,1,2), distances d₅^a are the nearest distances for units 1,2,3, and t₅(a)=ρ(d₅^a,y₅).

### Ten-unit experiment

In one-based labels, x_i=i for i=1,…,10, F={1,8,9,10}, z^j treats only unit j, and Ω₁₀={z¹,…,z¹⁰}. The observed assignment is z⁷. The constant schedule is

    Y⁰(z)=(1,2,2,2,2,3,2,3,2,1) for every z.

For row r=0,…,5, the treatment unit is r+2; write w^r=z^(r+2), Ω_T={w⁰,…,w⁵}, y_T=(Y⁰_1,Y⁰_8,Y⁰_9,Y⁰_10), d^r=(nearest distances for units 1,8,9,10), and ρ_r=ρ(d^r,y_T). `tenG(y,z)` computes this Spearman statistic from the four nearest distances and the four entries of y, in that order.

`TableDesign` is the conjunction of all of the following, with no assumptions:

* |Ω₁₀|=10, |Ω_T|=6, and Ω'(Ω₁₀,S,z⁷)=Ω_T.
* The uniform probability on Ω₁₀ of agreeing with z⁷ at all fixed units is 3/5.
* For every j=1,…,10, z^j treats exactly one unit and has probability 1/10 in Ω₁₀.
* For every row r, w^r has probability 1/6 in Ω_T, and that probability equals its unconditional probability in Ω₁₀ divided by the probability of the agreement event.

`TableNumerics` is the conjunction of all of the following:

* y_T=(1,3,2,1), and the six distance rows are

      (1,6,7,8), (2,5,6,7), (3,4,5,6),
      (4,3,4,5), (5,2,3,4), (6,1,2,3).

* (ρ₀,…,ρ₅)=(−1/sqrt(90),−1/sqrt(90),−1/sqrt(90),−5/6,−3/sqrt(10),−3/sqrt(10)).
* For every row, the treated variant set is nonempty and V(d^r)>0; V(y_T)=9/2.
* −3/sqrt(10)<−5/6<−1/sqrt(90)<0, and for every row ρ_r<0.
* |−1/sqrt(90)+0.105|<0.0005, |−5/6+0.833|<0.0005, and |−3/sqrt(10)+0.949|<0.0005.

`WeakMonotoneExample` is the conjunction that, for a=(1,2,3), b=(0,0,1), for every i,j, a_i≤a_j implies b_i≤b_j; V(a)=2; V(b)=3/2; ρ(a,b)=sqrt(3)/2; and sqrt(3)/2 is neither 1 nor −1.

`OutcomeNullNonconstant` is the conjunction: Y⁰ has no effects and no interference on Ω₁₀; Ω'(Ω₁₀,S,z⁷)=Ω_T; the fixed-outcome null holds for Y⁰ on Ω_T; w⁰,w³∈Ω_T; `TableNumerics` holds; and the actual statistics t(w⁰),t(w³), using Y⁰ and tenG, are unequal.

## SAP.P030.T15

Second, for every integers C,M, if 1≤C and 1≤M, both statements hold, with objective J(f)=f(C−f)M:
