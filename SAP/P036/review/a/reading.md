## Definitions and library semantics

All outcomes, coefficients, matrix entries, sums, integrals, variances, and means below are real valued. Cardinalities are natural numbers explicitly cast to reals before real arithmetic. In particular, every displayed size-minus-one denominator uses real subtraction, not truncated natural subtraction.

For a set A of units or sample points, `peers A i` is the real indicator D(i) = 1 if i is in A, and 0 otherwise. This is `Set.indicator` of the constant function 1, with value 0 off A. For k covariates, `covariateContribution X γ ω` is the finite sum C(ω) = sum over j in Fin k of X(ω,j)γ(j), including the empty sum when k = 0. `linearPredictor` is L(ω) = β₀ + β₁D(ω) + C(ω).

`regressorSigma A X` is the inverse-image sigma-algebra of the map ω ↦ (D(ω), X(ω)), into the product of the usual Borel real space and the usual finite product real measurable space. `assignmentSigma A` is the inverse-image sigma-algebra of D alone. Mathlib's `MeasurableSpace.comap` consists exactly of inverse images of measurable sets, and so gives the sigma-algebra generated by these maps. `Measurable X` means measurability into that finite product. `IsProbabilityMeasure μ` means μ(Ω) = 1. Equality `=ᵐ[μ]` means equality outside a μ-null set.

`μ[f | m]` is Mathlib's real conditional expectation given sigma-algebra m. Its definition returns zero when m is not a sub-sigma-algebra of the ambient space, when the trimmed measure is not sigma-finite, or when f is not integrable. In the T1 uses, ambient measurability supplies the sub-sigma-algebra condition, probability supplies finite trimmed measure, and the stated integrability and model equality imply integrability of the relevant response. Thus the intended mathematical reading here is ordinary integrable conditional expectation, with conclusions almost everywhere.

`conditionalArmMean μ A Y` is (integral of Y over A against μ) / μ.real(A). Here μ.real(A) = (μ(A)).toReal; an infinite measure would convert to 0, but every use has a probability measure. The integral is the Bochner integral against the restricted measure μ|A. Mathlib totalizes this integral to 0 for nonintegrable functions, and real division by 0 is also totalized. T1's arm hypotheses and model prevent those cases in its arm-mean clause.

For any finite set s, define n(s) = |s|, m(s,Y) = (sum over i in s of Y(i))/n(s), and Q(s,Y) = sum over i in s of (Y(i) - m(s,Y))². These are `armMean` and `armSS`. `sampleVariance s Y` is v(s,Y) = Q(s,Y)/(n(s)-1). All complements of finite sets below are taken in the entire finite type I. Its finite enumeration is supplied by `Fintype`; `DecidableEq` is a computational equality instance, not a mathematical restriction on finite populations.

For finite population I and treatment set s, `rss s Y b₀ b₁` is the sum over every i in I of (Y(i) - b₀ - b₁D(i))². `olsCoefficients s Y` is defined to be (m(sᶜ,Y), m(s,Y)-m(sᶜ,Y)); the definition alone does not assert that these coefficients minimize RSS. `tableCoefficients k₁ k₀` is (k₀/95, k₁/94-k₀/95), with counts cast to reals. `roundsToThree x d` means d-1/2000 ≤ x < d+1/2000, a half-open rounding interval with upward ties.

The two-entry design row is dᵢ = (1,D(i)), with coordinates a in Fin 2. `gram s a b` is G_ab = sum over i in I of dᵢa dᵢb. `gramInverse` is the explicitly defined matrix H = [[1/n₀,-1/n₀],[-1/n₀,1/n₁+1/n₀]], where n₁ = |s| and n₀ = |sᶜ|. Its being an inverse is a conclusion below, not assumed in the definition. `regressionCoefficient s Y a` is sum over b of H_ab times (sum over i of dᵢb Y(i)).

`olsResidual s Y i` is eᵢ = Y(i)-m(sᶜ,Y)-(m(s,Y)-m(sᶜ,Y))D(i). `leverage s i` is hᵢ = sum over a,b of dᵢa H_ab dᵢb. `hc2Covariance s Y a b` is sum over all i,u,v of H_au [eᵢ²/(1-hᵢ) times dᵢu dᵢv] H_vb. `hc2Variance` is its (1,1) entry, with 1 indexing the treatment slope. `neymanEstimate`, defined but not named in either statement, is v(s,Y)/n₁+v(sᶜ,Y)/n₀.

`binaryHC2Arm n k` is B(n,k) = k(n-k)/(n²(n-1)), using real arithmetic after casts. `tableHC2 k₁ k₀` is B(94,k₁)+B(95,k₀).

`assignments n₁` is the finite set of all n₁-element subsets s of I, implemented by `Finset.univ.powersetCard n₁`. `designExpectation n₁ F` is the uniform finite average of F(s) over those assignments. Mathlib's `Finset.expect` is the finite sum multiplied by the inverse of the cardinality as a nonnegative rational, acting on the reals. An empty average is 0, but the T2 size assumptions ensure the assignment collection is nonempty. `designVariance n₁ F` is the average of (F(s)-average F)², with no sample-size correction.

For fixed potential outcomes Y₁,Y₀ on I, `observedOutcome s Y₁ Y₀ i` is Y₁(i) if i is in s and Y₀(i) otherwise. `populationMean Y` is m(I,Y). `populationVariance Y` is S²(Y) = Q(I,Y)/(N-1), where N = |I|. `unitEffect Y₁ Y₀ i` is τ(i) = Y₁(i)-Y₀(i). `estimatedEffect Y₁ Y₀ s` is the slope component of `olsCoefficients` for the observed response, namely the treated observed mean minus the control observed mean.

`Real.sqrt` is the nonnegative square root for nonnegative inputs and 0 for negative inputs. The numerical inputs asserted below are positive.

Mathlib source definitions checked include `Algebra/Notation/Indicator`, `MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Basic` (comap), `MeasureTheory/MeasurableSpace/Defs` (measurability), `MeasureTheory/Function/ConditionalExpectation/Basic` (conditional expectation), `MeasureTheory/Function/L1Space/Integrable`, `MeasureTheory/Measure/MeasureSpaceDef` (real-valued measure), `MeasureTheory/Measure/Typeclasses/Probability`, `MeasureTheory/Integral/Bochner/Basic`, `MeasureTheory/OuterMeasure/AE`, `Data/Finset/Powerset`, `Algebra/BigOperators/Expect`, and `Analysis/Real/Sqrt`.

## SAP.P036.T1

This theorem asserts all the following clauses together. Quantifiers in one clause do not bind variables in any other clause.

1. For every type Ω, every measurable-space structure on Ω, every measure μ on it, every probability-measure instance for μ, every natural k, every peer set A, every covariate map X : Ω → (Fin k → ℝ), all real β₀,β₁, every coefficient vector γ : Fin k → ℝ, and every pair of real functions Y,ε on Ω, the following implication holds. Assume, in order: A is measurable; X is measurable; ε is μ-integrable; C = sum X_jγ_j is μ-integrable; Y = L+ε μ-almost everywhere; and Eμ[ε | σ(D,X)] = 0 μ-almost everywhere. Then Eμ[Y | σ(D,X)] = L μ-almost everywhere. There is no nondegenerate-assignment, positive k, or identifiability hypothesis in this clause.

2. For every type Ω, measurable-space structure, probability measure μ, set A, real β₀,β₁, and real functions Y,ε, assume in order: A is measurable; ε is integrable; Y = β₀+β₁D+ε almost everywhere; Eμ[ε | σ(D)] = 0 almost everywhere; 0 < μ.real(A); and μ.real(A) < 1. Then both (integral_A Y)/μ(A) - (integral_Aᶜ Y)/μ(Aᶜ) = β₁ and (integral_Aᶜ Y)/μ(Aᶜ) = β₀. Thus both conditioning events have strictly positive probability. This is an unadjusted model with no covariate term.

3. For every type I with a finite enumeration and decidable equality, every finite subset s, and every real response Y : I → ℝ, assume |s| > 0 and |sᶜ| > 0. Then all four assertions hold: every indicator value is 0 or 1; the indicator sums over I to |s|; there exists exactly one pair b in ℝ² minimizing RSS against every competing pair c in ℝ²; and, for every b in ℝ², b minimizes RSS against all c if and only if b = (m(sᶜ,Y),m(s,Y)-m(sᶜ,Y)). The existence-uniqueness quantifier is ∃! b, ∀ c, RSS(b) ≤ RSS(c), followed by the separate ∀ b characterization. No model or binary-outcome condition is imposed on Y.

4. For every finite type I with decidable equality, every finite s, every real Y, and then every natural count k₁,k₀, assume in order: |s| = 94; |sᶜ| = 95; for every i in the entire population Y(i) is 0 or 1; the sum of Y over s is k₁; and the sum of Y over sᶜ is k₀. Then `olsCoefficients s Y` = (k₀/95,k₁/94-k₀/95). There is no separately assumed count bound; binary outcomes and the arm sums imply it.

5. Unconditionally, `tableCoefficients 10 10` = (2/19,1/893).
6. Unconditionally, `tableCoefficients 10 7` = (7/95,146/4465).
7. Unconditionally, 1/893 lies in [0.0005,0.0015), certifying the displayed slope 0.001.
8. Unconditionally, 2/19 lies in [0.1045,0.1055), certifying the displayed intercept 0.105.
9. Unconditionally, 146/4465 lies in [0.0325,0.0335), certifying the displayed slope 0.033.
10. Unconditionally, 7/95 lies in [0.0735,0.0745), certifying the displayed intercept 0.074.

## SAP.P036.T2

This theorem asserts all the following clauses together, again with independent quantifier scopes between clauses.

1. For every finite type I with decidable equality, every finite s, and every real Y : I → ℝ, assume 2 ≤ |s| and 2 ≤ |sᶜ|. Write n₁ = |s|, n₀ = |sᶜ|, and N = |I|. Then:
   - For every a,b in Fin 2, G_ab is N when a=b=0, and n₁ otherwise.
   - For every a,b, both sum over c of H_ac G_cb and sum over c of G_ac H_cb equal 1 if a=b and 0 otherwise. Both left and right inverse identities are asserted.
   - The pair of coefficients obtained as H D'Y is (m(sᶜ,Y),m(s,Y)-m(sᶜ,Y)).
   - For every i, hᵢ = 1/n₁ if i belongs to s, and 1/n₀ otherwise.
   - The slope HC2 variance is the sum over treated i of eᵢ²/[n₁²(1-1/n₁)] plus the sum over control i of eᵢ²/[n₀²(1-1/n₀)].
   - The same HC2 variance is v(s,Y)/n₁+v(sᶜ,Y)/n₀.
   These are deterministic identities for every response and every such assignment; there is no stochastic assumption in this clause.

2. For every finite type I with decidable equality and then every natural n₁,n₀, assume, in order, 2 ≤ n₁, 2 ≤ n₀, and n₁+n₀ = |I|. For every real potential-outcome pair Y₁,Y₀ : I → ℝ, let E be the uniform assignment average of the HC2 slope variance of the observed response, and let V be the uniform assignment variance of the estimated effect. Write S₁² = S²(Y₁), S₀² = S²(Y₀), Sτ² = S²(Y₁-Y₀), and N = |I|. Then all nine assertions hold:
   - The average estimated effect equals m(I,Y₁)-m(I,Y₀).
   - V = S₁²/n₁ + S₀²/n₀ - Sτ²/N.
   - The average treated-arm observed sample variance is S₁².
   - The average control-arm observed sample variance is S₀².
   - E = S₁²/n₁+S₀²/n₀.
   - E-V = Sτ²/N.
   - 0 ≤ Sτ²/N.
   - V ≤ E.
   - E=V if and only if there exists a real c such that, for every i in I, Y₁(i)-Y₀(i)=c. The order is ∃ c, ∀ i; the constant may depend on the given population and potential outcomes.
   The potential outcomes are arbitrary fixed real functions before averaging over assignments. Uniformity is built into the finite expectation definition; independence of unit assignments is not assumed.

3. For every type I (with no finiteness assumption on the whole type), every finite s, and every real Y : I → ℝ, assume 2 ≤ |s|. For every natural k, assume that Y(i) is 0 or 1 for every i in s and that the sum of Y over s is k. Then v(s,Y) = k(|s|-k)/[|s|(|s|-1)], with all arithmetic in the displayed formula real. No condition is imposed on Y outside s.

4. For every finite type I with decidable equality, every finite s, every real Y, and every natural k₁,k₀, assume |s|=94, |sᶜ|=95, Y(i) is 0 or 1 for every population unit, the treated-arm sum is k₁, and the control-arm sum is k₀. Then HC2 slope variance equals B(94,k₁)+B(95,k₀).

5. Unconditionally, `tableHC2 10 10` = 50039/24720919.
6. Unconditionally, `tableHC2 10 7` = 1080506/618022975.
7. Unconditionally, sqrt(`tableHC2 10 10`) is in [0.0445,0.0455), certifying the displayed standard error 0.045.
8. Unconditionally, sqrt(`tableHC2 10 7`) is in [0.0415,0.0425), certifying the displayed standard error 0.042.

## Boundary observations from the blind reading

The only empty finite index allowed without additional size conditions is the covariate index Fin k in T1's first clause; k=0 has the usual meaning of no covariates. Every finite arm mean used in an OLS assertion has a nonempty arm. Every sample-variance or HC2 use has at least two units per relevant arm. Every population randomization assertion has N ≥ 4 and 2 ≤ n₁ ≤ N-2. Conditional arm means have positive probabilities, integrable responses follow from the model hypotheses, and the sigma-algebra conditions for ordinary conditional expectation follow from measurability. The standalone table formulas use fixed positive denominators. No claim or architect's classification has been consulted in making this reading.
