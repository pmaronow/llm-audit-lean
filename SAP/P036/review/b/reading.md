## Expanded definitions and conventions

All outcome and coefficient values below are real numbers. A finite population is a type `I` with a specified enumeration (`Fintype`) and, where requested, decidable equality. Every unqualified sum over `I` means a sum over the entire finite population. A finite arm `s` is a `Finset`, without repeated elements; `sᶜ` is its complement in the entire population. Write `N = |I|`, `n₁ = |s|`, and `n₀ = |sᶜ|` when discussing a particular assignment.

* `peers A i` is the ordinary indicator `1_A(i)`: one for membership in `A`, zero otherwise (Mathlib `Set.indicator`). Coercing a `Finset` to a set means its membership set.
* For `X : Ω → (Fin k → ℝ)` and `γ : Fin k → ℝ`, `covariateContribution X γ ω = Σ_{j=0}^{k-1} X_j(ω)γ_j`. The sum is zero when `k=0`. `linearPredictor A X β₀ β₁ γ = β₀ + β₁1_A + Xγ`.
* `regressorSigma A X` is the pullback (`MeasurableSpace.comap`) of the usual product measurable space on `ℝ × ℝ^k` by `ω ↦ (1_A(ω),X(ω))`. Thus it is the sigma algebra generated by the indicator and all coordinates of `X`. `assignmentSigma A` is the sigma algebra generated by `1_A` alone. Mathlib's real measurable space is the Borel one; the finite vector space uses the coordinate product measurable space. `Measurable X` means coordinatewise measurability relative to the ambient sigma algebra.
* `IsProbabilityMeasure μ` means `μ(Ω)=1`. `μ.real A` is `(μ A).toReal`, hence the ordinary real probability here. The `toReal` convention maps infinity to zero, but all sets have finite measure under the hypotheses used here.
* `f =ᵐ[μ] g` means equality outside a set of μ-measure zero. `Integrable f μ` is almost everywhere strong measurability together with finite integral of its norm (absolute value for real functions).
* `μ[f | G]` is Mathlib's conditional expectation. It is totalized to zero if `G` is not contained in the ambient measurable space, the trimmed measure is not sigma finite, or `f` is not integrable. Under the measurable-regressor, probability-measure, and integrability hypotheses of the statements, these exceptional branches are unavailable; integrability of `Y` follows from its almost everywhere model equation.
* `conditionalArmMean μ A Y = (∫_A Y dμ)/μ(A)`. The integral is the Bochner integral for the restricted measure. Mathlib assigns zero to a nonintegrable integrand, but the model hypotheses imply integrability here; the arm probabilities in its asserted uses are strictly positive.
* Write `m_s(Y) = (Σ_{i∈s}Y_i)/|s|`. This is `armMean`. `armSS s Y = Σ_{i∈s}(Y_i-m_s(Y))²`; `sampleVariance s Y = armSS(s,Y)/(|s|-1)`. The subtraction is in the reals, not truncated natural subtraction.
* `rss s Y b₀ b₁ = Σ_{i∈I}(Y_i-b₀-b₁1_s(i))²`. `olsCoefficients s Y` is defined as the pair `(m_{sᶜ}(Y),m_s(Y)-m_{sᶜ}(Y))`, before any minimization property is asserted.
* `tableCoefficients k₁ k₀ = (k₀/95,k₁/94-k₀/95)` for natural counts, cast to reals.
* `roundsToThree x d` means exactly `d-1/2000 ≤ x < d+1/2000`. This is a width-0.001 half-open rounding cell, with upward ties. There is no general assertion that `d` lies on a decimal grid, but all the displayed constants here do.
* The design row is `D_i=(1,1_s(i))`, indexed by `Fin 2 = {0,1}`; `gram s a b = G_ab = Σ_i D_ia D_ib`. The definition `gramInverse` is the explicit symmetric array `H = [[1/n₀,-1/n₀],[-1/n₀,1/n₁+1/n₀]]`. Calling it an inverse does not assume invertibility: the theorem asserts both inverse products.
* `regressionCoefficient s Y a = Σ_b H_ab Σ_i D_ib Y_i`. The residual definition is `e_i = Y_i-(olsCoefficients s Y).1-(olsCoefficients s Y).2 1_s(i)`. `leverage s i = h_i = Σ_a Σ_b D_ia H_ab D_ib`.
* `hc2Covariance s Y a b = C_ab = Σ_i Σ_u Σ_v H_au [e_i²/(1-h_i) · D_iu D_iv] H_vb`, with all matrix indices in `{0,1}`. Thus this is the finite HC2 sandwich, not a separately defined sample-variance formula. `hc2Variance s Y = C_11`. `neymanEstimate s Y = sampleVariance(s,Y)/n₁ + sampleVariance(sᶜ,Y)/n₀`.
* `binaryHC2Arm n k = k(n-k)/(n²(n-1))`, all arithmetic in the reals. `tableHC2 k₁ k₀ = binaryHC2Arm 94 k₁ + binaryHC2Arm 95 k₀`.
* `assignments n₁` is Mathlib's `univ.powersetCard n₁`, exactly the set of all subsets of `I` having `n₁` elements. `designExpectation n₁ F = (Σ_{s:|s|=n₁} F(s))/binomial(N,n₁)`. This follows from Mathlib `Finset.expect`, the uniform arithmetic average. The empty-set average is totalized to zero, but the randomization clause's size assumptions ensure that assignments exist.
* `designVariance n₁ F = designExpectation n₁ (s ↦ (F(s)-designExpectation n₁ F)²)`, the probability variance with no sample-size correction.
* `observedOutcome s Y₁ Y₀ i` is `Y₁(i)` if `i∈s`, otherwise `Y₀(i)`. These two potential-outcome arrays are fixed across the uniform assignments. `populationMean Y = m_I(Y)` and `populationVariance Y = Σ_i(Y_i-m_I(Y))²/(N-1)`. `unitEffect Y₁ Y₀ i = τ_i = Y₁(i)-Y₀(i)`. `estimatedEffect Y₁ Y₀ s = m_s(Y₁)-m_{sᶜ}(Y₀)` by the preceding definitions.
* All divisions are real division, which Lean totalizes by zero at a zero denominator. Each asserted arm mean, sample variance, inverse, HC2 denominator, population variance, and randomization average is used under explicit size/probability assumptions excluding this issue. The constant numerical expressions likewise have nonzero denominators. `Real.sqrt` is the nonnegative square root for nonnegative inputs, and zero for negative inputs; both numerical variance inputs are positive.

## `SAP.P036.T1`

This theorem has no external hypotheses. It asserts the conjunction of the following independent universally quantified or numerical statements, in the order shown.

1. For every type `Ω`, every measurable space on it, every measure `μ`, every probability-measure structure on `μ`, every natural `k` (including zero), every set `A⊆Ω`, every covariate array `X : Ω→ℝ^k`, every real `β₀,β₁`, every real vector `γ`, and every pair of functions `Y,ε : Ω→ℝ`: if, in order, `A` is measurable, `X` is measurable, `ε` is integrable, `Xγ` is integrable, `Y=β₀+β₁1_A+Xγ+ε` almost everywhere, and `Eμ[ε | σ(1_A,X)]=0` almost everywhere, then
   `Eμ[Y | σ(1_A,X)] = β₀+β₁1_A+Xγ` almost everywhere. There is no positive-arm-probability hypothesis in this clause and no independence hypothesis.
2. For every `Ω`, measurable space, `μ` and probability structure, then every set `A`, real `β₀,β₁`, and functions `Y,ε`: if `A` is measurable, `ε` is integrable, `Y=β₀+β₁1_A+ε` almost everywhere, `Eμ[ε | σ(1_A)]=0` almost everywhere, and `0<μ(A)<1`, then both
   `(∫_A Y dμ)/μ(A) - (∫_{Aᶜ}Y dμ)/μ(Aᶜ)=β₁` and `(∫_{Aᶜ}Y dμ)/μ(Aᶜ)=β₀`.
3. For every finite type `I`, enumeration and decidable equality, every finite subset `s`, and every outcome array `Y : I→ℝ`: if `|s|>0` and `|sᶜ|>0`, then all four assertions hold: (a) for every `i`, `1_s(i)=0` or `1_s(i)=1`; (b) `Σ_i1_s(i)=|s|`; (c) there exists exactly one pair `b∈ℝ²` such that for every `c∈ℝ²`, `rss(s,Y,b)≤rss(s,Y,c)`; and (d) for every pair `b`, that same global-minimum condition holds if and only if `b=(m_{sᶜ}(Y),m_s(Y)-m_{sᶜ}(Y))`. No binary-response assumption is imposed in this clause.
4. For every finite `I` with enumeration and decidable equality, every `s`, every `Y`, and then every natural `k₁,k₀`: if `|s|=94`, `|sᶜ|=95`, every `Y_i` is zero or one, `Σ_{i∈s}Y_i=k₁`, and `Σ_{i∈sᶜ}Y_i=k₀`, then `olsCoefficients(s,Y)=(k₀/95,k₁/94-k₀/95)`. The bound on each count follows from binary outcomes rather than being separately assumed.
5. Unconditionally, `tableCoefficients(10,10)=(2/19,1/893)` and `tableCoefficients(10,7)=(7/95,146/4465)`.
6. Unconditionally, the slope and intercept from counts `(10,10)` lie in the rounding cells for `0.001` and `0.105`, respectively; those from `(10,7)` lie in the cells for `0.033` and `0.074`, respectively.

The probability-model clauses and finite-sample clauses do not share or existentially link a dataset. The count calculations are conditional on the specified counts, rather than a claim that an external dataset actually has those counts.

## `SAP.P036.T2`

This theorem also has no external hypotheses. It asserts the following conjunction.

1. For every finite type `I`, enumeration and decidable equality, every `s⊆I`, and every real outcome array `Y`: if `|s|≥2` and `|sᶜ|≥2`, then:
   * For every `a,b∈{0,1}`, `G_ab=N` when `a=b=0`, and `G_ab=n₁` otherwise, so `G=[[N,n₁],[n₁,n₁]]`.
   * For every `a,b`, both `Σ_c H_ac G_cb=δ_ab` and `Σ_c G_ac H_cb=δ_ab`.
   * `(regressionCoefficient(s,Y,0),regressionCoefficient(s,Y,1))=(m_{sᶜ}(Y),m_s(Y)-m_{sᶜ}(Y))`.
   * For every `i`, `h_i=1/n₁` if `i∈s`, and `h_i=1/n₀` otherwise.
   * The sandwich entry equals
     `C_11 = Σ_{i∈s}e_i²/[n₁²(1-1/n₁)] + Σ_{i∈sᶜ}e_i²/[n₀²(1-1/n₀)]`.
   * It also equals `sampleVariance(s,Y)/n₁ + sampleVariance(sᶜ,Y)/n₀`.
2. For every finite type `I` with enumeration and decidable equality, then every natural `n₁,n₀`, if `n₁≥2`, `n₀≥2`, and `n₁+n₀=N`, then for every pair of fixed real potential-outcome arrays `Y₁,Y₀`, all assertions below hold. Write `S_z²=populationVariance(Y_z)`, `S_τ²=populationVariance(Y₁-Y₀)`, `τ̂(s)=m_s(Y₁)-m_{sᶜ}(Y₀)`, `E=E_design[C_11(s,Yobs(s))]`, and `V=Var_design(τ̂)`, with uniform averaging over all `n₁`-element assignments. Then:
   * `E_design[τ̂]=populationMean(Y₁)-populationMean(Y₀)`.
   * `V=S₁²/n₁+S₀²/n₀-S_τ²/N`.
   * `E_design[sampleVariance(s,Yobs(s))]=S₁²` and `E_design[sampleVariance(sᶜ,Yobs(s))]=S₀²`.
   * `E=S₁²/n₁+S₀²/n₀`.
   * `E-V=S_τ²/N` and `0≤S_τ²/N`.
   * `V≤E`.
   * `E=V` if and only if there exists a real constant `c` such that for every unit `i`, `Y₁(i)-Y₀(i)=c`. This is constancy across all units, not just almost everywhere or over an observed arm.
3. For every type `I` (no finiteness of the entire type is requested), every finite subset `s`, and every `Y : I→ℝ`, if `|s|≥2`, then for every natural `k`, if `Y_i∈{0,1}` for every `i∈s` and `Σ_{i∈s}Y_i=k`, then
   `sampleVariance(s,Y)=k(|s|-k)/[|s|(|s|-1)]`.
4. For every finite `I` with enumeration and decidable equality, every `s,Y`, then every natural `k₁,k₀`, if `|s|=94`, `|sᶜ|=95`, all responses are binary, and the arm sums equal `k₁,k₀`, then `C_11=tableHC2(k₁,k₀)`.
5. Unconditionally, `tableHC2(10,10)=50039/24720919` and `tableHC2(10,7)=1080506/618022975`.
6. Unconditionally, the square roots of these respective variances lie in the three-decimal rounding cells for `0.045` and `0.042`.

The covariance statement is for a two-column unadjusted design with intercept. No covariates enter T2's design. Its randomization moments concern fixed potential outcomes, uniform complete randomization, and expectation conservativeness; they do not assert a comparison of the estimated variance with the true design variance at each individual assignment.
