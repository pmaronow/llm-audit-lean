## SAP.P045b.T33 (P045-T33)

Source: `SAP/P045b/Challenge/Part2.lean:40` (docstring), theorem at line 42. Fingerprint: `24584448e913584d72470a0cbfa5a923fe12f3ab98e92cd1c6a8b1a5e76e66b7`.

The docstring describes an adjusted Horvitz–Thompson (HT) contrast variance estimator with exact positive marginal probabilities. It asserts an exact structural-zero squared-outcome surplus, conservativeness in expectation, and unbiasedness of the point estimator.

### Quantifiers, objects, and all hypotheses

`T33` asserts `T33Statement`, defined at `SAP/P045b/Defs/Part6.lean:121`. Universally and in this order:

1. Arbitrary types Ω and D (independent universes), and a natural number N.
2. A `Fintype Ω` instance (a finite enumerated sample space) and `DecidableEq D` (decidable exposure-label equality). D need not be finite.
3. The hypothesis `0 < N`.
4. A `Design Ω`: a real function p on Ω, with p(ω) ≥ 0 for every ω and Σω p(ω) = 1. These constraints are structure fields; they are not additional antecedents printed in `T33Statement`.
5. An arbitrary deterministic exposure mapping f: Ω × Fin N → D.
6. An arbitrary fixed real outcome schedule y: Fin N × D → ℝ.
7. Exposure labels a,b in D, the hypothesis a ≠ b, and then the two hypotheses, in order, that every unit's marginal probability of a is strictly positive and every unit's marginal probability of b is strictly positive.

The conclusion is the conjunction of the three formulas below. There is no conditioning, independence hypothesis, approximation to probabilities, simulation sample, or estimated outcome schedule. f and y are arbitrary subject to the displayed marginal-probability hypotheses. Observed outcomes within a cell are represented by y(i,d), so the statistic embodies outcomes depending on assignment only through exposure. The theorem is universal over finite designs, not an existential example or refutation.

### Unfolding all SAP definitions to finite sums and real arithmetic

All following sums over units range over the full `Finset.univ` of `Fin N`, and all sums over ω range over the full `Finset.univ` of Ω. Thus double unit sums are **ordered**, include the diagonal unless an explicit i ≠ j test removes it, and do not range over unordered pairs. Put n = (N : ℝ), an exact natural-number cast, and let I_i^d(ω) be 1 when f(ω,i)=d and 0 otherwise. Indicator notation is only shorthand for Lean's `if ... then ... else ...` on propositions.

- `Design`, `Design.mk`, and `Design.mass` (`Defs/Part1.lean:13–16`): the data p and the proofs of nonnegativity and total mass one just listed; `mass` is the projection p.
- `expect p t` (`Defs/Part1.lean:18–19`): E_p[t] = Σω p(ω)t(ω). The generic definition has values in any commutative semiring; here it is used only over ℝ.
- `momentVariance p t` (`Defs/Part3.lean:11–12`): Σω p(ω)t(ω)² − (Σω p(ω)t(ω))². Normalization of p makes this the usual variance.
- `exposureEffect y a b` (`Defs/Part3.lean:14–15`): τ = (Σi [y(i,a)−y(i,b)])/n.
- `exposureMarginal p f i d` (`Defs/Part3.lean:17–19`): π_i(d) = Σω if f(ω,i)=d then p(ω) else 0 = Σω p(ω)I_i^d(ω).

Consequently the fully unfolded conclusion, using only the explicit finite-sum expressions above, is:

1. Σω p(ω)Vhat(ω) − [Σω p(ω)T(ω)² − (Σω p(ω)T(ω))²] = B.

These diagonal terms are included in B. Finite-sum expectation is over the design only; probabilities and the outcome schedule are fixed.

### Junk-value and boundary-case audit

- Real division is total in Lean, with x/0 = 0. `weightedTerm`, `weightedMean`, `exposureEffect`, and `varianceEstimate` would therefore have unphysical zero values for certain zero denominators if called without assumptions. T33 prevents this: N > 0 implies n > 0 and n² > 0, and every w(i,a), w(i,b) is strictly positive. Other exposure labels need not have positive probability, but their weights and outcomes are not used in the theorem's estimators.
- The 1/q terms in V_d and C_ab occur only in branches with q > 0. Their zero-q branches are explicit squared-outcome corrections, not reliance on the value of 1/0. The factor 2 in correction denominators is the usual nonzero real numeral. The helper `_proof_1` is only its typeclass support.
- q is an exact finite sum of nonnegative masses, hence q ≥ 0. The definitions have an uncovered negative-q case (both the positive and zero tests fail), but it cannot occur in T33's instantiation. Same-label diagonal q(i,i,d,d)=w(i,d)>0, so the i ≠ j restriction on the same-label correction omits no zero diagonal. Cross-label diagonal zeros are intended structural zeros.
- The finite Ω type is syntactically allowed to be empty, but no `Design Ω` can then exist: an empty mass sum is 0, contradicting total mass 1. N=0 is syntactically permitted in the definitions but explicitly excluded in the theorem. Empty D, or D with fewer than two elements, cannot supply a ≠ b. These are ordinary impossible parameter choices, not an inconsistency in all hypotheses.
- Nonempty realized exposure cells are not required. If a realized cell is empty, its HT sum is the genuine empty sum 0, with a positive fixed N denominator. There is no division by the random number of exposed units and no empty-cell mean convention hidden here.
- States with p(ω)=0 may have arbitrary f-values. With exact probabilities a zero joint event has zero total probability and contributes nothing to expectations, even if its indicator happens to be true on such null states. All y-values are finite real numbers, so products with zero masses do not create undefined quantities.
- `momentVariance` is a difference of real moments, not truncated subtraction. No natural/integer subtraction, integer division, clipping, or lossy natural-number cast is used. N has the exact ℕ→ℝ cast.
- All expectations are finite algebraic sums of real values; there are no Bochner integrals, non-integrability defaults, conditional laws, or undefined moments. Finite discrete spaces provide measurability and sigma-finiteness if a measure-space interpretation is supplied.
- There is no Real.log, Real.sqrt, rpow, ENNReal.toReal, sInf/sSup, limsup/liminf, asymptotic limit, partial-function default, or choice of a default outcome in this closure.
