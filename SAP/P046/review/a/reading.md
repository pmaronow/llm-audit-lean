## Shared objects and Mathlib semantics

A full-data unit is `(W,X,Y0,Y1)` in `R x {0,1}^3`. The three binary coordinates
are stored as Booleans and converted to the real numbers zero and one. All these
spaces carry their usual product measurable structures. The observed outcome is
`Y=(1-X)Y0+XY1`, and the observed triple is `(W,Y,X)`. Thus consistency and
binary potential outcomes hold by construction, rather than by hypotheses.

A study `M` consists of a measure `P` on this full-data space, two real functions
`pi,m : R -> R`, and a real constant `delta`. `ValidStudy M` means all of:

1. `P` is a probability measure.
2. `P(W=w)=0` for **every** real `w` (an atomless covariate distribution).
3. `pi` and `m` are Borel measurable.
4. `delta>0`, and `delta<pi(W)<1-delta`, `P`-almost surely.
5. `E_P[X | sigma(W)]=pi(W)`, `P`-almost surely.
6. `E_P[Y1 | sigma(W)]=m(W)`, `P`-almost surely.
7. `0<=m(W)<=1`, `P`-almost surely.
8. Each of `Y0` and `Y1` is conditionally independent of `X` given `sigma(W)`.
   No conditional independence between the *pair* `(Y0,Y1)` and `X` is required.

Here the conditioning sigma algebra is precisely the comap of the Borel sigma
algebra by `W`. Mathlib's `CondIndepFun` uses conditional independence of the
sigma algebras generated by the two functions, equivalently the product formula
for conditional probabilities of their measurable inverse images. `condExp`
returns zero when its sigma algebra is unsuitable, its trimmed measure is not
sigma finite, or its input is not integrable. Those exceptional branches do not
apply to the bounded inputs and probability measures above.

`mu=E_P[Y1]`. For every natural `n`, a sample is a function `Fin n -> Unit`, and
`P_n=P^{otimes n}` is Mathlib's finite product measure. It gives independent
identically distributed coordinates; for `n=0` the sample space is a singleton.
Conditioning on the sample covariates means conditioning on exactly
`sigma(W_1,...,W_n)`.

For a sample `a`, write `p_i=pi(W_i)`. The statistics are

* `H_n=(1/n) sum_i X_i Y_i/p_i` (the HT estimator);
* `xi_n=(1/n) sum_i p_i^(-2)`;
* `muW_n=(1/n) sum_i m(W_i)`;
* `r_n(alpha)=sqrt(xi_n log(2/alpha)/(2n))` (printed radius);
* `r'_n(alpha)=(sqrt(xi_n)+1)sqrt(log(4/alpha)/(2n))`;
* `A_n(g)=(1/n) sum_i [X_i(Y_i-g(W_i))/p_i+g(W_i)]` for a fixed `g`.

Every displayed division is real division, including division by the real cast
of `n`. Real inverse at zero is zero, and `Real.sqrt` is zero on negative inputs.
At the sample sizes asserted in finite-sample probability conclusions, `n>0`,
and positivity makes every `p_i` nonzero almost surely. Asymptotic definitions
also include `n=0`, but their limits concern `n -> infinity`.

A `CrossFit n` consists of a deterministic map `s : Fin n -> Bool` and functions
`g_j(a,w)`, `j in Bool`, Borel measurable jointly in the full sample `a` and the
prediction argument `w`. For **all** `j,a,b`, if `observe(a_i)=observe(b_i)` for
every `i` with `s(i) != j`, then `g_j(a,.)=g_j(b,.)` as functions on all real
arguments. Thus the fit indexed by `j` uses only the opposite fold's observed
triples. Its adjusted estimate is
`A_n(F)=(1/n) sum_i [X_i(Y_i-g_{s(i)}(a,W_i))/p_i+g_{s(i)}(a,W_i)]`.
A sequence `F_n` is balanced if, for **each** Boolean `j`,
`#{i:s_n(i)=j}/n -> 1/2`. Neither fold need be nonempty at every finite `n`.

For real functions `g` on covariate space, define

* `q(w)=(1-pi(w))/pi(w)`;
* `R(g)=E_P[q(W)(Y1-g(W))^2]`;
* `||g||_q^2=E_P[q(W)g(W)^2]`;
* `||g||_2^2=E_P[g(W)^2]`;
* `V(g)=Var_P(Y1)+R(g)`;
* `B_H=E_P[m(W)(1-m(W))/pi(W)]+Var_P(m(W))`;
* `Q_T(g)=sum_{i in T} X_i(1-p_i)/p_i^2 (Y_i-g(W_i))^2`.

All expectations written with `integral` are **real Bochner integrals**, which
Mathlib defines as zero if the function is not integrable. In particular,
`l2Sq`, `weightedNormSq`, and `risk` alone do not assert integrability.
`MemLp (g o W) 2 P` means almost-everywhere strong measurability and finite
second moment. Mathlib's real `variance` is the extended second central moment
converted with `ENNReal.toReal`, hence also becomes zero for an infinite second
central moment. Below the bounded outcomes, positivity, and explicit L2
hypotheses prevent this variance convention from affecting asserted variances.
Every probability is first an extended nonnegative real measure of the event,
then converted by `toReal`; valid sample laws have finite total mass one.

The auxiliary convergence predicates have these exact meanings:

* `RootN(M,est)`: for every real `eps>0`, there exist real `C>0` and natural `N`
  such that for every `n>=N`,
  `P_n(sqrt(n)|est_n-mu|>C)<=eps`.
* `NormalLimit(M,est,v)`: `v>=0`, there exists a proof that `P` is a probability
  measure, every scaled error is almost-everywhere measurable, and the laws of
  `sqrt(n)(est_n-mu)` converge weakly to `N(0,v)`. The target at `v=0` is the
  point mass at zero, not an empty or undefined normal law. The `toNNReal` cast
  in this predicate does not truncate a permitted negative variance, since
  `v>=0` is asserted separately.
* `L2InProbability(M,F,j,g)`: for every real `eps>0`,
  `P_n(||g_{n,j}(a,.)-g||_2^2>eps)->0`.
* `L2InMeanSquare(M,F,j,g)`: the expectations
  `E_{P_n}[||g_{n,j}(a,.)-g||_2^2]` converge to zero, **and**, for every natural
  `n`, the real-valued squared-error function of `a` is integrable under `P_n`.
  Its definition alone does not rule out the inner Bochner integral's zero
  convention; T9 separately requires almost-sure L2 membership of every fit.
* `UniformRootN(delta)`: for every real `eps>0` there is a real `C>0`, chosen
  before the study and sample size, such that for **every** valid study whose
  stored constant equals `delta` and **every** natural `n>0`,
  `P_n(sqrt(n)|H_n-mu|>C)<=eps`.
* `ZeroBenchmark(M,G)`: either the identically zero function belongs to `G`, or
  there exists a sequence `h_k in G` whose real weighted squared norms tend to
  zero. In T5 every member of `G` is L2, so these are actual finite norms.

The Mathlib endpoints checked were `ConditionalExpectation/Basic.lean`
(`condExp`), `Probability/Independence/Conditional.lean` (`CondIndepFun` and its
conditional probability characterization), `MeasureTheory/Constructions/Pi.lean`

`Function/LpSeminorm/Defs.lean` (`MemLp`), `Probability/Moments/Variance.lean`
(`evariance`, `variance`), `Function/ConvergenceInDistribution.lean`

## SAP.P046.T2

This theorem has no hypotheses. Let `W,U` be independent uniform variables on
`[0,1]`; set `X=1{U<=9/10}`, `Y0=0`, `Y1=1{W>=1/2}`, `pi=9/10`,

2. `P_100(E)=ofReal((1/2)^100)>0`.

## SAP.P046.T3

For **every** study `M`, assuming `ValidStudy M`, both following clauses hold:

1. For **every** real `alpha` with `0<alpha<1`:
   * for every natural `n>0`, `P_n(r'_n(alpha)<=|H_n-mu|)<=alpha`;
   * for every natural `n>0`, almost surely under `P_n`,
     `r'_n(alpha)<(delta^(-1)+1)sqrt(log(4/alpha)/(2n))`;
   * there exists a real `C>0` (after `M,alpha` are fixed) such that for every
     natural `n>0`, almost surely under `P_n`, `2r'_n(alpha)<=C/sqrt(n)`.
2. For **every** real `b>0` such that `b<=pi(W)` almost surely, for **every**
   natural `n>0`, and then for **every** real `alpha` with `0<alpha<1`,
   `P_n(b^(-1)sqrt(log(2/alpha)/(2n))<=|H_n-mu|)<=alpha`.

No conditional-concentration, decomposition, or union-bound lemmas are conjuncts
of this type. The first probability includes equality at the interval boundary.

## SAP.P046.T5

The conclusion `EfficiencyComparison(M,F,gstar)` abbreviates exactly the three
conjuncts `NormalLimit(M,A(F),V(gstar))`, `NormalLimit(M,H,V(0))`, and
`V(gstar)<=V(0)`. T5 asserts **both** following universal implications.

Working-family clause: for every study `M`, assuming validity; for every natural
`d`, every family `g_theta : R -> R` indexed by `theta : Fin d -> R`, and every
sequence of cross-fits `F_n`, assume, in this order:

1. `F` is balanced.
2. For every `theta`, `g_theta(W)` belongs to L2(P).
3. Zero belongs to the family or there is a sequence of family members with
   weighted squared norms tending to zero.
4. For every natural `n` and Boolean `j`, for `P_n`-almost every `a`, there
   exists a parameter `theta` such that `g_{n,j}(a,.)=g_theta` pointwise on all
   real arguments and, for every parameter `eta`,
   `Q_{T_j}(g_theta)<=Q_{T_j}(g_eta)`.

Then for every deterministic function `gstar`, if it is a member of the family,
if `R(gstar)<=R(g_theta)` for every parameter `theta`, and if both folds'
predictions converge to `gstar` in L2 probability, the efficiency comparison
holds. There is no regularity assumption on the parametrization in `theta`, and
`d=0` is allowed (its parameter space has one element).

Direct clause: for every valid study `M`, every balanced cross-fit sequence `F`,
and every deterministic `gstar` such that `gstar(W)` is L2, assume both folds
converge to `gstar` in L2 probability, **and** for every natural `n` and Boolean
`j` the fitted function `g_{n,j}(a,W)` is L2(P) for `P_n`-almost every `a`.
If `R(gstar)<=R(0)`, the efficiency comparison holds. This clause assumes the
population risk comparison explicitly; it assumes no empirical optimization or
working family.

## SAP.P046.T7

First, for **every** real `delta>0`, `UniformRootN(delta)` holds with the
quantifier order stated above. Second, for **every** valid study `M`, all of:

1. For **every** natural `n>0`, `H_n` is integrable under `P_n` and
   `E_{P_n}H_n=mu`.
2. The variance under `P` of the single-observation summand `XY/pi(W)` is
   `v=E_P[m(W)/pi(W)]-mu^2`.
3. The scaled errors of `H_n` converge in distribution to `N(0,v)`, including
   the asserted nonnegativity of `v` and the point-mass convention at `v=0`.

The uniform constant may depend on `delta,eps`, but not on `M,n`. The statement
does not require that `delta>=1/2` admit a study; its nonempty positivity slices
are `0<delta<1/2`.

## SAP.P046.T8

For **every** study `M`, if valid, for **every** natural `n>0`, and for **every**
cross-fit `F : CrossFit n`, assume that for **each** sample index `i`,
`a -> g_{s(i)}(a,W_i)` is integrable under the **full** sample law `P_n`.
Then `E_{P_n}[A_n(F)]=mu`.

There is no balancing hypothesis, no regression correctness or convergence
hypothesis, and no separate stated integrability hypothesis on the final
adjusted estimator. Its integrability follows from the stated conditions and
positivity. The additional evaluated-prediction integrability is a real
hypothesis of this theorem.

## SAP.P046.T9

This is the conjunction of two implications:

1. For **every** valid study `M`, **every** balanced cross-fit sequence `F`, and
   **every** pair of fixed deterministic functions `g_j : R -> R`, assume:
   each `g_j(W)` is L2(P); for each Boolean `j`, the corresponding predictions
   converge to `g_j` in the precise mean-square sense above (including
   integrability of each outer squared-error variable); and for every natural
   `n` and Boolean `j`, the fitted function is L2(P) for `P_n`-almost every
   training sample. Then `RootN(M,A(F))` holds. The two limits may differ.
2. For **every** valid study `M` and **every** balanced cross-fit sequence `F`,
   assume the fits of **each** Boolean fold converge to the stored true
   regression `m` in L2 probability, and for every natural `n` and Boolean `j`
   the fitted function is L2(P) for `P_n`-almost every training sample. Then the
   scaled adjusted errors converge normally with variance
   `B_H=E[m(W)(1-m(W))/pi(W)]+Var(m(W))`.

The second clause asserts this numerical Gaussian variance; it does not define
a semiparametric model or an efficiency lower-bound comparison against other
regular estimators.

## SAP.P046.T10

For **every** estimator sequence `est_n : (Fin n -> Observed) -> R`, assuming
each `est_n` is Borel measurable, there exist real numbers `eps>0` and `c>0`
such that for **every** natural `N` there exist a natural `n>=N` with `n>0`
and a study `M` for which `ValidStudy M` and
`P_n(|est_n((W_i,Y_i,X_i)_i)-mu(M)|>eps)>=c`.

The same estimator sequence is chosen before the population law and receives
only observed triples, with no input for `pi`. The error threshold and lower
probability bound can depend on that estimator sequence, but not on `N,n,M`.
The adverse law can depend on `n`; no common value of the study's positivity
constant is required across these adverse laws. The assertion is failure of
uniform consistency over the class, not failure of pointwise consistency for
one fixed study.
