## Common objects and conventions

A population unit is a point `u = (W, A, (Y0, Y1))` of `R × Bool × (R × R)`, with the usual product measurable structure (Borel on the real coordinates and the discrete measurable structure on `Bool`). Write `I_a = 1{A = a}`, identify `true` with treatment 1 and `false` with treatment 0, and write `Y_a` for the corresponding potential outcome. The observed outcome is defined, on every point, as `Y = I_1 Y1 + I_0 Y0`. Thus consistency is built into the observation function. The conditioning sigma algebra `G` is `MeasurableSpace.comap W` of the Borel sigma algebra of R: exactly the sigma algebra generated by W.

A `CausalLaw L` consists of a probability measure P on this unit space, a Borel measurable real function e on R, and the following hypothesis, called `Ignorable`: for each arm a and each Borel measurable real function f, if `f(Y_a)` is P-integrable, then

`E_P[I_a f(Y_a) | G] = p_a(W) E_P[f(Y_a) | G]` P-almost surely,

where `p_1(W) = e(W)` and `p_0(W) = 1 - e(W)`. The quantifiers are `for all a, for all f, measurability implies integrability implies the displayed a.s. equality`. It is a condition separately for the two outcomes, not an explicit joint condition on `(Y0,Y1)`. Taking f identically one includes propensity calibration. No bounds on e or outcomes are explicit fields of `CausalLaw`.

The ATE is `theta = integral (Y1 - Y0) dP`; the treated mean is `mu1 = integral Y1 dP`. Define the real scores

`Z = I_1 Y / e(W) - I_0 Y / (1 - e(W))`,

`Z1 = I_1 Y / e(W)`.

The sample space is `N -> UnitData`, with `P_infty = Measure.infinitePi (fun _ => P)`. Mathlib's infinite product is the probability measure with the prescribed finite product marginals; thus its coordinates U_0, U_1, ... are independent and identically distributed with law P. For n a natural number,

`hat_theta_n = (sum_{i=0}^{n-1} Z(U_i)) / n`,

`hat_mu1_n = (sum_{i=0}^{n-1} Z1(U_i)) / n`.

Here n is cast to R for division. All subtraction in the formulas is real subtraction. The definitions give zero for these averages at n=0 using real division by zero, but all finite-sample assertions in T1 and T2 concern n>=1. T2's asymptotic sequence also has an n=0 term; that term does not affect its limit.

`BoundedOverlap L B epsilon` means, in full: `B > 0`, `epsilon > 0`, `epsilon <= 1/2`, for each a the inequality `|Y_a| <= B` holds P-a.s., and `epsilon <= e(W) <= 1-epsilon` holds P-a.s. The outcome bounds are per arm; their finite intersection also holds a.s.

`TreatedMeanModel L epsilon` means, in full: `epsilon > 0`, `0 <= Y1 <= 1` P-a.s., and `epsilon <= e(W)` P-a.s. There is no explicit bound on Y0, no explicit upper bound on e, and no explicit epsilon<=1/2 hypothesis in this model. The underlying `CausalLaw` conditions still apply.

Mathlib details relevant to all three statements:

* Integrals are Bochner integrals. Mathlib assigns zero to a nonintegrable integrand. The stated boundedness assumptions on the known-propensity scores/outcomes, and the binary outcomes in the unknown model, are relevant to whether this fallback can occur.
* `P[f | G]` is Mathlib conditional expectation: an a.s. version of the usual conditional expectation when G is a sub-sigma-algebra, P trimmed to G is sigma finite, and f is integrable. It is defined as zero if one of these conditions fails. Here G is generated by a measurable coordinate and P is a probability measure, so the first two conditions hold. Integrability still needs to be checked where the definitions are used.
* `P.real S` means `(P S).toReal`, where P is an extended nonnegative measure. In these statements P is always a probability measure (also for sample and seed products), so this is a number in [0,1], never a conversion of infinity to zero. Applied to nonmeasurable sets a measure gives outer measure; measurability of the actual events must therefore be checked.
* `Real.sqrt` and `Real.log` are the real square root and real logarithm with Mathlib's total extensions outside the usual domains. In the finite-sample width assertions n>=1 and 0<alpha<1 make their displayed arguments positive.
* `variance Z P` is `(integral^- ||Z - integral Z dP||_e^2 dP).toReal`. It is ordinary variance when the second moment is finite; infinite extended variance would be converted to zero. `v.toNNReal` truncates a negative real v to zero.
* `gaussianReal m v` is the real Gaussian probability measure of mean m and variance the nonnegative real v; at v=0 it is the Dirac measure at m.
* `TendstoInDistribution X atTop id measures nu` asserts a.e. measurability of every X_n and weak convergence of their pushforward probability measures to nu (the pushforward of nu by id).
* Suprema and infima here are real conditional suprema/infima. Each risk set is made nonempty by inserting zero, each coverage set by inserting one. Every element is in [0,1]; hence all these extrema are genuine bounded real extrema, including when the population class is empty. `Filter.limsup` is the infimum of eventual upper bounds, and `Filter.liminf` the supremum of eventual lower bounds, along the natural-number `atTop` filter. For these bounded sequences they have their ordinary meanings.

## SAP.P047.T1

The theorem is the conjunction of the following two universally quantified assertions. There are no theorem-level hypotheses beyond the hypotheses inside this proposition.

First, for every `CausalLaw L` and every real B and epsilon, if `BoundedOverlap L B epsilon`, then all of the following hold:

1. `E_P[Z | G] = E_P[Y1 - Y0 | G]` P-a.s.
2. `integral Z dP = theta`.
3. `|Z| <= B/epsilon` P-a.s.
4. For every natural n, if n>=1, then:
   * `integral hat_theta_n dP_infty = theta`;
   * for every real t>=0,
     `P_infty(t <= |hat_theta_n-theta|) <= 2 exp(-n t^2 epsilon^2 / (2 B^2))`;
   * for every real alpha, if alpha>0 and alpha<1, set
     `h_n = (B/epsilon) sqrt(2 log(2/alpha)/n)`.
     The closed random interval is `C_n = [hat_theta_n-h_n, hat_theta_n+h_n]` and its defined width is upper endpoint minus lower endpoint, not a separate set-diameter operation. Then all the following hold:
     - `P_infty(theta in C_n) >= 1-alpha`;
     - `h_n >= 0`;
     - for every sample s (not just a.s.), `width(C_n(s)) = 2 h_n` and
       `width(C_n(s)) = ((2 B/epsilon) sqrt(2 log(2/alpha))) / sqrt(n)`;
     - `sqrt(n) (2 h_n) = (2 B/epsilon) sqrt(2 log(2/alpha))`.

The quantifier nesting in item 4 is `for all n, n>=1 implies (unbiasedness and (for all t, t>=0 implies tail bound) and (for all alpha, alpha>0 implies alpha<1 implies all the coverage/width conclusions))`. In particular, B and epsilon are common population bounds fixed before n, t and alpha.

Second, for every `CausalLaw L` and every real epsilon, if `TreatedMeanModel L epsilon`, then:

1. `0 <= Z1 <= 1/epsilon` P-a.s.
2. For every natural n>=1:
   * `integral hat_mu1_n dP_infty = mu1`;
   * for every real t>=0,
     `P_infty(t <= |hat_mu1_n-mu1|) <= 2 exp(-2 n epsilon^2 t^2)`;
   * for every real 0<alpha<1, with
     `h1_n = (1/epsilon) sqrt(log(2/alpha)/(2n))`,
     `P_infty(mu1 in [hat_mu1_n-h1_n, hat_mu1_n+h1_n]) >= 1-alpha`.

The second component does not explicitly assert a conditional-mean identity for Z1, its population expectation, or a separate width equality for this treated-mean interval; it does assert unbiasedness of every positive-size treated-mean sample average.

## SAP.P047.T2

For every real B and epsilon, with hypotheses in order B>0, epsilon>0, epsilon<=1/2, the following conjunction holds:

1. Define

   `R_{B,epsilon}(K) = sup({0} union { P_infty(K < sqrt(n)|hat_theta_n-theta|) : L is a CausalLaw satisfying BoundedOverlap L B epsilon, n is natural, n>=1 })`.

   Then `R_{B,epsilon}(K) -> 0` as the real variable K tends to positive infinity. The supremum ranges jointly over all such population laws and all positive sample sizes. Its event has a strict inequality.

2. For every `CausalLaw L`, if `BoundedOverlap L B epsilon`, then:
   * for every natural n>=1 and every real K>=0,
     `P_infty(K <= sqrt(n)|hat_theta_n-theta|) <= 2 exp(-K^2 epsilon^2/(2 B^2))`;
   * `Var_P(Z) <= B^2/epsilon^2`;
   * the sequence of random variables `sqrt(n)(hat_theta_n-theta)`, under the same sample probability measure P_infty for each n, converges in distribution as n tends to infinity to `N(0, (Var_P(Z)).toNNReal)`, including a possible Dirac mass at zero.

The first conclusion is uniform over L and n for common B and epsilon. The normal limit in the second conclusion is separately for each fixed L; it does not quantify a common Gaussian approximation uniformly over laws. There is no positive-variance hypothesis.

## Additional objects for SAP.P047.T3

`uniform01` is real Lebesgue measure restricted to `(0,1]`; its total mass is one. `JointIgnorable L` asserts, for every arm a and Borel measurable `f : R^2 -> R` such that `f(Y0,Y1)` is P-integrable,

`E_P[I_a f(Y0,Y1) | G] = p_a(W) E_P[f(Y0,Y1) | G]` P-a.s.

`UnknownModel L` is exactly the conjunction:

1. the pushforward of P by W is uniform01;
2. `JointIgnorable L`;
3. for each arm a, `Y_a` equals zero or one P-a.s.;
4. `1/4 <= e(W) <= 3/4` P-a.s.

L is still a `CausalLaw`, so its separately stated ignorability, measurable e and probability requirements also apply. There are no smoothness requirements on e or the conditional outcome laws.

An observed unit is `(W,A,Y)` in `R × Bool × R`. For a natural n, `observedSample n` returns the function on `Fin n` listing these triples for sample indices 0 through n-1. A procedure receives exactly this finite observed sample and a random seed; it is not given L, e, Y0 or Y1.

`fairBool` assigns probability 1/2 to each Boolean value. `baselineLaw` is the pushforward of `uniform01 × (fairBool × fairBool)` under

`(w,a,b) -> (w,a,(0, if b then 1 else 0))`.

Thus the fixed baseline population has independent uniform W, fair treatment A and fair binary Y1, with Y0=0. Its measure is asserted to be a probability measure by an instance, but none of T3's conclusions separately assert that a `CausalLaw` with this measure satisfies `UnknownModel`.

Fix an arbitrary type R in universe u with an arbitrary measurable-space structure, a probability measure Q on R, and measurable procedures as below. Probabilities for a population L are under `P_infty × Q`. This product means the seed is independent of the whole sample, with the same seed law Q for all populations and n.

For real-valued estimators `E_n : ((Fin n -> ObservedUnit) × R) -> R`, define

`risk_n(eta) = sup({0} union { (P_infty × Q)(eta <= |E_n(observedSample_n, seed)-theta_L|) : L satisfies UnknownModel })`,

`strictRisk_n(eta) = sup({0} union { (P_infty × Q)(eta < |E_n(observedSample_n, seed)-theta_L|) : L satisfies UnknownModel })`.

For interval endpoints `lo_n, hi_n : ((Fin n -> ObservedUnit) × R) -> EReal`, define

`coverage_n = inf({1} union { (P_infty × Q)(lo_n <= theta_L <= hi_n) : L satisfies UnknownModel })`.

Here the finite real theta_L is embedded into extended reals. No hypothesis orders the endpoints or makes them finite. For finite ordered endpoints the coverage event is membership in their closed interval; reversed endpoints give empty coverage.

Finally, under the single baseline sample law and independent seed Q, define

`widthProb_n = (baselineLaw_infty × Q)(lo_n + 1/6 < hi_n)`.

The addition and strict comparison in this expression are in `EReal`. For finite endpoints it is exactly the event `hi_n-lo_n > 1/6`. For infinite endpoints the written extended-real formula, not subtraction or a separate diameter convention, defines the event. In particular an interval with a finite endpoint and an infinite opposite endpoint, or the whole extended line, satisfies it; equal infinite endpoints do not.

## SAP.P047.T3

For every type R in universe u, every measurable-space instance on R, every probability measure Q on R, the following two assertions hold together:

1. For every sequence of real-valued estimators E_n with the input types above, if E_n is measurable for every natural n, then both:
   * for every natural n>=1, `risk_n(1/12) >= 1/2`;
   * there exists a real eta>0 (after E, so eta may depend on the procedure and seed model) such that `limsup_{n->infinity} strictRisk_n(eta) > 0`.

2. For every two sequences of extended-real endpoints lo_n and hi_n with the input types above, if every lo_n is measurable and every hi_n is measurable, then for every real alpha>0 with alpha<1/2, the additional hypothesis

   `liminf_{n->infinity} coverage_n >= 1-alpha`

   implies the conjunction

   `liminf_{n->infinity} widthProb_n >= 1-2 alpha`

   and `1-2 alpha > 0`.

Thus the width conclusion uses the same one fixed baseline distribution for every n, while the hypothesis is asymptotic coverage uniformly over the entire specified unknown-propensity population class. No consistency, unbiasedness, finite width, endpoint ordering, or nonrandomization hypothesis is imposed on procedures. The finite error statement uses weak inequality at 1/12; the asymptotic error statement uses strict inequality at a separately existential positive eta. The theorem does not explicitly quantify over model classes containing `UnknownModel`, and does not formulate a separate testability or sufficiency assertion.
