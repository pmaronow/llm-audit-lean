# P002: Randomization-based confidence sets for the local average treatment effect

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P002.json)

P. M. Aronow, Haoge Chang, and Patrick Lopatto (2026). Randomization-based confidence sets for the local average treatment effect. Biometrika 113(2):asag010.

DOI: [10.1093/biomet/asag010](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asag010)

arXiv: [2404.18786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18786)

## Overview

The work constructs confidence sets for the local average treatment effect by inverting studentized Anderson–Rubin randomization tests, with and without covariate adjustment. The Lean development covers finite-sample validity under constant additive effects, corrected asymptotic coverage lower bounds, regression and variance identities, and correctness of corrected algorithms for computing Monte Carlo confidence sets. Counterexamples distinguish failures of printed variance bounds and coverage equalities from the lower-bound coverage conclusions.

Areas: Randomization inference, Causal identification, Optimization and computation.

## Key results

Under complete randomization, exclusion, monotone receipt, a nonempty complier set and a constant additive receipt effect, both full-distribution confidence sets cover the LATE with probability at least $`1-\alpha`$ for $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$.

With centered covariates, invertible arm Gram, instrument Gram and IV cross matrices, and a nonzero adjusted receipt contrast, the coefficient on receipt in the interacted IV regression equals the adjusted Wald ratio.

For $`M\ge1`$ simulated Anderson–Rubin curves and $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$, with the observed and simulated curves regular and generated from common data, the Monte Carlo quantile-index set is constant on every nonempty open interval containing no intersection of distinct simulated curves and is included in the index set at each finite endpoint.

Algorithm 1 has a terminating run for every such list, and Algorithm 2 does when the list is nonempty; every run returns the Monte Carlo confidence set. For an empty list, comparison with any curve in the quantile-index set at any parameter value gives the same confidence set.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed Lemma 5 and bound (E.10) use $`1-\delta`$ for every $`\delta\lt 1`$; an admissible 12-unit population with $`\delta=-1/2`$ has actual randomization variances $`5/396`$ and $`36/55`$, both strictly below those bounds.

[`AronowAudit.P002.negative_delta_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P002/P002_Audit.lean#L781) · [P002-T1](#p002-t1)

The printed Lemma 5 variance lower bound uses $`1-\delta`$; replacing that factor by $`1-\max\{\delta,0\}`$ gives a valid bound for every population in the same parameter class $`\Theta_n`$.

[`AronowAudit.P002.Wave1.corrected_lemma5_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P002/Wave1/Gap/GLP0021B1.lean#L699) · [P002-T2](#p002-t2)

The printed oracle variance bound (E.10) uses $`1-\delta`$; replacing that factor by $`1-\max\{\delta,0\}`$ gives a valid finite-population bound under the same $`\Xi_n`$ assumptions with positive covariate dimension.

[`AronowAudit.P002.Wave1.corrected_E10_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P002/Wave1/Gap/GP0021B2.lean#L680) · [P002-T2](#p002-t2)

## Caveats

- Potential outcomes and receipt decisions are fixed; the probability law is uniform over all subsets of the prescribed treatment-arm size, and exclusion is encoded by making outcomes depend on receipt alone.
- Adjusted regressions use a fixed residual-sum-of-squares minimizer on singular arms; finite sharp-null coverage includes this deterministic extension, while the IV identity explicitly assumes the required matrices are invertible.
- The corrected Theorems 1 and 2 give asymptotic coverage lower bounds. Corrected Remark 3 additionally establishes nominal limiting coverage and equality of the worst-case liminf for the unadjusted method when an admissible strictly increasing sequence has constant effects and positive baseline variance. The lower-bound limit filters exclude sizes with empty parameter classes; a bounded admissible size set gives a vacuous asymptotic case.
- The algorithm theorem concerns the corrected index pairing, initialization and squared critical-value formulas; it assumes common-data regularity with positive variance denominators for every real parameter, and full-rank arm designs in the adjusted branch.
- Finite coverage for the full randomization distribution and algorithmic correctness for a fixed finite collection of simulated assignments are separate results; neither statement alone certifies the replication software.

The main Lean statement is Lemma 20 with the denominator of (E.61) corrected as in P002-E003, and it contains all four claims of the lemma. It concerns an increasing sequence of sample sizes, a model in $`\Xi_n(k,\delta,\tilde\varepsilon,A,B,r)`$ at each size, with $`k\ge1`$ and the constants of Theorem 2, and assignments defined on one probability space, each with the law of complete randomization. It supposes that the population residuals $`\varepsilon_i(1,\beta_n)`$ and $`\varepsilon_i(0,\beta_n)`$ have positive variance at every size. The Supplement supposes throughout its proofs that the variances $`\sigma^2_{w(a)}`$ are positive, and it says that similar comments apply with covariates (p. 23).
