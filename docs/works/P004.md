# P004: Design-Based Inference for Spatial Experiments under Unknown Interference

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P004.json)

Ye Wang, Cyrus Samii, Haoge Chang, and P. M. Aronow (2025). Design-Based Inference for Spatial Experiments under Unknown Interference. Annals of Applied Statistics 19(1):744–768.

DOI: [10.1214/24-AOAS1986](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOAS1986)

arXiv: [2010.13599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13599)

## Overview

The work studies average marginalized effects in spatial experiments where assignments at intervention nodes can affect outcomes elsewhere. The Lean development covers Horvitz–Thompson unbiasedness, design invariance under additive effects, and central limit theorems for Horvitz–Thompson and Hájek estimators under local interference and variance nondegeneracy. It also develops corrected formulas and counterexamples concerning estimated propensity scores, spatial variance estimation, Monte Carlo tests, aggregation across distance bands, and the existence of coverage limits.

Areas: Interference and networks, Experimental design and estimation, Asymptotic statistics.

## Key results

Under independent Bernoulli assignment with node probabilities strictly between zero and one and arbitrary interference, the Horvitz–Thompson estimator is exactly unbiased for the average marginalized effect; the observational version uses the corrected control weights $`1/(1-p_i)`$ in place of the printed $`1/p_i`$.

[`AronowAudit.P004.ht_unbiased`](../../AronowAudit/P004/P004_Spatial.lean#L287) · [P004-T39](#p004-t39), [P004-T11](#p004-t11)

For additive spatial potential outcomes and well-defined spatial averages, every normalized nonnegative marginal assignment law gives the same point, band, and average marginalized effects, determined by the additive response functions.

[`AronowAudit.P004.additive_spatial_design_invariance`](../../AronowAudit/P004/P004_Spatial.lean#L2156) · [P004-T40](#p004-t40)

With a fixed Bernoulli probability in $`(0,1)`$, uniformly bounded outcomes, local interference, bounded fixed-radius node counts, well-defined spatial averages, and an eventual positive lower bound on $`N\mathrm{Var}(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{HT}})`$, the centered Horvitz–Thompson estimator divided by its actual standard deviation converges to the standard normal law.

[`AronowAudit.P004.ht_central_limit`](../../AronowAudit/P004/P004_SpatialCLTCompletion.lean#L238) · [P004-T41](#p004-t41)

Under the corresponding spatial locality conditions and an eventual positive lower bound on $`N`$ times the variance of the corrected Hájek linearization, the centered Hájek estimator divided by that linearization's standard deviation converges to the standard normal law.

## Corrections and counterexamples

Supplementary equation (36) claims a negligible root-$`N`$ difference between the printed linearization and IPW with fitted logistic propensities; Lean proves a cyclic three-type spatial counterexample satisfying overlap, interiority, homophily, and variance guards in which that difference is not $`o_p(N^{-1/2})`$.

[`AronowAudit.P004.Wave1.ipw_linearization_counterexample_nat`](../../AronowAudit/P004/Wave1/Gap/DecideP004T1.lean#L453) · [P004-T1](#p004-t1)

The printed observational Proposition 4 combines linearization, variance, and normal-limit assertions; Lean refutes this conjunction through a spatial counterexample to its eventual variance identity, without separately refuting its other conjuncts in this declaration.

[`AronowAudit.P004.Wave1.printed_ipw_proposition_refuted`](../../AronowAudit/P004/Wave1/IpwProposition.lean#L30) · [P004-T35](#p004-t35)

The printed observational SAH formula applies inverse-propensity weights again to already weighted residuals; for deterministic arm fits, symmetric self-containing neighborhoods, interior Bernoulli probabilities, and zero residual covariance outside neighborhoods, Lean proves that the corrected once-weighted raw-residual SAH has expectation at least the variance of the mean residual contrast.

[`AronowAudit.P004.raw_sah_bound`](../../AronowAudit/P004/P004_Spatial.lean#L842) · [P004-T4](#p004-t4)

The printed Monte Carlo rule claims an exact level-$`\alpha`$ test; Lean proves, for $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$, level at most $`\alpha`$ for the corrected absolute-statistic rule $`(1+B)/(P+1)`$ under any finite assignment law with the observation and $`P\ge1`$ resamples drawn independently from that law, and bounds the raw $`B/P`$ rule by $`\alpha+(1-\alpha)/(P+1)`$.

[`AronowAudit.P004.Wave1.mc_exact_level`](../../AronowAudit/P004/Wave1/Gap/FidP004.lean#L15) · [P004-T13](#p004-t13)

The area-effect reading of the summed distance-specific estimates would identify an unweighted sum of bin effects with an area-weighted regional effect; Lean refutes the corresponding universal population equality using positive bin masses and binary outcomes.

[`AronowAudit.P004.ConfidenceFollowup02.printed_area_sum_refuted`](../../AronowAudit/P004/P004_ConfidenceCompletion.lean#L15) · [P004-T37](#p004-t37)

The printed experimental Proposition 4 requires ordinary coverage limits; Lean refutes its universal conjunction by a spatial sequence whose SAH coverage at $`\alpha=1/2`$ has distinct subsequential limits, leaving conservative liminf coverage and the separate HAC clause unrefuted by this declaration.

## Caveats

- Spatial averages require positive finite region mass and integrability; the formal setting permits general metric measurable spaces, including the paper's planar setting.
- The central limit theorems require uniform spatial locality conditions and nondegeneracy of the relevant actual variance; the Hájek theorem uses the corrected control denominator $`N(1-p)`$ in its linearization and assigns zero means to empty arms.
- The three-type IPW witness uses natural-number remainder before real coercion to obtain the intended cyclic types; its theorem establishes failure of the printed expansion, without a separate limiting-coverage assertion.
- The selected SAH correction is a finite expectation bound for deterministic fits and specified covariance neighborhoods, rather than a complete fitted-residual coverage theorem.
- The Monte Carlo correction counts ties as exceedances and abstracts sharp-null outcomes into a fixed assignment statistic; both observed and simulated assignments have the same law.
- The aggregation refutation concerns population effects represented by bin masses, rather than the numerical empirical estimates or their reported randomization p-value.
- Near-epoch-dependence results elsewhere in the development use the corrected neighborhood-count bound $`C_b(1+s^2)`$, with one constant and one eventual sample-size threshold for all radii $`s\ge0`$; the printed $`C_bs^2`$ bound fails at radius zero, and the formal quantifier order is stronger than the literal printed one.

Proposition 4 of the article

**Kind:** `headline_result`

**Location:** p. 755 (PDF p. 12), equations (19)–(20), C5, Proposition 4, footnotes 10–12; proof: Supplement A, pp. 6–11 (A.4.1 Proposition 1, A.4.2 Proposition 2, A.4.3)

The universal coverage claim refuted by the main Lean statement concerns a sequence of spatial designs under C1 with $`0\lt p\lt 1`$, C2, C3 and C4, circle averages that are well defined for all large $`N`$, a distance $`d\ge0`$ and a cutoff $`h\ge0`$ valid in C3 at $`d`$, and a constant $`v\gt 0`$ such that $`N\times\mathrm{AVar}(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{HA}}(d))\ge v`$ for all large $`N`$. For each $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$ the claim requires item (i): the probability $`\Pr\Biggl(z_{\frac\alpha2}\le\frac{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{HA}}(d)-\mathrm{AME}(d;\eta)}{\sqrt{\widehat{\mathrm V}_{\mathrm{SAH}}(d)}}\le z_{1-\frac\alpha2}\Biggr)`$ has a limit, and the limit is at least $`1-\alpha`$. The claim also requires item (ii), the same with $`\widehat{\mathrm V}_{\mathrm{HAC}}(d)`$, if the sum in C5 is nonnegative for all large $`N`$. The kernel is uniform with cutoff $`h`$, the neighbourhoods are $`\mathcal B(i;d)=\{j:d_{ij}\le h\}`$, and $`\widehat{\mathrm V}_{\mathrm{HAC}}(d)`$ is the $`(2,2)`$ entry of (19). Two supporting statements for a fixed $`N`$ give the bounds behind the two items. The first is the identity of Lemma A.4, which states that the difference $`\tilde{\mathrm V}_{\mathrm{HA}}-\mathrm{Var}(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm L}_{\mathrm{HA}}(d))`$ equals $`N^{-2}`$ times the double sum of C5, together with the equivalence between a nonnegative double sum and $`\tilde{\mathrm V}_{\mathrm{HA}}\ge\mathrm{Var}(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm L}_{\mathrm{HA}}(d))`$. Where a variance estimate is zero or negative, the ratio has the value that Lean’s conventions give it, since the square root of a negative number and a quotient by zero are zero.

Lemma C.4 and Proposition 5 of Supplement A, Appendix C

**Kind:** `headline_result`

**Location:** Supplement A, p. 28 (Lemma C.4); p. 29 (Proposition 5, equation (80)); assumptions on pp. 25–27; proof on pp. 28–30; announced in Section 6.5, p. 759 (PDF p. 16)

The main Lean statement concerns a sequence of spatial designs at a fixed $`d\ge0`$, with the circle averages well defined for all sufficiently large $`N`$, under C1 with $`0\lt p\lt 1`$, C2, and the conditions C3 and C4 of Appendix C.
