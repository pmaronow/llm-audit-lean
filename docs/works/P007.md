# P007: Listwise Deletion in High Dimensions

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P007.json)

J. Sophia Wang and P. M. Aronow (2023). Listwise Deletion in High Dimensions. Political Analysis 31(1):149–155.

DOI: [10.1017/pan.2022.5](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2022.5)

arXiv: [2101.11470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11470)

## Overview

The work bounds the probability that listwise deletion removes every row when the number of variables, or groups of variables, grows. The Lean development proves the finite lower bounds and superlogarithmic-growth limits, repairs null-prefix and endpoint arguments, and corrects the interpretation and integer inversion of the probability bounds. It also proves exact retention-counting identities and arithmetic consequences of the archived State Failure missingness log; these are conditional checks of counts and bounds, not a reconstruction of the dataset.

Areas: Probability, Asymptotic statistics.

## Key results

For positive numbers of rows and variables, mutually independent rows and a sequential conditional observation bound $`q_*\in[0,1)`$ imply that the probability of deleting all rows is at least $`(1-q_*^k)^n`$.

[`AronowAudit.P007.lemma3`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_Probability.lean#L197) · [P007-T5](#p007-t5), [P007-T14](#p007-t14)

With one missingness indicator per variable group, mutual row independence and the same sequential observation bound give an all-row deletion probability of at least $`(1-q_*^g)^n`$ for positive $`n`$ and $`g`$.

[`AronowAudit.P007.lemma8`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_Probability.lean#L213) · [P007-T15](#p007-t15)

For row-independent array laws that may change with $`n`$, one fixed sequential observation bound $`q_*\lt 1`$ and $`k_n/\log n\to\infty`$ imply that the all-row deletion probability tends to one.

[`AronowAudit.P007.proposition5`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_Probability.lean#L425) · [P007-T16](#p007-t16)

For the grouped indicator model with changing row-independent laws, one fixed sequential observation bound $`q_*\lt 1`$ and $`g_n/\log n\to\infty`$ imply that the all-row deletion probability tends to one.

[`AronowAudit.P007.proposition10`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_Probability.lean#L440) · [P007-T17](#p007-t17)

For any fixed array with $`n\gt 0`$, uniformly choosing $`k\le K`$ distinct columns retains an expected row fraction $`\sum_i\binom{r_i}{k}/[n\binom{K}{k}]`$, where $`r_i`$ is the number of observed cells in row $`i`$.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed State Failure threshold implies more than 99% expected loss at four variables; every $`8580\times1205`$ array with 3,482,533 observed cells instead has expected four-variable loss at most $`1898556329293/1923069889510\lt 0.99`$.

[`AronowAudit.P007.fourVariablesCannotLoseMoreThan99Percent`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_StateFailure.lean#L105) · [P007-T1](#p007-t1), [P007-T2](#p007-t2)

Against the printed 66.8% missingness and four-variable threshold, any binary mask with 8,580 rows and 1,224 columns, whose pre-removal column-average missingness lies within $`10^{-9}`$ of 0.668390827582004 and whose 19 designated removed columns are wholly missing, has missingness $`6856367/10338900`$ among the 1,205 retained columns, rounding to 66.3%, and expected four-variable loss strictly below 99% under uniform column selection without replacement.

[`AronowAudit.P007.logged_mask_correction`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_StateFailure.lean#L304) · [P007-T2](#p007-t2), [P007-T12](#p007-t12)

The proofs of Lemmas 3 and 8 identify $`q_*=0`$ with every variable or group being fully missing; a one-row law with the first variable always missing and the second always observed satisfies the assumptions at $`q_*=0`$ while still giving all-row deletion probability one.

[`AronowAudit.P007.zero_bound_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_Probability.lean#L579) · [P007-T3](#p007-t3)

The printed proof bounds every conditional observation probability by $`q_*`$; a law satisfying the assumptions at $`q_*=1/2`$ has a null prefix for which every value in $`[0,1]`$ satisfies the conditional-probability multiplication identity, so the assumptions do not bound that unspecified value.

[`AronowAudit.P007.null_prefix_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_Probability.lean#L628) · [P007-T4](#p007-t4)

The Figure 4 discussion uses a floored logarithmic inverse as the smallest crossing and to claim an upper bound on the actual all-row deletion probability. For the lower bound $`B(k)=(1-q_*^k)^n`$ and $`r=\log(1-a^{1/n})/\log q_*`$ with $`n\gt 0`$ and $`0\lt q_*,a\lt 1`$, Lean proves that the floor is the largest nonnegative integer with $`B(k)\le a`$ and the ceiling is the smallest with $`B(k)\ge a`$.

[`AronowAudit.P007.threshold_floor_ceiling`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_Probability.lean#L1164) · [P007-T8](#p007-t8)

The Figure 5 discussion calls $`1-q_*^h`$ an upper bound on the expected removed fraction; the sequential observation assumption instead makes it a lower bound for each row’s expected deletion indicator and for the expected removed fraction, without row independence.

[`AronowAudit.P007.expected_deletion_lower_bound`](../../AronowAudit/P007/P007_Probability.lean#L817) · [P007-T10](#p007-t10)

## Caveats

- The probability model is a finite joint law of Boolean missingness indicators, which represents every possible law of the finite indicator array; mutual row independence does not require identical row distributions or independence between columns.
- The observation bound applies to later conditionals only when the preceding all-observed prefix has positive probability; the repaired proofs handle null prefixes explicitly, and the original lemma conclusions remain valid.
- The asymptotic results use one fixed $`q_*\in[0,1)`$ across population sizes; in the grouped model the growth condition concerns the number of groups, not the total number of variables.
- The State Failure arithmetic assumes the archived pre-removal average is within $`10^{-9}`$ of 0.668390827582004 and that the 19 designated removed columns are fully missing; the resulting counts are inferred from that log, not recounted from raw data.
- The exact four-variable bound is conservative and does not locate the exact expected-loss threshold; the reported six-variable crossing concerns archived Monte Carlo estimates, not a proved exact expectation.
- The Figure 4 inversion concerns the lower bound $`B(k)`$: $`B(k)\le a`$ does not imply that the actual all-row deletion probability is at most $`a`$; reaching the ceiling instead guarantees that this probability is at least $`a`$ under the lemma assumptions.

### P007-T1

An exact bound that contradicts the sentence “With more than three variables, the loss is more than 99%” of Section 3.2

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Section 3.2, p. 153 (PDF p. 5), third paragraph, last sentence; Table 1 and Section 3.1, p. 152 (PDF p. 4)

Seven proved statements establish the exact bound of P007-E010 with four variables. The expectation is the average over all sets of $`k`$ columns, as in the uniform draw without replacement of Section 3.1. The first statement is the counting identity: for an array with $`n\ge1`$ rows, $`K`$ columns and $`r_i`$ observed cells in row $`i`$, and for $`k\le K`$, the expected proportion of rows retained equals $`\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{r_i}{k}\Big/\binom{K}{k} .`$ The main statement concerns every array with 8,580 rows and 1,205 columns such that $`\sum_i r_i=3{,}482{,}533`$. It asserts that the expected proportion of rows retained with four variables is at least $`24{,}513{,}560{,}217/1{,}923{,}069{,}889{,}510`$, which exceeds $`1/100`$, and that the expected loss is at most $`1{,}898{,}556{,}329{,}293/1{,}923{,}069{,}889{,}510\lt 99/100`$. Four supporting statements give the steps, among them the inequality $`\binom{r}{4}\ge\binom{405}{4}+(r-405)\binom{405}{3}`$ for $`0\le r\le1{,}205`$ and the value $`8{,}580\binom{1{,}205}{4}=749{,}997{,}256{,}908{,}900`$. A last statement exhibits one array with this number of observed cells, in which 7,633 rows have 406 observed cells and the other 947 have 405. It asserts that the expected loss of this array with four variables is below 99%. The number of observed cells is a hypothesis, which I inferred from the saved log of the replication files. The exhibited array does not reconstruct the State Failure data. The statements do not determine the number of variables at which the expected loss first exceeds 99%.

### P007-T3

A counterexample to the case $`q_*=0`$ in the proofs of Lemma 3 and Lemma 8, as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Appendix, proof of Lemma 3, p. 154 (PDF p. 6), first three sentences; supplement p. 4, proof of Lemma 8, first three sentences

Two proved counterexamples concern the sentence in P007-E003 that reads $`q_*=0`$ as all variables being fully missing. The first takes one row with two variables such that $`(M_{11},M_{12})=(1,0)`$ with probability one. It asserts that Assumption 1 holds, that Assumption 2 holds with $`q_*=0`$, that $`\Pr(M_{12}=0)=1`$, and that $`p_{all}=1=(1-0^2)^1`$. The second takes the row $`(1,1)`$ with probability one and asserts that Assumptions 1 and 2 hold for all $`q_*\in[0,1)`$, so that complete missingness does not force $`q_*=0`$. The words “fully missing” are read as $`\Pr(M_{ij}=1)=1`$ for all $`i`$ and $`j`$. Assumption 2 keeps its proviso: the bound on variable $`j`$ is imposed only if $`\Pr(\mathbf{M}_{i(j-1)}=0)\gt 0`$. For the proof of Lemma 8 the same two laws are read with each variable as a group of one variable, and there is no separate statement for groups. The statements contradict a sentence of the proofs and not the lemmas, since $`p_{all}=1`$ holds in the first example.

### P007-T4

A counterexample to the step “By Assumption 2, $`q_{ij}\leq q_*`$, for all $`j`$” in the proofs of Lemma 3 and Lemma 8, as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Appendix, proof of Lemma 3, p. 154 (PDF p. 6); supplement p. 4, proof of Lemma 8

Two proved counterexamples concern the examples of P007-E003 in which a conditioning event of the proof has probability zero. I write $`s_{ij}=\Pr(\mathbf{M}_{ij}=0)`$ for the probability that the first $`j`$ entries of row $`i`$ are observed, so that the printed $`q_{ij}`$ is the ratio $`s_{ij}/s_{i(j-1)}`$ for $`j\gt 1`$. The first statement takes one row with two variables, equal to $`(1,0)`$ with probability one, and $`q_*=1/2`$. It asserts that Assumptions 1 and 2 hold, that $`s_{11}=s_{12}=0`$, and that $`s_{12}=v\,s_{11}`$ for all $`v\in[0,1]`$. The value $`v=1\gt q_*`$ is among them, and Assumption 2 bounds no value that could be given to $`q_{12}`$. The second statement takes $`q_*=1/2`$ and two independent rows with three variables, each equal to $`(1,0,0)`$ or $`(0,1,0)`$ with probability $`1/2`$. It asserts that both assumptions hold and that $`s_{i1}=1/2`$ and $`s_{i2}=s_{i3}=0`$ for both rows, so that the conditioning event for the third variable has probability zero. Both statements include the conclusion of the lemma, which is $`p_{all}\ge3/4`$ in the first example and $`p_{all}=1\ge49/64`$ in the second. The statements are written in terms of the probabilities $`s_{ij}`$, and they give no value to a conditional probability given an event of probability zero. They describe a gap in the printed proofs and are not counterexamples to Lemma 3 or Lemma 8. For Lemma 8 the same laws are read with groups of one variable.

### P007-T5

The proofs of Lemma 3 and Lemma 8, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Appendix, proof of Lemma 3, p. 154 (PDF p. 6); supplement p. 3 (definition of $`\mathbf{M}_{ij}`$) and p. 4 (proof of Lemma 8)

Eight Lean statements give the steps of the corrected proof in P007-E003, for $`n`$ rows of $`h`$ indicators, where $`h=k`$ for Lemma 3 and $`h=g`$ for Lemma 8. Let $`s_{ij}=\Pr(\mathbf{M}_{ij}=0)`$ for $`j\ge1`$, and let $`s_{i0}=1`$. Under Assumption 2 alone, the statements assert that $`s_{ij}\le q_*\,s_{i(j-1)}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{ij}\le q_*^{\,j}\qquad\text{for all } j\in\{1,\dots,h\},`$ and the case $`s_{i(j-1)}=0`$ is included. For the chain rule, $`q_{ij}`$ is defined as $`s_{ij}/s_{i(j-1)}`$ if $`s_{i(j-1)}\gt 0`$ and as $`0`$ otherwise. Two statements assert that $`0\le q_{ij}\le q_*`$ under Assumption 2 and that $`s_{ij}=q_{i1}q_{i2}\cdots q_{ij}`$ for every law. The two changes of wording in the supplement, “up to group $`j`$” and the name of the independence assumption, are separate from these mathematical statements.
