# P008: Inference in Spatial Experiments with Interference using the SpatialEffect Package

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P008.json)

Cyrus Samii, Ye Wang, Jonathan Sullivan, and P. M. Aronow (2023). Inference in Spatial Experiments with Interference using the SpatialEffect Package. Journal of Agricultural, Biological, and Environmental Statistics 28(1):138–156.

DOI: [10.1007/s13253-022-00517-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13253-022-00517-y)

arXiv: [2106.15081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15081)

## Overview

This tutorial develops the average marginalized response for spatial experiments with interference and illustrates estimation and inference with the SpatialEffect package. The Lean development covers finite randomization-test validity, spatial averaging geometry, and the regression form of spatial-HAC variance estimates. It also supplies exact counterexamples and corrected statements for percentile calibration, design-law substitution, variance cutoffs, cumulative-effect normalization, and selected printed or archived-code formulas; these do not constitute a reanalysis of the Uganda experiment.

Areas: Interference and networks, Randomization inference, Regression and variance estimation.

## Key results

For any finite probability law and fixed statistic, its inclusive absolute-tail p-value satisfies $`\Pr(p\le\delta)\le\delta`$ for every $`0\le\delta\le1`$ when observations follow that same law.

[`AronowAudit.P008.design_tail_valid`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L227) · [P008-T2](#p008-t2)

When the observed assignment and $`B`$ simulated assignments are independent draws from the same finite design law, the inclusive absolute-tail rank p-value with the plus-one correction is superuniform.

[`AronowAudit.P008.monte_carlo_rank_valid`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L1719) · [P008-T4](#p008-t4)

With both treatment arms nonempty, regressing circle averages on an intercept and binary treatment gives the Hajek contrast weights and group residuals, and the treatment entry of the uniform-kernel spatial-HAC sandwich equals the corresponding scalar residual-score quadratic form.

[`AronowAudit.P008.matrix_hac_reduction`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L1099) · [P008-T6](#p008-t6)

For a planar circle of positive radius $`d`$ centered at $`p`$, the distance from any point $`x`$ to the circle is $`|\mathrm{dist}(x,p)-d|`$.

[`AronowAudit.P008.distance_to_circle`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L1931) · [P008-T17](#p008-t17)

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed interpolated-percentile rule need not have its stated 5% sharp-null error rate: an exact four-node spatial model with all six assignments enumerated rejects with probability $`1/3`$, both at a single radius and for the sum over five radii.

[`AronowAudit.P008.spatial_percentile_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L2732) · [P008-T3](#p008-t3)

Against treating unrestricted permutations as the design reference law in the application, four-node sharp-null examples give inclusive tails of $`1/3`$ versus $`1/2`$, or $`2/3`$ versus $`1/2`$, under unrestricted and blocked assignment laws; they do not determine the application p-value.

[`AronowAudit.P008.reference_laws_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L489) · [P008-T1](#p008-t1)

A cutoff covering the range of effects need not cover dependence between node contributions: in the two-node example the diagonal-only Hajek linearization variance is $`1/8`$, whereas its true variance is $`1/4`$.

[`AronowAudit.P008.effect_range_cutoff_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L1346) · [P008-T5](#p008-t5)

The printed interpretation of an unscaled sum as a probability change fails in a synthetic binary model with indexed circles: the four-radius sum is $`-0.0321`$, while the normalized probability difference is $`-0.008025`$.

[`AronowAudit.P008.summed_risk_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L1425) · [P008-T7](#p008-t7)

The printed grid with step $`5/111`$ has no point in the printed test interval $`[1/111,2.5/111]`$, so every selected observed or permutation sum is zero, contrary to the three reported nonzero values.

[`AronowAudit.P008.printed_grid_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L288) · [P008-T12](#p008-t12)

The later archived code rule that adds grid position to a positive cutoff does not implement a fixed distance threshold: for the same reduced circle-average data it gives variances $`1`$ and $`1/2`$ at positions one and two, while retaining the physical cutoff gives $`1`$ at both.

[`AronowAudit.P008.grid_position_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P008/P008_Audit.lean#L1236) · [P008-T9](#p008-t9)

## Caveats

- Randomization validity requires the reference law to be the actual design law, or a justified conditional design law; inclusive ties and the Monte Carlo sampling assumptions are part of the stated results.
- The percentile counterexample fixes type-7 interpolated quantiles and a suitable counting measure on a spatial rectangle; it does not refute every possible discrete critical-value rule.
- The cutoff counterexample concerns the Hajek linearization, not the exact finite-sample variance of a ratio estimator; the HAC algebra alone gives no interval-coverage theorem.
- The cumulative-risk construction verifies binary circle counts, marginalization and normalization on indexed circles, without certifying a Euclidean realization or coordinate injectivity; its baseline risk is synthetic.
- The positive-cutoff-plus-position rule is a premise read from a later archived package, not a theorem about the historical release; its selected counterexample uses reduced outcome vectors rather than constructing the full spatial field.
- Neither the examples nor the validity theorems compute a design-valid Uganda p-value or establish the reported empirical significance.
- The “later archived version” means SpatialEffect 0.1.0 in the replication materials for Ye Wang, Cyrus Samii, Haoge Chang and P. M. Aronow (2025), “Design-Based Inference for Spatial Experiments under Unknown Interference,” Annals of Applied Statistics 19(1):744–768 (P004; [doi:10.1214/24-AOAS1986](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOAS1986)). The source is the [SpatialEffectPackage replication bundle at commit 7c873da50f023c75eb31b9ba90d3a7a249c7a753](https://github.com/YeWang1576/SpatialEffectPackage/tree/7c873da50f023c75eb31b9ba90d3a7a249c7a753/WangSamiiChangAronow_2024_replication), file `WangSamiiChangAronow_2024_replication/SpatialEffect/R/SpatialEffect.R` (SHA-256 `ab1aebc0fc12a9d9e1be3573a8f0b60b3d037483d034f63a0bf5759f7efe6fee`). This is the external software premise for P008-T9, P008-T10, P008-T16, P008-T17 and P008-T19; it is not established to be the historical release cited by P008.

### P008-T1

Counterexamples to the permutation test of the cumulative effect between 1 and 2.5 km (Section 6), read as the randomization test of the sharp null for the experiment

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 153 (PDF p. 16); the sentences on the design, p. 151 (PDF p. 14), p. 145 (PDF p. 8) and p. 142 (PDF p. 5)

Four Lean statements formalize the four-unit example of P008-N012 and a further example with 121 units. The examples contradict the claim that the observed assignment and its number of treated units determine the law under which the test is computed. In the first example an assignment of 121 units is represented by its set of treated units. Let $`Q_U`$ be the uniform law on the $`\binom{121}{60}`$ sets of 60 units, and let $`Q_B`$ be the uniform law on the $`\binom{10}{5}\binom{111}{55}`$ sets that contain five of the first ten units and 55 of the other 111. The statement asserts that the support of $`Q_B`$ is a proper subset of the support of $`Q_U`$ and that one assignment, which stands for the observed one, has positive probability under both laws. It asserts further that a second assignment with 60 treated units has positive probability under $`Q_U`$ and probability $`0`$ under $`Q_B`$. The second example has four nodes with fixed circle averages $`y`$, the Hajek contrast $`T`$ with two nodes in each arm, and the observed assignment $`1010`$. There $`Q_U`$ is uniform on the six assignments with two treated nodes, and $`Q_B`$ is uniform on the four assignments that treat one node in each of the pairs $`\{1,2\}`$ and $`\{3,4\}`$. For a law $`Q`$, the tail probability is $`p_Q=Q(\{w:|T(w)|\ge|T(1010)|\})`$. The statement asserts that $`p_{Q_U}=1/3`$ and $`p_{Q_B}=1/2`$ for $`y=(0,1,0,1)`$, and that $`p_{Q_U}=2/3`$ and $`p_{Q_B}=1/2`$ for $`y=(0,1/3,2/3,1)`$. Two supporting statements assert that the two lists of assignments are exactly these supports. The laws $`Q_B`$ are examples of a blocked design, and they are not the seven subcounty lotteries of the source study. No statement concerns the percentiles, the statistic or the $`p`$-value of the application.

### P008-T2

Section 6, the claim “The cumulative effect between 1 km and 2.5 km is thus significant with $`p \lt  0.05`$”, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 153 (PDF p. 16); see also p. 151 (PDF p. 14), p. 145 (PDF p. 8), p. 144 (PDF p. 7) and p. 142 (PDF p. 5)

Five Lean statements, one of them auxiliary, give the formal content of the correction of P008-N012. A design is a probability law $`Q`$ on a finite set of assignments, and for a statistic $`T`$ the tail probability $`p_Q(z)=Q(\{w:|T(w)|\ge|T(z)|\})`$ counts $`z`$ and all ties. The first statement asserts that $`Q(\{z:p_Q(z)\le\delta\})\le\delta`$ for all $`\delta\in[0,1]`$, all laws and all statistics. The second asserts the same bound for the conditional law $`Q(\cdot\mid A)`$, for all events $`A`$ such that $`Q(A)\gt 0`$. The third concerns $`N`$ independent assignments with a common probability $`p\in(0,1)`$. For all $`M\le N`$, it asserts that the event of $`M`$ treated nodes has positive probability and that, given this event, each assignment with $`M`$ treated nodes has probability $`1/\binom{N}{M}`$. The fourth applies the second statement to the paper’s objects. Under the sharp null on the spatial field, the sum of the Hajek estimates over a finite set of radii is a fixed function of the assignment. The test that rejects when the tail probability of this sum is at most $`\delta`$ then has level $`\delta`$, where the tail probability is computed under the law of the design given an event of positive probability. The fourth statement assumes a measurable field, $`N\gt 0`$, circles of positive finite measure, integrable outcomes and nonempty arms at every assignment of positive conditional probability. The case in which the law of the design is not the law of unrestricted permutations has no statement of its own, and it rests on the counterexamples to the printed test. The lotteries of the source study are not represented, and nothing is asserted about the $`p`$-value of the application.

### P008-T5

A counterexample to the cutoff of the toy example (“cutoff = .4”) as a use of the instruction of Section 3: a cutoff equal to the range of the effects leaves out half of the variance

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 145 (PDF p. 8); pp. 147–148 (PDF pp. 10–11); table on p. 149 (PDF p. 12)

Seventeen Lean statements formalize the counterexample of P008-N002 to the reading that a cutoff equal to the range of the effects accounts for the dependence between nodes. In the first four, two nodes lie at $`0`$ and $`2`$ on a line and the field consists of the points $`-1,0,1,2,3`$ with counting measure. A treated node raises by $`1`$ the outcome of every point within distance $`1`$ of it, the assignments are independent with probability $`1/2`$, and $`d=1`$. These statements assert that $`\mu_1=Z_1+Z_2/2`$ and $`\mu_2=Z_2+Z_1/2`$, with marginal means $`5/4`$ under treatment and $`1/4`$ under control. With $`\phi_i=2Z_i(\mu_i-5/4)-2(1-Z_i)(\mu_i-1/4)`$, they assert that $`\phi_1=\phi_2=(1/2)(2Z_1-1)(2Z_2-1)`$ and that $`\mathrm{Var}(\phi_1)=\mathrm{Cov}(\phi_1,\phi_2)=1/4`$. The variance of $`(\phi_1+\phi_2)/2`$ is then $`1/4`$, while a cutoff of $`1`$ excludes the pair of nodes, which are $`2`$ apart, and leaves $`1/8`$. The other thirteen statements place $`M`$ such pairs on the line, with nodes at $`6g`$ and $`6g+2`$ for $`g=0,\dots,M-1`$, and $`N=2M`$. For all $`M\ge1`$ they assert that the outcomes lie in $`[0,2]`$, that distinct nodes are at least $`2`$ apart, and that each circle average depends only on the two assignments of its own pair. They assert further that $`\mathrm{Var}\Bigl(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\phi_i\Bigr)=\frac{1}{2N},\qquad\frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Cov}(\phi_i,\phi_j)\,\mathbf{1}\{D_{ij}\le1\}=\frac{1}{4N} .`$ The points have rational coordinates on a line, and separate statements identify their distances with Euclidean distances in the plane. The variances are those of the linearization and not of the ratio estimator. No statement concerns a limit as $`N\to\infty`$, either of the estimator or of a variance estimate.

### P008-T6

Section 3, the instruction on the spatial-HAC cutoff, and the intervals of the toy example with cutoff 0.4, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 145 (PDF p. 8); pp. 147–149 (PDF pp. 10–12)

Fourteen Lean statements cover the four parts of the correction of P008-N002. Let $`q_i=Z_i/N_1-(1-Z_i)/N_0`$, let $`e_i`$ be the deviation of the circle average $`M_i`$ from the mean of its arm, and let $`\widehat V(c)=\sum_{i,j}q_iq_je_ie_j\mathbf{1}\{D_{ij}\le c\}`$. For two nonempty arms, the first statements assert that $`\widehat\tau_{HA}(d)=\sum_iq_iM_i`$ and that $`q_i`$ and $`e_i`$ are the weights and the residuals of the regression of $`M_i`$ on an intercept and $`Z_i`$. ### P008-T28

Section 2.3: “under a set of mild regularity conditions, the Hajek estimator is consistent and asymptotically normal” (attributed to Wang et al. 2022)

**Kind:** `headline_result`

**Location:** p. 144 (PDF p. 7); the estimator on pp. 143–144 (PDF pp. 6–7); p. 145 (PDF p. 8)

The printed sentence is not stated in Lean. The paper names two of the conditions of Wang et al. (2022), the separation of the nodes and a bound on interference, and does not list the others. The cited theorem is not formalized here; the consistency result below has a separately stated sufficient set of assumptions. The 22 Lean statements formalize instead a sufficient version of consistency that I derived. For each $`N`$, the assignments are independent with a common probability $`p\in(0,1)`$, and the circle average of node $`i`$ at a fixed $`d`$ is an arbitrary function $`M_i(\mathbf{z})`$ of the assignment such that $`|M_i(\mathbf{z})|\le H`$. The pairs $`(Z_i,M_i(\mathbf{Z}))`$ have a dependency graph of maximum degree at most $`D`$, which means that events determined by two sets of pairs with no edge between them are independent. The numbers $`p`$, $`H`$ and $`D`$ do not depend on $`N`$. The main statement asserts that, for all $`\varepsilon\gt 0`$, $`\Pr\bigl(|\widehat\tau_{HA}(d)-\tau_N|\gt \varepsilon\bigr)\to0\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty ,`$ where $`\tau_N`$ is the AMR of the $`N`$th experiment and the estimator is given a fixed value when an arm is empty. A finite version bounds this probability by an explicit constant divided by $`N`$, and the other statements are the steps. With $`U=N_1/N`$, the normalized numerators have means $`pr_1`$ and $`(1-p)r_0`$, where $`r_1`$ and $`r_0`$ are the averages of the marginal circle means, and $`U`$ has mean $`p`$ and variance $`p(1-p)/N`$. The variance of each numerator is at most $`H^2(D+1)/N`$, and $`|S/U-r|\le(2/p)\{|S-pr|+H|U-p|\}`$ whenever $`U\ge p/2`$ and $`|r|\le H`$. The rest are the decomposition of $`\widehat\tau_{HA}(d)-\tau_N`$ into two ratio errors, a bound on the probability that $`U\lt p/2`$ or $`1-U\lt (1-p)/2`$, Chebyshev’s inequality and facts about finite expectations. Asymptotic normality and the conservativeness of the intervals are not stated in any form.
