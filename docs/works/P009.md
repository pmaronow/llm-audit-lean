# P009: Average treatment effects in the presence of unknown interference

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P009.json)

Fredrik Sävje, P. M. Aronow, and Michael G. Hudgens (2021). Average treatment effects in the presence of unknown interference. Annals of Statistics 49(2):673–701.

DOI: [10.1214/20-AOS1973](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1973)

arXiv: [1711.06399](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06399)

## Overview

This work studies average treatment effects when potential outcomes may depend on the entire treatment assignment, distinguishing the expected average treatment effect (EATE) from the average distributional shift effect (ADSE). The Lean development covers estimand identities, consistency and stochastic rates for Horvitz–Thompson and Hájek estimators under several randomization designs, variance inflation, and bounds on changes of estimand across designs. Separate counterexamples and corrected statements address proof inequalities, mixing claims, normal approximation, and the passage from variance conservativeness to interval coverage.

Areas: Interference and networks, Experimental design and estimation, Asymptotic statistics.

## Key results

For a nonempty finite population, EATE reduces to ATE under no interference, has the stated average-of-potential-means representation, and equals ADSE under Bernoulli randomization.

[`AronowAudit.P009.estimand_identities`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_Audit.lean#L2420) · [P009-T35](#p009-t35)

Under Assumptions 1–2 and Bernoulli randomization, the Horvitz–Thompson and Hájek estimators are consistent for EATE with errors of order $`O_p(\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{AVG}}/n})`$.

[`AronowAudit.P009.bernoulli_consistency`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_BernoulliCompletion.lean#L747) · [P009-T41](#p009-t41)

Under the common regularity conditions and paired randomization, with $`d_{\mathrm{AVG}}=o(n)`$, $`e_{\mathrm{AVG}}=o(n)`$ and $`R_{\mathrm{SUM}}=o(n)`$, both estimators are consistent for EATE with errors of order $`O_p(\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{AVG}}/n}+\sqrt{e_{\mathrm{AVG}}/n}+R_{\mathrm{SUM}}/n)`$.

[`AronowAudit.P009.paired_consistency`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_PairedCompletion.lean#L567) · [P009-T44](#p009-t44)

For the same potential outcomes on a nonempty population, with every assignment-specific unit effect bounded in absolute value by $`K`$, the EATE difference between designs $`P`$ and $`Q`$ is at most $`2K\delta(P,Q)`$, where $`\delta`$ is total variation distance.

[`AronowAudit.P009.external_validity_total_variation`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_Audit.lean#L2536) · [P009-T37](#p009-t37)

For the same potential outcomes on a nonempty population, with every assignment-specific unit effect bounded by $`K`$ in absolute value and $`r\ge1`$, the EATE difference is at most $`2Kn^{-1/r}C_{r/(r-1)}W_r(P,Q)`$, with $`C_\infty`$ at $`r=1`$ and $`W_r`$ the order-one transport distance using the $`L_r`$ ground metric.

[`AronowAudit.P009.external_validity_wasserstein`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_TransportCompletion.lean#L171) · [P009-T39](#p009-t39)

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed implication from scaled variance conservativeness to conservative inference is replaced by a coverage theorem: under common regularity, Bernoulli randomization, nonnegative estimated variance, Definition 10, and $`\liminf(n/d_{\mathrm{AVG}})\mathrm{Var}(\hat\tau_{\mathrm{HT}})\gt 0`$, the interval centered at HT with radius $`\sqrt{\hat V/\alpha}`$ has limiting lower coverage at least $`1-\alpha`$ for $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$.

[`AronowAudit.P009.bernoulli_corrected_coverage`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_Audit.lean#L2381) · [P009-T2](#p009-t2)

The printed signed-covariance bounds in the proofs of Lemmas A9, A22, A23 and A25 fail in explicit two-unit experiments satisfying the relevant regularity and design assumptions; these examples refute the proof inequalities, without refuting the final lemma statements.

[`AronowAudit.P009.signed_weight_counterexamples`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_Audit.lean#L1078) · [P009-T3](#p009-t3)

The printed normal-approximation claim based on bounded maximum dependence is corrected by adding $`\liminf n\mathrm{Var}(\hat\tau_{\mathrm{HT}})\gt 0`$: under Assumption 1, Bernoulli randomization and uniformly bounded $`d_{\mathrm{MAX}}`$, HT centered at EATE and divided by its true standard deviation converges to a standard normal law.

[`AronowAudit.P009.corrected_normal_approximation`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_NormalCompletion.lean#L445) · [P009-T8](#p009-t8)

The printed assertion of vanishing mixing for every pair with $`d_{ij}=0`$ is replaced by uniform vanishing outside exceptional sets whose size, divided by the number of ordered zero-dependence pairs, tends to zero under complete randomization and bounded $`d_{\mathrm{AVG}}`$.

[`AronowAudit.P009.corrected_exceptional_pair_mixing`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_OverwhelmingCompletion.lean#L32) · [P009-T10](#p009-t10)

The printed implication from direct interference to nonzero expected spillover fails in a fair three-unit Bernoulli experiment with $`y_2(z)=z_1(2z_3-1)`$: unit 1 interferes with unit 2, but both expected spillovers and their difference are zero.

[`AronowAudit.P009.direct_spillover_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_Audit.lean#L1322) · [P009-T13](#p009-t13)

The printed off-diagonal mixing bound in the proof of Lemma A23 fails for a growing family of independent fair assignments with no interference: the full treatment-indicator covariance sum is $`n/4`$ while the off-diagonal mixing sum is zero.

[`AronowAudit.P009.diagonal_omission_family`](../../AronowAudit/P009/P009_DiagonalCompletion.lean#L161) · [P009-T15](#p009-t15)

## Caveats

- Finite experiments use the full Boolean assignment cube, including specified potential outcomes off the support of the randomization law; expectations and probabilities are exact weighted finite sums.
- The asymptotic model allows positive population sizes tending to infinity along a general index; paired designs require even sizes and explicit pairings.
- Definition 10 concerns a scaled lower tail of variance-estimation error, with an inner limit for each positive tolerance; the variance-inflation results alone do not establish confidence-interval coverage.
- The corrected normal limit uses the true standard deviation and the positive variance lower bound; it makes no assertion about studentization by an estimated standard error.
- The external-validity bounds compare designs on the same potential-outcome schedule with effects bounded on the full assignment cube; the subscript of the transport distance specifies its ground metric, while its transport order is one.

**Location:** p. 689 (PDF p. 17), Section 6.1, the two sentences after the display of $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{BER}}(\hat\tau_{\mathrm{HT}})`$; Definition 10, p. 691 (PDF p. 19)

### P009-T7

A counterexample to the sentence of Section 6.3 on Theorem 2.7 of Chen and Shao (2004), as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 693 (PDF p. 21), Section 6.3 (Tail bounds), first paragraph

The Lean statements formalize the first example of P009-E001: for each $`n\ge1`$ the assignments are independent with probability $`1/2`$, and $`y_1(\mathbf{z})=z_1`$ and $`y_i(\mathbf{z})=0`$ for $`i\ge2`$. The statements concern the sufficiency of the condition $`d_{\mathrm{MAX}}=\mathcal{O}(1)`$ and assert nothing about the theorem of Chen and Shao.
