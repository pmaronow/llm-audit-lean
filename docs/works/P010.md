# P010: A Note on Dropping Experimental Subjects who Fail a Manipulation Check

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P010.json)

P. M. Aronow, Jonathon Baron, and Lauren Pinson (2019). A Note on Dropping Experimental Subjects who Fail a Manipulation Check. Political Analysis 27(4):572–589.

DOI: [10.1017/pan.2019.5](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2019.5)

## Overview

The paper studies treatment-effect identification after excluding subjects who fail a manipulation check, with particular attention to subjects who would pass under every treatment. The Lean development covers population identification and non-identification results, corrected sharp bounds based on outcome distributions among passers, and counterexamples to printed formulas and proof steps. It also checks an overlap claim for the unweighted empirical preference bounds and the direction of transport weights, within the recorded data and weighting conventions.

Areas: Causal identification, Partial identification and bounds, Experimental design and estimation.

## Key results

With SUTVA, independence of each arm's potential outcome and passing status from assignment, positive assignment probabilities and integrable focal outcomes, each observed arm mean identifies its population potential-outcome mean and their contrast identifies the average treatment effect.

[`AronowAudit.P010.lemma_one`](../../AronowAudit/P010/P010_Population.lean#L489) · [P010-T19](#p010-t19)

Under the paper's corrected assumptions, integrable focal outcomes and almost-sure equality of potential passing statuses across arms, the observed mean contrast among passers equals the average treatment effect among subjects who would always pass.

[`AronowAudit.P010.corollary_one`](../../AronowAudit/P010/P010_Population.lean#L585) · [P010-T18](#p010-t18)

There exists a two-arm population satisfying the paper's assumptions, with positive always-passer probability and observed passing independent of assignment, in which the contrast among observed passers differs from the always-passer average treatment effect.

[`AronowAudit.P010.corollary_three`](../../AronowAudit/P010/P010_Population.lean#L1150) · [P010-T21](#p010-t21)

There exists a two-arm population with a constant real covariate satisfying covariate ignorability, positive passing rates and covariate balance conditional on passing, in which the observed passer contrast at that covariate value differs from the always-passer effect.

[`AronowAudit.P010.corollary_five`](../../AronowAudit/P010/P010_Population.lean#L1163) · [P010-T23](#p010-t23)

## Corrections and counterexamples

Proposition 1 omits passing from the outer conditioning events in its printed finite bounds; under the corrected assumptions, trimming ratio below one, positive always-passer probability and an atomless, integrable focal passer outcome law, Lean proves the corrected passer-tail bounds valid for every compatible population and proves both endpoints attainable.

[`AronowAudit.P010.Wave1.proposition_one_finite_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P010/Wave1/PropositionOneFiniteTailC.lean#L336) · [P010-T4](#p010-t4)

Proposition 1 prints universally infinite sharp bounds when the trimming ratio is at least one; under the corrected assumptions, positive always-passer probability, atomless observed outcomes and focal passer integrability, Lean instead identifies the sharp infimum and supremum as the focal passer law's essential endpoints, with finite endpoints unattained.

[`AronowAudit.P010.Wave1.proposition_one_else_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P010/Wave1/PropositionOneElseTailC.lean#L165) · [P010-T6](#p010-t6)

Against the printed claim that all treatment bounds overlap for both outcomes, the reconstructed unweighted preference bounds using the deposited strict whole-observation trimming rule put the 90–90 interval disjoint from each other arm, precluding a common three-arm intersection.

[`AronowAudit.P010.empirical_overlap_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P010/P010_Empirical.lean#L131) · [P010-T1](#p010-t1)

The printed proof of Corollary 1 infers joint independence of the potential-outcome vector and passing status from assignment, but Lean gives a four-state population satisfying per-arm ignorability and common passing status in which that joint independence fails.

[`AronowAudit.P010.joint_independence_step_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P010/P010_Population.lean#L904) · [P010-T7](#p010-t7)

Footnote 7 uses odds $`p/(1-p)`$ to reweight replication subjects under its printed study coding; in an exact two-cell example with true propensities, Lean proves these odds give cell mass $`27/28`$ instead of the original $`1/4`$, while reciprocal odds recover both original cell masses.

[`AronowAudit.P010.transport_odds_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P010/P010_Transport.lean#L46) · [P010-T11](#p010-t11)

## Caveats

- The population model builds SUTVA into observed outcomes and checks by selecting the assigned potential variables; ignorability is imposed separately for each arm's pair of potential outcome and passing status, without assuming independence of the full potential vector from assignment.
- Conditional means are integrals over events divided by their probabilities; mean identities require the indicated first moments, and always-passer bounds require positive probability of that subpopulation.
- Sharpness fixes the entire observed law of outcomes, passing status and assignment across compatible populations; it does not fix the always-passer probability or assert that every intermediate mean is attained.
- In the branch with trimming ratio at least one, infinite sharp endpoints require the focal passer distribution to have positive mass arbitrarily far into both tails; unbounded support of all observed outcomes alone does not suffice.
- The empirical overlap certificate takes aggregate counts as inputs and concerns unweighted identification bounds under strict rank trimming; it does not establish a result about weighted Figure 1, bootstrap confidence-bar overlap or sampling uncertainty.
- The transport counterexample uses true pooled study propensities and exact cell masses; the selected theorem does not certify estimated regression weights or numerical optimization.

### P010-T23

Corollary 5

**Kind:** `headline_result`

**Location:** Section 2.3, p. 576 (PDF p. 5)

Corollary 5 says that an implication fails, and the Lean statement asserts that an example exists. It asserts that there exists a population with $`K=2`$ and one real covariate that satisfies Assumptions 1, 3 and 4, in which $`\mathbf{X}`$ is independent of $`Z`$ given $`S`$ and $`\Pr[S(1)=S(2)=1,\mathbf{X}=0]\gt 0`$, such that $`\mathrm{E}[Y\mid S=1,Z=2,\mathbf{X}=0]-\mathrm{E}[Y\mid S=1,Z=1,\mathbf{X}=0]\ne\mathrm{E}[Y(2)-Y(1)\mid S(1)=S(2)=1,\mathbf{X}=0].`$ The example intended is that of Corollary 3 with the covariate constant and equal to zero, as the paper’s remark on the proof indicates. With a constant covariate, Assumption 4 reduces to Assumption 2, the conditional independence holds because a constant is independent of every variable, and the two sides are $`1/2`$ and $`0`$. The statement itself names no example. It asserts existence for $`K=2`$, for the pair $`z=2`$, $`z'=1`$ and for the value $`\mathbf{x}=0`$, and one such population is what the corollary requires. The conditional independence means that $`\mathbf{X}`$ and $`Z`$ are independent under the law conditional on each value of $`S`$ that has positive probability.
