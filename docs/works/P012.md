# P012: Identification of homophily and preferential recruitment in respondent-driven sampling

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P012.json)

Forrest W. Crawford, P. M. Aronow, Li Zeng, and Jianghong Li (2018). Identification of homophily and preferential recruitment in respondent-driven sampling. American Journal of Epidemiology 187(1):153–160.

DOI: [10.1093/aje/kwx208](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwx208)

arXiv: [1511.05397](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05397)

## Overview

The work studies identification of homophily and preferential recruitment from respondent-driven sampling records with partially observed networks. The Lean development proves sharp marginal endpoint formulas over all compatible graph-and-trait completions, identification criteria and the four extrema in Figure 1. It also proves counterexamples to printed proposal mechanisms, proof steps and Table 1 lower limits under the stated network totals, and convergence of the corrected annealing chain for its concrete homophily objective.

Areas: Interference and networks, Partial identification and bounds, Optimization and computation.

## Key results

For admissible recruitment data with positive reported degree totals in both trait classes, homophily has sharp closed-form lower and upper bounds over every compatible completion, attained by opposite-trait and same-trait private-leaf completions respectively.

[`AronowAudit.P012.exact_h_region`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L2774) · [P012-T6](#p012-t6)

For admissible data with at least one nonseed, preferential recruitment has sharp closed-form bounds obtained from obligatory later children and recruiter spare degrees, attained simultaneously across recruitment events by same-trait and opposite-trait private leaves.

[`AronowAudit.P012.exact_p_region`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L3103) · [P012-T7](#p012-t7)

On the domain where both functionals are defined at every compatible completion, homophily and preferential recruitment are both point identified exactly when every sampled degree equals its recruitment degree, equivalently when every compatible graph is the recruitment skeleton.

[`AronowAudit.P012.joint_identification`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L3393) · [P012-T22](#p012-t22)

Under admissibility, positive reported degree totals in both trait classes and at least one nonseed, preferential recruitment alone is point identified exactly when every sampled vertex that recruited a child has no spare degree.

[`AronowAudit.P012.preferential_identification`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L3258) · [P012-T22](#p012-t22)

For the explicit Figure 1 data, the sharp homophily endpoints are $`-5/8`$ and $`79/105`$, and the sharp preferential-recruitment endpoints are $`-11/80`$ and $`49/120`$.

[`AronowAudit.P012.figure1_extrema`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L3169) · [P012-T23](#p012-t23)

For admissible data with positive reported degree totals in both sampled trait classes and at least one nonseed, the corrected chain on the full compatible state space modulo unsampled-vertex renaming, with a fixed kernel having exactly the corrected proposal support and the corrected logarithmic cooling schedule, places probability tending to one on the global maximizers of $`J(h)=1/(1+\epsilon+h)`$ for $`\epsilon\gt 0`$, equivalently the global minimizers of homophily.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed proposal mechanisms are said to propose another compatible pair, but the explicit two-subject compatible state has a permitted unsampled-vertex branch producing an incompatible state for either fresh trait.

[`AronowAudit.P012.proposal_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L499) · [P012-T1](#p012-t1)

The printed proposal can leave the compatible set; moving line 22 inside the sampled-vertex branch, deleting only isolated unsampled vertices and making an empty trait pool a no-op ensures that every corrected proposal preserves compatibility for admissible data.

[`AronowAudit.P012.corrected_proposal_preserves`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L779) · [P012-T2](#p012-t2)

The three printed Table 1 homophily lower limits exceed a compatible value for any admissible data with 527 sampled vertices, six seeds and degree sum 4487: the opposite-trait private-leaf completion has defined homophily at most $`-178409/241987`$, below all three printed limits.

[`AronowAudit.P012.table_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L2493) · [P012-T3](#p012-t3)

Printed display (A2) keeps only one recruitment contribution, but an explicit compatible two-child example changes the full preferential-recruitment functional by $`-5/12`$, rather than either printed single-child value $`-1/6`$ or $`-1/4`$.

[`AronowAudit.P012.a2_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P012/P012_Identification.lean#L865) · [P012-T12](#p012-t12)

The appendix calls its positive cooling schedule nondecreasing while requiring convergence to zero; Lean proves that no positive nondecreasing real sequence can have that limit.

## Caveats

- Graphs are finite, simple and undirected, traits are binary, and recruitment times enter through their distinct order; admissibility includes an earlier recruiter for every nonseed and reported degrees at least the recruitment degrees.
- Homophily is defined on every compatible completion exactly when each sampled trait class has a positive reported degree total; preferential recruitment requires at least one nonseed, and undefined functionals are represented explicitly rather than assigned a real value.
- The sharp endpoints concern the separate marginal ranges of homophily and preferential recruitment; no assertion that every point of the enclosing rectangle is jointly attainable is made, and extra graph restrictions would change the compatible set.
- The Table 1 contradiction is conditional on the published totals and the degree sum 4487; it does not reconstruct Hartford microdata, determine corrected trait-specific numerical endpoints or adjudicate the printed upper limits.
- The annealing theorem uses the corrected proposal, a finite quotient by unsampled labels and $`T_t=(\epsilon\log t)^{-1}`$ for $`t\ge2`$, with $`\epsilon\gt 0`$ and a finite first temperature at least $`(\epsilon\log2)^{-1}`$; it concerns the specified homophily objective, and the closed-form endpoint results already avoid a search for these extrema.
