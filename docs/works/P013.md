# P013: Estimating Average Causal Effects Under General Interference, with Application to a Social Network Experiment

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P013.json)

P. M. Aronow and Cyrus Samii (2017). Estimating Average Causal Effects Under General Interference, with Application to a Social Network Experiment. Annals of Applied Statistics 11(4):1912–1947.

DOI: [10.1214/16-aoas1005](https://doi.org/10.1214/16-aoas1005)

arXiv: [1305.6156](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.6156)

## Overview

This work develops estimation and inference for exposure contrasts under general interference, with exposure probabilities induced by a known randomization design. The Lean development covers Horvitz–Thompson identities, conservative variance estimation, fixed-prediction adjustment, consistency, exposure-map misspecification, and Monte Carlo estimation of exposure probabilities. It also supplies corrected coverage conditions and counterexamples concerning simulation bias, dependence definitions, location shifts, recorded outcomes, and the distinction between blocked and complete school randomization.

Areas: Interference and networks, Experimental design and estimation, Randomization inference.

## Key results

With each marginal probability for the two comparison exposures strictly between zero and one, the Horvitz–Thompson exposure contrast is unbiased for their population average causal effect.

[`AronowAudit.P013.htEffect_unbiased`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L229) · [P013-T13](#p013-t13)

For distinct comparison exposures with each marginal probability strictly between zero and one, variance estimator (11) is conservative in design expectation, including zero joint exposure probabilities and outcomes of either sign.

[`AronowAudit.P013.effectVariance_conservative`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L643) · [P013-T15](#p013-t15)

For a selected exposure with each marginal probability strictly between zero and one, the difference estimator with predictions fixed independently of assignment is unbiased for the population potential-outcome total.

[`AronowAudit.P013.differenceTotal_unbiased`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L992) · [P013-T16](#p013-t16)

Under Condition 7 and marginal probabilities strictly between zero and one for distinct comparison exposures, the expected Horvitz–Thompson contrast with arbitrary assignment-dependent outcomes equals the average difference of the two exposure-conditional randomization-outcome expectations.

[`AronowAudit.P013.misspecified_contrast`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L1161) · [P013-T17](#p013-t17)

Under nested populations, standing positivity, uniform Condition 3, and Condition 4 requiring only $`o(N^2)`$ potentially dependent ordered pairs, the Horvitz–Thompson exposure contrast minus its population causal effect converges in probability to zero.

[`AronowAudit.P013.htEffect_consistency`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L2610) · [P013-T19](#p013-t19)

For a fixed finite population and independent replicate assignments with exactly the design law, the smoothed same-exposure and unsmoothed cross-exposure probability matrices converge entrywise almost surely to the exact joint probabilities.

## Corrections and counterexamples

Printed Proposition 6.2 is replaced by an at-least-nominal Wald coverage theorem for nested populations with bounded comparison outcomes and inverse probabilities, a genuine bounded-degree dependency graph, $`N\mathrm{Var}(\hat\tau_N)\to c\gt 0`$, and the added condition $`\Pr\{N\hat V_N\lt N\mathrm{Var}(\hat\tau_N)-\delta\}\to0`$ for every $`\delta\gt 0`$.

[`AronowAudit.P013.Wave1.corrected_wald_coverage_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P013/Wave1/Gap/GLP013P012B2.lean#L13) · [P013-T2](#p013-t2)

The printed replicate rule does not guarantee its relative-bias target for signed outcomes: an explicit independent single-replicate design satisfies the rule with $`b=1/2`$ and $`\pi_0=1/3`$, but has absolute relative bias greater than $`1/2`$.

[`AronowAudit.P013.signed_log_rule_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L2941) · [P013-T3](#p013-t3)

The printed assertion that Condition 5 subsumes Condition 4 fails under its literal nonmembership clauses: a shared-Bernoulli sequence satisfies standing positivity, Condition 3 and printed Condition 5, but violates Condition 4.

[`AronowAudit.P013.shared_sequence_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L3041) · [P013-T5](#p013-t5)

The printed location-invariance claim fails when numerical predictions are held fixed: in an explicit three-unit design, shifting every outcome by one changes the estimated contrast on every supported assignment while leaving the true contrast unchanged.

[`AronowAudit.P013.location_translation_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L3515) · [P013-T21](#p013-t21)

The interpretation of zero-scored missing reports as effects on wearing is not identified: three invented two-unit worlds have identical recorded data on every assignment but different wearing effects, so no function of those records recovers all three effects.

[`AronowAudit.P013.two_report_nonidentification`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Audit.lean#L4157) · [P013-T23](#p013-t23)

The identification of the released complete-randomization school lottery with the stated two-of-four blocked lottery fails: the assignment hosting all four schools in seven blocks has positive probability under uniform selection of 28 of 56 schools and probability zero under the blocked design.

[`AronowAudit.P013.school_support_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P013/P013_Later.lean#L393) · [P013-T25](#p013-t25)

## Caveats

- Design expectations use a finite positive-weight supported assignment space; potential outcomes are indexed by exposure, encoding the exposure model except in the separate misspecification results.
- Several finite results require marginal probabilities strictly between zero and one only for the exposures used, extending the paper’s global-positivity hypotheses; zero denominators use the stated zero-division convention, including the Hájek estimator on empty exposure groups.
- Variance conservativeness is an inequality in design expectation, not a pointwise guarantee or a coverage result; corrected coverage additionally uses independence of separated full exposure vectors, rather than merely pairwise independence.
- The corrected Wald theorem counts negative variance estimates as noncoverage and zero estimates as singleton intervals; it asserts a lower limit of coverage, without asserting convergence of the estimated-to-true variance ratio.
- The Monte Carlo matrix result assumes independent whole-design draws and fixes the population as the number of replicates grows; it does not formalize sampling without replacement from finite support or validate duplicate rejection under a nonuniform design.
- The location counterexample holds numerical predictions fixed; jointly translating predictions and outcomes is a separate valid equivariance result.
- The wearing and school-lottery counterexamples use artificial populations and no student records; they do not calculate Table 2 standard errors or refute unbiasedness theorems applied with the correct outcome and design probabilities.
- The wearing counterexample concerns the printed interpretation combined with scoring missing reports zero; that scoring rule is used in the public-data reconstruction and is not stated in the article.

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 1926 (PDF p. 15); proof in Appendix A.7, p. 1943 (PDF p. 32)

The Lean statements formalize the counterexample of P013-E001, in its variant with $`\epsilon_N=N^{-2}`$ and even $`N\ge4`$. The design, the exposure mapping and the outcomes are those of the finding. The pairs are randomized independently, each of the two equal assignments of a pair has probability $`\epsilon_N`$, and the outcomes of a pair are $`1`$ and $`-1`$. The main statement asserts that every exposure probability is $`1/2`$ and that the relation of belonging to the same pair satisfies the printed definition of $`h_{ij}`$ and the corrected one, with row sums at most $`2`$. ### P013-T2

Proposition 6.2, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 1926 (PDF p. 15); proof in Appendix A.7, p. 1943 (PDF p. 32)

The main Lean statement is the corrected proposition of P013-E001 in its weaker form, with a one-sided condition on the variance estimator in place of the bound on the joint probabilities. The populations are nested as in Section 6. The potential outcomes of a unit and the two exposures $`d_k\ne d_l`$ are the same at every $`N`$, while the design and the exposure mapping may change with $`N`$. Let $`0\lt \pi_i(d)\lt 1`$ for both exposures, let Condition 3 hold for $`d_k`$ and $`d_l`$ with constants that do not depend on $`N`$, let Condition 5 hold for a symmetric $`h`$ in the corrected sense of P013-E002, and let Condition 6 hold. Suppose that, for every $`\delta\gt 0`$, $`\Pr\bigl\{N\widehat{V}_N\lt N\mathrm{Var}[\widehat{\tau_{\mathrm{HT}}}(d_k,d_l)]-\delta\bigr\}\to0 ,`$ where $`\widehat{V}_N`$ is the estimator of equation (11). The statement asserts that, for every $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$, $`\liminf_{N\to\infty}\Pr\bigl\{|\widehat{\tau_{\mathrm{HT}}}(d_k,d_l)-\tau(d_k,d_l)|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat{V}_N}\bigr\}\ge1-\alpha .`$ Four further statements give sufficient conditions for the one-sided condition. The first two conditions are $`N(\widehat{V}_N-\mathrm{E}[\widehat{V}_N])\to0`$ in probability and, as a special case, $`\mathrm{Var}(N\widehat{V}_N)\to0`$. In the other two statements the populations are nested and Conditions 3 and 5 hold as above. If every joint exposure probability of two distinct units that enters (11) is either zero or at least a constant $`b\gt 0`$, then the one-sided condition holds and $`\mathrm{Var}(N\widehat{V}_N)\le C/N`$ for some constant $`C`$ and all large $`N`$. If $`b_N`$ is the smallest positive one of these probabilities at each size $`N`$ where one exists, then $`\mathrm{Var}(N\widehat{V}_N)\le C/(Nb_N)`$, again for some constant $`C`$ and all large $`N`$, and the one-sided condition holds if $`Nb_N\to\infty`$. Of the five supporting statements, two give the one-sided Chebyshev bound and the implication from the printed Condition 3 to the form used here. Three concern the second moment of a term of (7) or (10): if its joint probability $`p`$ is positive and $`|y_i(d)|/\pi_i(d)\le c_3`$ for both of its units, this moment is at most $`4c_3^4/p`$. A negative variance estimate is counted as noncoverage, which is stricter than the truncation at zero in the corrected proposition.

### P013-T6

The definition of the dependency indicator $`h_{ij}`$, as corrected, and the statement that Condition 5 subsumes Condition 4

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 1925 (PDF p. 14); p. 1926 (PDF p. 15)

Seven Lean statements cover the corrected definition of P013-E002 and its consequences. With the four signs corrected, disjoint sets $`\Gamma_1`$ and $`\Gamma_2`$ satisfy the premise exactly when no pair $`(i,j)`$ such that $`h_{ij}=1`$ has one index in each set, in either order. For symmetric $`h`$ this reads $`h_{ij}=0`$ for all $`i\in\Gamma_1`$ and $`j\in\Gamma_2`$.
