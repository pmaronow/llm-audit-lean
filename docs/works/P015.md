# P015: Does Regression Produce Representative Estimates of Causal Effects?

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P015.json)

P. M. Aronow and Cyrus Samii (2016). Does Regression Produce Representative Estimates of Causal Effects?. American Journal of Political Science 60(1):250–267.

DOI: [10.1111/ajps.12185](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12185)

## Overview

The work studies how regression weights heterogeneous causal effects and how the resulting effective population differs from the nominal population. The Lean development covers the OLS weighted-effect limit, effective covariate means, random-assignment consistency, and the logistic linearization under explicit assumptions. It also supplies corrections and finite counterexamples concerning positivity, inverse-link substitution, pooling group coefficients, and Taylor approximation.

Areas: Regression and variance estimation, Causal identification, Asymptotic statistics.

## Key results

Under iid sampling, affine potential outcomes, conditional unconfoundedness, a linear treatment conditional mean, explicit moment laws of large numbers, and nonsingularity, the OLS treatment coefficient converges in probability to $`\mathrm{E}[w\tau]/\mathrm{E}[w]`$, where $`w=(D-\mathrm{E}[D\mid X])^2`$ and $`\mathrm{E}[w]\gt 0`$.

[`AronowAudit.P015.ols_weighted_effect_limit`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L4974) · [P015-T22](#p015-t22), [P015-T15](#p015-t15)

Under iid sampling, a linear treatment conditional mean, the stated covariate moment laws of large numbers, nonsingular control moments, and positive residual variance, the mean of $`Z`$ weighted by fitted squared treatment residuals converges in probability to $`\mathrm{E}[wZ]/\mathrm{E}[w]`$.

[`AronowAudit.P015.effective_covariate_mean_limit`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L4955) · [P015-T23](#p015-t23)

For iid draws with affine potential outcomes and treatment independent of the joint baseline-outcome, effect, and covariate vector, the stated moment and nonsingularity assumptions imply that the OLS treatment coefficient converges in probability to $`\mathrm{E}[\tau]`$.

[`AronowAudit.P015.randomized_ols_limit`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L4996) · [P015-T24](#p015-t24)

For measurable binary treatment and covariates on a probability space, the conditional treatment variance equals $`p(X)(1-p(X))`$ almost surely, where $`p(X)=\mathrm{E}[D\mid X]`$.

[`AronowAudit.P015.binary_conditional_variance`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L1330) · [P015-T19](#p015-t19)

In Appendix B’s four-cell population, conditional regression weights are $`4/25`$ and $`44/225`$ while conditional population-scaled leverages are $`34/7`$ and $`50/21`$, giving opposite comparisons across covariate profiles and the stated rounded values.

[`AronowAudit.P015.leverage_weight_comparison`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L979) · [P015-T20](#p015-t20)

For a binary sample with an intercept, invertible $`W^{\mathsf T}W`$, and nonzero affine slope $`c`$, the affine surrogate residual equations have a unique root whose treatment coefficient multiplied by $`c`$ equals the OLS treatment coefficient.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed implication from approximately zero regression weight to failed positivity is contradicted by a four-atom model with strict positivity, effective profile mass $`999999/250000999999\lt 1/250000`$, and an observed-outcome IPW variable whose expectation is the population effect $`3/2`$.

[`AronowAudit.P015.rare_weight_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L170) · [P015-T1](#p015-t1)

The printed claim that positivity holds in the effective population by construction fails for a multivalued contrast: a four-atom model gives every unit positive regression weight but lacks treatment value $`1`$ at one represented profile, with observationally identical unconfounded models having different nominal and effective $`0`$-versus-$`1`$ effects.

[`AronowAudit.P015.multivalued_support_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L237) · [P015-T2](#p015-t2)

The printed inference from zero estimated profile weights to failed population positivity is contradicted by a sample with probability $`1/256`$ under a strictly positive binary population, zero fitted weights at one profile, and a nonsingular full regression Gram matrix.

[`AronowAudit.P015.fitted_zero_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L285) · [P015-T3](#p015-t3)

The printed rule of substituting an inverse link into the logistic residual equation fails for a Bernoulli odds link: the explicit 72-observation construction has zero substituted residual equation but genuine likelihood score $`(2/3,1/3,-2/3)`$.

[`AronowAudit.P015.odds_score_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L483) · [P015-T5](#p015-t5)

The printed scalar precision-pooling description fails in an exact two-group construction where both within-group OLS slopes are zero but full-vector precision pooling gives slope $`-10/61`$, which no scalar combination of those slopes can equal.

[`AronowAudit.P015.group_pooling_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P015/P015_Regression.lean#L880) · [P015-T9](#p015-t9)

The printed claim of arbitrarily precise Taylor approximation fails for the constructed 200-observation logistic sample: its unique finite MLE has predictors $`\pm\log 99`$ outside the expansion radius $`\pi`$ about zero, and every Taylor degree has probability error at least $`49/100`$ at each predictor.

## Caveats

- The asymptotic results use an iid superpopulation reading of the sampling assumptions; the main OLS limits also assume a standard Borel sampling space and measurable coordinates.
- OLSRegularity requires integrability and convergence in probability for eight moment families, nonsingular population control moments, and a positive finite residual second moment; the effective-covariate result uses its separate seven-family regularity condition.
- Sample matrix inverses use Lean’s zero extension at singular matrices; the asymptotic theorems retain nonsingular population moments.
- The random-assignment corollary assumes a common treatment distribution independent of the entire baseline-outcome, effect, and covariate vector; it does not cover unequal covariate-dependent assignment probabilities.
- The logistic scaling identity concerns the affine surrogate root; it supplies no approximation-error bound for the exact maximum likelihood estimator.
- The leverage calculation uses population-scaled leverage, not an exact expectation of finite-iid-sample leverage.
- The finite counterexamples are constructed populations or samples, not findings about the application data; the multivalued positivity example uses the section that drops affine potential outcomes, and does not refute Theorem A.1.

### P015-T2

A counterexample to the sentence that “the effective sample that multiple regression generates is one for which positivity holds by construction”

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 261 (PDF p. 12), right column; the positivity display is on the same page, and $`\tau_i(d,d')`$ and Expression (11) are on p. 260 (PDF p. 11)

Eight Lean statements formalize Example B of P015-E006, in which $`(X_i,D_i)`$ takes the values $`(0,0)`$, $`(0,1)`$, $`(1,0)`$ and $`(1,2)`$ with probability $`1/4`$ each. The main statement asserts that $`\mathrm{E}[D_i\mid X_i]=1/2+X_i/2`$ and that the weights $`w_i`$ on the four cells are $`1/4`$, $`1/4`$, $`1`$ and $`1`$. It asserts that the cells have the probabilities $`1/10`$, $`1/10`$, $`2/5`$ and $`2/5`$ in the effective population, so that the profiles $`X_i=0`$ and $`X_i=1`$ have effective probabilities $`1/5`$ and $`4/5`$. It asserts that $`\Pr[D_i=1\mid X_i=1]=0`$ in the population and in the effective population, and that, under either law, a value $`d`$ has positive conditional probability at both profiles if and only if $`d=0`$. No pair $`d\neq d'`$ then satisfies the positivity display at both profiles. Two sets of potential outcomes are compared: $`Y_i(d)=d`$ for every unit, and the same except that $`Y_i(1)=3`$ when $`X_i=1`$. For both, the statement asserts that $`(Y_i(0),Y_i(1),Y_i(2))`$ is conditionally independent of $`D_i`$ given $`X_i`$ and that the observed outcome equals $`D_i`$ in every cell. It asserts that the mean of $`\tau_i(0,1)`$ is $`1`$ and $`2`$ in the population, and $`1`$ and $`13/5`$ in the effective population. The last clause concerns independent draws from the four cells and any sequence of functions $`T_n`$ of the first $`n`$ observed triples $`(X_i,D_i,Y_i)`$. It asserts that $`T_n`$ cannot converge in probability to $`1`$ under the first set of outcomes and to $`2`$ under the second, nor to $`1`$ and to $`13/5`$. In the Lean statements the population is a finite set of four cells with probabilities, and conditional independence is the factorization of the cell probabilities within each profile. Unconfoundedness is asserted for the three potential outcomes at the values that $`D_i`$ takes, which implies Expression (11) for each pair of these values. A supporting statement repeats this impossibility for the two pairs of effects, $`1,2`$ and $`1,13/5`$, and for every sequence of estimators under independent draws from these four cells. The reason is that the observed triples coincide under the two sets of outcomes.

### P015-T12

The Taylor approximation of the inverse logit for the weights, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 257 (PDF p. 8), left column

Two Lean statements give the formal content of the corrected passage of P015-E009. Let $`f(a)=1/[1+\exp(-a)]`$, let $`0\lt P_Y\lt 1`$ and let $`c=\mathrm{logit}(P_Y)`$. The first statement is the local first-order approximation. It asserts that $`f(a)-\bigl[P_Y+P_Y(1-P_Y)(a-c)\bigr]=O\bigl((a-c)^2\bigr)\qquad\text{as } a\to c .`$ The second is the statement about expansions of increasing order. Let $`0\le r\lt \rho\lt \sqrt{c^2+\pi^2}`$, let $`T_N`$ be the Taylor polynomial of $`f`$ of degree $`N`$ at $`c`$, and let $`M_\rho`$ be the supremum of $`|f(z)|`$ over the complex $`z`$ such that $`|z-c|=\rho`$. The statement asserts that the power series of $`f`$ at $`c`$, over the complex numbers, has radius of convergence exactly $`\sqrt{c^2+\pi^2}`$. It asserts that, for every $`N`$ and every real $`a`$ such that $`|a-c|\le r`$, $`|f(a)-T_N(a)|\le M_\rho\,\frac{(r/\rho)^{N+1}}{1-r/\rho},`$ and that this bound tends to $`0`$ as $`N\to\infty`$. Both statements concern the mean function only. Neither asserts anything about the accuracy of the fitted coefficients or of the weights, which agrees with the last sentence of the correction. The Lean statements for the counterexample of the same finding exhibit failure at predictors outside the convergence radius.

### P015-T8

The extension of the logistic argument of Appendix C to other maximum likelihood models, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Appendix C, pp. 264–265 (PDF pp. 15–16); main text, pp. 256–257 (PDF pp. 7–8)

The fifteen Lean statements describe the score equations and Taylor identity underlying the correction of P015-E008. For a finite binary sample $`Y_i\in\{0,1\}`$ with $`W_i=(1,D_i,X_i)`$, let $`m`$ be differentiable on an open set $`I`$ with values in $`(0,1)`$. Put $`h=m'/[m(1-m)]`$ and $`\eta_i=W_i\theta`$. At coefficient vectors with all $`\eta_i\in I`$, the directional derivative of the log likelihood is $`\sum_i h(\eta_i)\{Y_i-m(\eta_i)\}W_iv`$; grouping observations with the same design row replaces $`Y_i-m(\eta_i)`$ by $`k_i-n_im(\eta_i)`$. For the logit, $`h=1`$. A scalar canonical log likelihood with one common dispersion $`s\gt 0`$ has directional derivative $`s^{-1}\sum_i\{Y_i-\kappa'(\eta_i)\}W_iv`$, so its score equations are the residual equations. The sum of the first Taylor polynomials of the scalar score contributions, evaluated at $`W_i(\theta_0+\delta)`$ and multiplied by $`W_i'`$, is exactly $`h_0r+(-h_0f_0A+h_1B)\delta`$. The Taylor assumptions are differentiability of $`m`$ on an open set containing $`\eta_0`$, values in $`(0,1)`$ there, and differentiability of $`m'`$ at $`\eta_0`$. They give a first-order little-$`o`$ remainder for the actual score. A quadratic remainder requires additional smoothness, for example $`m`$ of class $`C^3`$ near $`\eta_0`$. The Taylor component here is the exact polynomial identity; ordered, multinomial and duration models require separate likelihood calculations. Supporting grouped calculus statements allow real counts or designs without an intercept.

### P015-T18

The extension of the OLS weighting result to nonlinear models fit by maximum likelihood (Appendix C and pp. 256–257), as printed

**Kind:** `result_touched_by_finding`

**Location:** Appendix C, pp. 264–265 (PDF pp. 15–16); main text, pp. 256–257 (PDF pp. 7–8)

The passages discussed in P015-E008 contain three claims. (A) For logistic regression, the maximum likelihood estimator solves the displayed estimating equation. (B) For each of the other listed models, the maximum likelihood estimating equation is obtained by substituting the predicted mean or the inverse link of the model. (C) To a first approximation, the maximum likelihood coefficient on the treatment in any generalized linear model then differs from the OLS coefficient only by a scaling factor that depends solely on the mean of $`Y_i`$. Two Lean statements address these claims. One combines the logistic score result (A) for binary outcomes and a finite global likelihood maximizer, a counterexample to (B), and a local population counterexample to (C). The other states the same refutation of (B) separately, for binary outcomes with a scalar inverse link. Let $`Y_i\in\{0,1\}`$, let $`W_i`$ be a row of regressors whose first entry is $`1`$, and let $`m`$ be differentiable and one-to-one on an open set $`I`$, with values in $`(0,1)`$. The assertion being refuted is that these conditions always imply $`\sum_iW_i'\{Y_i-m(W_i\theta)\}=0 .`$ An identity-link example with no controls has rows $`W_i=(1,3/4),(1,2/3),(1,3/5)`$, outcomes $`(1,0,1)`$, domain $`I=(0,1)`$ and a global maximizer $`\theta=(0,1)`$, but the intercept entry of this residual sum is $`-1/60`$. The quantified claim includes probit and other binary-outcome models with one linear predictor; the identity-link example does not itself establish a failure for probit. ### P015-T17

The paragraph of p. 261 that connects positivity and the multiple regression weights, as printed

**Kind:** `result_touched_by_finding`

**Location:** “Estimating Representative Causal Effects”, p. 261 (PDF p. 12), last paragraph of the left column, continued in the right column; the positivity display is on the same page

The paragraph corrected in P015-E006 contains three implications. (A) Positivity for $`(d,d')`$ at every $`x`$ implies that $`\mathrm{Var}[D_i\mid X_i=x]\gt 0`$ for every $`x`$. (B) If the regression places approximately or exactly no weight on a covariate profile, then positivity is violated and no consistent, extrapolation-free estimator of $`\mathrm{E}[\tau_i(d,d')]`$ exists for the target population. (C) Positivity holds in the effective sample. Two Lean statements contain the same positivity identities and three counterexamples. For measurable covariates with countably many values and measurable singletons, and measurable treatment with $`\mathrm{E}[D_i^2]\lt \infty`$, the correction concerns cells with $`\Pr[X_i=x]\gt 0`$. Positive conditional probabilities for two distinct treatment values imply positive cell variance, and zero cell variance forces the treatment to equal its cell mean almost surely within the cell. For binary treatment it gives $`\mathrm{Var}[D_i\mid X_i=x]=p_x(1-p_x)`$, where $`p_x=\Pr[D_i=1\mid X_i=x]`$. If the squared-residual weight $`w_i`$ is integrable with $`\mathrm{E}[w_i]\gt 0`$, then, with $`Q(A)=\mathrm{E}[w_i\mathbf{1}_A]/\mathrm{E}[w_i]`$, these statements assert $`Q(X_i=x)=\frac{\Pr[X_i=x]\mathrm{Var}[D_i\mid X_i=x]}{\mathrm{E}[w_i]},`$ including the equivalence between zero effective-cell mass and zero cell variance. For binary treatment and $`0\lt p_x\lt 1`$, it also gives $`Q(D_i=1\mid X_i=x)=1-p_x`$ and $`Q(D_i=0\mid X_i=x)=p_x`$. Three counterexamples address the invalid implications: arbitrarily small positive effective-profile mass despite positivity, with a consistent inverse-probability estimator in a fixed example; a multivalued treatment with missing pair support and observationally identical potential-outcome worlds having different nominal and effective causal effects; and a positive-probability sample with zero fitted weights in a profile despite population positivity. The multivalued example also excludes one observed-data estimator being consistent in both worlds for the different effects. A third statement gives a concrete counterexample to (C): the nominal law is uniform on four atoms with $`X=(0,0,1,1)`$ and $`D=(0,1,0,2)`$. Both treatment values $`0`$ and $`1`$ occur nominally, but the squared-residual effective law satisfies $`Q(X=1)\gt 0`$ and $`Q(D=1\mid X=1)=0`$. These statements describe the correction and explicit failures of the printed implications. They do not define a universal threshold for “approximately” zero weight or a general class of extrapolation-free estimators, and the conditional-cell results do not cover continuously distributed covariates. The sentences about Jensen (2003) and Gerber and Huber (2010) are applied conclusions and are not formalized.
