# P017: Data-Adaptive Causal Effects and Superefficiency

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P017.json)

P. M. Aronow (2016). Data-Adaptive Causal Effects and Superefficiency. Journal of Causal Inference 4(2).

DOI: [10.1515/jci-2016-0007](https://doi.org/10.1515/jci-2016-0007)

arXiv: [1601.01413](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01413)

## Overview

This work constructs a data-adaptive causal target by clipping an effect estimator to an interval centered on the population mean, with radius determined by the effect distribution's support. The Lean development proves that the target's distance from that mean is of order $`n^{-1/2}`$ in probability and the estimator's probability and mean-square rates relative to the adaptive target. It also refutes the printed all-realizations nonnegative-weight representation and supplies corrected representation conditions and replacements for erroneous intermediate steps in the rate proofs.

Areas: Asymptotic statistics, Causal identification.

## Key results

If the original estimator is root-$`n`$ asymptotically normal for the population mean effect, the clipped adaptive target differs from that mean by $`O_p(n^{-1/2})`$, including zero and infinite clipping radii.

[`AronowAudit.P017.proposition2`](../../AronowAudit/P017/P017_Audit.lean#L1104) · [P017-T16](#p017-t16)

With positive effect-support radius and a root-$`n`$ asymptotically normal original estimator, the estimator's error relative to its actual clipped adaptive target is $`o_p(n^{-1/2})`$.

[`AronowAudit.P017.proposition3`](../../AronowAudit/P017/P017_Audit.lean#L1072) · [P017-T14](#p017-t14)

For a positive effect-support radius and a measurable estimator satisfying $`\sqrt n(\hat\theta_n-\mu)=Z_n+\varepsilon_n`$ almost surely with $`\mu`$ the population mean, centered Gaussian $`Z_n`$ of fixed positive variance and $`E[\varepsilon_n^2]=o(n^{-1/2})`$, mean squared error relative to the actual clipped target is $`o(n^{-1})`$.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed Proposition 1 claim that every realized adaptive target is a nonnegative weighted population effect is false: a genuine empirical-law IPW estimator satisfying the asymptotic-normality premise can clip to an unattainable support endpoint.

[`AronowAudit.P017.Wave1.proposition1_printed_empirical_counterexample_printed_negation`](../../AronowAudit/P017/Wave1/Checks/FidP017Contracts.lean#L58) · [P017-T9](#p017-t9)

In place of the proof's assertion that nonnegative weighting attains every value between support endpoints, the corrected characterization says a real value $`t`$ is an admissible weighted mean exactly when the effect has positive mass at $`t`$ or positive probability on both sides of $`t`$.

[`AronowAudit.P017.weighted_mean_characterization`](../../AronowAudit/P017/P017_Audit.lean#L515) · [P017-T2](#p017-t2)

Replacing Definition 2's radius $`c\gt 0`$ by any fixed finite $`d`$ with $`0\lt d\lt c`$ repairs Proposition 1's every-realization representation: every clipped estimate is an admissible nonnegative weighted population effect.

[`AronowAudit.P017.interior_radius_representation`](../../AronowAudit/P017/P017_Audit.lean#L708) · [P017-T4](#p017-t4)

In place of the printed Gaussian-tail calculation for the auxiliary normal estimate minus the actual adaptive target, the corrected Proposition 3 proof establishes $`\hat\theta_n-\theta_{F_n}=o_p(a_n)`$ for every positive deterministic sequence $`a_n`$ under root-$`n`$ asymptotic normality and positive clipping radius.

[`AronowAudit.P017.corrected_proposition3`](../../AronowAudit/P017/P017_Audit.lean#L832) · [P017-T7](#p017-t7)

Where the printed Proposition 4 proof incorrectly treats the auxiliary normal estimate's deviation from the actual adaptive target as zero in the central interval, the corrected finite-positive-radius proof bounds actual clipping-error MSE by twice the auxiliary Gaussian clipping-residual MSE plus $`2E[\varepsilon_n^2]/n`$, without assuming independence of the Gaussian and remainder.

[`AronowAudit.P017.Wave1.corrected_proposition4_finite_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P017/Wave1/Gap/GLP017B4.lean#L632) · [P017-T8](#p017-t8)

Replacing the post-Proposition 3 description of the effect support as the zero-error region, the corrected statement identifies zero error exactly with membership in Definition 2's clipping interval and proves that this event's probability tends to one under positive radius and root-$`n`$ asymptotic normality.

[`AronowAudit.P017.corrected_zero_error_sentence`](../../AronowAudit/P017/P017_Audit.lean#L1033) · [P017-T13](#p017-t13)

## Caveats

- The adaptive target itself depends on the realized estimator and on the population mean and support radius; these rates concern error relative to that target, not superefficient estimation of a fixed population mean.
- Support means closed support of the effect's probability law, rather than the range of the effect function; a finite endpoint without an atom is not an admissible nonnegative weighted mean.
- Admissible weights are measurable and nonnegative with finite positive expectation and integrable weighted effect, and may depend on all full-data coordinates; no bounded-weight restriction is imposed.
- Population and sampling probability laws are separate in the general rate results because the paper specifies no sampling design; the representation refutation nevertheless uses an actual i.i.d. empirical-data estimator.
- Asymptotic normality is read as weak convergence to a Gaussian with positive finite variance; the MSE result separately requires a same-space Gaussian-plus-remainder decomposition and its mean-square remainder condition, without independence.
- Infinite radius makes clipping the identity; positive-radius assumptions are required for the small-error conclusions, while Proposition 2 also allows radius zero.
- The smaller-radius repair changes the adaptive target and the sufficient support condition in its corollary; it is not an all-realizations proof for the original target.
- The corrected zero-error probability theorem supplies convergence to one without a quantitative rate.

### P017-T1

A counterexample to Proposition 1 and to the fact given as its proof

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Proposition 1 and the sentence that follows it, PDF p. 2 (the pages are not numbered); Assumption 1, PDF p. 1

The Lean statements give the counterexample of P017-E001, which is my construction and is not in the paper. Let $`\tau`$ be uniform on $`(0,1)`$, let $`B`$ equal $`-1`$ or $`1`$ with probability $`1/2`$ each, and let $`T`$ be a fair treatment assignment, with $`\tau`$, $`B`$ and $`T`$ independent. Let $`Y(0)=B`$ and $`Y(1)=B+\tau`$, and for $`n`$ independent units let $`\hat\theta=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^nX_i`$ with $`X_i=2T_iY_i-2(1-T_i)Y_i`$. The main statement asserts that $`\mathrm{E}_G[\tau]=1/2`$, the support is $`[0,1]`$, $`c=1/2`$ and neither endpoint has positive probability, and that $`X_i`$ has mean $`1/2`$ and variance $`53/12`$, that $`\hat\theta`$ has mean $`1/2`$ and variance $`53/(12n)`$, and that $`\sqrt n(\hat\theta-1/2)`$ converges in distribution to $`N(0,53/12)`$. Further, it asserts that every measurable nonnegative weighting $`w`$ with $`0\lt \mathrm{E}_G[w]\lt \infty`$ satisfies $`0\lt \mathrm{E}_G[w\tau]/\mathrm{E}_G[w]\lt 1`$. For every $`n\ge1`$, the event that $`(B_i,T_i)=(-1,0)`$ for all $`i`$ has probability $`4^{-n}`$; on this event $`\hat\theta=2`$ and $`\theta_{F_n}=1`$, the empirical distribution $`F_n`$ is the point mass at $`(T,Y)=(0,-1)`$, and no weighting has weighted mean $`\theta_{F_n}`$. The disjoint event that $`(B_i,T_i)=(1,0)`$ for all $`i`$ also has probability $`4^{-n}`$; there $`\hat\theta=-2`$, $`\theta_{F_n}=0`$ and $`F_n`$ is the point mass at $`(T,Y)=(0,1)`$, again with no weighted representation. Four supporting statements each assert a part of the main one: the probability $`4^{-n}`$ of each event, the values $`2`$ and $`-2`$ of the estimator, the disjointness of the two events, and the bound $`\mathrm{E}_G[w\tau]/\mathrm{E}_G[w]\lt 1`$ on an arbitrary measure space on which $`\tau\lt 1`$ almost everywhere. The population is the product of the laws of $`\tau`$, $`B`$ and $`T`$, the sample is an infinite sequence of independent units, and a weighting may depend on all three variables.

### P017-T2

The fact given as the proof of Proposition 1, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Sentence after Proposition 1, PDF p. 2 (the pages are not numbered)

The Lean statements give the corrected fact of P017-E001, which says which values a weighted mean with nonnegative weights attains. Let $`G`$ be a probability measure, let $`\tau`$ be a measurable function with $`\mathrm{E}_G[|\tau|]\lt \infty`$, and let $`t`$ be a real number. The main statement asserts that there exists a nonnegative weighting $`w`$ such that $`\mathrm{E}_G[w\tau]/\mathrm{E}_G[w]=t`$ if and only if $`\Pr_G(\tau=t)\gt 0,\quad\text{or}\quad \Pr_G(\tau\lt t)\gt 0\ \text{ and }\ \Pr_G(\tau\gt t)\gt 0.`$ A second statement asserts the support consequences: every real $`t`$ strictly between the infimum and the supremum of the support is attained, a finite endpoint is attained if and only if it has positive probability, and no value beyond the two bounds is attained. Six supporting statements assert the steps: if $`\Pr_G(\tau=t)\gt 0`$, then there exists a weighting with $`\mathrm{E}_G[w]=1`$ and weighted mean $`t`$; if $`\Pr_G(\tau\lt t)\gt 0`$ and $`\Pr_G(\tau\gt t)\gt 0`$, then there exists a weighting with weighted mean $`t`$; and if $`\tau\lt t`$ almost surely or $`\tau\gt t`$ almost surely, then no such weighting exists. A value strictly between the bounds of the support has positive probability on both sides, a measurable set $`S`$ with $`\Pr_G(\tau\in S)\gt 0`$ meets the support, and every weighted mean lies between the bounds. A weighting is a measurable function $`w\ge0`$ on the full-data space with $`w`$ and $`w\tau`$ integrable and $`\mathrm{E}_G[w]\gt 0`$; the support is the closed support of the law of $`\tau`$, with possibly infinite bounds. For the case with positive probability on both sides, the supporting statement asserts existence only, not the displayed weight in P017-E001 or $`\mathrm{E}_G[w]=1`$.

### P017-T3

Proposition 1, as corrected with Definition 2 unchanged

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Proposition 1 and the paragraph that follows it, PDF p. 2 (the pages are not numbered)

The Lean statement gives the alternative correction of P017-E001, which keeps the radius $`c`$ in Definition 2 and weakens Proposition 1. Let $`\mu=\mathrm{E}_G[\tau]`$, suppose that Assumption 1 holds, and write $`C(x)`$ for the value $`x`$ clipped to $`[\mu-c,\ \mu+c]`$, so that $`\theta_{F_n}=C(\hat\theta)`$. The statement asserts, first, that for all real $`x`$, $`C(x)`$ lies between the support bounds, and that there exists a nonnegative weighting $`w`$ with $`\mathrm{E}_G[w\tau]/\mathrm{E}_G[w]=C(x)`$ if and only if $`C(x)`$ lies strictly between these bounds, or equals one of them and has positive probability under $`G`$. Second, such a weighting exists whenever $`|x-\mu|\lt c`$. Third, it exists for all $`x`$ if $`c=+\infty`$, or if each of $`\mu-c`$ and $`\mu+c`$ that is an endpoint of the support has positive probability. Fourth, if $`\hat\theta`$ is root-$`n`$ consistent and asymptotically normal for $`\mu`$, then $`\Pr(|\hat\theta-\mu|\lt c)\to1`$, and the probability that such a weighting exists for $`\theta_{F_n}`$ tends to one. The first three parts are stated for every real value of the estimate, since the target depends on $`F_n`$ only through $`\hat\theta`$. The estimator is a sequence of measurable functions on a sampling probability space that is separate from $`G`$, and Definition 1 is read as convergence in distribution of $`\sqrt n(\hat\theta-\mu)`$ to $`N(0,\sigma^2)`$ for some $`\sigma^2\gt 0`$.

### P017-T4

Definition 2 and Proposition 1, as corrected with a fixed radius $`0\lt d\lt c`$

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Definition 2 and Proposition 1, PDF p. 2 (the pages are not numbered)

The Lean statements give the correction of P017-E001, in which a fixed finite radius $`d`$ with $`0\lt d\lt c`$ replaces $`c`$ in Definition 2. Let $`\mu=\mathrm{E}_G[\tau]`$, suppose that $`\tau`$ has finite expectation and Assumption 1 holds (allowing $`c=+\infty`$), and write $`\theta^{(d)}_{F_n}`$ for $`\hat\theta`$ clipped to $`[\mu-d,\ \mu+d]`$.
