[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P019.json)

P. M. Aronow, Alexander Coppock, Forrest W. Crawford, and Donald P. Green (2015). Combining List Experiment and Direct Question Estimates of Sensitive Behavior Prevalence. Journal of Survey Statistics and Methodology 3:43–66.

DOI: [10.1093/jssam/smu023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jssam/smu023)

arXiv: [1312.1268](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1268)

## Overview

This work combines direct answers with list-experiment responses to estimate sensitive-behavior prevalence and constructs placebo checks of the maintained response and assignment assumptions. The Lean development covers population identification, asymptotic variance and normal inference, strict efficiency relative to the standard list estimator, and the two placebo tests. Corrected statements address finite-sample conditioning and product variance, a sign in the distributional comparison, simulation response coding, Fisher combination, and the reading-versus-watching interpretation of one survey item.

Areas: Experimental design and estimation, Asymptotic statistics, Causal identification.

## Key results

Under Assumptions 1–4, sensitive-behavior prevalence equals the direct-answer mean plus the negative-answer share times the treatment-control list-mean difference among negative direct answers.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The simulated design effect is an increase rather than the printed ceiling suppression: for $`W\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(4,2/5)`$ and affected share $`\rho\in[0,1]`$, affected confessors report $`W+2`$ below four and five at four, and the mixture calculation gives $`\beta=1+(609/625)\rho`$ instead of $`1-(16/625)\rho`$. Within confessors, this mixture equals the conditional-mean contrast if design-affected status is independent of $`W`$, treatment is independent of the joint pair consisting of $`W`$ and design-affected status, and both treatment arms have positive probability.

[`AronowAudit.P019.ceiling_corrected`](../../AronowAudit/P019/P019_Audit.lean#L614) · [P019-T2](#p019-t2)

The printed product-variance equality is corrected under Assumptions 1–4 and $`n\ge2`$ by conditioning on the event $`D`$ that both negative-answer treatment cells are occupied: the exact expression differs from the printed three-term formula by $`\mathrm{Cov}(A^2,h\mid D)`$, with $`A`$ the negative-answer sample share and $`h`$ the sum of the two population cell variances divided by their sample counts.

[`AronowAudit.P019.Wave1.corrected_product_variance_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P019/Wave1/Gap/GP019B4.lean#L707) · [P019-T4](#p019-t4)

The printed comparison with treated $`V+1`$ is corrected to treated $`V-1`$: under Assumptions 1–3 and positive confessor-cell probabilities, its conditional distribution equals that of control $`V`$ and of baseline count $`W`$ among confessors.

[`AronowAudit.P019.placebo_distribution_corrected`](../../AronowAudit/P019/P019_Audit.lean#L447) · [P019-T6](#p019-t6)

The printed diagnosis omits failure of treatment independence: with both confessor cells defined, a population contrast $`\beta\ne1`$ implies failure of at least one of monotonicity, the no-liars/no-design-effects response rule, or treatment independence.

[`AronowAudit.P019.beta_departure_diagnosis`](../../AronowAudit/P019/P019_Audit.lean#L436) · [P019-T8](#p019-t8)

The printed Fisher reference is replaced by a sufficient joint-premise theorem: with Assumptions 1–4 for each of five lists, mutually independent assignments, and the whole assignment vector independent of all lists’ latent response variables, the five p-values converge jointly to independent uniforms and their Fisher statistic converges to $`\chi^2_{10}`$.

[`AronowAudit.P019.Wave1.fisher_corrected_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P019/Wave1/Gap/GLP019B1.lean#L537) · [P019-T10](#p019-t10)

The watching-only interpretation of the CNN list and combined estimates is corrected under truthful instruments, assignment and watching probabilities strictly between zero and one, and independence of assignment from baseline count, reading and watching: both population functionals equal reading-or-watching prevalence and differ from watching prevalence by exactly the reader-only share.

## Caveats

- The population model is an arbitrary real-weighted finite observable law with Boolean behavior, direct-answer and assignment indicators and bounded natural list counts; sampling uses independent draws from that law.
- Assumptions 1–4 include no false confessions, the response rule V=W+XZ, independence of assignment from the whole (W,X,Y) triple, positive probabilities for all four response/assignment cells, and the stated positive variances.
- Asymptotic results complete undefined empty-cell means with arbitrary fixed real constants; sample variances below two observations are zero, and an additional fixed value completes an otherwise undefined whole variance estimate.
- A zero or negative standard error is assigned studentized value zero and p-value one in the placebo tests; positive cell probabilities and limiting variances make these exceptional events asymptotically negligible.
- The corrected finite product-variance declaration concerns conditioning on occupied cells; the asymptotic order of its covariance discrepancy is established by separate declarations.
- The Fisher theorem assumes its additional joint assignment premise and does not verify that premise for the survey or certify the reported empirical joint p-value; the simulation formulas likewise do not certify Monte Carlo power.
- The CNN correction assumes fully truthful answers to the two differently worded instruments; a nonsignificant placebo test does not establish that the underlying behavioral constructs coincide.
- The asymptotic-inference theorem gives a Gaussian limit, consistent variance estimation and the corrected Corollary 1 coverage statement in P019-T11 part (c), under its standing assumptions; it does not establish the other corrections grouped in P019-T11.
