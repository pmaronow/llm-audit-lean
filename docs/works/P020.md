# P020: Unbiased Estimation of the Average Treatment Effect in Cluster-Randomized Experiments

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P020.json)

Joel A. Middleton and P. M. Aronow (2015). Unbiased Estimation of the Average Treatment Effect in Cluster-Randomized Experiments. Statistics, Politics and Policy 6(1–2):39–75.

DOI: [10.1515/spp-2013-0002](https://doi.org/10.1515/spp-2013-0002)

## Overview

The work compares estimators of the individual average treatment effect in cluster-randomized experiments with unequal cluster sizes. The Lean development covers Horvitz–Thompson and fixed-coefficient Des Raj unbiasedness, exact and conservative variance formulas, optimal coefficients, independent blocks, and an explicit growing-cluster consistency regime. It also gives counterexamples to unrestricted efficiency claims and the printed logistic-model specification, alongside corrected covariance and transformation identities.

Areas: Experimental design and estimation, Regression and variance estimation, Asymptotic statistics.

## Key results

Under uniform allocation of a fixed positive number of clusters to each arm, the Horvitz–Thompson estimators are unbiased for both potential-outcome totals and the individual average treatment effect.

[`AronowAudit.P020.ht_unbiased`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L657) · [P020-T12](#p020-t12)

For fixed covariate cluster totals and fixed real adjustment coefficients, the covariate-augmented Des Raj estimators are unbiased for both potential totals and the individual average treatment effect under complete cluster randomization.

[`AronowAudit.P020.des_raj_covariates_unbiased`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L778) · [P020-T13](#p020-t13)

With positive cluster-size variance, $`k_*`$ uniquely minimizes Des Raj randomization variance, strict improvement over Horvitz–Thompson holds exactly when $`k^2\lt 2kk_*`$, and equality holds exactly at $`k=0`$ or $`k=2k_*`$.

[`AronowAudit.P020.optimal_raj_variance`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L2928) · [P020-T14](#p020-t14)

With at least two clusters per arm, the combined Horvitz–Thompson variance estimator has expectation equal to $`V_{\mathrm{apx}}`$ and at least as large as the true randomization variance.

[`AronowAudit.P020.combined_variance_conservative`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L1486) · [P020-T15](#p020-t15)

Under independent complete randomizations within blocks, the population-size-weighted Horvitz–Thompson estimator is unbiased and its variance is the sum of within-block variances multiplied by squared population-size weights.

[`AronowAudit.P020.independent_blocks_unbiased_variance`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L1968) · [P020-T15](#p020-t15)

As independent copies of the original cluster population are added, each with its own fixed-count randomization, the four normalized arm totals converge in probability to their stated limits and the pooled difference in means is consistent for the original average treatment effect.

[`AronowAudit.P020.growing_clusters_consistent`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L2314) · [P020-T17](#p020-t17)

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed guarantee that asymptotic bias entails eventual relative inefficiency fails along within-cluster replication of a three-cluster population: sample size is $`7h`$, difference-in-means bias remains $`-1/315`$, and its MSE is smaller than that of every fixed-coefficient Des Raj estimator, including Horvitz–Thompson.

[`AronowAudit.P020.efficiency_all_replications_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L2611) · [P020-T1](#p020-t1)

The logistic model with the printed coefficients and cluster size measured as a count cannot have the reported average effect $`0.007`$: for any nonempty finite population with sizes at least one, its average treatment probability contrast differs from $`7/1000`$.

[`AronowAudit.P020.printed_model_average_ne_reported`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Logistic.lean#L56) · [P020-T3](#p020-t3)

Equation (31) prints the same covariance twice; the corrected expectation of the estimated-coefficient Des Raj effect is the ATE plus $`M/N`$ times the covariance of the coefficient with control mean size minus its covariance with treatment mean size.

[`AronowAudit.P020.estimated_coefficient_bias`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L816) · [P020-T5](#p020-t5)

The second line of equation (25) incorrectly uses treatment potential totals in the control sum; using control totals there proves that transforming outcomes by $`b_0+b_1Y`$ and the coefficient by $`b_0+b_1k`$ transforms the Des Raj effect estimate by the factor $`b_1`$.

[`AronowAudit.P020.des_raj_affine_invariant`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L847) · [P020-T7](#p020-t7)

The expectation bracket in equation (35) closes after only one factor; for a nonempty cluster list, enclosing both factors gives the covariance of the differenced totals and its expansion into the original covariance, both size covariances, and the squared-coefficient size-variance term.

[`AronowAudit.P020.differenced_covariance`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L361) · [P020-T8](#p020-t8)

The printed sharp-null moment facts omit total superscripts; applying them to cluster totals proves that the observed-total sharp-null variance formula equals the true Horvitz–Thompson randomization variance under the individual sharp null.

[`AronowAudit.P020.sharp_null_exact_variance`](../../AronowAudit/P020/P020_Design.lean#L1444) · [P020-T10](#p020-t10)

## Caveats

- A population is represented by positive integer cluster sizes and fixed real potential cluster totals, with uniform allocation of $`m_t`$ clusters and $`0\lt m_t\lt M`$; expectations concern this finite randomization law.
- Des Raj unbiasedness uses coefficients fixed independently of assignment; an estimated coefficient is an arbitrary function of assignment and generally introduces the stated covariance bias.
- Optimal-coefficient uniqueness assumes positive cluster-size variance; with equal cluster sizes, the Des Raj estimator equals Horvitz–Thompson for every coefficient.
- The consistency theorem adds independent copies of an experiment and randomizes separately within each copy; it does not use a single complete randomization of the pooled clusters.
- Enlarging a fixed set of clusters by copying individuals preserves the estimator distribution in the efficiency counterexample; the comparison concerns Horvitz–Thompson and fixed-coefficient Des Raj estimators, not all unbiased estimators.
- The logistic refutation uses size as the printed count and exact printed coefficients; a separate scale-in-thousands repair is conditional and does not recover the fitted predictor scale or establish the reported 0.007 effect.
- Finite cluster variances and covariances use divisor M; the generic covariance expansion also permits arbitrary real size features.

A counterexample to the sentences on asymptotic bias and relative efficiency, as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 40 (PDF p. 2); p. 42 (PDF p. 4)

The Lean statements give the counterexample of P020-E001, in which the population has $`M=3`$ clusters of sizes $`(1,2,4)`$, with $`m_t=1`$, control totals $`(0,1,1)`$ and treatment totals $`(0,0,0)`$, so that $`N=7`$ and $`\Delta=-2/7`$. One statement asserts that these totals are the sums of the individual outcomes $`(0)`$, $`(1,0)`$ and $`(1,0,0,0)`$ under control and of zeros under treatment. A second asserts that, over the three equally likely assignments, the difference in means $`\widehat{\Delta_S}`$ takes the values $`(-1/3,-1/5,-1/3)`$, with bias $`-1/315`$ and mean squared error $`131/33075`$. The last two statements assert that $`7h\to\infty`$ and that $`\widehat{\Delta_S}`$ does not converge in probability to $`\Delta`$, the latter for the original population repeated along the sequence, connected to the copied populations by the replication identities. The comparison concerns only the Horvitz–Thompson estimator and the Des Raj estimators with fixed $`k`$.

### P020-T2

The sentences on asymptotic bias and relative efficiency, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 40 (PDF p. 2); p. 42 (PDF p. 4)

The Lean statements give the result stated in the corrected sentences of P020-E001. A randomization distribution is represented by a finite set of outcomes with nonnegative weights that sum to one, and an estimator by a real function on that set. For an estimator $`T`$ of a real number $`\Delta`$, write $`b=\mathrm{E}[T]-\Delta`$ and $`\mathrm{MSE}(T)=\mathrm{E}[(T-\Delta)^2]`$. Two statements assert that $`\mathrm{MSE}(T)=\mathrm{V}(T)+b^2`$ and that $`b^2\le\mathrm{MSE}(T)`$, and a third asserts that $`P(|T-\Delta|\gt \varepsilon)\le\mathrm{MSE}(T)/\varepsilon^2`$ for every $`\varepsilon\gt 0`$. The remaining two concern a sequence of such distributions indexed by $`h`$, with estimators $`T_h`$ and $`U_h`$ of $`\Delta_h`$. The first asserts that, if the bias of $`T_h`$ converges to a limit $`b\ne0`$ and $`\mathrm{MSE}(U_h)\to0`$, then there exists $`h_0`$ such that $`\mathrm{MSE}(U_h)\lt \mathrm{MSE}(T_h)`$ for all $`h\ge h_0`$. The second asserts that, if $`\mathrm{MSE}(U_h)\to0`$, then $`P(|U_h-\Delta_h|\gt \varepsilon)\to0`$ for every $`\varepsilon\gt 0`$. The statements are broader than the setting of the paper: the distributions need not come from a randomization of clusters, and the sets of outcomes may change with $`h`$. The index $`h`$ starts at $`0`$ and not at $`1`$, which does not affect the limits.

### P020-T3

A refutation of the logistic regression model of Section 6.2 with the coefficients as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 61 (PDF p. 23); definition of $`n_j`$ on p. 44 (PDF p. 6)

The Lean statements assert that the printed model, the printed coefficients $`\tau=0.4`$ and $`\phi=-0.9`$, and the reported average effect $`0.007`$ cannot hold together when $`n_j`$ is the number of individuals in cluster $`j`$ (see P020-E003). Let $`L(x)=1-(1+e^x)^{-1}`$ denote the printed link. Three statements assert that $`L`$ is strictly increasing, that $`2/5-(9/10)n\le-1/2`$ for every real $`n\ge1`$, and that $`L(\eta+2/5-(9/10)n)\lt L(\eta)`$ for every real $`\eta`$ and every real $`n\ge1`$. The main statements concern a population of $`N\ge1`$ individuals, in which individual $`i`$ has control linear predictor $`\eta_i`$ and cluster size $`n_i\ge1`$. They assert that $`\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N\Bigl(L\Bigl(\eta_i+\frac{2}{5}-\frac{9}{10}n_i\Bigr)-L(\eta_i)\Bigr)\lt 0,`$ and that this average is not $`7/1000`$. The intercept, the coefficient $`\beta`$ and the block coefficients enter only through $`\eta_i`$, which is an arbitrary real number, and the decimals $`0.4`$, $`0.9`$ and $`0.007`$ are taken as exact fractions. The statements are broader than the printed model, since $`\eta_i`$ and $`n_i`$ need not agree for individuals of the same cluster, and $`n_i`$ may be any real number that is at least $`1`$. A last statement gives the scalar instance $`n=1`$ and $`\eta=0`$, for which $`L(0)=1/2`$ and $`L(-1/2)\lt 1/2`$, without specifying a cluster design. Nothing is asserted about the scale of the predictor in the authors' fit.

The main Lean statement asserts that $`\mathrm{E}\bigl[\hat{\mathrm{V}}^N(\widehat{\Delta_{HT}})\bigr]\ge\mathrm{V}(\widehat{\Delta_{HT}})`$ if there exists a constant $`\tau`$ such that $`Y^T_{1j}-Y^T_{0j}=\tau`$ for every cluster $`j`$, or if $`m_c=m_t`$. Here $`\hat{\mathrm{V}}^N(\widehat{\Delta_{HT}})=M^4\sigma^2(Y^T_j)/(N^2(M-1)m_cm_t)`$, where $`\sigma^2(Y^T_j)`$ is the variance, with divisor $`M`$, of the cluster totals observed under the given assignment. The expectation and the variance $`\mathrm{V}(\widehat{\Delta_{HT}})`$ are taken over the equally likely sets of $`m_t`$ treated clusters, and the population is represented by its cluster sizes and its potential cluster totals. For every population, with $`d_j=Y^T_{1j}-Y^T_{0j}`$ and $`\overline{d}`$ the mean of the $`d_j`$, a second statement asserts that $`\mathrm{E}\bigl[\hat{\mathrm{V}}^N(\widehat{\Delta_{HT}})\bigr]-\mathrm{V}(\widehat{\Delta_{HT}})=\frac{M^2}{N^2(M-1)}\left[\frac{M(m_t-m_c)}{m_tm_c}\bigl(\sigma^2(Y^T_{1j})-\sigma^2(Y^T_{0j})\bigr)+\overline{d}^{\,2}+\frac{M-2}{M-1}\,\sigma^2(d_j)\right],`$ in which the first term vanishes under either condition. A third statement gives the expectation of $`\sigma^2(Y^T_j)`$, and five more give identities for means and variances that the derivation uses. The word “conservative” is taken in the sense of p. 51, a nonnegative bias. The inequality is therefore in expectation, and it is not asserted for each assignment (see P020-E009). The phrase “constant (at the cluster scale)” is taken as a constant effect on the cluster totals, so that a constant individual effect in clusters of unequal size is not covered. The statements concern the Horvitz–Thompson estimator, as the printed sentence does.

### P020-T17

Equations (9) and (10): consistency as the number of clusters grows, and an unchanged estimate as the clusters grow

**Kind:** `headline_result`

**Location:** pp. 45–47 (PDF pp. 7–9); Figure 1 on p. 46 (PDF p. 8)

For equation (9), the population of step $`h`$ consists of $`h`$ copies of the original $`M`$ clusters. In each copy $`m_t`$ clusters are allocated to treatment, independently across copies and with all allocations equally likely, and not in one draw from the pooled $`hM`$ clusters. The main Lean statement asserts that the four averages on p. 46 converge in probability to their printed limits, and that $`\widehat{\Delta_S}`$, computed from all $`hM`$ clusters, converges in probability to $`\Delta`$. Two statements assert that the sum of a quantity $`v_j`$ over one arm $`a`$ of one copy has expectation $`(m_a/M)\sum_jv_j`$ and absolute value at most $`\sum_j|v_j|`$. A third asserts that the average of $`h\ge1`$ such sums has the same expectation and $`1/h`$ times the variance, and a fourth that the right-hand side of equation (9) equals $`(Y_1^T-Y_0^T)/N`$. Thirteen further statements are steps of a derivation that uses bounds on mean squared errors in place of the weak law of large numbers and Slutsky’s theorem. The last of them asserts that $`\mathrm{E}\bigl[(\widehat{\Delta_S}-\Delta)^2\bigr]\le\frac{2C_1+2C_0}{h}\qquad\text{for every }h\ge1,`$ where $`C_1`$ and $`C_0`$ are constants of the original population, and this bound is more than the convergence that the paper states. For equation (10), the population of step $`h\ge1`$ has cluster sizes $`hn_j`$ and potential totals $`hY^T_{0j}`$ and $`hY^T_{1j}`$, with $`M`$ and $`m_t`$ fixed. Two statements assert that $`N_h=hN`$, and that $`\Delta`$, $`\widehat{\Delta_S}`$, the Horvitz–Thompson estimate and the Des Raj estimate with fixed $`k`$ are unchanged. The second extends equation (10) to the last two estimators and to every subset of clusters, with Lean’s value zero for a quotient whose denominator is zero when an arm is empty. A last statement asserts that, if the original bias of $`\widehat{\Delta_S}`$ is nonzero, it is unchanged for every $`h\ge1`$ and $`\widehat{\Delta_S}`$ does not converge in probability to $`\Delta`$. The non-convergence is written for the original population repeated along the sequence, connected to the growing populations by the replication identities.
