# P022: Sharp bounds on the variance in randomized experiments

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P022.json)

P. M. Aronow, Donald P. Green, and Donald K. K. Lee (2014). Sharp bounds on the variance in randomized experiments. Annals of Statistics 42(3):850–871.

DOI: [10.1214/13-aos1200](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-aos1200)

arXiv: [1405.6555](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.6555)

## Overview

The work derives sharp bounds on the randomization variance of the difference in means from the marginal potential-outcome distributions and studies estimation of those bounds. The Lean development covers the exact variance formula, sharp covariance bounds over all compatible couplings, estimator ordering, consistency, bounded-outcome tail rates and asymptotic Wald coverage. It separately refutes printed Lemma 2 and supplies its left-limit correction, verifies a corrected exact-arithmetic quantile algorithm, and proves several numerical and proof-level corrections without invalidating the main propositions.

Areas: Experimental design and estimation, Partial identification and bounds, Asymptotic statistics.

## Key results

For uniform assignment to fixed treatment and control groups of at least two units each, the difference in means is unbiased with the stated randomization variance, all couplings of the empirical marginal laws obey the sharp covariance bounds, and similarly or oppositely ordered quantiles attain the corresponding upper or lower bounds.

[`AronowAudit.P022.Wave1.sharp_bounds_variance_and_all_couplings`](../../AronowAudit/P022/Wave1/SharpBounds.lean#L24) · [P022-T13](#p022-t13)

When the entire population is assigned to treatment or control, every assignment satisfies $`\widehat V_n^{b-}\le\widehat V_n^L\le\widehat V_n^H\le\widehat V_n^{b+}\le\widehat V_n^a`$.

Under the original conditions 1–4, the N-scaled population variance bounds have the printed limits and the two estimated endpoints jointly have error $`o_P(1/N)`$, with the required second moments and cross-product integrability for every compatible limiting law.

Under conditions 1–3 and uniformly bounded potential outcomes, both N-scaled endpoint estimation errors satisfy finite scaled-tail limsup bounds giving the printed $`O(1/N)`$ probability rates for every positive tolerance allocation.

## Corrections and counterexamples

Printed Lemma 2 claims uniform CDF convergence and scaled-tail bounds from conditions 1–3, but the nested outcomes $`y_{1i}=y_{0i}=-1/i`$ satisfy those conditions while both population and every sampled CDF remain at uniform distance one from their point-mass limit and both scaled-tail limsups are infinite.

[`AronowAudit.P022.lemma2_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P022/P022_Variance.lean#L849) · [P022-T1](#p022-t1)

Printed Lemma 2 lacks control of left limits at limiting atoms; adding convergence of each marginal CDF left limit at every limiting discontinuity gives uniform convergence and both scaled-tail bounds with the explicit common integer $`K(\eta)=2\lceil4/\min(\eta,1)\rceil`$.

[`AronowAudit.P022.Wave1.lemma2_corrected_explicit_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P022/Wave1/Gap/GLP022B3.lean#L624) · [P022-T2](#p022-t2)

The last equality of printed equation (11) sets the sampled-CDF variance to its upper bound, but with four all-one outcomes, two treated units and threshold zero, that variance is zero while the printed bound equals $`1/12`$.

[`AronowAudit.P022.equation11_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P022/P022_Variance.lean#L305) · [P022-T5](#p022-t5)

The printed Appendix B routine is said to compute the mathematical bound estimates, but on the four-unit all-one example its exact-arithmetic specification returns $`-1/50331648`$ in both modes while both mathematical estimates and the true randomization variance are zero.

[`AronowAudit.P022.appendixB_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P022/P022_Variance.lean#L426) · [P022-T9](#p022-t9)

The printed 7% nominal-variance reduction is incompatible with the Table 1 rounding bins and a common positive Wald multiplier: the reduction is between $`2400/397`$ and $`1204000/190969`$ percent, strictly between 5.5% and 6.5%, so it rounds to 6%.

[`AronowAudit.P022.table1_corrected_reduction`](../../AronowAudit/P022/P022_Numerical.lean#L53) · [P022-T11](#p022-t11)

The quoted sharp-null bias of $`-0.007`$ cannot describe the same finite simulation collection as the stated rounded variance and mean-width summaries under the common Wald construction: the resulting multiplier bound and squared-mean-width inequality make their simultaneous numerical constraints inconsistent.

## Caveats

- The design is uniform random assignment with population size $`N\ge4`$, treatment and control sizes at least two, and their sum at most N; permutations encode allocations with constant multiplicity.
- Conditions 1–3 include limiting allocation fractions, weak convergence of the nested potential-outcome populations and CDF convergence at limiting discontinuities; condition 4 is uniform square integrability, and the coverage result instead assumes bounded third absolute moments and nonsingularity.
- The added left-limit hypothesis belongs to corrected Lemma 2; the credited proofs of Propositions 1 and 2 retain their original hypotheses and do not assume that corrected lemma.
- The quantile and Appendix B results use exact real arithmetic, including a fixed $`2^{-52}`$ machine-epsilon constant in the printed-code specification; they do not certify floating-point R execution.
- The coverage theorem proves a limit and nominal lower bound; the wording about the narrowest assured Wald interval is outside its target.
- The Table 1 reduction and sharp-null numerical incompatibility depend on the stated rounding and common Wald multiplier, and the latter treats bias as the mean estimate minus true variance for the same realized simulation collection; neither theorem recovers unavailable unrounded results or determines a unique replacement bias.

### P022-T7

A counterexample to equation (23) and to the concluding display for $`\hat V_N^H`$ as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 868 (PDF p. 19); statement of Proposition 2 on p. 863 (PDF p. 14)

The main Lean statement covers the two failures described in P022-E006. The outcomes $`(y_{1i},y_{0i})`$ run through the cycle $`(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)`$, the population $`U_N`$ consists of the first $`N`$ units, $`n=N`$, $`m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor`$ and $`C=1`$. The statement asserts that conditions 1–3 hold with $`\theta=1`$, $`\rho=1/2`$ and $`H`$ the uniform distribution on the four points, that the left limits converge as the corrected Lemma 2 requires, and that the outcomes are bounded by $`1`$. It then asserts that the left side of (23) is infinite when the tolerance $`1-\varepsilon_1`$ is $`0`$, and that, for every $`0\lt \varepsilon\lt 1/2`$, $`\limsup_N N\,\mathrm{P}\bigl(N|\hat V_N^H-V_N|\ge\varepsilon\bigr)=\infty`$ along the population sizes $`N`$ divisible by $`4`$. A second statement asserts that, for every sequence of populations and every real function $`f`$ of the assignment, the event $`\{|f|\ge0\}`$ has probability one, and that $`N`$ times this probability has limit superior $`\infty`$. A third asserts, for the population in which every outcome is $`0`$, that conditions 1–3 hold with $`H`$ the point mass at $`(0,0)`$, that the outcomes are bounded by $`1`$, and that the left side of (23) with tolerance $`0`$ is infinite. This third statement uses a different population from the one in the correction, and it concerns the tolerance only and not the display with $`V_N`$. The choice $`\varepsilon=3`$ and $`\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_2=\varepsilon_3=1`$ enters the statements only through the tolerance $`1-1=0`$, and the statements do not include the clause that the right side $`C^4\kappa_1(1)`$ of (23) is finite.
