# P026: A Class of Unbiased Estimators of the Average Treatment Effect in Randomized Experiments

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P026.json)

P. M. Aronow and Joel A. Middleton (2013). A Class of Unbiased Estimators of the Average Treatment Effect in Randomized Experiments. Journal of Causal Inference 1(1):135–154.

DOI: [10.1515/jci-2012-0009](https://doi.org/10.1515/jci-2012-0009)

## Overview

The paper studies Horvitz–Thompson and generalized difference estimation of average treatment effects under finite randomized designs, including unequal probabilities and cluster assignment. The Lean development covers unbiased totals and effects, prediction adjustment, affine transformations, and exact variance and covariance identities with original-unit normalization. Counterexamples and corrected results address missing directed cross-arm support, fitting under pairwise independence, numerical table entries and the covariate-adjusted branch of the ri package.

Areas: Experimental design and estimation, Regression and variance estimation.

## Key results

For a nonempty finite unit population, a normalized nonnegative randomization law and positive treatment and control marginals make both Horvitz–Thompson totals and their population-size-normalized difference unbiased for the corresponding totals and average treatment effect.

[`AronowAudit.P026.unit_ht_unbiased`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_FiniteDesign.lean#L1726) · [P026-T20](#p026-t20)

With exactly $`n`$ of $`N`$ units treated and common marginals $`n/N`$ and $`(N-n)/N`$, where $`0\lt n\lt N`$, Horvitz–Thompson estimation equals the observed difference in means on positive-probability allocations and this difference is unbiased.

[`AronowAudit.P026.complete_units`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_FiniteDesign.lean#L1110) · [P026-T21](#p026-t21)

For $`0\lt m\lt M`$ clusters treated with common marginals $`m/M`$, the unit-normalized Horvitz–Thompson estimator equals $`M/N`$ times the difference in mean observed cluster totals and is unbiased for the original-unit average effect, allowing unequal cluster sizes.

[`AronowAudit.P026.PaperScope.complete_clusters_T21`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_CompletePaper.lean#L15) · [P026-T21](#p026-t21)

For a finite unit experiment with positive marginals, zero covariance between each treatment indicator and its fitted prediction makes both generalized totals and the generalized average treatment effect estimator unbiased.

[`AronowAudit.P026.PaperScope.unit_generalized_unbiased_T22`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_PaperScope.lean#L424) · [P026-T22](#p026-t22)

Transforming both potential outcomes and every evaluated prediction by $`x\mapsto b_0+b_1x`$ multiplies the generalized effect estimator by $`b_1`$ on every allocation, including when predictions depend on assignment.

[`AronowAudit.P026.PaperScope.generalized_affine_T23`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_PaperScope.lean#L483) · [P026-T23](#p026-t23)

For predictions fixed across randomizations, positive cluster marginals and positive disagreement probability for every distinct cluster pair, the generalized estimator has the full Horvitz–Thompson variance expression applied to residual cluster totals, including zero-joint-probability terms and normalization by the squared original unit count.

[`AronowAudit.P026.PaperScope.generalized_fixed_variance_T24`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_PaperScope.lean#L509) · [P026-T24](#p026-t24)

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed deduction from positive pairwise disagreement to positive directed cross-arm probability, and the resulting universal conservativeness claim, fail in a two-cluster sharp-null design: a directed cross-arm joint is zero and the printed variance estimator has expectation $`23/8`$ below the true variance $`27/8`$.

[`AronowAudit.P026.asymmetricTwo_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_FiniteDesign.lean#L251) · [P026-T1](#p026-t1)

Equation [35] is not conservative for every allowed design; adding the missing bound for pairs with zero directed cross-arm probability gives a corrected variance estimator whose exact bias is a nonnegative sum of squared gaps under the standing positive-marginal and cluster-disagreement conditions.

[`AronowAudit.P026.PaperScope.corrected_variance_bias_T2`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_PaperScope.lean#L131) · [P026-T2](#p026-t2)

The printed universal conservativeness claim for equations [34]–[35] can instead be narrowed by requiring every directed cross-arm probability between distinct clusters to be positive; the retained estimators then have the stated covariance bound and conservative variance, with sharp-null equality additionally requiring positive same-arm joint probabilities.

[`AronowAudit.P026.PaperScope.support_repair_T3`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_PaperScope.lean#L174) · [P026-T3](#p026-t3)

The printed claim that pairwise independent fitting assignments suffice for unbiased nonlinear adjustment is false: a three-unit XOR design has all off-diagonal assignment cells of probability $`1/4`$, yet an XOR-based estimator has expectation 0 and a product-based estimator has expectation $`7/6`$ when the average effect is 1.

[`AronowAudit.P026.xorThree_counterexamples`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_FiniteDesign.lean#L526) · [P026-T4](#p026-t4)

In place of the printed inference from pairwise independence, independence of each unit's treatment indicator from the whole vector of assignments used in its outcome-based fit ensures unbiased generalized totals and average effect estimation.

[`AronowAudit.P026.PaperScope.vector_fitting_unbiased_T5`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_PaperScope.lean#L229) · [P026-T5](#p026-t5)

The ri package help describes the covariate-adjusted HT=TRUE branch as unbiased Horvitz–Thompson estimation; a full-rank three-unit OLS example instead gives adjusted estimates $`(1/4,1,1/4)`$ with expectation $`1/2`$ when the average effect and every unadjusted Horvitz–Thompson estimate are 1.

[`AronowAudit.P026.package_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P026/P026_FiniteDesign.lean#L583) · [P026-T15](#p026-t15), [P026-T16](#p026-t16)

## Caveats

- Potential outcomes are fixed and randomization has a normalized nonnegative finite law with positive treatment and control marginals. Cluster results use actual sums over a partition of the units and divide by the number of original units, not the number of clusters.
- The complete-assignment identities are stated using fixed treatment counts and common marginals; they also cover nonuniform assignment laws with those properties.
- When a directed cross-arm joint probability is zero, the counterexamples evaluate the zero-omission continuation of equation [34]; the literal printed denominator would be undefined. The variance correction and the added-support repair are separate alternatives.
- The variance and cluster covariance results retain the standing positive disagreement probability between distinct clusters. Sharp-null equality of the support-repaired variance estimator also needs positive same-arm joint probabilities; the added-term repair need not be exactly unbiased under every sharp null.
- General unbiasedness requires zero treatment-prediction covariance or the stated vector-independence condition. Affine equivariance requires transforming the evaluated predictions along with outcomes, and the displayed residual variance formula requires predictions fixed across randomizations.
- The package counterexample certifies a finite OLS fit and its estimator values from the documented covariate branch; it is not a formal execution of R code. Rational counterexamples use exact arithmetic.

### P026-T5

The second option for deriving $`\theta_i`$ in Section 5.1 and footnote 5, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 142 (PDF p. 8)

The Lean statements give the corrected rule of P026-E001. For a unit $`i`$ and a set $`J_i`$ of units, let $`\theta_i=G((Y_j)_{j\in J_i})`$ and $`f(\mathbf{X}_i,\theta_i)=P(\theta_i)`$, where $`G`$ is an arbitrary function and $`P`$ an arbitrary real function, both of which may involve the fixed covariates. Two random quantities $`A`$ and $`B`$ are called independent when $`\mathrm{E}[u(A)v(B)]=\mathrm{E}[u(A)]\,\mathrm{E}[v(B)]`$ for all real functions $`u`$ and $`v`$, which is the usual notion on a finite probability space. The main statement asserts that, if $`T_i`$ is independent of the vector $`(T_j)_{j\in J_i}`$, then $`T_i`$ is independent of $`\theta_i`$ and of $`f(\mathbf{X}_i,\theta_i)`$, and $`\mathrm{Cov}(T_i,f(\mathbf{X}_i,\theta_i))=0`$. A second asserts the same conclusions when $`T_i`$ is independent of the vector of observed outcomes $`(Y_j)_{j\in J_i}`$. Two statements assume nonnegative weights summing to one and positive marginal probabilities in both arms. If the premise on the assignments holds for every unit, they assert that $`\mathrm{E}[\widehat{Y_1^{T*}}]=Y_1^T`$, $`\mathrm{E}[\widehat{Y_0^{T*}}]=Y_0^T`$ and $`\mathrm{E}[\widehat{\Delta}_G]=\Delta`$. In both unbiasedness statements, $`N`$ is the number of units. For the additive prediction $`a_i+\sum_{j\in J_i}b_{ij}Y_j`$, a statement assumes nonnegative weights summing to one and independence of $`T_i`$ from each $`T_j`$ separately, and asserts that $`\mathrm{Cov}(T_i,f(\mathbf{X}_i,\theta_i))=\sum_{j\in J_i}b_{ij}(Y_{1j}-Y_{0j})\mathrm{Cov}(T_i,T_j)=0.`$ Two statements concern the examples of the paper. One asserts that, if the assignments of the units are mutually independent, then $`T_i`$ is independent of $`(T_j)_{j\ne i}`$. The other asserts that, if the assignment vectors of the blocks are mutually independent, then $`T_i`$ is independent of the assignments of all units outside its block. The remaining six statements are rules for independence and covariance. The three single-unit fitting statements use a nonempty finite unit population, nonnegative weights summing to one and strictly positive treatment and control marginals for every unit. The six auxiliary independence and covariance rules omit probability or marginal assumptions when their algebraic assertions do not require them.
