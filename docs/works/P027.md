# P027: Conservative Variance Estimation for Sampling Designs with Zero Pairwise Inclusion Probabilities

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P027.json)

P. M. Aronow and Cyrus Samii (2013). Conservative Variance Estimation for Sampling Designs with Zero Pairwise Inclusion Probabilities. Survey Methodology 39(1):231–241.

## Overview

The work studies variance estimation for Horvitz–Thompson totals when some pairs of units have zero joint inclusion probability. The Lean development proves the main exact bias identity, conservative corrections based on conjugate exponents, and the three corollaries concerning equality, the quadratic correction, and its pointwise optimality among constant exponents. Supplementary results give exact periodic-design calculations, qualifications of aggregation and outcome-averaged conservativity, and corrections concerning PPS probabilities and two-stratum comparisons.

Areas: Survey sampling, Regression and variance estimation.

## Key results

For any finite sampling design with positive first-order inclusion probabilities and fixed real outcomes, the expected usual Horvitz–Thompson variance estimator equals the true total variance plus the sum of $`y_ky_l`$ over ordered distinct pairs with zero joint inclusion probability.

[`AronowAudit.P027.proposition1`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L237) · [P027-T20](#p027-t20)

For any finite sampling design with positive first-order inclusion probabilities, the correction using finite conjugate exponents at each ordered zero-inclusion pair has expectation at least the true Horvitz–Thompson total variance for arbitrary fixed real outcomes.

[`AronowAudit.P027.proposition2`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L314) · [P027-T21](#p027-t21)

Under the positive-inclusion and conjugate-exponent hypotheses, equality of the two powered absolute outcomes and $`-y_ky_l=|y_k||y_l|`$ at every ordered zero-inclusion pair suffice for the corrected variance estimator to be unbiased.

[`AronowAudit.P027.corollary1`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L324) · [P027-T22](#p027-t22)

For any finite sampling design with positive first-order inclusion probabilities and arbitrary fixed real outcomes, the quadratic correction $`C2`$ has expectation at least the true Horvitz–Thompson total variance.

[`AronowAudit.P027.corollary2`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L378) · [P027-T23](#p027-t23)

For every sample from a finite design with positive first-order inclusion probabilities and arbitrary fixed real outcomes, exponents $`a=b=2`$ attain the smallest corrected variance estimate among all constant finite conjugate exponent pairs.

[`AronowAudit.P027.corollary3`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L407) · [P027-T24](#p027-t24)

## Corrections and counterexamples

The supplement attributes near-unbiasedness in its periodic design to cancellation of the bias term; for every centered population in the formalized periodic design, Lean proves that the expected uncorrected total-variance estimator equals the true variance minus the sum of squared phase totals, so centering alone does not make the bias zero.

[`AronowAudit.P027.periodic_ht_expectations`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L1920) · [P027-T2](#p027-t2), [P027-T6](#p027-t6)

The supplement infers conservativity of the uncorrected variance estimator from its size-based outcome model; Lean proves an eight-unit fixed-outcome PPS example with the printed inclusion probabilities in which the mean variance is $`4/3`$ but the expected variance estimate is $`13/12`$, a bias of $`-1/4`$.

[`AronowAudit.P027.random8_mean_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L2252) · [P027-T10](#p027-t10)

The supplement's conservativity assertion needs an averaging qualification: for fixed sizes $`x_k\ge1`$, a fixed positive-inclusion design, and outcomes $`y_k=\beta x_k+\epsilon_k`$ with mean-zero uncorrelated errors and integrable products, Lean proves nonnegative bias after averaging over the errors, without asserting it for each realized outcome vector.

[`AronowAudit.P027.outcome_averaged_conservativity`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L1587) · [P027-T11](#p027-t11)

The printed PPS formula $`mx_k/\sum_lx_l`$ needs a feasibility restriction; for every positive size vector and $`0\lt m\lt N`$, Lean proves that some $`c\gt 0`$ makes $`\sum_k\min(1,cx_k)=m`$, and that these redistributed probabilities equal the printed formula whenever $`mx_k\le\sum_lx_l`$ for every unit.

[`AronowAudit.P027.capped_pps_exists`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L846) · [P027-T13](#p027-t13)

The supplement's comparison of $`C2`$ with collapsed-stratum estimation requires a precise bias criterion: Lean proves that the corrected scalar bias formulas differ by $`(M-2-2\alpha^2)/(2M)`$, so $`C2`$ has the smaller formula exactly when $`M\lt 2(1+\alpha^2)`$, with their identification as averaged estimator biases supplied separately.

[`AronowAudit.P027.strata_bias_ranking`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L1023) · [P027-T15](#p027-t15)

The printed ratio $`\alpha^2/(\alpha^2+1/2)`$ is labeled an intra-class correlation; Lean proves that $`Y=\alpha S+\epsilon`$ and $`Y'=\alpha S+\epsilon'`$ have ordinary correlation $`\alpha^2/(\alpha^2+1)`$ for a balanced sign $`S`$, mean-zero unit-variance errors, and zero cross moments.

[`AronowAudit.P027.same_stratum_correlation`](../../AronowAudit/P027/P027_Audit.lean#L2274) · [P027-T17](#p027-t17)

## Caveats

- Sampling designs are finite normalized distributions over actual subsets; first- and second-order inclusion probabilities come from sample indicators, outcomes are fixed for the main theorems, and zero-pair sums are ordered and exclude the diagonal.
- Conservativity means an inequality in expectation under the sampling design; Corollary 3 is a samplewise minimum over constant exponents and makes no claim of optimality over pair-specific exponents or by mean squared error.
- Periodic calculations use the formalized phase probabilities and distinguish original units from totalled phases; mean-scale comparisons keep the original population-size squared divisor, and the formulas do not verify the printed simulations.
- Totalling units always selected together preserves the Horvitz–Thompson total but can increase the quadratic correction's bias in explicit populations; these exceptions do not refute the paper's unspecified heuristic tendency.
- Outcome-averaged conservativity fixes sizes and the design before averaging over mean-zero uncorrelated errors; independence and Gaussianity are not required, and the finite negative-bias example alone does not establish its probability under the full simulation generator.
- The PPS existence result establishes feasible redistributed probabilities; a separate declaration constructs a uniform-start systematic design, and matching either repair to the simulation code is outside this result.
- The two-stratum ranking compares explicit bias formulas, whose model-expectation interpretation is proved in a separate component; it is not an MSE ranking.

### P027-T2

Supplement, Section C, last paragraph, as clarified: in the periodic design the bias term is minus the sum of the squared sample totals

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Supplement, Section C, last paragraph before the References, p. A4 (supplement PDF p. 4); the design is described on pp. A3–A4; Proposition 1 is on p. 234 of the main text (PDF p. 6)

The Lean statements give the mathematics behind the clarified passage of P027-E004. They hold for every even $`q=N/m`$ and every $`m\ge 1`$, with the sizes 2 and 10 in alternation. The design is the list of the $`q`$ possible samples $`G_g=\{g,g+q,\dots,g+(m-1)q\}`$, where $`G_g`$ is drawn with probability $`p_g=1/(3q)`$ if its units have size 2 and $`p_g=5/(3q)`$ if they have size 10. A separate statement asserts that systematic selection by cumulative sizes in the order $`2,10,2,10,\dots`$, with one uniform start on $`[0,6q)`$, selects exactly these samples with these probabilities; the supplement does not display its selection rule. Let $`z`$ be any outcome vector such that $`\sum_kz_k=0`$, and let $`T_g=\sum_{k\in G_g}z_k`$. The main statement asserts that $`A=-\sum_{g=1}^{q}T_g^2\le 0,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(\hat t)=\sum_{g}\frac{T_g^2}{p_g},\qquad \mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\hat t)\bigr]=\mathrm{Var}(\hat t)-\sum_gT_g^2,`$ that $`A=0`$ if and only if every $`T_g`$ is zero, and that $`-5/(3q)\le A/\mathrm{Var}(\hat t)\le -1/(3q)`$ when $`\mathrm{Var}(\hat t)\gt 0`$. The statements on the outcome model take $`z_k=y_k-\bar y`$ with $`y_k=\beta x_k+\epsilon_k`$, where $`\beta`$ is a constant and the errors have mean zero, a common variance $`\tau^2`$ and zero covariances. Neither independence nor a Gaussian law is assumed. Let $`\mathbb{E}_y`$ denote the expectation over the errors, and set $`D=16m^2\beta^2+m\tau^2(1-1/q)`$. These statements assert that $`\mathbb{E}_y(T_g^2)=D`$ for every $`g`$, that $`\mathbb{E}_y(A)=-qD`$ and $`\mathbb{E}_y\mathrm{Var}(\hat t)=9q^2D/5`$, and that the ratio of the averaged bias to the averaged variance is $`-5/(9q)`$ when $`D\gt 0`$. They assert further that $`\mathbb{E}_y\Bigl(\frac{A}{N^2}\Bigr)=-\frac{16\beta^2}{q}-\frac{\tau^2(1-1/q)}{N},`$ and that for $`\tau^2=900(1-\rho^2)`$ and $`\beta^2=900\rho^2(N-1)/(16N)`$ this quantity tends to $`-900\rho^2/q`$ as $`m`$ grows with $`q`$ fixed. This is the calibration of the supplement with the variance of the sizes taken with divisor $`N-1`$; it enters through $`\beta^2`$ only, so the sign of $`\beta`$ is free. The remaining statements are steps of these calculations. One of them, on the correction term of $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{C2}(\hat t)`$, is stated for outcomes that need not sum to zero.

### P027-T3

Two examples where combining units raises the variance estimator’s bias, illustrating exceptions to the heuristic in Section 3.1

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Section 3.1, paragraph after the proof of Corollary 1, p. 237 (PDF p. 9); $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{C2}(\hat t)`$ is defined on the same page

The two Lean statements prove the three-unit examples of P027-N002, which are mine and are not in the paper. The original design is the list of the two possible samples $`\{1,2\}`$ and $`\{3\}`$, each with probability $`1/2`$. The totalled design has two units, the cluster $`\{1,2\}`$ and unit 3, and it draws one of them with probability $`1/2`$ each. The first statement takes $`y=(1,1,1)`$, so that the totalled outcomes are $`(2,1)`$. It asserts that all first-order inclusion probabilities are positive in both designs, that units 1 and 2 have the same indicator on every sample, and that $`\hat t`$ takes the same value before and after totalling on every sample. It asserts also that $`\mathrm{Var}(\hat t)=1`$, $`A=4`$ and $`A^*=4`$ for the original units, that $`A^*=5`$ after totalling, and that $`\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{C2}(\hat t)]`$ is $`9`$ before totalling and $`10`$ after it. Since $`\hat t`$ takes the same values in both designs, its variance is also unchanged, and the bias rises from $`8`$ to $`9`$. The second statement takes $`y=(1,1,-1/4)`$ and the totalled outcomes $`(2,-1/4)`$. It asserts that $`\mathrm{Var}(\hat t)=81/16`$ and $`A=-1`$ in both designs, that $`A^*`$ is $`17/8`$ before totalling and $`65/16`$ after it, and that the expectations of $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{C2}(\hat t)`$ are $`99/16`$ and $`65/8`$. Subtracting the variance, I find that the bias rises from $`9/8`$ to $`49/16`$. Here $`A^*`$ is the value for $`a_{kl}=b_{kl}=2`$. The clarity note tabulates the estimators on each sample; those sample values and the pair counts are not asserted by these two statements. The statements show that totalling can raise the bias in these two populations, and do not refute the paragraph’s unspecified tendency over populations.

### P027-T4

A precise qualification of Section 3.1, last paragraph: what totalling preserves, the exact change in the bias of $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{C2}(\hat t)`$, and a sufficient condition for no increase

**Kind:** `result_touched_by_finding`

**Location:** Section 3.1, paragraph after the proof of Corollary 1, p. 237 (PDF p. 9)

The Lean statements give the mathematics behind the optional precise qualification of P027-N002, for $`a_{kl}=b_{kl}=2`$. Clusters are represented by a finite probability distribution over samples of a finite set of groups, with positive first-order inclusion probabilities, and a size $`n_g\ge 1`$ for each group. The original population has $`n_g`$ units with fixed real outcomes for each group $`g`$, and a unit is in the sample exactly when its group is. Units of one group then have the same indicator on every sample, which agrees with the paper’s condition $`\Pr(I_k\ne I_l)=0`$ once samples of probability zero are removed. Let $`Y_g`$ and $`S_g`$ be the total and the sum of squares of the outcomes in group $`g`$, and let $`q_{gh}`$ be the joint inclusion probability of groups $`g`$ and $`h`$. The first main statement asserts that totalling leaves $`\hat t`$ and the uncorrected estimator unchanged on every sample, and that $`\mathrm{Var}(\hat t)`$ and $`A`$ are unchanged. The second asserts that the bias of $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{C2}(\hat t)`$ before totalling minus its bias after totalling equals $`\frac12\sum_{g}\ \sum_{h\ne g:\,q_{gh}=0}\bigl(n_hS_g+n_gS_h-Y_g^2-Y_h^2\bigr).`$ The sum runs over ordered pairs, and with the factor $`1/2`$ it is the sum over unordered pairs in the clarity note. The same statement asserts that this difference is nonnegative if $`n_g=n_h`$ whenever $`q_{gh}=0`$, and that it equals $`-(v^2/2)\sum_g\sum_{h\ne g:\,q_{gh}=0}(n_g-n_h)^2`$ if every outcome equals $`v`$. It asserts also that both biases are nonnegative. A third statement asserts that for a constant $`v\ne 0`$ totalling strictly raises the bias when some pair of groups with $`q_{gh}=0`$ has $`n_g\ne n_h`$. A fourth asserts that the difference is at least $`\sum_g(M_g-m_gn_g)S_g`$, where $`m_g`$ is the number of groups $`h\ne g`$ with $`q_{gh}=0`$ and $`M_g`$ is the sum of their sizes. The other statements are steps, among them the formula for $`A^*`$ of the original units in terms of the $`S_g`$ and the inequality $`Y_g^2\le n_gS_g`$. Some supporting steps allow empty groups, broader than the nonempty-group setting above. No statement concerns exponents other than 2; these results do not refute the paragraph’s unspecified tendency over populations. The clarity note’s example in which totalling lowers the bias from 20 to 0 is not stated.

### P027-T24

Corollary 3

**Kind:** `headline_result`

**Location:** Section 3.2, pp. 237–238 (PDF pp. 9–10), with its proof

The Lean statement is for every sampling design such that $`\pi_k\gt 0`$ for all $`k`$, all fixed real outcomes and every sample. It asserts that $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{Cab}(\hat t)`$ with $`a=b=2`$ equals $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{C2}(\hat t)`$ on that sample, and that $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{C2}(\hat t)\ \le\ \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{Cab}(\hat t)`$ on that sample for all positive constants $`a`$ and $`b`$ such that $`1/a+1/b=1`$. The minimum over $`(a,b)`$ of the realized estimate is then attained at $`(2,2)`$, which is the corollary. The inequality holds sample by sample, as in the printed proof, and not only in expectation. Here $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{Cab}(\hat t)`$ is $`\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_C(\hat t)`$ with $`a_{kl}=a`$ and $`b_{kl}=b`$ for every pair, and the design is a finite list of possible samples with their probabilities. A second statement asserts the symmetry step of the proof: a sum over the ordered pairs $`(k,l)`$ of distinct units with $`\pi_{kl}=0`$ is unchanged when $`k`$ and $`l`$ are exchanged in the summand. The paper remarks that an optimal choice of exponents varying over $`k`$ and $`l`$ could be derived from all the $`y`$ values. This remark is not stated.
