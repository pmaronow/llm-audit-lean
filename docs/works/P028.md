# P028: Sharp Bounds for Complier Average Potential Outcomes in Experiments with Noncompliance and Incomplete Reporting

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P028.json)

P. M. Aronow and Donald P. Green (2013). Sharp Bounds for Complier Average Potential Outcomes in Experiments with Noncompliance and Incomplete Reporting. Statistics and Probability Letters 83(3):677–679.

DOI: [10.1016/j.spl.2012.11.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spl.2012.11.012)

## Overview

The work derives sharp bounds and identification formulas for complier average potential outcomes under randomized assignment with noncompliance and bounded outcomes. The Lean development proves the bounds, constructs attaining probability models, verifies the LATE identity and the full-data identification formula, and repairs the proof at full compliance. Separate missing-outcome results exhibit observationally identical models with different complier means and state additional response conditions or sensitivity bounds for incomplete recording.

Areas: Partial identification and bounds, Causal identification, Experimental design and estimation.

## Key results

Under randomized binary assignment, exclusion, monotonicity, positive complier probability, and outcomes in $`[0,1]`$, both complier means satisfy Proposition 1’s intervals and their difference equals the reduced-form outcome contrast divided by the complier probability.

[`AronowAudit.P028.proposition1_valid`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_Results.lean#L1088) · [P028-T8](#p028-t8)

For every compatible triple $`0\lt p\le1`$, $`e_0,e_1\in[0,1]`$, and $`|e_1-e_0|\le p`$, each $`x_1`$ in Proposition 1’s treated-mean interval is attained by a bounded randomized model with that triple, treated complier mean $`x_1`$, and control complier mean $`x_1-(e_1-e_0)/p`$.

[`AronowAudit.P028.proposition1_sharp`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_Results.lean#L1108) · [P028-T8](#p028-t8)

When all four treatment-by-assignment cells have positive probability, Corollary 1 identifies both complier potential-outcome means from assignment-arm outcome means, take-up probabilities, and the always-taker and never-taker pure-cell means under the stated causal model.

[`AronowAudit.P028.corollary1`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_Results.lean#L1153) · [P028-T9](#p028-t9)

With randomized binary assignment, exclusion, monotonicity, integrable potential outcomes, and a nonzero first stage, the complier average causal effect equals the reduced-form outcome contrast divided by the complier probability.

[`AronowAudit.P028.late_population_ratio`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_Results.lean#L504) · [P028-T10](#p028-t10)

Two bounded randomized models have the same $`(p,e_0,e_1)=(1/2,3/4,3/4)`$ and zero complier average effect but complier mean pairs $`(1/2,1/2)`$ and $`(1,1)`$, showing that these three figures need not identify the levels.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The application’s point-identification inference from incompletely recorded data does not follow from the stated causal assumptions alone: two explicit bounded monotone randomized laws agree on $`(Z,D,R,RY)`$ but have different complier means under each treatment.

[`AronowAudit.P028.missing_outcome_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_MissingOutcomes.lean#L239) · [P028-T1](#p028-t1)

The application’s inference requires additional response assumptions; under the stated causal model with potential outcomes in $`[0,1]`$, positive response in both assignment arms and both pure noncomplier cells, and equality of respondent and full-population arm means and pure-cell means, the mixed available-case formulas identify both complier means.

[`AronowAudit.P028.response_mixed_identification`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_Results.lean#L1307) · [P028-T2](#p028-t2)

In place of unrestricted point identification from incomplete outcomes, bounded potential outcomes in $`[0,1]`$ yield intervals for both complier means using recorded outcome moments and missing-outcome probabilities, without a response-independence assumption.

[`AronowAudit.P028.response_sensitivity_bounds`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_Results.lean#L1645) · [P028-T2](#p028-t2)

The proof of Proposition 1 prints an upper bound of $`1`$ on the noncomplier contribution; replacing it by $`1-p`$ gives a system equivalent, for $`p\gt 0`$, to the stated treated-mean interval and the implied control mean.

[`AronowAudit.P028.corrected_interval_system`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_Results.lean#L105) · [P028-T4](#p028-t4)

The printed proof divides by $`1-p`$ without excluding full compliance; using the unnormalized noncomplier contribution proves the mixture and observable-moment formulas for every $`0\lt p\le1`$, with contribution zero and complier means equal to arm means at $`p=1`$.

[`AronowAudit.P028.boundary_proof_repair`](../../AronowAudit/P028/P028_Results.lean#L1055) · [P028-T6](#p028-t6)

## Caveats

- The general causal model is on an arbitrary probability space: assignment has two positive-probability arms independent of the joint potential variables, treatment is monotone and binary, exclusion is built into observed outcomes, and the first stage is nonzero.
- The bounds and the cited mixed available-case response-identification theorem require potential outcomes in $`[0,1]`$; the LATE and full-data Corollary 1 formulas require integrable outcomes without a boundedness restriction.
- Finite support is used for sharpness and non-identification witnesses, while validity of the bounds is proved on arbitrary probability spaces.
- The ordinary conditional-mean version of Corollary 1 requires all four treatment-by-assignment cells to be positive; product-moment formulas separately handle empty cells and full compliance.
- The proof repairs preserve Proposition 1’s intervals and sharpness; the full-compliance issue concerns an intermediate proof step and does not refute Corollary 1 under its positive-cell premise.
- The response equalities and response-independence sufficient conditions are additional assumptions, not claims established for the experiment; the sensitivity intervals are population identification bounds, not sampling confidence intervals.
- The missing-outcome witnesses are possible population laws matching an observed table, not estimates of the experiment’s unknown full-population law; non-identification from three summary figures is existential, not asserted for every compatible triple.

### P028-T6

Proof of Proposition 1, as corrected to cover full compliance, $`P_c = 1`$, with the formula of Corollary 1 written without a mean over the noncompliers

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Proof of Proposition 1, p. 678 (PDF p. 2); proof of Corollary 1, p. 679 (PDF p. 3)

The twenty-two proved statements give the argument in P028-E002 that uses no mean over the noncompliers. The setting of Section 2 is represented by a probability space with a binary assignment $`Z`$, the type of the unit (always-taker, never-taker or complier) and integrable potential outcomes $`Y(0)`$ and $`Y(1)`$; the joint vector of type and potential outcomes is independent of $`Z`$, both values of $`Z`$ have positive probability, $`\mathrm{E}[D(1) - D(0)] \neq 0`$, and the observed variables are $`D = D(Z)`$ and $`Y = Y(D)`$. Let $`A`$ and $`N`$ denote the always-takers and the never-takers, and define $`m = \mathrm{E}[\mathbf{1}_A Y(1)] + \mathrm{E}[\mathbf{1}_N Y(0)]`$.
