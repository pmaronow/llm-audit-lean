# P030: A General Method for Detecting Interference Between Units in Randomized Experiments

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P030.json)

P. M. Aronow (2012). A General Method for Detecting Interference Between Units in Randomized Experiments. Sociological Methods and Research 41(1):3–16.

DOI: [10.1177/0049124112437535](https://doi.org/10.1177/0049124112437535)

## Overview

The work develops conditional randomization tests for interference by holding selected units' treatments fixed and comparing a statistic over the remaining assignments. The Lean development proves one-tailed validity, complete-randomization exposure expectations, and the worked example's reference calculation. It also gives counterexamples to the printed two-tailed rule and statistic-invariance null, together with corrected testing and rank-correlation statements; additional targets concern the distance kernel, sample-count arithmetic, and the fixed-subset size recommendation.

Areas: Randomization inference, Interference and networks, Experimental design and estimation.

## Key results

Under no interference or the weaker fixed-outcome null and a uniform nonempty conditional assignment law, the assignment-counted one-tailed p-value is super-uniform, with rejection probability equal to each attainable p-value and otherwise to the largest attainable value at or below the cutoff, including zero.

[`AronowAudit.P030.one_tailed_randomization_valid`](../../AronowAudit/P030/P030_Audit.lean#L558) · [P030-T12](#p030-t12)

For complete randomization of $`M`$ among $`N\ge2`$ units and a fixed self-excluding neighborhood of size $`K_i`$, expected treated-neighbor counts are $`K_iM/N`$, $`K_i(M-1)/(N-1)`$ given treatment, and $`K_iM/(N-1)`$ given control, with the conditional formulas restricted to positive-probability conditioning events.

The six Table 1 rank correlations have the stated exact radical values and printed roundings, are all negative, and give an observed correlation of $`-3/\sqrt{10}`$ with assignment-counted lower-tail p-value $`1/3`$.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed two-tailed exactness claim fails in a five-unit experiment with no effects of any kind and a defined Spearman statistic: for every $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$, the printed rule rejects with probability one, while the corrected inclusive-tail rule rejects with probability zero.

[`AronowAudit.P030.five_unit_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P030/P030_Audit.lean#L236) · [P030-T1](#p030-t1)

Against the printed two-tailed exactness claim, a ten-unit no-effect population realizes Table 1’s nonconstant reference law conditional on the fixed units being untreated, with six equiprobable assignments and conditional rejection probability $`1/2`$ under the printed rule and zero under the corrected rule for $`0\lt \alpha\le2/3`$, exceeding the nominal level at $`\alpha=1/20`$.

[`AronowAudit.P030.ten_unit_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P030/P030_Audit.lean#L805) · [P030-T2](#p030-t2)

Replacing the printed rule's upper-side condition on the lower tail by an inclusive upper-tail probability below $`\alpha/2`$ gives rejection probability at most $`\alpha`$ for every finite probability law and $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$, retaining ties.

[`AronowAudit.P030.corrected_two_tailed_valid`](../../AronowAudit/P030/P030_Audit.lean#L366) · [P030-T3](#p030-t3)

Contrary to the implication used in printed Equation (1), a no-effect, no-interference schedule can keep fixed-unit outcomes invariant while producing distinct statistics on the same conditional assignment set and an observed lower-tail p-value of $`1/3`$.

[`AronowAudit.P030.statistic_invariance_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P030/P030_Audit.lean#L843) · [P030-T5](#p030-t5)

Replacing Equation (1)’s statistic-invariance null by invariance of the fixed units’ outcomes makes observed-data imputation reproduce each conditional-assignment statistic and its null distribution under uniform conditional assignment, and no interference implies this corrected outcome null.

[`AronowAudit.P030.corrected_outcome_null`](../../AronowAudit/P030/P030_Audit.lean#L473) · [P030-T6](#p030-t6)

The printed rank-correlation denominator needs a square root, and for nonconstant vectors the corrected average-rank correlation lies in $`[-1,1]`$ and has absolute value one exactly when the centered rank vectors are nonzero scalar multiples, replacing the unqualified monotonicity claim.

## Caveats

- The one-tailed theorem assumes that the actual conditional law, including conditioning induced by fixed-subset selection, is uniform; it does not derive uniformity for every way of selecting that subset or justify Monte Carlo approximation of the reference distribution.
- Exactness means conditional Type I error at most the nominal level, with equality at attainable one-tailed p-values; discreteness does not give equality at every nominal level.
- The ten-unit no-effect counterexample is a separate potential-outcome schedule with the same reference law as Table 1; the paper's own illustrative data-generating process has interference.
- The corrected Spearman theorem uses average ranks for ties and nonconstant input vectors, which ensure positive rank variances; its moment-divisor invariance holds for every positive divisor.
- The two-tailed counterexamples do not establish changes in the reported simulation rates or the application's one-tailed result.

### P030-T1

A counterexample with five units to the two-tailed rule after Equation (2) and to the sentence “This procedure is an exact, distribution-free test”, as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 7 (PDF p. 5)

The Lean statement describes the first example of P030-E001. Five units lie on a line at the positions $`0,4,8,-1,-2`$. The first three units are fixed, and $`\Omega`$ consists of the two assignments that treat exactly one of the other two units. The outcome of unit $`i`$ is $`c_i`$ under every binary assignment of the five units, where $`c=(0,1,2,0,0)`$. The statement asserts that $`H_{0'}`$ and $`H_{0''}`$ hold, that no fixed unit is treated under either assignment, and that $`\Omega'=\Omega`$. It asserts that under both assignments neither vector of ranks is constant, that $`\rho=1`$, and that $`\Pr(t_0\le t)=\Pr(t_0\ge t)=1`$, where a probability is a proportion of the two assignments. For every $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$ it asserts that the printed rule rejects with probability $`1`$, and that the rule that rejects when $`\Pr(t_0\le t)\lt \alpha/2`$ or $`\Pr(t_0\ge t)\lt \alpha/2`$ rejects with probability $`0`$. Here $`D_{\mathit{nearest}}`$ is the least distance from a fixed unit to a treated unit outside the fixed subset, and $`\rho`$ is the Pearson correlation of average ranks, with the square root in its denominator.

### P030-T2

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 7 (PDF p. 5); Table 1 and the example on p. 9 (PDF p. 7)

Ten units lie at the positions $`1,\ldots,10`$, exactly one unit is treated, the units $`1,8,9,10`$ are fixed, and the outcome of unit $`i`$ is $`b_i`$ under every binary assignment, where $`b=(1,2,2,2,2,3,2,3,2,1)`$. The main statement asserts that $`H_{0'}`$ and $`H_{0''}`$ hold, and that conditioning on $`Z_{\mathit{fixed}}=(0,0,0,0)`$, an event of probability $`3/5`$, leaves the six assignments that treat one of the units $`2,\ldots,7`$. A probability over $`\Omega'`$ is a proportion of its six assignments, and the statements for the worked example assert that each has conditional probability $`(1/10)/(6/10)=1/6`$. ### P030-T3

The two-tailed rule after Equation (2), as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 7 (PDF p. 5)

In the Lean statements, the conditional distribution of the assignment $`Z`$ is a finite set with nonnegative masses that sum to one, and the statistic is an arbitrary real function $`f`$ on that set. For real $`x`$, let $`p_L(x)=\Pr(f(Z)\le x)`$ and $`p_U(x)=\Pr(f(Z)\ge x)`$, so that every assignment is counted and tied values keep their probability. The main statement asserts that, for every $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$, $`\Pr\bigl(p_L(f(Z))\lt \alpha/2\ \text{ or }\ p_U(f(Z))\lt \alpha/2\bigr)\le\alpha .`$ This is the rule with strict endpoints in the corrected passage of P030-E001. Two statements concern the rule with $`p_2=\min\{1,2\min(p_L,p_U)\}`$ in that passage: for $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$, the inequality $`p_2\le\alpha`$ holds exactly when $`p_L\le\alpha/2`$ or $`p_U\le\alpha/2`$, and $`\Pr(p_2\le\alpha)\le\alpha`$. Further statements contain the steps of the derivation: the identity $`p_U(x)=1-p_L(x)+\Pr(f(Z)=x)`$, the bounds $`\Pr(p_L(f(Z))\le u)\le u`$ and $`\Pr(p_U(f(Z))\le u)\le u`$, the union bound, and elementary properties of probabilities. Another asserts that every rejection by the rule with strict endpoints is a rejection by the printed rule. The statements are broader than the corrected passage in several respects: the distribution need not be uniform, and the two bounds are stated for every $`u\ge 0`$. The comparison with the printed rule holds for every real $`\alpha`$, and the equivalence for $`p_2`$ for arbitrary real numbers in place of $`p_L`$ and $`p_U`$. They do not assert that the reference values are the values of the statistic when there is no interference, which belongs to the corrected Equation (1).

### P030-T4

Equation (2) and the two-tailed rule, with the sentence “This procedure is an exact, distribution-free test for detecting interference between units”, as printed

**Kind:** `result_touched_by_finding`

**Location:** p. 7 (PDF p. 5)

The article gives this claim no formal statement. I read “exact” as follows: if there is no interference, then the rule rejects with conditional probability at most $`\alpha`$. The Lean formulation quantifies over every number of units, every finite set $`\Omega`$ of binary assignments, every fixed subset, and every pattern of treatments of the fixed units for which $`\Omega'`$ is nonempty. It quantifies also over all potential outcomes that satisfy $`H_{0''}`$, every statistic that is a real function of $`Y_{\mathit{fixed}}`$ and the assignment, and every $`\alpha\in[0,1]`$. The assignments in $`\Omega'`$ are equally likely. For each realized assignment, $`p`$ is the proportion of assignments in $`\Omega'`$ whose reference value, computed from the observed $`Y_{\mathit{fixed}}`$, is at most the observed statistic. The claim is that the rule “reject unless $`\alpha/2\le p\le 1-\alpha/2`$” rejects with probability at most $`\alpha`$. The Lean statement is the negation of this claim. The first example of P030-E001, with the Spearman statistic of the article and $`\alpha=1/20`$, contradicts it. The statement does not address the validity of Equation (2) as a one-tailed $`p`$ value.

### P030-T5

A counterexample to Equation (1), as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 7 (PDF p. 5)

The Lean statement uses the population of P030-E002. Ten units lie at the positions $`1,\ldots,10`$, exactly one unit is treated, the units $`1,8,9,10`$ are fixed, and the outcome of unit $`i`$ is $`b_i`$ under every binary assignment, where $`b=(1,2,2,2,2,3,2,3,2,1)`$. The statement asserts that $`H_{0'}`$ and $`H_{0''}`$ hold, and that the outcomes of the fixed units are the same under the six assignments of $`\Omega'`$. It asserts that the assignments that treat unit 2 and unit 5 belong to $`\Omega'`$ and that their statistics differ. The equality $`t_Z=t_{Z'}`$ of Equation (1) therefore fails in a population without interference. The statement asserts also that $`p=1/3`$ at the assignment that treats unit 7, which is the article’s $`0.333`$. A second statement is general: if a statistic takes the same value at every element of a nonempty finite set, then at each element the proportion of elements whose value is at most its own is $`1`$. This is the value $`p=1`$ that Equation (1), as printed, would impose on Equation (2). The second statement is conditional and does not say that the procedure of the article returns $`p=1`$.

### P030-T6

Equation (1), as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 7 (PDF p. 5)

In the Lean statement, $`\Omega`$ is a finite set of binary assignments, the potential outcomes $`Y_{i,Z}`$ are real numbers, and $`Z^{\mathit{obs}}\in\Omega`$ is the realized assignment. The set $`\Omega'`$ consists of the assignments in $`\Omega`$ that agree with $`Z^{\mathit{obs}}`$ on the fixed units. The corrected hypothesis of P030-E002 is $`H_0:Y_{i,Z}=Y_{i,Z'}`$ for every $`i`$ with $`S_i=1`$ and all $`Z,Z'\in\Omega'`$. The statistic is $`t_Z=g(Y_{\mathit{fixed},Z},Z)`$ for an arbitrary real function $`g`$, and the reference value of $`Z`$ is $`t_0(Z)=g(Y_{\mathit{fixed},Z^{\mathit{obs}}},Z)`$. The statement asserts that $`H_{0''}`$ implies $`H_0`$. It asserts that under $`H_0`$, for every $`Z\in\Omega'`$, the vector $`Y_{\mathit{fixed},Z}`$ equals the observed $`Y_{\mathit{fixed}}`$ and $`t_0(Z)=t_Z`$, and that for every real $`x`$ $`\frac{|\{Z\in\Omega':t_0(Z)\le x\}|}{|\Omega'|}=\frac{|\{Z\in\Omega':t_Z\le x\}|}{|\Omega'|} .`$ The right side is $`\Pr(t_Z\le x)`$ when the assignments in $`\Omega'`$ are equally likely. The statement is written with the two counts, and equal probability is not a separate hypothesis. The function $`g`$ may depend on the whole assignment; on $`\Omega'`$ this is the same as dependence on $`Z_{\mathit{variant}}`$, and pretreatment characteristics are part of $`g`$. The last sentence of the corrected passage, that the values $`t_Z`$ need not be equal, is not part of this statement. That assertion is the counterexample to Equation (1), as printed.

### P030-T7

The definition of $`\Omega'`$ and the null hypothesis $`H_{0''}`$, as corrected, with the consequence that dependence of $`Y_{\mathit{fixed}}`$ on $`Z_{\mathit{variant}}`$ over $`\Omega'`$ contradicts $`H_{0''}`$

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 6 (PDF p. 4); introduction, pp. 5–6 (PDF pp. 3–4)

The Lean statements use the two conditions as corrected in P030-N001: the prime is on the assignment, and $`Z_i=Z'_i`$ compares the treatment of one unit under two assignments. An assignment is a vector of $`N`$ binary treatments, $`\Omega`$ is a finite set of assignments, and the potential outcome $`Y_{i,Z}`$ is a real function of the unit and of the whole assignment. For a pattern $`a`$ of treatments, $`\Omega'`$ is the set of all $`Z\in\Omega`$ such that $`Z_i=a_i`$ for every fixed unit $`i`$. The first of five statements asserts that any two assignments in $`\Omega'`$ give every fixed unit the same treatment. The second asserts that, if $`H_{0''}`$ holds, then $`Y_{i,Z}=Y_{i,Z'}`$ for every fixed unit $`i`$ and all $`Z,Z'\in\Omega'`$. The third is the same implication in its other form: if the outcome of a fixed unit differs between two assignments in $`\Omega'`$, then $`H_{0''}`$ is false. The fourth asserts that outcomes $`Y_{i,Z}=c_i+\tau_iZ_i`$, with arbitrary real $`c_i`$ and $`\tau_i`$, satisfy $`H_{0''}`$. The fifth asserts that $`H_{0'}`$ implies $`H_{0''}`$. The statements do not require $`a`$ to agree with an assignment in $`\Omega`$ on the fixed units, and they hold also when $`\Omega'`$ is empty. They concern the potential outcomes and not the test: they do not say that a rejection by the test shows interference.

### P030-T8

Counterexamples to the formula for $`\rho`$ and to the sentence “$`\rho`$ has a perfect correlation coefficient (of 1 or -1) whenever $`D_{\mathit{nearest}}`$ has a monotone relationship with $`Y_{\mathit{fixed}}`$”, as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 8 (PDF p. 6); Table 1 on p. 9 (PDF p. 7)

Three Lean statements concern the passage of P030-E004: two concern the printed formula, and one the sentence that follows it. In each, the average rank of a value is the number of smaller values plus $`(e+1)/2`$, where $`e`$ is the number of values equal to it, itself included. The printed expression is the covariance of the ranks divided by the product of their variances, where the covariance and the variances divide by the same number $`m`$. The first statement takes the six rows of Table 1, with $`Y_{\mathit{fixed}}=(1,3,2,1)`$. It asserts that the printed expression equals $`-4/45,-4/45,-4/45,-20/27,-4/5,-4/5`$ for $`m=4`$ and $`-1/15,-1/15,-1/15,-5/9,-3/5,-3/5`$ for $`m=3`$. It asserts also that neither $`-4/5`$ nor $`-3/5`$ equals $`-3/\sqrt{10}`$, the correlation in the last row. The second statement takes $`D_A=(1,2,1,1)`$ and $`D_B=(0,2,1,1)`$ with the same $`Y_{\mathit{fixed}}`$ and $`m=4`$. It asserts that the correlations are $`\sqrt{6}/3`$ and $`5/6`$, that the printed expression equals $`8/9`$ and $`20/27`$, and that $`\sqrt{6}/3\lt 5/6`$ while $`8/9\gt 20/27`$. The third statement takes $`D=(1,2,3)`$ and $`Y=(0,0,1)`$, and it asserts that $`Y`$ is nondecreasing in $`D`$ and that $`\rho=\sqrt{3}/2`$, which is neither $`1`$ nor $`-1`$. In the second statement $`D_A`$ and $`D_B`$ are vectors of numbers and are not derived from positions of units, and no statement concerns the simulations of the article. The third statement does not contradict the printed sentence if “monotone” is read as strictly monotone.
