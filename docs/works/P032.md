# P032: Dyadic Clustering in International Relations

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P032.json)

Jacob Carlson, Trevor Incerti, and P. M. Aronow (2024). Dyadic Clustering in International Relations. Political Analysis 32(2):186–198.

DOI: [10.1017/pan.2023.26](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2023.26)

arXiv: [2109.03774](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03774)

## Overview

The paper studies dyadic clustering robust variance estimation and its use in reanalyses of international-relations studies. The Lean development proves the variance decomposition and supporting covariance identities, characterizes when omitting covariance between non-incident dyads gives a lower population variance, and supplies counterexamples to unconditional lower-bound claims. Further exact results examine record-level versus id-clustered subtraction, positive-semidefinite adjustment and the order of variance adjustment and multiple-imputation pooling.

Areas: Regression and variance estimation, Interference and networks, Foundations.

## Key results

For a full-rank OLS design with two distinct unordered members per dyad and all $`N`$ members represented, the residual dyadic sandwich equals the sum of member-cluster sandwiches minus the repeated-dyad sandwich minus $`(N-2)`$ times the record-level sandwich, using common bread and no hidden finite-sample factors.

[`AronowAudit.P032.headline_decomposition`](../../AronowAudit/P032/P032_Dyadic.lean#L338) · [P032-T11](#p032-t11)

For two square-integrable dyadic outcomes, the variance of their average is one quarter of the sum of their variances plus one half of their covariance.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed assertion that dyadic restriction still underestimates uncertainty with dependence between non-incident dyads is false under both the universal weak and strict population-variance readings formalized in Lean.

[`AronowAudit.P032.printed_population_claim_false`](../../AronowAudit/P032/P032_Dyadic.lean#L856) · [P032-T5](#p032-t5)

The printed unconditional lower-bound claim requires a sign condition: for any real matrix and contrast-weight vector, Lean proves the dyadically restricted quadratic form is at most the full form exactly when the ordered sum of omitted weighted entries is nonnegative, with strict inequalities determined by its sign.

[`AronowAudit.P032.variance_sign_conditions`](../../AronowAudit/P032/P032_Dyadic.lean#L196) · [P032-T2](#p032-t2)

The implementation's substitution of id-clustered subtraction for the decomposition's record-level subtraction fails in an exact Cox example, where Lean proves the direct dyadic variance is $`5/144`$ and the id-substituted value is $`23/432`$.

[`AronowAudit.P032.cox_substitution_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P032/P032_Dyadic.lean#L1112) · [P032-T3](#p032-t3)

The software's documented positive-semidefinite guarantee fails for a diagonal-gated adjustment: Lean gives a ten-dyad OLS variance matrix with positive diagonal, negative determinant and a negative contrast variance that the gate leaves unchanged.

[`AronowAudit.P032.positive_diagonal_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P032/P032_Dyadic.lean#L1203) · [P032-T6](#p032-t6)

In place of the diagonal-gated guarantee, Lean proves that taking the spectral positive part of any real symmetric matrix always returns a positive-semidefinite matrix.

[`AronowAudit.P032.positive_part_psd`](../../AronowAudit/P032/P032_Dyadic.lean#L529) · [P032-T7](#p032-t7)

Undoing finite-sample factors after Rubin pooling does not in general recover the Rubin-pooled dyadic variance: in the exact five-imputation logit example, Lean proves the pooled-first procedure is smaller than the within-imputation construction by $`(27/1300)(\log 3)^2`$.

[`AronowAudit.P032.mi_pooling_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P032/P032_Dyadic.lean#L1446) · [P032-T8](#p032-t8)

## Caveats

- Dyads are unordered pairs of distinct members; a member-cluster component places observations not containing that member in singleton clusters, and all decomposition components must use common scores, bread, observations and scaling.
- The sign-condition theorem is an algebraic identity for arbitrary matrices and vectors; its OLS interpretation uses the separately proved bridge for a fixed full-rank design with centered square-integrable errors, and concerns population variances rather than residual estimates.
- The bounded counterexamples refute universal claims and do not determine the sign of omitted weighted covariances in the reanalyzed studies; the development also supplies distinct residual-expectation and replicated-block limit counterexamples.
- The Cox and logit examples are exact mathematical constructions with computed scores and bread; they do not assert what Stata returns or recompute the affected historical studies.
- The general multiple-imputation repair assumes common fits, fixed component factors and samples, and an inactive PSD adjustment: remove the factors and form the dyadic variance within each imputation, then add Rubin's between-imputation term once.

### P032-T4

The decomposition on p. 191 with the conditions under which it is an identity, and its value when an id-clustered matrix is subtracted

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 191 (PDF p. 6), display for $`\widehat V_r`$ and the sentence that defines its terms; dcr.ado, lines 108–119 and 163–190

The Lean statements cover the corrected statement of P032-N004. Let each observation $`r`$ have an unordered dyad $`d_r`$ of two distinct members of a finite set of $`N`$ members, and let $`z_r`$ be an arbitrary vector. With $`\widehat V_0`$, $`\widehat V_D`$ and $`\widehat V_{c,i}`$ defined from the $`z_r`$ as in the finding, the main statement asserts that $`\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat V_{c,i}-\widehat V_D-(N-2)\widehat V_0=\sum_{r,s:\,d_r\cap d_s\ne\varnothing}z_rz_s^\top .`$ A companion statement gives the count behind the identity. The product $`z_rz_s^\top`$ has coefficient $`N-1-(N-2)=1`$ if $`r=s`$, $`2-1=1`$ if $`r\ne s`$ and $`d_r=d_s`$, $`1`$ if the dyads differ and share a member, and $`0`$ if they share none. For any matrix $`W`$ and any real $`\alpha`$, a further statement asserts that subtracting $`(N-2)\alpha W`$ in place of $`(N-2)\widehat V_0`$ returns $`\widehat V_r+(N-2)(\widehat V_0-\alpha W)`$. For ids $`g_r`$ and $`\widehat V_{\mathrm{id}}=\sum_{r,s:\,g_r=g_s}z_rz_s^\top`$, the statements assert that subtracting $`(N-2)\widehat V_{\mathrm{id}}`$ returns $`\widehat V_r-(N-2)\sum_{r\ne s:\,g_r=g_s}z_rz_s^\top`$, and that $`\widehat V_{\mathrm{id}}=\widehat V_0`$ when every id has a single observation. Nothing ties the $`z_r`$ to an estimator, so the identity holds for any fit in which all components use the same scores and the same bread matrix. The set of members may contain members that occur in no observation, as the corrected finding explicitly permits; each such member contributes the record-level matrix $`\widehat V_0`$. No statement concerns what Stata returns.

### P032-T2

The statements of pp. 187, 191 and 196 on dependence between non-incident dyads, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 187 (PDF p. 2), paragraph beginning “Note, however”; p. 191 (PDF p. 6), paragraph beginning “Note that the above approach”; p. 196 (PDF p. 11), Conclusion, first and third paragraphs

The Lean statements cover the condition in the corrected text of P032-E001. Let $`X`$ be a fixed design matrix such that $`X^TX`$ is invertible, and let $`Y=X\beta+u`$, where the errors are random variables with mean zero and finite variances. Fix a vector $`c`$, and set $`h=X(X^TX)^{-1}c`$ and $`\Omega=\mathbb{E}[uu^T]`$. Let $`d_r`$ be the dyad of observation $`r`$, an unordered set of two distinct members, and let $`\Omega_D`$ be $`\Omega`$ with the entry $`(r,s)`$ set to zero whenever $`d_r`$ and $`d_s`$ share no member, as in Table 4. The statements assert that $`c^T\widehat\beta-c^T\beta=h^Tu`$, that $`\mathrm{Var}(c^T\widehat\beta)=h^T\Omega h=c^TV_{sandwich}c`$ for the matrix $`V_{sandwich}`$ of p. 189, and that $`h^T\Omega h-h^T\Omega_Dh=\Delta,\qquad \Delta=\sum_{(r,s):\,d_r\cap d_s=\varnothing}h_rh_s\Omega_{rs}.`$ They assert that $`h^T\Omega_Dh\le h^T\Omega h`$ if and only if $`\Delta\ge0`$, that the inequality is strict if and only if $`\Delta\gt 0`$, and that $`h^T\Omega_Dh\gt h^T\Omega h`$ if and only if $`\Delta\lt 0`$. The equivalence involving every $`c`$ requires the matrix in place of $`\Omega`$ to be symmetric. A separate statement connects them to the variance of the least-squares contrast. The statements compare the population values and assert nothing about estimates computed from residuals.

### P032-T11

The decomposition of the DCR variance estimator (unnumbered display, Section 3)

**Kind:** `headline_result`

**Location:** pp. 190–191 (PDF pp. 5–6): the sentence beginning “More specifically” at the foot of p. 190, the display on p. 191 and the sentence that defines its terms, with footnotes 2 and 3

The Lean statement asserts that $`\widehat V_r=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat V_{c,i}-\widehat V_D-(N-2)\widehat V_0`$ for every design matrix $`X`$ such that $`X^TX`$ is invertible, every vector $`Y`$ of outcomes and every assignment of a dyad to each observation. A dyad is an unordered set of two distinct members, and $`N`$ is the number of members, each of which occurs in some observation. Each matrix is the plug-in estimator $`(X^TX)^{-1}X^T\widehat\Omega X(X^TX)^{-1}`$ of p. 189, without a finite-sample factor. Here $`\widehat\Omega_{rs}=\widehat u_r\widehat u_s`$ for the pairs of observations that are kept and $`\widehat\Omega_{rs}=0`$ otherwise, and $`\widehat u`$ is the vector of least-squares residuals. The pairs kept are those whose dyads share a member for $`\widehat V_r`$, those with the same dyad for $`\widehat V_D`$, and the pairs such that $`r=s`$ for $`\widehat V_0`$. For $`\widehat V_{c,i}`$ they are the pairs whose dyads both contain member $`i`$, together with the pairs such that $`r=s`$ for each observation whose dyad does not contain $`i`$. The paper does not define $`N`$ beside the display or say how $`\widehat V_{c,i}`$ treats the remaining observations. The Lean statement uses the reading of P032-N005, with two distinct members in each dyad (P032-N003). The statement is for least squares, the model of p. 189, and the version for arbitrary scores is the corrected statement of P032-N004. The eigenvalue step of footnote 3 and the sentence on square roots are not part of the statement.

### P032-T12

The variance of the average of two dyadic outcomes that share a member (two unnumbered displays, Section 2.1)

**Kind:** `headline_result`

**Location:** pp. 188–189 (PDF pp. 3–4), Section 2.1, from “This clustering structure affects statistical inference” to the end of the section

The two displays are covered by three Lean statements. Let $`\widehat\mu=\tfrac12(Y_{US,UK}+Y_{US,FR})`$ and $`\mathrm{V}_{naive}=\tfrac14(\mathrm{V}[Y_{US,UK}]+\mathrm{V}[Y_{US,FR}])`$. The first statement asserts that $`\mathrm{V}[\widehat\mu]=\mathrm{V}_{naive}+\tfrac12\mathrm{Cov}[Y_{US,UK},Y_{US,FR}]`$ for any two outcomes with finite variances, with no assumption of independence. The second takes $`Y_{US,UK}=aX_{US}+bX_{UK}+U_{US,UK}`$ and $`Y_{US,FR}=cX_{US}+dX_{FR}+U_{US,FR}`$, where the five variables $`X_{US}`$, $`X_{UK}`$, $`X_{FR}`$, $`U_{US,UK}`$ and $`U_{US,FR}`$ have finite variances and are pairwise uncorrelated. It asserts that $`\mathrm{Cov}[Y_{US,UK},Y_{US,FR}]=ac\,\mathrm{V}[X_{US}]`$. The third asserts under the same conditions that $`\mathrm{V}[\widehat\mu]=\mathrm{V}_{naive}+\frac{ac}{2}\mathrm{V}[X_{US}],`$ that $`\mathrm{V}_{naive}\lt \mathrm{V}[\widehat\mu]`$ if $`ac\gt 0`$ and $`\mathrm{V}[X_{US}]\gt 0`$, that $`\mathrm{V}_{naive}\gt \mathrm{V}[\widehat\mu]`$ if $`ac\lt 0`$ and $`\mathrm{V}[X_{US}]\gt 0`$, and that the two are equal if $`ac=0`$ or $`\mathrm{V}[X_{US}]=0`$. The paper assumes that “the $`X`$ and the $`U`$ are independent, and all $`X`$ are pairwise-independent”. For the additive covariance formula and its consequences, I additionally assume $`\mathrm{Cov}[U_{US,UK},U_{US,FR}]=0`$, which the printed text does not explicitly state. Pairwise uncorrelatedness weakens the stated independence requirements for the other pairs, but these results are conditional on that additional condition. The first variance identity requires no independence. They also make explicit the condition $`\mathrm{V}[X_{US}]\gt 0`$, which the sentence on $`a`$ and $`c`$ of the same sign needs. The case of opposite signs is not stated in the paper.

### P032-T1

Counterexamples to the statement on p. 187 that DCRSEs “will still underestimate uncertainty” under dependence between non-incident dyads

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 187 (PDF p. 2), paragraph beginning “Note, however”; p. 191 (PDF p. 6), paragraph beginning “Note that the above approach”; p. 196 (PDF p. 11), Conclusion, first and third paragraphs

The Lean statements give Examples 1 to 5 of P032-E001 as exact least-squares models. In the notation of that finding, they compare the true variance $`V=h^T\Omega h`$ of a coefficient with the population dyadic sandwich value $`V_D=h^T\Omega_Dh`$. For two observations on dyads with no common member, they assert that $`V=1/4`$ and $`V_D=1/2`$ for the intercept when the covariance of the two errors is $`-1/2`$, and for the slope on $`x=(-1,1)^T`$ when it is $`1/2`$. For the intercept with covariance $`1/2`$, they assert that $`V=3/4`$ and $`V_D=1/2`$. For the intercept in Example 5, on the dyads $`\{A,B\}`$, $`\{A,C\}`$, $`\{D,E\}`$ and $`\{D,F\}`$, they assert that $`V=3/16`$ and $`V_D=5/16`$. For the intercept in Example 4, on the six dyads among four members, they assert that $`V=1/6`$ and $`V_D=1/4`$, and that the value under the naive restriction of Table 2 is also $`1/6`$. For $`m`$ independent copies of Example 5 or of Example 4 on disjoint sets of members, let $`\widehat V_r`$ be the DCR variance estimator, computed from least-squares residuals without a small-sample factor. The statements assert that $`\mathbb{E}[\widehat V_r]=\frac{10m-3}{32m^2},\quad \mathrm{Var}(\widehat\beta)=\frac{3}{16m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \mathbb{E}[\widehat V_r]=\frac{3}{2n}-\frac{5}{n^2},\quad \mathrm{Var}(\widehat\beta)=\frac{1}{n}\quad(n=6m)`$ for Example 5 and for Example 4 respectively, so that the expectation exceeds the variance for every $`m\ge1`$ in the first case and exactly when $`m\ge2`$ in the second. They also assert that $`\widehat V_r/\mathrm{Var}(\widehat\beta)`$ converges in probability to $`5/3`$ for Example 5 and that $`n\widehat V_r`$ converges almost surely to $`3/2`$ for Example 4. Each error distribution is explicit, on finitely many points, with mean zero and a covariance matrix that is positive definite. A separate statement asserts that there exists a probability space that carries the independent copies. The Lean statements have the scope of the examples and say nothing about the sign of the sum of the omitted weighted covariances in any of the 22 reanalyzed studies.
