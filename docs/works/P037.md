# P037: Changing Climates of Conflict: A Social Network Experiment in 56 Schools

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P037.json)

Elizabeth Levy Paluck, Hana Shepherd, and P. M. Aronow (2016). Changing Climates of Conflict: A Social Network Experiment in 56 Schools. PNAS 113(3):566–571.

DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1514483113](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1514483113)

## Overview

The work reports a school-randomized social-network experiment and uses school regressions and exposure-weighted estimators to study conflict and peer influence. The Lean development concerns mathematical properties of these procedures and synthetic counterexamples, rather than verification of the reported empirical effects. It includes expected-regression identification and bias identities, independent-block limit theorems, exposure and resampling calculations, and corrections to selected printed or code-described procedures. The composition counterexample limits identification from randomization and marginal linearity alone; it does not refute the qualified conditional response model with its sufficient residual moment condition.

Areas: Interference and networks, Causal identification, Regression and variance estimation.

## Key results

For complete four-school blocks satisfying the stated design moments, control-span and positive-variation assumptions, any expected-normal-equation solution under $`Y_i=a_i+D_i(\tau_0+\eta_i+\beta C_i)`$ has composition-coefficient bias $`b-\beta=\mathrm{E}[\sum_i r_iD_i\eta_i]/\mathrm{E}[\sum_i r_i^2]`$, where $`r`$ is the design-expected projection residual.

[`AronowAudit.P037.ExpectedProjection.block_model_bias_identity`](../../AronowAudit/P037/P037_Review.lean#L303) · [P037-T43](#p037-t43)

Under the same complete-block design-moment, control-span, positive-variation and expected-normal-equation assumptions, the response model $`Y_i=a_i+D_i(\tau_0+\beta C_i)`$ identifies the original regression's composition slope as $`\beta`$ for arbitrary fixed untreated levels $`a_i`$.

[`AronowAudit.P037.ExpectedProjection.common_intercept_identifies_slope`](../../AronowAudit/P037/P037_Review.lean#L363) · [P037-T43](#p037-t43)

Along independently randomized finite blocks with uniformly bounded retained block sizes, outcomes and fixed predictions, exposure probabilities bounded away from zero, retained-population size growing at least linearly, and total block variance bounded below linearly, the oracle augmented exposure mean standardized by its actual standard deviation converges to a standard Gaussian law.

[`AronowAudit.P037.independent_blocks_oracle_normal`](../../AronowAudit/P037/P037_FiniteDesign.lean#L2459) · [P037-T11](#p037-t11)

When a student's exposure depends only on that school's treatment and independent within-school seed draws, its exposure probabilities depend on the school-assignment law only through the school's treatment marginal, so laws sharing marginal $`1/2`$ give the same probabilities.

[`AronowAudit.P037.school_local_exposure_invariance`](../../AronowAudit/P037/P037_Design.lean#L78) · [P037-T32](#p037-t32)

For a fixed full-column-rank OLS design and response, a raw-residual wild bootstrap whose multipliers have integrable first and second products, mean zero and identity second-moment matrix has coefficient mean equal to the fitted coefficient and covariance exactly equal to HC0.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The causal interpretation of the composition coefficient does not follow from randomization, the eligible-composition main-effect control and marginal average linearity alone: an explicit normalized design has zero within-school composition effects but unique population-projection composition coefficient $`-16/31`$, while violating the qualified response model's sufficient restriction.

[`AronowAudit.P037.composition_witness_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P037/P037_FiniteDesign.lean#L1189) · [P037-T1](#p037-t1)

Under the direct-independent-uniform reading of the table notes' Bayesian-bootstrap weights, normalization gives a different law from Dirichlet$`(1,\ldots,1)`$ for every sample size at least two: the event $`p_1/(p_1+p_2)\lt 1/4`$ has probabilities $`1/6`$ and $`1/4`$, respectively.

[`AronowAudit.P037.Wave1.uniform_weights_not_bayesian_bootstrap_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P037/Wave1/Gap/GP037B4b.lean#L403) · [P037-T4](#p037-t4)

The assertion that between-school randomization makes schools independent fails for uniform two-of-four treatment within a block: distinct school indicators each have mean $`1/2`$, joint treatment probability $`1/6`$, and covariance $`-1/12`$.

[`AronowAudit.P037.within_block_dependence`](../../AronowAudit/P037/P037_FiniteDesign.lean#L133) · [P037-T10](#p037-t10)

Against the description of simulations as following the experimental design, uniform 28-of-56 school assignment and independent two-of-four block assignment have different supports and pair probabilities, and exact eight-school examples show that omitting blocks can either raise or lower the variance of a synthetic sharp-null mean contrast.

[`AronowAudit.P037.Wave1.school_sampler_counterexample_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P037/Wave1/Gap/GP037B3.lean#L396) · [P037-T31](#p037-t31)

For the analysis restriction described as administrative-data availability, the released code's outcome-threshold selection need not preserve a causal comparison: a complete-data synthetic design with constant effect $`0.03`$ has expected selected difference $`E[\Delta]=0.015`$, while the ratio of the expected block-regression numerator to the expected denominator is $`E[w\Delta]/E[w]=0.012`$, with $`w=n_Tn_C/(n_T+n_C)`$ for the retained arm sizes.

[`AronowAudit.P037.outcome_selection_finite_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P037/P037_Sample.lean#L68) · [P037-T19](#p037-t19)

Against the claim that the alternative permutation comparison is restricted to social influence effects, a synthetic ten-student reciprocal-pair design with outcomes determined only by own seed status has exact permutation p-value $`1/126`$ for one-seed-per-pair assignments and rejection probability $`8/63`$ at the strict $`0.01`$ cutoff.

[`AronowAudit.P037.Wave1.direct_effect_permutation_counterexample_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P037/Wave1/Gap/GLP037B4.lean#L504) · [P037-T35](#p037-t35)

## Caveats

- The composition counterexample has treatment moderation by eligible-pool composition and does not establish that the supplement's qualified conditional response model fails in the study.
- The expected-projection results concern solutions of expected normal equations on complete indexed four-school blocks: they assume the composition first moment, that demeaned controls span the demeaned expected composition, nonzero treatment variation and positive residual variation; they do not assert finite-sample OLS unbiasedness, sampling consistency or validity on arbitrary incomplete blocks.
- In the bias identity, writing $`M`$ for block demeaning, $`t=MD`$, $`z=M(DC)`$ and $`V=MX`$, the residual is $`r=z-\rho t-pV\lambda`$, with $`\rho=\mathrm{E}[z^{\mathsf T}t]/\mathrm{E}[t^{\mathsf T}t]`$, $`M\mu=V\lambda`$, $`\mathrm{E}[D_i]=p`$ and $`\mathrm{E}[D_iC_i]=p\mu_i`$; it is distinct from the seed innovation $`D(C-\mu)`$.
- The selected block CLT uses fixed retained students, fixed predictions and exact exposure probabilities, with unrestricted dependence inside blocks; it does not verify these hypotheses for the 56 schools or cover fitted nuisance quantities by itself.
- The normalized-uniform counterexample assumes independent direct uniform multipliers, an interpretation not stated explicitly in the printed notes, and does not identify the implemented weight transformation or quantify changes in reported standard errors.
- The separate normalized-exponential-to-Dirichlet construction is conditional on the published gamma-to-Dirichlet theorem represented by the DevroyeGammaDirichlet hypothesis (Devroye, Non-Uniform Random Variate Generation, 1986, Chapter XI, Theorem 4.1, p. 594; density definition in Section XI.4.1, p. 593).
- School-local exposure invariance preserves marginal student probabilities under its stated independence and locality assumptions, while the joint assignment law and randomization variances can change.
- The selection, sampler and direct-effect witnesses are synthetic designs and do not calculate changes in the study's empirical effects, standard errors or p-values; the direct-effect witness does not refute validity under the global sharp null of no effects of any kind.

### P037-T1

A counterexample to identification from randomization and marginal average linearity alone

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** SI Appendix, pp. 13–14 (supplement PDF pp. 13–14), repeated on pp. 15–16 and in the note to Table S7 (p. 42); main text, p. 568 (PDF p. 3)

The Lean statements give the example in P037-E001 as a limitation of identification from the design alone. A block has four schools whose seed-eligible groups have compositions $`q=(1/10,1/10,3/10,3/10)`$, and two of the schools are treated, each pair with probability $`1/6`$. In a treated school, five of the ten students of each of four strata are drawn as seeds. The outcomes are $`Y_i(0)=3/10`$ and $`Y_i(1,c)=3/10-q_i`$ for every composition $`c`$ of the seed group. In the finite probability space, a state consists of the treated pair and the number of social referents among the seeds of each, a sum of four independent hypergeometric counts. The main statement asserts that $`\Pr(D_i=1)=1/2`$, $`\mathrm{E}[C_i\mid D_i=1]=q_i`$ and $`\mathrm{Var}(C_i\mid D_i=1)=q_i(1-q_i)/36`$, and that each composition $`0,1/20,\ldots,4/20`$ has positive probability for every school when it is treated. It also asserts that composition has no effect on any school, that the average treatment effect is $`-1/5`$ at every $`c`$, and that the expected normal equations of regression (2), with the block mean removed in each realization, have the single solution $`(\tau_0,\gamma,\beta)=\Bigl(-\frac{3}{31},\,-\frac{15}{62},\,-\frac{16}{31}\Bigr).`$ A second statement replaces the outcomes by $`Y_i=a+\tau D_i+b_1D_i(C_i-q_i)+b_2D_i(q_i-1/5)+g\,q_i`$ with arbitrary real constants. It asserts a unique solution of the expected normal equations, with coefficient $`(15b_1+16b_2)/31`$ on $`DC`$, equal to $`b_1`$ if and only if $`b_1=b_2`$. A third statement asserts that the inverse-formula coefficient on $`DC`$, computed from $`B`$ independent copies of the block and set to zero when the matrix is singular, converges in probability to $`-16/31`$ as $`B\to\infty`$. The other 36 statements are steps toward the first two, and one of them asserts that $`b_1`$ is the slope of $`Y_i(1,c)`$ in $`c`$ for every school. The Lean statements define the potential outcomes for every real $`c`$, which is broader than the compositions that can occur. The first two concern expected normal equations, not the expectation of a finite-sample least-squares coefficient; none concerns the 56 schools of the study. The example satisfies randomization, the eligible-composition main-effect control and a marginal linear average causal curve. Its treatment effect is $`-q_i`$, however, so that eligible-pool types have different treatment effects even at the common feasible composition $`c=0`$. It violates the sufficient response restriction of the supplement’s naturally qualified interaction model. The formal statements therefore do not refute identification under a valid conditional average response with its residual moment condition, or establish that this condition fails in the study. Such a response model need not make realized treatment effects identical in every school.

### P037-T2

Expected-equations identification under a response moment condition, and optional centered or augmented regressions

**Kind:** `result_touched_by_finding`

**Location:** SI Appendix, pp. 13–14 and 15–16 (supplement PDF pp. 13–16); main text, p. 568 (PDF p. 3)

The Lean statements describe expected-equations identification under a residual moment condition and the centered or augmented alternatives in P037-E001. The design is a finite probability space with schools in blocks of four; I write $`M`$ for the matrix that removes block means before taking cross-products. For the response model, one statement supposes that $`Y=\alpha+X\theta+\varepsilon`$, where $`\alpha`$ is constant within blocks and $`\mathrm{E}[X'M\varepsilon]=0`$, and asserts that $`\theta`$ solves $`\mathrm{E}[X'MX]\,\theta=\mathrm{E}[X'MY]`$, uniquely when the map $`v\mapsto\mathrm{E}[X'MX]\,v`$ is injective. For the alternatives, let $`R`$ satisfy $`\mathrm{E}[f(D)R_i]=0`$ for every school $`i`$ and function $`f`$ of the treatment vector $`D`$, expressing $`\mathrm{E}[R_i\mid D]=0`$. Two statements are the identity $`a+Dt+bDC=a+D(t+b\mu)+bD(C-\mu)`$, in which $`\mu`$ stands for the mean of the composition $`C`$ of the seed group under the draw of the seeds, and the orthogonality $`\mathrm{E}[R'MV]=0`$ for every vector $`V`$ that is a function of $`D`$. Two more suppose that $`(\delta,\beta)`$ solves the expected normal equations of the regression on columns $`W(D)`$ and on $`R`$, for $`Y=A(D)+bR`$ with $`A`$ any function of the treatment vector and for $`Y_i=a_i+D_it_i+bD_iC_i`$ with $`R=D(C-\mu)`$; each asserts that $`\beta=b`$ when $`\mathrm{E}[R'MR]\gt 0`$. A fifth concerns the regression with $`D\mu`$ added: if $`Z=R+W_e`$ for one column $`W_e`$ of $`W`$, then $`(\delta,\beta)`$ solves the equations with $`Z`$ if and only if $`(\delta+\beta e,\beta)`$ solves those with $`R`$, where $`e`$ is the unit vector of that column. Four statements concern $`\mu`$, for nonempty strata $`h`$ with $`N_h`$ seed-eligible students, $`K_h`$ social referents and $`n_h`$ seeds drawn uniformly without replacement, independently across strata, with at least one seed in total. Three of them assert that the composition $`C`$ of the seed group has mean and variance $`\mu=\frac{\sum_hn_hK_h/N_h}{\sum_hn_h},\qquad \mathrm{Var}(C)=\frac{1}{\bigl(\sum_hn_h\bigr)^{2}}\sum_{h:N_h\gt 1}n_h\frac{K_h}{N_h}\Bigl(1-\frac{K_h}{N_h}\Bigr)\frac{N_h-n_h}{N_h-1},`$ that $`\mu=q`$ when $`n_h/N_h`$ is the same in every stratum, where $`q=\sum_hK_h/\sum_hN_h`$, and that $`D(C-q)=D(C-\mu)+D(\mu-q)`$. The fourth takes four strata with $`(N_h,K_h,n_h)`$ equal to $`(13,2,7)`$, $`(14,1,7)`$, $`(14,0,7)`$ and $`(14,0,7)`$, the counts of one control school on the public data, and asserts that $`q=3/55`$, that $`\mu=41/728`$ and that $`\mu-q=71/40040`$. These counts are a premise of the statement, not something that Lean proves about the data. A further statement evaluates the counterexample, in which $`\mu=q`$: it asserts that $`\mathrm{E}[R'MR]=1/160`$ and that the expected normal equations of six regressions have a unique solution, with coefficient on the composition term $`\begin{aligned} &0 \quad\text{for } 3/10-Dq \text{ with the columns } (D,q,DC,Dq)\\ &\qquad\text{and with } (D,q,D(C-q)),\\ &-49/155 \text{ and } 1/5 \quad\text{for } 3/10-Dq+DC/5\\ &\qquad\text{with the printed columns and with either of the other two sets},\\ &1/5 \quad\text{for } 3/10-D/5-q/2+DC/5, \text{ which has the form (2),}\\ &\qquad\text{with the printed columns}. \end{aligned}`$ The statement of the mean and variance concerns the draw of the seeds alone. Coupling that draw independently to school assignment would give $`\mathrm{E}[C_i\mid D_i=1]=\mu_i`$ and the condition on $`R=D(C-\mu)`$; that coupling is not stated. The remaining 13 statements are steps; none takes a limit or covers slopes that differ between schools. No statement here asserts finite-sample unbiasedness. Here $`R`$ denotes the seed innovation $`D(C-\mu)`$, not the residual of raw $`DC`$ under a design-expected projection. The response-model statement imposes its residual moment; it does not require a zero residual or identical realized treatment effects in every school. A common conditional average response with a sufficient residual condition can therefore support the causal interpretation. The model-conforming numerical example is a sufficient special case, not a requirement that every school’s untreated outcome follow an exact linear equation. The result allowing arbitrary untreated levels with a common treatment intercept, and the bias identity that gives the weaker zero-numerator condition, are stated in P037-T43. The centered and augmented regressions are alternatives for allowing arbitrary fixed treatment intercepts under their stated conditions; these statements do not establish a need to replace the original model or change its empirical estimates.

Expected projection and a sufficient response model for the original slope

**Location:** SI Appendix, p. 13, equation (2), repeated on pp. 15–16; main text, p. 568 (PDF p. 3)

Two proved statements concern a finite randomization law and a fixed collection of complete four-school blocks. Let $`M`$ subtract each block’s mean, $`t=MD`$, $`z=M(D\odot C)`$, and $`V=MX`$ for fixed controls $`X`$. Assume $`E[D_i]=p`$, $`E[D_iC_i]=p\mu_i`$, and $`M\mu=V\lambda`$.
