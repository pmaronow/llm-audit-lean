# P039: Ideologically Extreme Candidates in U.S. Presidential Elections, 1948-2012

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P039.json)

Marty Cohen, Mary C. McGrath, P. M. Aronow, and John Zaller (2016). Ideologically Extreme Candidates in U.S. Presidential Elections, 1948-2012. Annals of the American Academy of Political and Social Science 667(1):126–142.

DOI: [10.1177/0002716216660571](https://doi.org/10.1177/0002716216660571)

## Overview

The work is an empirical study of candidate extremism and U.S. presidential elections, with discounted-income predictors, residual plots and fitted-effect summaries. Its Lean development covers narrower algebraic and finite-data questions: the implemented income weighting, residual-regression versus full-model OLS, extremism-score selection, and consistency of conventional standard-error entries. The general regression results and synthetic counterexamples do not establish the empirical election conclusions or exact inference for the nonlinear bread-and-peace models.

Areas: Regression and variance estimation.

## Key results

For full-rank linear OLS controls containing an intercept, an added regressor outside their span and positive residual degrees of freedom, regressing the first-stage outcome residuals on the unadjusted regressor attenuates its slope by the residualized-to-centered regressor sum-of-squares ratio, with exact SSE, conventional-SE and guarded t-statistic relations to the full fit.

## Caveats

- The general regression results use finite linear OLS, full column rank and conventional inverse-Gram standard errors with residual degrees of freedom; comparisons involving t statistics retain their positive-SSE guards and do not establish inference for the nonlinear bread-and-peace fits.
- The six-observation residual-regression witness is synthetic; it does not correct the plotted line's nominal p-value of 0.26 or prove an empirical full-model p-value for the election data.
- The nominal Student-tail functions in the selected five-percent witness are supplied closed-form expressions; that declaration does not derive those expressions by integrating Student densities.
- The income comparison uses the deposited implementation's normalization and a sixteen-quarter extension defined for the formalization; the printed paper gives quarter weights but does not print that denominator.
- Extremism scores, election sample sets and the 17 input rows for the Table 2 fits are supplied exact decimal rationals; their construction from raw empirical sources is outside these statements, as are the printed significance stars.
- Maximum effects are fitted contributions relative to score zero, not newly identified causal effects; the sign of the selected extremism score is preserved.

### P039-T3

A counterexample to the $`p`$-value of the line in Figure 4, panel B, read as a test of extremism given the fundamentals

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 138 (PDF p. 13), last sentence, and Figure 4, panel B; compare Table 2, column B3, p. 137 (PDF p. 12), and Table 3, row (3), p. 139 (PDF p. 14)

The Lean statements concern the counterexample with six observations in P039-E002, with its vectors $`u`$, $`v`$ and $`w`$, its first-step regressors (the constant and $`u`$) and its outcome $`y=u+(3/2)v+w`$. A regression is represented by a real design matrix $`A`$ of full column rank, with coefficient vector $`(A^\top A)^{-1}A^\top y`$ and conventional standard errors. Seven statements give exact calculations, including four fits asserted to be unique least-squares minimizers. They give the first-step residual $`r=(3/2)v+w`$ and the matrix that maps $`y`$ to $`r`$, which is symmetric and idempotent of rank 4. With $`x=v`$, the regression of $`r`$ on the constant and $`x`$ and the regression of $`y`$ on the constant, $`u`$ and $`x`$ have the same slope $`3/2`$ and the same residual vector $`w`$, with $`t^2=9`$ on 4 degrees of freedom and $`t^2=27/4`$ on 3. With $`x=u+v`$ the two slopes are $`3/4`$ and $`3/2`$, and the sum of squares of $`x`$ falls from 4 to 2 when $`x`$ is adjusted for the first-step regressors. Two statements of the counterexample collect the unequal $`t^2`$ values for $`x=v`$ and unequal slopes for $`x=u+v`$, and assert that for $`x=v`$ the $`t`$ statistics are $`3`$ and $`3\sqrt{3}/2`$, with nominal $`p`$-values below and above $`1/20`$ respectively. One statement evaluates these $`p`$-values as $`1-45/(13\sqrt{13})`$ and $`1/2-(2/\pi)(\arctan(1/5)+6/13)`$. In these statements the $`p`$-values are defined by closed-form expressions for the two-sided tails of Student’s distributions with 4 and 3 degrees of freedom, and the expressions are not derived from the densities. For $`x=v`$ and $`y`$ equal to a linear combination of the first-step regressors plus independent $`N(0,\sigma^2)`$ errors with $`\sigma\gt 0`$, one distributional statement asserts that the $`t`$ statistic of the regression of the residuals is distributed as $`\sqrt{4/3}\,T_3`$, where $`T_k`$ denotes a Student variable with $`k`$ degrees of freedom. It also asserts that this statistic has second moment 4 and is not distributed as $`T_4`$, whose second moment is 2. Five auxiliary statements are general facts on least squares, which concern sums of squares, solutions of the normal equations, the uniqueness of the minimizer under full column rank, and a criterion for full column rank. The statements concern the procedure in a linear model, and not the value .26, which is the correct nominal $`p`$-value of the plotted line, or the nonlinear models of the paper.

### P039-T4

The sentence on Figure 4, panel B, at the foot of p. 138, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 138 (PDF p. 13), last sentence

The Lean statements give the algebra behind the corrected sentence of P039-E002, for least squares in a linear model. Let $`Z`$ be an $`n\times k`$ matrix of full column rank whose column space contains the constant vector, let $`x`$ be a vector outside this column space, and let $`n\gt k+1`$. Let $`M=I-Z(Z^\top Z)^{-1}Z^\top`$ and $`r=My`$, and let $`S_M=x^\top Mx`$ and $`S_c`$ be the sums of squares of $`x`$ after adjustment for $`Z`$ and after centring. The main statement asserts that the regression of $`r`$ on the constant and $`x`$ has slope $`a=x^\top r/S_c`$ and that the coefficient of $`x`$ in the regression of $`y`$ on $`Z`$ and $`x`$ is $`b=x^\top r/S_M`$. It asserts that $`0\lt S_M\le S_c,\qquad a=b\,S_M/S_c,\qquad \frac{t_{\mathrm{res}}^2}{n-2}\le\frac{t_{\mathrm{full}}^2}{n-k-1},`$ where $`t_{\mathrm{res}}`$ and $`t_{\mathrm{full}}`$ are the conventional $`t`$ statistics of $`a`$ and $`b`$, the last inequality when the full regression has a positive residual sum of squares. Under the same condition, it asserts that if the centred $`x`$ is orthogonal to the columns of $`Z`$, then $`a=b`$, the two residual vectors agree, and $`t_{\mathrm{res}}=\sqrt{(n-2)/(n-k-1)}\,t_{\mathrm{full}}`$. The statement also gives the residual sums of squares and the conventional standard errors of the two regressions. Five supporting statements give parts of the main statement: three are on the matrices $`Z^\top Z`$ and $`M`$ and on the uniqueness of the least-squares fit, and two are identities for real numbers under the condition $`0\lt S_M\le S_c`$. A second general statement concerns errors $`\varepsilon`$ with mean zero and covariance $`\sigma^2I`$. It asserts that $`r=M\varepsilon`$ has covariance $`\sigma^2M`$, where $`M`$ has rank $`n-k`$. If $`k\ge2`$, the centred $`x`$ is orthogonal to the columns of $`Z`$, and $`t_{\mathrm{full}}`$ has Student’s distribution with $`n-k-1`$ degrees of freedom, the statement asserts that $`t_{\mathrm{res}}`$ is distributed as $`\sqrt{(n-2)/(n-k-1)}`$ times such a variable and does not have Student’s distribution with $`n-2`$ degrees of freedom. This statement takes the distribution of $`t_{\mathrm{full}}`$ as a hypothesis and does not derive it from Gaussian errors. I verified the rank $`n-k`$ of the covariance of $`r`$ and its difference from $`\sigma^2I`$ only for $`\sigma\gt 0`$. The bread and peace models are nonlinear in the decay parameter, and no statement asserts the application to them or the approximate $`p`$-value of .30.

### P039-T6

The rule for the maximum effect on p. 139 and note 15, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 139 (PDF p. 14); note 15, p. 142 (PDF p. 17); Table 3, p. 139 (PDF p. 14)

The Lean statements concern the corrected rule of P039-E004: the maximum effect is the fitted coefficient times the observed score of greatest absolute magnitude, with its sign retained. The scores are the 64 values of the replication files, each decimal taken as the exact rational number that it denotes, and they are taken as given. Two statements give the elections selected. They assert that for measures (1) to (5) of Table 3 the score of greatest absolute value occurs in 1980, 1972, 1980, 1972 and 1964, in that election only, and that it is negative. They also give the exact margin over the nearest other election, which is 1972, 2004, 1972, 2008 and 1968; the margins are about $`.133`$, $`.172`$, $`.165`$, $`.242`$ and $`.104`$. A third statement concerns the two estimation samples, of which the one for the bread and peace models omits 1948 for measures (1) and (3). It asserts that the same election is selected in both samples, and that the numbers of elections less five and less six fitted parameters are the degrees of freedom printed in Table 3. Two statements concern the product for an arbitrary real coefficient $`\beta`$. They assert that $`(g+\beta x)-(g+\beta\cdot 0)=\beta x`$ for a fitted value of the form $`g+\beta x`$, that $`|\beta x|`$ is largest among the observed scores at the selected one, and that the sign of the product is that of $`-\beta`$. The last statement is the arithmetic of the worked example: $`-2.23\times(-.62)=1.3826`$, which rounds to $`1.38`$, and the score of the refined zero-centered measure for 1980 rounds to $`-.62`$. The general product statements hold for every real $`\beta`$ and are in this respect broader than the rule; estimation of the fitted coefficients is not formalized.

The Lean statements concern P039-E003, in three parts: the inequality for nested regressions, the contradiction among the printed entries, and the recalculation. Let two least-squares regressions of one outcome have design matrices of full column rank, with more observations than regressors, and a common regressor $`q`$. Suppose that the span of the other regressors of the first is contained in the span of those of the second. Let $`R_j`$ be the residual sum of squares when $`q`$ is regressed on the other regressors of regression $`j`$, and let $`s_j`$ be the residual standard error of regression $`j`$. Independently of the outcome, the full general statement asserts that each $`R_j`$ is attained by a least-squares control fit, that $`0\lt R_2\le R_1`$, and that the inverse-Gram diagonal belonging to $`q`$ is $`1/R_j`$. For every outcome with positive residual sums of squares in both regressions, it gives the conventional standard error $`\mathrm{SE}_j=s_j/\sqrt{R_j}`$ and the inequality $`\mathrm{SE}_1\le \mathrm{SE}_2\,s_1/s_2 .`$ Two supporting statements give $`R_2\le R_1`$ and the step from it to the display for positive real numbers. One statement asserts that no nonnegative real numbers satisfy the display together with $`\mathrm{SE}_1\ge1.35`$, $`\mathrm{SE}_2\le.965`$, $`s_1\le4.625`$ and $`s_2\ge4.695`$. Another asserts that $`.965\times4.625/4.695=7141/7512\lt .951`$. A further statement uses the general statement to combine the first two parts, asserting that no two nested regressions of full column rank on 17 observations, with two and three regressors besides $`q`$, have standard errors of $`q`$ and residual standard errors in the rounding intervals of the printed $`1.4`$, $`.96`$, $`4.62`$ and $`4.70`$. For the recalculation, the 17 rows of the replication data are entered as exact rational numbers. For each of columns A1, A2 and A3, three statements assert that the design matrix has full column rank, that a stated exact coefficient vector is the unique least-squares fit, and that the income coefficient lies in $`[6.385,6.395)`$, $`[6.365,6.375)`$ and $`[6.385,6.395)`$ respectively. A last statement asserts that the squared standard error of the income coefficient of A1 lies in $`[.935^2,.945^2)`$, so that the standard error rounds to $`.94`$. The additional statement AronowAudit.P039.PaperDomains.nested_standard_error_bound has the same hypotheses and conclusion as the full general statement, including the outcome-independent conclusions about $`R_j`$. I read the parenthesized entries as conventional standard errors and RMSQ as the residual standard error, as the replication script does, and the construction of the data and the significance stars are not formalized.
