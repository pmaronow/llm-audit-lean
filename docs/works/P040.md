# P040: Chocolate Scents and Product Sales: A Randomized Controlled Trial in a Canadian Bookstore and Cafe

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P040.json)

Mary C. McGrath, P. M. Aronow, and Vivien Shotwell (2016). Chocolate Scents and Product Sales: A Randomized Controlled Trial in a Canadian Bookstore and Cafe. SpringerPlus 5:670.

DOI: [10.1186/s40064-016-2303-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40064-016-2303-5)

## Overview

The work reports a randomized trial of chocolate scent and daily sales in a bookstore and café, together with a fifteen-response scent-detection check. The Lean development covers the narrower mathematical tasks of binomial interval calibration and exact arithmetic and reference-distribution calculations for the recorded sales regressions. It establishes corrected binomial confidence sets and the distinction between normal and Student-t p-values, including preservation of the reported lack of significance at the 0.10 threshold.

Areas: Experimental design and estimation, Regression and variance estimation, Probability.

## Key results

For a binomial count from fifteen independent responses with common detection probability $`p\in[0,1]`$, both the tail-inverted equal-tailed confidence set and the one-sided lower confidence set cover $`p`$ with probability at least $`0.95`$.

The thirty-row unadjusted and adjusted regression designs have ranks two and nine, respectively, including rank nine for the original ten-column adjusted design, giving residual degrees of freedom twenty-eight and twenty-one.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed detection interval excludes $`0.80`$ at fourteen detections out of fifteen; under the added binomial model, Lean proves that every deterministic count-based rule doing so has coverage at $`p=0.80`$ at most $`5298209257/6103515625\lt 0.95`$, ruling out an exact uniform finite-sample guarantee for that rule.

[`AronowAudit.P040.method_free_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P040/P040_Binomial.lean#L115) · [P040-T1](#p040-t1)

In place of the printed interval $`(0.81,1.06)`$ and its inference above $`0.80`$, Lean proves that the exact binomial equal-tailed interval for fourteen of fifteen detections has endpoints in $`(0.680515,0.680516)`$ and $`(0.998313,0.998314)`$, while the one-sided 95% lower endpoint lies in $`(0.720603,0.720604)`$, so both corrected sets contain $`0.80`$.

[`AronowAudit.P040.corrected_binomial_endpoints`](../../AronowAudit/P040/P040_Binomial.lean#L488) · [P040-T2](#p040-t2)

The Methods describe normal-reference p-values, but Lean proves that the actual adjusted café and bulk-sales regressions give normal-reference values rounding to $`0.58`$ and $`0.28`$, respectively, rather than the printed $`0.59`$ and $`0.29`$.

[`AronowAudit.P040.recorded_normal_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P040/P040_Regression.lean#L2072) · [P040-T4](#p040-t4)

Replacing the printed normal-reference description with a Student-t reference with twenty-one residual degrees of freedom, Lean proves that the two exact adjusted squared statistics give p-values rounding to the printed $`0.59`$ and $`0.29`$.

## Caveats

- The binomial analysis adds independent responses with a common detection probability, a sampling model not specified by the paper; generalization from the fifteen respondents to patrons is not formalized.
- The coverage obstruction concerns exact uniform finite-sample coverage for deterministic count-based rules, rather than refuting the conventional nominal or asymptotic interpretation of a Wald interval; it does not establish that the true detection probability is below $`0.80`$.
- The regression calculations use the thirty transcribed daily rows as exact rational values, centered time and weekday controls, and HC1 scaling $`N/(N-K)`$; the redundant seventh centered weekday indicator leaves the adjusted rank at nine.
- Normal and Student-t p-values are integrals under reference distributions; no declaration here asserts that the robust statistic has either exact null distribution or that its test has exact size.
- The formal significance result concerns the eight recorded regression statistics and the stated references, rather than establishing an absence of a treatment effect.

### P040-T1

An exact finite-sample coverage obstruction for the printed interval, under an added binomial model

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Results and discussion, third paragraph, p. 3 (PDF p. 3), right column

The Lean statements concern the counterexample of P040-E001. The fifteen responses are represented by the law of the number $`X`$ of detections, with $`\mathbb{P}_p(X = k) = \binom{15}{k} p^k (1 - p)^{15 - k}, \qquad k = 0, \ldots, 15 .`$ This is the model of independent responses with a common detection probability $`p \in [0, 1]`$, which the paper does not state. A rule assigns a set of real numbers to each count, and its coverage at $`p`$ is the probability that the set assigned to $`X`$ contains $`p`$. The main statement asserts that every rule whose set at the count 14 does not contain $`4/5`$ has coverage at $`p = 4/5`$ at most $`1 - \mathbb{P}_{4/5}(X = 14) = 1 - \frac{805306368}{6103515625} = \frac{5298209257}{6103515625} \lt  \frac{19}{20} .`$ A second statement gives the same bound for $`\mathbb{P}_{4/5}(\ell(X) \le 4/5)`$, where $`\ell`$ assigns a real lower limit to each count and $`\ell(14) \gt  4/5`$. A third asserts that $`4/5`$ lies outside the printed interval $`(.81, 1.06)`$ and is less than every number that rounds to $`.81`$. Four statements concern the reconstructed normal-approximation rule $`k/15 \pm z \sqrt{(k/15)(1 - k/15)/15}`$, for every multiplier $`z`$ with $`1.9599 \le z \le 1.9601`$. They assert that the interval contains $`4/5`$ exactly when $`9 \le k \le 13`$, with coverage $`24866193408/30517578125`$ at $`p = 4/5`$, and that its lower limit is at most $`4/5`$ exactly when $`k \le 13`$, with lower-bound coverage $`25417304461/30517578125`$; both coverages are less than $`19/20`$. The lower limit therefore exceeds $`4/5`$ exactly when $`k \ge 14`$, with probability $`5100273664/30517578125`$ at $`p = 4/5`$. Six auxiliary statements give the value of $`\mathbb{P}_{4/5}(X = 14)`$, the bound for a set of counts that does not contain 14, the nonnegativity and the total of the probabilities $`\mathbb{P}_p(X = k)`$, the square of the half-width, and the equivalent squared comparison for interval membership when $`z \ge 0`$. The main statement is broader than a statement about intervals, since the sets of a rule are arbitrary. The declaration named printed_patron_claim_refuted asserts only that no deterministic count-based rule assigning the printed interval $`(.81, 1.06)`$ to count 14 can have coverage at least $`19/20`$ for every $`p \in [0, 1]`$. This is an obstruction to an exact uniform finite-sample 95 % coverage guarantee under the added binomial model; it does not refute the conventional nominal or asymptotic interpretation of the printed interval. No statement asserts that the normal quantile $`1.95996\ldots`$ lies in the stated range of $`z`$, or that the detection probability is $`.80`$ or less.

### P040-T2

The interval of the manipulation check and the inference drawn from it, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Results and discussion, third paragraph, p. 3 (PDF p. 3), right column

The Lean statements concern the corrected statement of P040-E001, under the binomial model of the counterexample, in which the number $`X`$ of detections among the fifteen responses is binomial with parameters $`15`$ and $`p`$. For a count $`k`$, let $`S_k(p) = \mathbb{P}_p(X \ge k)`$ and $`F_k(p) = \mathbb{P}_p(X \le k)`$. The exact equal-tailed 95% confidence set and the exact one-sided 95% lower confidence set are defined by inversion of the tail tests. These sets are $`C(k) = \{ p \in [0, 1] : S_k(p) \ge 1/40 \text{ and } F_k(p) \ge 1/40 \}, \qquad C^{+}(k) = \{ p \in [0, 1] : S_k(p) \ge 1/20 \} .`$ One statement asserts that each of the sets $`C(X)`$ and $`C^{+}(X)`$ contains $`p`$ with probability at least $`19/20`$, for every $`p \in [0, 1]`$, without asserting equality. For the observed count, the statements give $`S_{14}(p) = p^{14} (15 - 14 p)`$ and $`F_{14}(p) = 1 - p^{15}`$. They assert that $`S_{14}`$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $`[0, 1]`$, with derivative $`210 p^{13} (1 - p)`$, and that $`F_{14}`$ is strictly decreasing on $`[0, 1]`$. The main statement asserts that there exist unique $`L`$, $`L_1`$ and $`U`$ in $`[0, 1]`$ such that $`S_{14}(L) = 1/40`$, $`S_{14}(L_1) = 1/20`$ and $`U^{15} = 39/40`$, and that $`C(14) = [L, U], \qquad C^{+}(14) = [L_1, 1], \qquad L \lt  L_1 \lt  4/5 .`$ It places $`L`$, $`U`$ and $`L_1`$ in the intervals $`(.680515, .680516)`$, $`(.998313, .998314)`$ and $`(.720603, .720604)`$ respectively, and it asserts that $`L`$ and $`U`$ round to $`.68`$ and $`1.00`$ at two decimals. Further statements assert that $`L`$ and $`U`$ round to $`.680515`$ and $`.998314`$ at six decimals, and that $`14/15`$ rounds to $`.93`$. Another asserts that $`4/5`$ belongs to $`C(14)`$ and to $`C^{+}(14)`$, with $`S_{14}(4/5) = 5100273664/30517578125`$. The auxiliary statements concern event inclusion, complements and unions; for $`p \in [0, 1]`$ and $`\alpha \ge 0`$, they bound by $`\alpha`$ the total probability of counts with $`S_k(p) \lt  \alpha`$, and separately of counts with $`F_k(p) \lt  \alpha`$. Others give endpoint brackets, existence and uniqueness of bracketed roots, and $`S_{14}(0) = 0`$ and $`S_{14}(1) = 1`$. The two identities for $`S_{14}`$ and $`F_{14}`$, the continuity and the derivative are asserted for every real $`p`$, which is broader than the corrected statement. The last sentence of the corrected passage, on generalization from the surveyed customers to patrons, is not proved.

### P040-T4

A counterexample to “with $`p`$ values computed under a normal approximation”, for the two $`p`$-values printed on p. 5

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 4 (PDF p. 4), left column, first paragraph, last sentence; p. 5 (PDF p. 5), left column, last paragraph; Table 1, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)

The Lean statements concern the counterexample of P040-E003: for the two regressions whose $`p`$-values the paper prints, the $`p`$-value under a normal approximation does not round to the printed number. The 30 daily observations of the replication data, for August 1–4 and August 6–31, are entered as exact rational numbers. The two columns of the unadjusted design matrix are the constant and the treatment indicator $`T`$. The adjusted design matrix has nine columns, which are these two, the centred day number, and six centred indicators of the day of the week, coded as the day number modulo 7 as in the analysis script. For a design matrix $`X`$ with $`K`$ columns and an outcome $`y`$, let $`G = (X^\top X)^{-1} X^\top`$, $`\hat\beta = G y`$ and $`e = y - X \hat\beta`$. The HC1 variance of the coefficient of $`T`$ and the squared ratio of this coefficient to its robust standard error are $`v = \frac{30}{30 - K} \sum_{i = 1}^{30} (G_{T i} \, e_i)^2, \qquad q = \hat\beta_T^2 / v .`$ The $`p`$-value under the normal approximation, $`2 \Phi(-\sqrt{q})`$, is defined by the integral $`p_N(q) = 1 - \sqrt{2/\pi} \int_0^{\sqrt{q}} e^{-u^2/2} \, du .`$ A group of statements carries out the eight regressions of Table 1 in exact arithmetic, from the matrix $`X^\top X`$ and its inverse to the residuals and the variances. For the adjusted regressions, they give $`\hat\beta_T = -146561/16484`$ for café sales and $`\hat\beta_T = 955199/41210`$ for bulk sales, with the exact values of $`v`$ and $`q`$. One statement asserts that the two coefficients and the square roots of the two variances round to the entries $`-8.9`$ $`(16.2)`$ and $`23.2`$ $`(21.2)`$ of Table 1. The statements of the counterexample assert that $`.58198 \lt  p_N(q_{\text{café}}) \lt  .58199, \qquad .27530 \lt  p_N(q_{\text{bulk}}) \lt  .27531 ,`$ so that the two values round to $`.58`$ and $`.28`$ and not to the printed $`.59`$ and $`.29`$. This is asserted for the two values of $`q`$ stated as rational numbers and for the values of $`q`$ computed from the 30 rows, which are equal. The auxiliary statements bound the integral by the integral of a polynomial with an explicit error and assert that $`p_N`$ is nonincreasing for $`q \ge 0`$. The seventh indicator of the day of the week is omitted from the design, since it is a linear combination of the other columns. The 30 rows are taken as given; their agreement with the replication file is not proved. The reading of “normal approximation” as $`p_N`$ is mine, since the paper prints no formula for a $`p`$-value.

The Lean statements concern the corrected sentence of P040-E003, in which the $`p`$-values are computed from a Student-$`t`$ reference distribution with the residual degrees of freedom of the regression. The data, the design matrices, the squared ratio $`q`$ and the $`p`$-value $`p_N`$ under the normal approximation are those of the counterexample. The two-tailed $`p`$-value with $`\nu`$ degrees of freedom is $`p_T(q; \nu) = 1 - 2 \int_0^{\sqrt{q}} f_\nu(t) \, dt, \qquad f_\nu(t) = \frac{\Gamma((\nu + 1)/2)}{\sqrt{\nu \pi} \, \Gamma(\nu/2)} \left(1 + \frac{t^2}{\nu}\right)^{-(\nu + 1)/2} .`$ The first of three parts gives the degrees of freedom: with 30 observations, the unadjusted design has rank 2, and the adjusted design of the analysis script has ten columns and rank 9, with its seven centred indicators of the day of the week summing to zero in every row. The residual degrees of freedom are therefore $`\nu = 28`$ and $`\nu = 21`$. A statement asserts that omitting the seventh indicator preserves the space of fitted values. In the second part, the statements assert that $`.58779 \lt  p_T(q_{\text{café}}; 21) \lt  .58780, \qquad .28766 \lt  p_T(q_{\text{bulk}}; 21) \lt  .28767 .`$ These two values round to the printed $`.59`$ and $`.29`$. The third part concerns the sentence on p. 4 that no estimate of a treatment effect is significant at the two-tailed $`.10`$ level. For each of the eight regressions of Table 1, the statements give $`q`$ as an exact rational number, equal to the value computed from the 30 rows, and assert that $`p_T(q; \nu) \gt  1/10`$, with $`\nu = 28`$ or $`\nu = 21`$ as appropriate, and $`p_N(q) \gt  1/10`$. The smallest values are those of unadjusted bulk sales, for which $`.22533 \lt  p_T(q; 28) \lt  .22534`$ and $`.21504 \lt  p_N(q) \lt  .21505`$. The alternative correction, which keeps the normal approximation and prints $`.58`$ and $`.28`$, is the content of the statements of the counterexample. The auxiliary statements give the day mean and weekday frequencies and bounds on the eight statistics, and assert that $`p_N`$ and $`p_T`$ are nonincreasing in $`q \ge 0`$, for each fixed $`\nu \gt  0`$ in the Student-$`t`$ case. They also assert continuity and nonnegativity of $`f_\nu`$ for $`\nu \gt  0`$ and give the explicit forms of $`f_{21}`$ and $`f_{28}`$ and polynomial bounds for these two densities and their integrals. The Student-$`t`$ distribution is a reference distribution only: no statement asserts that the ratio of an estimate to its robust standard error has this distribution under the null hypothesis. The number of observations is the 30 of the replication data, and not the 31 experimental days of the Methods.
