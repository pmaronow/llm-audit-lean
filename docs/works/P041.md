# P041: Editorial Bias in Crowd-Sourced Political Information

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P041.json)

Joshua L. Kalla and P. M. Aronow (2015). Editorial Bias in Crowd-Sourced Political Information. PLOS ONE 10(9):e0136327.

DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0136327](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0136327)

## Overview

The work reports randomized Wikipedia edits and compares their removal and survival across valence, citation status and senator groups. The Lean results concern exact arithmetic from stipulated counts and rounded regression summaries, algebra of OLS and hazard-ratio reparameterization, and hypothetical countermodels for interpretation and adaptive implementation. They do not reproduce the empirical data processing or establish the actual magnitude of implementation bias or the population effect on former-senator pages.

Areas: Experimental design and estimation, Regression and variance estimation, Causal identification.

## Key results

For a uniform balanced assignment of citation status to $`2m`$ ranks with $`m\ge1`$, if each outcome depends only on rank and its own citation status, the expected cited-minus-uncited contrast equals the average direct effect over ranks.

[`AronowAudit.P041.random_order_without_interference`](../../AronowAudit/P041/P041_Order.lean#L234) · [P041-T6](#p041-t6)

For a full-rank finite OLS design and an intercept-representing vector $`e_1`$ satisfying $`Xe_1=1`$, complementing the outcome changes its coefficient vector from $`\widehat\beta`$ to $`e_1-\widehat\beta`$.

[`AronowAudit.P041.ols_complement_coefficients`](../../AronowAudit/P041/P041_OLS.lean#L40) · [P041-T9](#p041-t9)

Under the conversion from first-election year to tenure, the per-year hazard-ratio multiplier becomes its reciprocal, with the corresponding sign reversal on the log scale.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed 36%, 29% and 64% are rounded symmetric percentage differences, whereas the directional relative increases from the stipulated removal counts round to 44%, 33% and 94%, respectively.

[`AronowAudit.P041.risk_percentages_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P041/P041_Risks.lean#L30) · [P041-T3](#p041-t3)

The interpretation that smaller former-senator contrasts confine relative bias to active politicians is contradicted by two explicit exponential-survival countermodels with the same nonnull positive-to-negative hazard ratio $`2/3`$, 12-hour absolute contrasts differing by a factor greater than 13, and positive binomial expressions at the recorded counts.

[`AronowAudit.P041.common_bias_countermodel`](../../AronowAudit/P041/P041_Hazards.lean#L99) · [P041-T1](#p041-t1)

In place of interpreting the former-senator nondetection as evidence of confinement, the independent-sample comparison reconstructed from the four rounded hazard-ratio and standard-error entries has Wald statistic strictly between 0.18 and 0.21 throughout their rounding intervals.

[`AronowAudit.P041.corrected_between_relative_effects`](../../AronowAudit/P041/P041_Wald.lean#L448) · [P041-T2](#p041-t2)

The assurance that random edit order alone protects comparisons after adaptive implementation changes fails in a hypothetical balanced four-edit scrutiny system, whose mean cited-minus-uncited contrast is $`1/6`$ under the accelerated rule and zero under the slow rule.

[`AronowAudit.P041.random_order_block_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P041/P041_Order.lean#L94) · [P041-T5](#p041-t5)

The printed six-hour citation standard error 0.645 is replaced by 0.064: the square root of the stipulated HC1 variance $`2047/492500`$ lies in $`[0.0635,0.0645)`$ and its coefficient-to-standard-error ratio exceeds 4.89.

[`AronowAudit.P041.corrected_citation_standard_error`](../../AronowAudit/P041/P041_OLS.lean#L205) · [P041-T7](#p041-t7)

S11 describes its outcome as removal, but the stipulated exact survival contrasts agree with all twelve printed coefficients within 0.0005, while the corresponding removal contrasts have the opposite signs and fail those enclosures.

[`AronowAudit.P041.s11_survival_slopes_and_not_removal`](../../AronowAudit/P041/P041_OLS.lean#L238) · [P041-T9](#p041-t9)

## Caveats

- Counts, finite regression sufficient statistics and their links to physical time horizons are stipulated inputs; Lean does not verify extraction from the deposited observations or repair timestamps.
- The two exponential-survival examples and the scrutiny response are hypothetical mathematical countermodels; they establish failures of the stated implications without fitting the actual former-senator hazards or estimating actual protocol bias.
- The hazard comparison treats the two samples as independent and uses the reconstructed log-scale standard error s/h, with each printed input in a closed rounding interval of radius 0.0005; the bound alone neither proves population equality nor evaluates a normal tail.
- The 44% twelve-hour calculation uses 75 negative removals as recorded in its target; the separate timestamp-repair alternative of 76 is not substituted.
- The tenure identity changes first-election year to tenure by $`L=C-Y`$ and is algebraic, rather than a new Cox refit; interpreting the fitted models requires the accompanying baseline and main-effect reparameterization.
- The OLS complement identity reverses every non-intercept slope when the intercept-representing vector is the first coordinate vector; the repeated former-senator rows in S11 use the same stipulated counts.

### P041-T1

A counterexample to the conclusion, in “Replication on Wikipedia Pages of Dead and Retired Senators” and in the Discussion, that the editorial bias is limited to active politicians

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** “Replication on Wikipedia Pages of Dead and Retired Senators”, last two sentences, p. 5 (PDF p. 5); Discussion, first paragraph, p. 5 (PDF p. 5)

The Lean statements cover the example of P041-E006, which is mine and is not in the article. For $`0\lt q\lt 1`$, positive facts are removed at the constant hazard $`\lambda_+=-2\log(q)/12`$ per hour and negative facts at $`\lambda_-=-3\log(q)/12`$. A fact survives $`t\ge0`$ hours with probability $`e^{-\lambda_\pm t}`$. The pages of sitting senators have $`q=4/5`$ and those of former senators $`q=99/100`$. The statements assert that $`\lambda_+/\lambda_-=2/3`$ for every $`q`$ in $`(0,1)`$, and that the probabilities of surviving 12 hours are $`q^2`$ and $`q^3`$, with difference $`q^2(1-q)`$. At the two values of $`q`$, they assert that the differences are $`16/125`$ and $`9801/1000000`$, with ratio $`128000/9801\gt 13`$, and that the hazard ratio is $`2/3`$ in both populations. They assert also that the binomial expression $`\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}`$ is positive at the four counts of facts removed within 12 hours in the replication data (75 and 52 of 150 for sitting senators, 1 of 76 and 3 of 75 for former senators), with $`p`$ the probability of removal in the example. No random removal times are constructed; interpreting the binomial expression as a count probability assumes independent removals, which are not asserted. The survival identities extend algebraically to every $`q\gt 0`$ and every real $`t`$, giving $`q^{t/6}`$ and $`q^{t/4}`$, even when these values exceed $`1`$. Positivity holds for every $`0\lt p\lt 1`$ and $`k\le n`$, and nothing is asserted about the hazard ratio on the actual pages of former senators.
