## Overview

This chapter reviews exposure mappings, Horvitz–Thompson and Hajek estimators, two-stage designs under partial interference, graph clustering, and contagion decompositions. The Lean development proves exact-probability exposure-estimation identities, the structural-zero variance surplus, corrected graph-net construction, and the household contagion decomposition. It also supplies finite counterexamples and repairs for Bernoulli arm ratios, singleton-arm variance calculations, exposure coding, missing-tie bias, and printed estimator decompositions; a separate example tests a stronger finite-simulation variance guarantee rather than refuting the chapter’s contextual exact-probability result.

Areas: Interference and networks, Experimental design and estimation, Randomization inference.

## Key results

For any finite assignment design and correctly specified finite exposure mapping, positive exact exposure probabilities give unbiased Horvitz–Thompson totals and, when both exposures have positive probabilities for every unit, unbiased population exposure contrasts.

[`AronowAudit.P045.ht_unbiased_headline`](../../AronowAudit/P045/P045_Spillover.lean#L311) · [P045-T30](#p045-t30)

For two distinct supported exposures under a finite design, the expectation of the adjusted HT contrast variance estimator minus the true randomization variance equals the structural-zero square surplus computed from exact joint exposure probabilities.

[`AronowAudit.P045.ExposureVariance.exact_variance_surplus`](../../AronowAudit/P045/P045_ExposureVariance.lean#L551) · [P045-T33](#p045-t33)

For every finite undirected graph and integer $`\epsilon\ge1`$, a corrected net with pairwise centre distances at least $`\epsilon`$ and coverage within $`\epsilon-1`$ hops exists, and valid completed greedy runs produce such nets.

[`AronowAudit.P045.epsilon_net_correction`](../../AronowAudit/P045/P045_Spillover.lean#L3194) · [P045-T21](#p045-t21)

For a positive finite population of households with a binary mediator and real recipient outcomes, the indirect effect equals contagion at treatment one plus other mechanisms at zero, and also contagion at zero plus other mechanisms at one.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The printed Bernoulli within-arm ratio unbiasedness claim fails under interference: in the explicit two-group four-member construction, both marginal direct-effect targets are zero but every defined direct estimate is $`-1`$.

[`AronowAudit.P045.bernoulli_ratio_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P045/P045_Spillover.lean#L823) · [P045-T1](#p045-t1)

The toy variance calculation need not be unbiased under additive effects after singleton-arm terms are dropped: in the six-group three-member construction, the true direct-estimator variance is $`1/2`$ and the expected computed variance is $`1/3`$ for either treatment count.

[`AronowAudit.P045.singleton_variance_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P045/P045_Spillover.lean#L1853) · [P045-T3](#p045-t3)

The package’s complement of all-treated is not its documented all-control exposure: in a two-unit fair-assignment linear-outcome example, the coded HT contrast has expectation $`4/3`$ instead of the all-versus-none effect $`2`$, while the corrected all-control event gives $`2`$.

[`AronowAudit.P045.full_neighborhood_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P045/P045_Spillover.lean#L2018) · [P045-T5](#p045-t5)

The printed claim that bias increases with the proportion of mismeasured ties is not universal: on the specified ten-unit network with positive spillovers and one treated unit, absolute Hajek bias strictly decreases as zero, one, and two undirected ties are uniformly deleted.

[`AronowAudit.P045.nonmonotone_hajek_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P045/P045_Spillover.lean#L2769) · [P045-T8](#p045-t8)

The printed equality replacing own-treatment denominators by exposure-specific denominators fails on every assignment of a two-treated ten-cycle example, with the two sides having expectations $`11/9`$ and $`3`$.

[`AronowAudit.P045.naive_denominator_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P045/P045_Spillover.lean#L3345) · [P045-T12](#p045-t12)

The printed sum of two subclass means differs from the required pooled mean: an explicit 450-unit tract construction yields treated and control pooled means $`13/6`$ and $`4/3`$, versus $`17/4`$ and $`21/8`$ from the printed expression.

## Caveats

- The exposure HT and variance results assume fixed real exposure-specific potential outcomes, a correctly specified mapping, positive exact marginals for the exposures concerned, and exact joint probabilities; an empty realized exposure cell contributes zero to an HT total and does not license dropping assignments.
- The structural-zero surplus uses ordered pairs, including diagonal pairs in the cross-exposure term; its nonnegative squares explain conservativeness in expectation, rather than a pointwise upper bound on variance.
- The separate twelve-unit three-clique example shows that substituting probabilities from 65 of 66 assignments need not preserve an exact finite-simulation conservativeness guarantee; it does not refute the chapter’s exact-probability theorem or supply a general Monte Carlo approximation theorem.
- The singleton-arm and corrected direct-variance results use independent within-group assignments across groups and independent of the uniform first-stage selection. The singleton-arm result concerns the coded rule that drops undefined sample-variance terms; the corrected direct-variance domain requires at least two observations in each arm and is unavailable in the three-member toy groups.
- The selected code-based witnesses assume the stated mapping or estimator formula; agreement with package source is separate from these Lean statements, and a coding example alone does not refute a numerical simulation table.
- The missing-tie witness averages over genuine Hajek ratios with all relevant exposure cells nonempty; the contagion result is an algebraic decomposition and does not establish identification of its components from observed data.

### P045-T2

The unbiasedness statements of Section 16.5.1 for the estimators of the marginal effects, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Section 16.5.1, p. 310 (PDF p. 22)

The Lean statements cover the parts of the corrected passage in P045-E001. A group has $`n\ge1`$ units, and the outcome of a unit is an arbitrary real function of the assignment of its group, so that only partial interference is imposed. Let $`y_{ig}(z;p)`$ be the expected outcome of unit $`i`$ given $`Z_{ig}=z`$ when the units are treated independently with probability $`p\in(0,1)`$. The first part asserts that $`\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{I}(Z_{ig}=z)\,Y_{ig}]=p^{z}(1-p)^{1-z}\,y_{ig}(z;p)`$, and that the Horvitz–Thompson mean of the correction has expectation $`n^{-1}\sum_{i}y_{ig}(z;p)`$ over all assignments. The second part concerns one saturation, where a set of $`C`$ of the $`G`$ groups is chosen uniformly, with $`1\le C\le G`$, and the assignment within each group has an arbitrary distribution. The within-group assignments are independent across groups and independent of the first-stage selection. It asserts that the mean of a group statistic over the chosen groups has expectation equal to the average over all $`G`$ groups of the expectations of that statistic. The third part asserts that, under complete random assignment with $`k`$ treated units, $`0\lt k\lt n`$, the arm mean $`\widehat{y}_g(z;\psi)`$ is unbiased for the average over units of the expected outcome given $`Z_{ig}=z`$ under that design. The fourth part asserts that the mean outcome of all units in a group has expectation $`n^{-1}\sum_i\mathbb{E}[y_{ig}(\mathbf{Z}_g)]`$ under every distribution of the assignment. For the four-unit groups of the counterexample, the statements assert that both Horvitz–Thompson means have expectation $`3p`$. They assert also that, with exactly $`k\in\{1,2,3\}`$ treated units, the direct effect defined for that design is $`-1`$, while it is $`0`$ under Bernoulli assignment with probability $`k/4`$. One statement joins these parts, and six supporting statements give single parts and the formulas for the two Horvitz–Thompson means in the counterexample. The Lean statements do not assert the unbiasedness of the direct, indirect, total and overall contrasts separately. It follows from the parts above by linearity of the expectation.

### P045-T4

The property of the variance estimator for a direct effect, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** Section 16.5.1, p. 310 (PDF p. 22); toy example, pp. 310–311 (PDF pp. 22–23)

The Lean statements follow the derivation of the bias in P045-N008, at one saturation. A set of $`C`$ of the $`G`$ groups is chosen uniformly, with $`2\le C\le G`$, and in group $`g`$, of size $`n_g`$, a uniformly chosen set of $`m_g`$ units is treated. The within-group assignments are independent across groups and independent of the first-stage selection. The potential outcomes under treatment and under control are arbitrary real numbers. Let $`\theta_g`$ be the difference of their means in group $`g`$, let $`S^2_\theta`$ be the variance of the $`\theta_g`$ with divisor $`G-1`$, and let $`S^2_{\delta,g}`$ be the variance of the unit effects in group $`g`$ with divisor $`n_g-1`$. The main statement assumes that $`2\le m_g\le n_g-2`$ for every group. It asserts the formula of the finding for the variance $`V_g`$ of the difference in means in group $`g`$. It asserts that the estimator $`\widehat\tau`$ of the direct effect is unbiased for the average of the $`\theta_g`$, and that the variance estimator $`\widehat V`$ satisfies $`\mathrm{Var}(\widehat\tau)=\frac{1-C/G}{C}\,S^2_\theta+\frac{1}{CG}\sum_{g=1}^{G}V_g,\qquad \mathbb{E}[\widehat V]-\mathrm{Var}(\widehat\tau)=\frac{1}{G^2}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{S^2_{\delta,g}}{n_g}\ \ge\ 0 ,`$ with equality in the last relation if and only if the unit effects are constant within every group. A second statement asserts the two formulas of the finding for the bias when every group has three units and the term of an arm with one unit is omitted, for $`m_g=2`$ and for $`m_g=1`$. A third specializes them to the multipliers of p. 310. With $`S^2_{x,g}`$ the variance of the baseline outcomes in group $`g`$, it asserts that the biases are $`-(11/8)\,G^{-2}\sum_gS^2_{x,g}`$ and $`-(13/6)\,G^{-2}\sum_gS^2_{x,g}`$, and that both are negative when the baseline outcomes vary within some group. Among seven supporting statements, one asserts that, in the toy design, the estimator that requires both sample variances of every group is undefined on every assignment, whatever the outcomes. The variance estimator is the formula that the function estimates_hierarchical of the package computes, since the chapter does not print one.

### P045-T7

A counterexample to the package’s functions for removing and adding ties (Section 16.4.4, “a network that is randomly missing varying proportions of ties”)

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Section 16.4.4, p. 303 (PDF p. 15), and Figure 16.5, p. 304 (PDF p. 16); R package interference, file R/functions.R, lines 36 and 55

The Lean statements formalize the counterexample for the code in P045-E012, which is mine and is not in the chapter. The cells above the diagonal and the cells below it are listed column by column. The last line of both functions is represented by the map that gives the $`k`$th cell below the diagonal the value of the $`k`$th cell above it. The main statement asserts that, for a matrix with four rows, the cells above the diagonal are listed as $`(1,2),(1,3),(2,3),(1,4),(2,4),(3,4)`$ and those below as $`(2,1),(3,1),(4,1),(3,2),(4,2),(4,3)`$. ### P045-T32

A counterexample to an exact finite-simulation extension of the nonnegative-bias guarantee in Section 16.4.2

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Section 16.4.2, pp. 298–299 (PDF pp. 10–11); the exposure mapping on p. 294 (PDF p. 6)

The Lean statements formalize the finite-simulation counterexample of P045-N016, which is mine and is not in the chapter. It concerns a universal finite-simulation extension, rather than the chapter’s contextual exact-probability guarantee or its use of sufficiently accurate approximation. Twelve units form three disjoint cliques of four, two units are treated by complete randomization, all potential outcomes equal $`1`$, and the contrast is $`(d_{01},d_{00})`$. The exposure probabilities are simulated from 65 of the 66 assignments, and the omitted assignment is uniform among the 66. For counts in the retained bank, the marginal input is $`(c_i(d)+1)/66`$, the same-exposure joint input is $`(c_{ij}(d,d)+\mathbf{1}\{i=j\})/66`$, and the cross-exposure joint input is $`c_{ij}(d,e)/65`$ for $`d\ne e`$. The main statement first asserts the hypotheses of the example. The 66 assignments are exactly those with two treated units, the network is symmetric and has no isolates, and every unit has $`\pi_i(d_{01})=9/22`$ and $`\pi_i(d_{00})=14/33`$. With the exact probabilities, it asserts that the Horvitz–Thompson estimator of the contrast has mean $`0`$, and that its variance and the expectation of its variance estimator both equal $`6050/11907`$. With the simulated probabilities, it asserts that the expectation of the variance estimator minus the variance of the estimator equals $`-\frac{953130413}{23499020160}\qquad\text{and}\qquad-\frac{20161943}{522324075}`$ when the realized assignment is uniform among all 66 assignments, independently of the simulated set, and when it is uniform among the 65 simulated assignments, respectively. Here the mean and the variance are over the omitted assignment and the realized one together. A second statement fixes the set of simulated assignments and takes the realized assignment uniformly from all 66 assignments. It asserts that the expectation of the variance estimator is below the variance for each of the 66 sets, by $`1551761941/23499020160`$ when the omitted assignment treats two units of one clique and by $`72513467/2349902016`$ otherwise. Ten further statements give the counts of assignments behind the probabilities, the two distributions of the pair of assignments, the values with exact probabilities, and the relabeling of the units that reduces the 66 sets to two. The computations are in rational arithmetic, and one of the ten asserts the two inequalities for the same quantities over the real numbers. The estimator, the variance estimator and the formulas for the simulated probabilities are those in the two examined versions of the R package interference; the chapter does not print these formulas. That the package computes them is a fact about its source and is not part of the Lean statements. This example does not test the chapter’s $`R=10{,}000`$ simulations or its maximum with the constant-effects variance estimator.

### P045-T33

The guarantee of nonnegative bias for the variance estimators of Section 16.4.2, with its exact-probability scope explicit

**Kind:** `result_touched_by_finding`

**Location:** Section 16.4.2, pp. 298–299 (PDF pp. 10–11)

The Lean statements are the identity and the inequality for a contrast in the derivation of P045-N016, making the chapter’s contextual exact-probability result explicit. The design is a probability distribution on a finite set, and the exposure of each of the $`N\ge1`$ units is a function of the outcome of the randomization. The potential outcomes $`y_i(d)`$ are fixed real numbers. Let $`\pi_i`$ and $`\pi_{ij}`$ be the exact marginal and joint exposure probabilities, and let $`d_k\neq d_l`$ be two exposures such that $`\pi_i(d_k)\gt 0`$ and $`\pi_i(d_l)\gt 0`$ for every unit $`i`$. The estimator $`\widehat{\tau}_{HT}(d_k,d_l)`$ and its variance estimator are computed with these probabilities, by the formulas displayed in that clarification. The first statement asserts that the expectation of the variance estimator minus $`\mathrm{Var}\,[\widehat{\tau}_{HT}(d_k,d_l)]`$ equals $`\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2N^2}\sum_{i\neq j:\,\pi_{ij}(d_k,d_k)=0}\bigl(y_i(d_k)+y_j(d_k)\bigr)^2+\frac{1}{2N^2}\sum_{i\neq j:\,\pi_{ij}(d_l,d_l)=0}\bigl(y_i(d_l)+y_j(d_l)\bigr)^2\\ &\qquad+\frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{i,j:\,\pi_{ij}(d_k,d_l)=0}\bigl(y_i(d_k)-y_j(d_l)\bigr)^2 , \end{aligned}`$ where the last sum is over all ordered pairs, including those with $`i=j`$. The second asserts that $`\mathrm{Var}\,[\widehat{\tau}_{HT}(d_k,d_l)]`$ is at most the expectation of its variance estimator. A third asserts that $`\widehat{\tau}_{HT}(d_k,d_l)`$ is unbiased for $`\tau(d_k,d_l)`$ under the same hypotheses. The statements are broader than the clarification in one respect. The exposure of a unit may be any function of the outcome of the randomization, and it need not come from a network. They are narrower in another. The inequality in the clarification for the variance estimator of a single total, $`\widehat{y^T}_{HT}(d_k)`$, is not stated as an inequality between an expectation and a variance. The three listed statements concern the contrast and its unbiasedness; no separate variance bound for a single total is listed here. The clarification also distinguishes sufficiently accurate Monte Carlo approximation from an exact finite-simulation guarantee. These statements contain no theorem about Monte Carlo approximation; the separate counterexample concerns only an exact finite-simulation extension.
