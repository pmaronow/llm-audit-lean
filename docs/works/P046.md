# P046: Nonparametric identification is not enough, but randomized controlled trials are

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P046.json)

P. M. Aronow, James M. Robins, Theo Saarinen, Fredrik Sävje, and Jasjeet Sekhon (2025). Nonparametric identification is not enough, but randomized controlled trials are. Observational Studies 11(1):3–16.

DOI: [10.1353/obs.2025.a956837](https://doi.org/10.1353/obs.2025.a956837)

arXiv: [2108.11342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11342)

## Overview

The work contrasts nonparametric identification with uniform estimation and studies Horvitz–Thompson and cross-fitted augmented estimators when propensity scores are known. The Lean development covers an observational uniform-inconsistency statement, exact unbiasedness, common-positivity root-n control, and fixed-law normal limits under explicit nuisance-fit assumptions. It also supplies continuous-covariate counterexamples to the printed confidence interval and regression-efficiency claim, with widened-interval and benchmark-qualified corrections. The efficiency-related normal limit is proved at the numerical Hahn benchmark without a formal semiparametric lower-bound theorem.

Areas: Causal identification, Asymptotic statistics, Regression and variance estimation.

## Key results

For every positive i.i.d. sample size under the stated continuous-covariate, binary-outcome, consistency, unconfoundedness and strict-positivity model, the Horvitz–Thompson estimator using the known propensity score is unbiased for $`\mu=\mathrm{E}[Y(1)]`$.

[`AronowAudit.P046.ht_unbiased`](../../AronowAudit/P046/P046_Audit.lean#L931) · [P046-T7](#p046-t7)

With known propensity scores, Horvitz–Thompson estimation is uniformly root-n tight over valid laws sharing a common positive positivity constant and, for each fixed valid law, has a centered Gaussian limit with variance $`\mathrm{E}[m(W)/\pi(W)]-\mu^2`$.

[`AronowAudit.P046.proposition2_asymptotic`](../../AronowAudit/P046/P046_Audit.lean#L1917) · [P046-T7](#p046-t7)

For every positive sample size and deterministic two-fold partition, the augmented estimator is unbiased when each fit is a measurable function of the opposite fold and every evaluated prediction is integrable, even if the working regression is misspecified.

[`AronowAudit.P046.Wave1.proposition4a_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P046/Wave1/Gap/GP046B4.lean#L216) · [P046-T8](#p046-t8)

For a fixed valid study and asymptotically balanced folds, almost-surely square-integrable cross-fits whose integrated squared prediction errors converge to zero in training-sample expectation around deterministic square-integrable limits yield a root-n-tight augmented estimator.

[`AronowAudit.P046.Wave1.proposition4b_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P046/Wave1/Gap/GP046B4.lean#L579) · [P046-T9](#p046-t9)

For a fixed valid study with asymptotically balanced folds and almost-surely square-integrable cross-fits converging in $`L^2(F_W)`$ in probability to $`m`$, the augmented estimator has centered Gaussian limit variance $`B_H=\mathrm{E}[m(W)(1-m(W))/\pi(W)]+\mathrm{Var}(m(W))`$.

[`AronowAudit.P046.Wave1.proposition4d_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P046/Wave1/Gap/GP046B5.lean#L636) · [P046-T9](#p046-t9)

For every measurable estimator based only on observed data, there are fixed positive error and probability thresholds such that arbitrarily large positive sample sizes admit a valid observational law, with unknown propensity and a law-specific positivity bound, whose probability of exceeding the error threshold is at least the probability threshold.

## Corrections and counterexamples

Printed Proposition 3 claims finite-sample $`1-\alpha`$ coverage for $`\hat\mu\pm\sqrt{\xi\log(2/\alpha)/(2n)}`$; a valid continuous-covariate study with $`n=100`$, $`\mu=3/5`$ and $`\alpha=10^{-7}`$ instead has exact strict noncoverage probability greater than $`119/10^9\gt \alpha`$, with exactly 1946 failing count states.

[`AronowAudit.P046.Wave1.counterexample_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P046/Wave1/Gap/GP046B1.lean#L544) · [P046-T1](#p046-t1)

The proof's population-centered conditional Hoeffding claim fails in a valid study with $`\mu=1/2`$: on a covariate event of probability $`2^{-100}`$, the conditional tail probability for the printed radius at $`\alpha=1/20`$ is one almost everywhere, exceeding $`1/20`$.

[`AronowAudit.P046.ConditionalExample.counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P046/P046_Audit.lean#L1685) · [P046-T2](#p046-t2)

Printed Proposition 3 uses radius $`\sqrt{\xi\log(2/\alpha)/(2n)}`$ for the population mean; replacing it by $`(\sqrt{\xi}+1)\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)/(2n)}`$, where $`\xi=n^{-1}\sum_i\pi(W_i)^{-2}`$, gives population-centered tail probability at most $`\alpha`$ for every positive sample size and $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$ under the standing assumptions.

[`AronowAudit.P046.Wave1.proposition3_corrected_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P046/Wave1/Gap/GP046B2.lean#L679) · [P046-T3](#p046-t3)

Printed Proposition 4(c) claims that fits with finitely many parameters obtained by empirical weighted-risk minimization are at least as asymptotically efficient as Horvitz–Thompson; a valid continuous-covariate study with balanced opposite-fold empirical minimizers and convergent predictions instead has adjusted and Horvitz–Thompson variances $`169/(400n)`$ and $`79/(400n)`$, with Gaussian limits having the corresponding variance constants.

[`AronowAudit.P046.Wave1.regression_counterexample_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P046/Wave1/Gap/RepairT4.lean#L414) · [P046-T4](#p046-t4)

Printed Proposition 4(c) compares empirical weighted-risk fits with Horvitz–Thompson without requiring a zero benchmark or convergence to a population risk minimizer. The corrected theorem gives a Gaussian limit with variance no larger than Horvitz–Thompson for a fixed square-integrable family with finitely many real parameters, zero in the family or its weighted $`L^2`$ closure, and measurable opposite-fold empirical weighted-risk minimizers converging in $`L^2(F_W)`$ in probability to one deterministic population risk minimizer, with asymptotically balanced folds and almost-sure fitted $`L^2`$ membership.

[`AronowAudit.P046.Wave1.proposition4c_corrected_proof`](../../AronowAudit/P046/Wave1/Gap/GP046B3b.lean#L262) · [P046-T5](#p046-t5)

## Caveats

- The targets concern the 2025 published version. [arXiv:2108.11342v2 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11342v2) is an earlier preprint: its Proposition 3 uses radius $`\sqrt{\log(2/\alpha)/(2n\delta^2)}`$ rather than the published random-$`\xi`$ radius, and its Proposition 4 has no part (d).
- The estimand is the population treated potential-outcome mean $`\mu=\mathrm{E}[Y(1)]`$; probability is over i.i.d. superpopulation samples, with an atomless real covariate, binary treatment and potential outcomes, consistency, separate conditional independence for each potential outcome, and propensities bounded strictly between $`\delta`$ and $`1-\delta`$.
- Uniform root-n control for known propensities fixes one common $`\delta\gt 0`$ before varying the law; the observational impossibility statement instead ranges over the union of laws with law-specific positive bounds, and its proof does not establish the stronger common-delta impossibility claim.
- Cross-fits are jointly measurable training-data functions of the opposite deterministic fold; the finite unbiasedness theorem requires integrable evaluated predictions, while the selected asymptotic theorems retain almost-sure fitted $`L^2`$ membership and fold proportions tending to one half.
- Part 4(b) uses convergence of expected integrated squared error, including all-sample-size integrability, to deterministic limits that may differ across folds; part 4(d) instead uses $`L^2`$ convergence in probability to the true regression and imposes no nuisance convergence rate.
- The normal limit at $`B_H`$ does not prove a canonical-gradient or semiparametric lower-bound result, local regularity, or optimality for the smaller model with known covariate distribution.
- The interval counterexample establishes unconditional undercoverage at $`\alpha=10^{-7}`$; the separate $`\alpha=0.05`$ example refutes a conditional proof step and does not establish unconditional 95% undercoverage.
- The corrected efficiency comparison uses population risk $`R(g)=\mathrm{E}[(1-\pi(W))(Y(1)-g(W))^2/\pi(W)]`$ and empirical criterion $`\sum_{i\in T}X_i(1-\pi(W_i))(Y_i-g(W_i))^2/\pi(W_i)^2`$ on each opposite fold; it assumes empirical minimization, zero in the working family or its corresponding weighted $`L^2`$ closure, and convergence to a population risk minimizer.
- Uniform generators use Lebesgue measure on $`[0,1]`$, whose endpoint convention agrees almost surely with the informal open-interval uniform law; the finite count certificates are connected to the actual continuous sampling law.

### P046-T2

A counterexample to the conditional display in the proof of Proposition 3, as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** Appendix A, p. 16 (PDF p. 15)

The Lean statements concern the conditional display in the proof, which bounds by $`\alpha`$ the probability, given $`\mathbf{W}`$, that $`|\hat\mu-\mu|`$ is at least the printed radius (see P046-E001). The conditional mean is $`n^{-1}\sum_i\mathrm{E}[Y_i(1)\mid W_i]`$, whereas $`\mu=\mathrm{E}[Y_i(1)]`$ is the unconditional mean. They use a simpler design than the finding does: $`n=100`$, $`\alpha=1/20`$, $`W_i`$ is uniform on $`(0,1)`$, $`\pi(w)=9/10`$ for every $`w`$, $`Y_i(1)=\mathbf{1}\{W_i\ge1/2\}`$ and $`Y_i(0)=0`$, so that $`\mu=1/2`$ and $`\xi=100/81`$. The main statement asserts that this design satisfies the assumptions of Section 2, that $`\mu=1/2`$, that the event $`E=\{W_i\lt 1/2\text{ for every }i\}`$ has probability $`2^{-100}`$, and that almost surely on $`E`$ $`\Pr\Bigl(|\hat\mu-\mu|\ge\sqrt{\xi\log(2/\alpha)/(2n)}\Bigm|\mathbf{W}\Bigr)=1\gt \frac{1}{20},`$ where the conditional probability is the conditional expectation of the indicator of the event given the covariate vector. Of the eighteen supporting statements, five are arithmetic: $`1/20\lt 9/10\lt 19/20`$, the bounds $`\log(40)/162\lt 1/4`$ and $`\sqrt{\log(40)/162}\lt 1/2`$ on the printed radius, $`2^{-100}\gt 0`$, and $`2\exp(-\log40)=1/20`$ for the right-hand side of the printed display. Eleven concern the design, in which the uniform distribution on $`(0,1)`$ is Lebesgue measure on $`[0,1]`$. They include the law of $`W_i`$, the values $`\mu=1/2`$ and $`\xi=100/81`$, the probability $`2^{-100}`$ of $`E`$, the assumptions of Section 2, and the conditional probability in the display. The other two assert in general that independence is preserved under a measurable map, and conditional independence under an almost sure change of one of the variables. The statements concern one step of the proof, and they do not assert that the printed interval undercovers at this level. The propensity score of this design is constant, and the printed interval is then valid.

### P046-T3

Proposition 3, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 7 (PDF p. 6); proof in Appendix A, pp. 15–16 (PDF pp. 14–15)

The main Lean statement is the corrected proposition of P046-E001: for every population that satisfies the assumptions of Section 2, every $`n\ge1`$ and every $`0\lt \alpha\lt 1`$, $`\Pr\Bigl(|\hat\mu-\mu|\ge(\sqrt{\xi}+1)\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)/(2n)}\Bigr)\le\alpha.`$ A population is represented by a probability law for one unit $`(W,X,Y(0),Y(1))`$, with $`W`$ real and without atoms and the other three variables binary, together with the functions $`\pi`$ and $`m(w)=\mathrm{E}[Y_i(1)\mid W_i=w]`$ and the constant $`\delta`$ of the positivity condition. The sample consists of $`n`$ independent units. Ten supporting statements follow the corrected proof, with $`\mu_{\mathbf{W}}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n m(W_i)`$. Three give the centring identities $`\mathrm{E}[\hat\mu\mid\mathbf{W}]=\mu_{\mathbf{W}}`$ almost surely, $`\mathrm{E}[m(W_i)]=\mu`$ and $`\mathrm{E}[\mu_{\mathbf{W}}]=\mu`$. Three give Hoeffding bounds for every $`t\ge0`$: the two bounds of the proof, $`\Pr\bigl(|\hat\mu-\mu_{\mathbf{W}}|\ge t\bigm|\mathbf{W}\bigr)\le2\exp(-2nt^2/\xi)\ \text{ almost surely},\qquad\Pr\bigl(|\mu_{\mathbf{W}}-\mu|\ge t\bigr)\le2\exp(-2nt^2),`$ and the bound $`2\exp(-2nt^2/b^2)`$ for the deviation from its expectation of the mean of $`n`$ independent copies of a measurable variable with values almost surely in $`[0,b]`$, where $`b\gt 0`$. One gives the union bound for two error events of probability at most $`\alpha/2`$ each, and it is written for an arbitrary probability space, which is broader than the proof needs. Two bound the width: $`\sqrt{\xi}\lt \delta^{-1}`$ when every score exceeds $`\delta`$, and almost surely the radius is less than $`(\delta^{-1}+1)\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)/(2n)}`$ and the width is at most $`C/\sqrt{n}`$, where $`C`$ depends on the population and on $`\alpha`$. The last is the alternative interval: if $`b\gt 0`$ and $`\pi(W_i)\ge b`$ almost surely, then $`\Pr(|\hat\mu-\mu|\ge b^{-1}\sqrt{\log(2/\alpha)/(2n)})\le\alpha`$. The main statement has the scope of the corrected proposition.

### P046-T4

A counterexample to Proposition 4(c), as printed

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 8 (PDF p. 7)
