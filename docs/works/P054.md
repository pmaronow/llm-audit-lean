# P054: Policy Misperceptions and Support for Gun Control Legislation

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P054.json)

P. M. Aronow and Benjamin T. Miller (2016). Policy Misperceptions and Support for Gun Control Legislation. The Lancet 387(10015):223.

DOI: [10.1016/S0140-6736(16)00042-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)00042-8)

## Overview

This empirical letter reports weighted survey proportions relating gun-law knowledge, policy preferences and political engagement. Its Lean development is limited to two finite algebraic reconstructions: the residual and normalization identities for a weighted-ratio variance calculation, and the classical intercept-only weighted-least-squares variance formula. The letter prints no variance formula, and these results neither identify the historical procedure nor establish survey-design coverage or a causal effect of correcting misperceptions.

Areas: Survey sampling, Regression and variance estimation.

## Key results

For $`N\ge2`$ fixed binary responses and group indicators, positive weights and a nonempty group, weighted-ratio residuals sum to zero and their normalized squared sum equals $`N/(N-1)`$ times the squared-weight residual variance expression, whose inner expression reduces to $`\hat p(1-\hat p)/n`$ under equal weights.

[`AronowAudit.P054.ratio_reconstruction`](../../AronowAudit/P054/P054_Reconstruction.lean#L117) · [P054-T1](#p054-t1)

For fixed binary responses and group indicators, positive weights and group count $`n\ge2`$, weighted residual sum of squares equals $`W\hat p(1-\hat p)`$, so the classical intercept-only WLS variance is $`\hat p(1-\hat p)/(n-1)`$.

## Caveats

- Here $`n`$ is the unweighted group count, $`W`$ is its total weight and $`\hat p`$ is the weighted group proportion; the first identity additionally uses the full observation count $`N`$.
- These are audit reconstruction identities for the independent-respondent linearisation, not theorems printed in the letter; compatibility with rounded reported intervals does not independently identify the original analysis method.
- Neither result establishes the inverse-variance assumptions for classical WLS standard errors, national survey-design validity, or 95% coverage.
- No general inequality compares the unequal-weight survey variance with $`\hat p(1-\hat p)/n`$; the simple reduction in the first result requires equal weights.
- The Lean results do not formalize the respondent-level reanalysis, validate the empirical percentages, or identify the effect of an information intervention.

### P054-T1

Conditional weighted-ratio reconstruction identities

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** The independent-respondent linearisation in the evidence for P054-N001; the letter prints no variance formula

The proved statement takes $`N\ge2`$ fixed observations, binary group indicators $`d_i`$ and answers $`y_i`$, positive weights $`w_i`$, and $`n=\sum_i d_i\gt 0`$. Put $`W=\sum_i d_iw_i`$, $`\hat p=\sum_i d_iw_iy_i/W`$ and $`z_i=d_iw_i(y_i-\hat p)`$. It asserts $`\sum_i z_i=0`$ and $`\frac{\sum_i z_i^2/[N(N-1)]}{(W/N)^2} =\frac{N}{N-1}\frac{\sum_i d_iw_i^2(y_i-\hat p)^2}{W^2}.`$ If all weights equal a positive constant, the inner ratio on the right equals $`\hat p(1-\hat p)/n`$. Positivity of $`W`$ is derived from the stated conditions. These are finite identities used by the conditional reconstruction. They neither identify the original procedure nor establish national survey coverage.

### P054-T2

Conditional classical intercept-only weighted-least-squares identity

**Kind:** `result_touched_by_finding`

**Location:** The classical-WLS reconstruction in the evidence for P054-N001; the letter prints no variance formula

With the preceding fixed binary data and positive weights, now require $`n\ge2`$. The proved statement gives $`\sum_i d_iw_i(y_i-\hat p)^2=W\hat p(1-\hat p),\qquad \frac{\sum_i d_iw_i(y_i-\hat p)^2}{(n-1)W} =\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n-1} =\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}n.`$ The displayed variance uses the classical intercept-only formula. Its algebra establishes neither the inverse-variance assumptions that justify classical standard errors nor the historical use of that procedure. No general comparison of the unequal-weight survey variance with the unweighted formula is asserted.
