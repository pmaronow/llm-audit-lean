# P061: On the Foundations of the Design-Based Approach

[All works](README.md) · [Machine-readable detail](../../catalog/works/P061.json)

P. M. Aronow, Austin Jang, and Molly Offer-Westort (2026). On the Foundations of the Design-Based Approach. Political Analysis.

DOI: [10.1017/pan.2026.10045](https://doi.org/10.1017/pan.2026.10045)

arXiv: [2505.10519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10519)

## Overview

The work defines design-conditional outcome means and average exposure contrasts when raw potential outcomes can depend on the full assignment. The Lean development covers their behavior under SUTVA and its support-restricted counterpart NURVA, Horvitz–Thompson estimation and variance formulas, covariate adjustment without NURVA under explicit moment conditions, and consistency under the stated dependence and outcome bounds. It also gives counterexamples to unrestricted moment-existence claims and to comparisons with average direct effects, together with corrected first-moment and inference-transfer statements.

Areas: Experimental design and estimation, Interference and networks, Causal identification.

## Key results

Under SUTVA, changing the design while retaining the feasible assignments, outcome schedule and exposure mapping leaves the unit conditional outcome means and average exposure contrast unchanged whenever both target exposures have positive probability for every unit under both designs.

[`AronowAudit.P061.sutva_design_invariance`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L2874) · [P061-T12](#p061-t12)

Every NURVA outcome schedule has a globally SUTVA schedule agreeing on the design support and inducing the same law of observed assignments and outcome vectors.

[`AronowAudit.P061.nurva_sutva_observational_equivalence`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L531) · [P061-T13](#p061-t13)

Under NURVA and positive probabilities for both target exposures, revealed target-cell outcomes equal their conditional means, yielding the exact Horvitz–Thompson variance and cross-exposure covariance formulas and the variance decomposition of their contrast.

[`AronowAudit.P061.nurva_exact_variances`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L3306) · [P061-T18](#p061-t18)

Under NURVA and positive marginal exposure probabilities, the Horvitz–Thompson variance estimator is unbiased when all same-exposure joint probabilities are positive, and the estimator using Young bounds for missing pairs is conservative in expectation without that joint-positivity condition.

[`AronowAudit.P061.nurva_variance_estimators`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L3773) · [P061-T19](#p061-t19)

Under NURVA, positive target-exposure probabilities, the reweighted-outcome cap and dependence-rate Conditions 1.1–1.3, the Horvitz–Thompson estimator minus the population-average conditional outcome mean converges in probability to zero.

[`AronowAudit.P061.ht_consistency`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L3613) · [P061-T21](#p061-t21)

For $`N\gt 0`$, positive target-exposure probabilities, finite absolute first moments of target-cell outcomes and integrable assignment-dependent predictions $`F_i`$, the regression-adjusted Horvitz–Thompson estimator is integrable and unbiased for the average conditional outcome mean whenever $`\mathrm{E}[F_i\mathbf{1}\{D_i=d\}/\pi_i(d)]=\mathrm{E}[F_i]`$ for every unit, without requiring NURVA.

## Corrections and counterexamples

The conclusion claims asymptotically equivalent inference on the average exposure contrast and the displayed average direct effect when dependence and unmodeled interference are sufficiently local. Under the raw-coordinate reading, independent ternary assignments coarsened to binary exposures instead give contrast $`3/2`$ and direct effect one at every positive population size, despite bounded noninterfering outcomes.

[`AronowAudit.P061.coarsened_version_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L1293) · [P061-T1](#p061-t1)

The conclusion substitutes exposure-coordinate hybrids into the direct-effect expression; under the paper’s two-unit network mapping, both target exposures have positive probability and SUTVA holds, yet the two hybrids are never both in the feasible exposure image and both literal raw-coordinate hybrids are feasible with probability only one half.

[`AronowAudit.P061.network_domain_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L668) · [P061-T2](#p061-t2)

The inference-equivalence claim is not justified by degree-one dependence and interference alone: an explicit $`2m^8`$-unit paired design has direct effect zero, average exposure contrast $`m^{-2}`$ and intervals of five actual standard errors covering the exposure contrast with probability at least $`24/25`$, but covering zero with probability at most $`20/m^4`$ for $`m\ge5`$.

[`AronowAudit.P061.local_inference_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L3996) · [P061-T4](#p061-t4)

In place of the unqualified inference-equivalence claim, for identity-coordinate exposures, feasible integrable hybrids and existing targeted moments, a limiting law centered at the average exposure contrast transfers to a studentized direct-effect statistic when the target gap is $`o(s_N)`$ and the estimated scale is consistent, with interval coverage convergence at continuity boundaries of the limiting law.

[`AronowAudit.P061.corrected_inference_transfer`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L2290) · [P061-T5](#p061-t5)

Positivity alone is claimed to make the design-conditional targets well-defined; a one-unit atomic design with both exposure probabilities $`1/2`$ instead has a nonsummable signed treated-outcome numerator and a nonintegrable Horvitz–Thompson estimator.

[`AronowAudit.P061.missing_moment_counterexample`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L893) · [P061-T7](#p061-t7)

The positivity-only existence and unbiasedness claims become valid after adding finite absolute first moments within each target exposure cell: conditional means then exist and the Horvitz–Thompson mean and, when both exposures meet the conditions, its contrast are integrable and unbiased.

[`AronowAudit.P061.corrected_first_moment`](../../AronowAudit/P061/P061_Foundations.lean#L374) · [P061-T8](#p061-t8)

## Caveats

- Designs are normalized nonnegative atomic laws on arbitrary feasible assignment types; support may be infinite, and zero-probability feasible assignments are retained.
- NURVA requires that equal own exposure fixes the outcome on the design support; SUTVA imposes the same restriction over every feasible assignment, so their observational equivalence is relative to a specified design.
- Targeted first moments require absolute summability of weighted outcomes within each exposure cell; a real-valued Lean definition using totalized sums does not itself establish existence of the corresponding signed expectation.
- The inference-transfer theorem assumes identity-coordinate exposures, coordinate hybrids that are feasible for every supported original assignment, integrable hybrid outcomes, a valid first-order limiting law, consistent scale estimation and a negligible target gap relative to that scale; interval endpoints must have zero mass under the limiting law.
- The paired counterexample establishes a precise degree-one design and explicit coverage bounds; it does not assign a formal meaning to the paper’s undefined qualifier “sufficiently local.”
- The consistency theorem retains both $`b_N/N^2\to0`$ and $`b_Nc_N^2/N^2\to0`$, where $`b_N`$ is the sum of absolute exposure-indicator covariances and $`c_N`$ bounds reweighted target-cell outcomes almost surely; it does not add a uniform exposure-positivity bound over $`N`$.
- Covariate adjustment assumes the prediction-moment balance condition for every unit and integrability of the predictions and target-cell outcomes; it does not establish that arbitrary fitted predictions satisfy that condition.

### P061-T1

A counterexample to the comparison of the AEED with the “average direct effect” in Section 8, for a coarsened exposure mapping

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 14 (PDF p. 14); the exposure mapping is that of Section 6.2, p. 11 (PDF p. 11)

The Lean statements formalize the second example of P061-N019. For each $`N\ge1`$, the assignments $`Z_1,\ldots,Z_N`$ are independent and uniform on $`\{0,1,2\}`$, the exposure mapping is $`g_i(\mathbf z)=\mathbf 1\{z_i\gt 0\}`$, and the outcomes are $`y_i(\mathbf z)=z_i`$. The main statement asserts that the exposures $`D_1,\ldots,D_N`$ are mutually independent, that $`0\le y_i(\mathbf z)\le2`$, that $`y_i(\mathbf z)`$ depends on $`z_i`$ only, and that Condition 4.1 holds for the exposures $`1`$ and $`0`$. It asserts further that $`\Pr[Z_i=1\mid D_i=1]=\Pr[Z_i=2\mid D_i=1]=1/2`$ and that $`\tau(1,0)=3/2`$. With $`1`$ and $`0`$ read as assignment coordinates, it asserts that the contrast $`y_i(1;\cdot)-y_i(0;\cdot)`$ equals $`1`$ at every assignment, whether the other coordinates are the assignments $`\mathbf Z_{-i}`$ or the exposures $`\mathbf D_{-i}`$. It concludes that the average direct effect is $`1`$ and that the two targets differ by $`1/2`$ at every $`N`$. Five further statements assert that $`\pi_i(1)=2/3`$, $`\pi_i(0)=1/3`$, $`\overline y_i(1)=3/2`$ and $`\overline y_i(0)=0`$, that the average of the contrast is $`1`$ with either choice of the other coordinates, and that the difference between the two targets does not tend to zero as $`N\to\infty`$. For $`N=2`$ they assert that no function of the exposure vector equals the outcome of unit $`1`$ at every assignment. They also assert that the Horvitz–Thompson estimator of the AEED equals $`(3/(2N))\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_i`$, with expectation $`3/2`$ and variance $`3/(2N)`$, and that $`\Pr\bigl[|\widehat\tau(1,0)-3/2|\ge\epsilon\bigr]\le\frac{3}{2N\epsilon^2}`$ for every $`\epsilon\gt 0`$. The convergence of the estimator to $`3/2`$ in probability is given through this bound for each $`N`$, and not as a limit. Nine supporting statements give elementary probability and expectation identities used in these calculations. The fifteen statements are all proved. The design is the uniform distribution on all of $`\{0,1,2\}^N`$, and probabilities and expectations are exact finite sums.

### P061-T2

A counterexample to the formula for the “average direct effect” in Section 8, for the exposure mapping of Equation (3)

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 14 (PDF p. 14); Equation (3), p. 5 (PDF p. 5)

The Lean statements formalize the first example of P061-N019. There are two adjacent units, each assignment in $`\{0,1\}^2`$ has probability $`1/4`$, and the outcomes are $`y_i(\mathbf z)=z_i`$. The exposure mapping is that of Equation (3), which here reads $`g_1(\mathbf z)=z_1+2z_2`$ and $`g_2(\mathbf z)=z_2+2z_1`$. The main statement asserts that $`\pi_i(e)=1/4`$ for both units and every $`e\in\{0,1,2,3\}`$, that $`\overline y_i(1)=1`$, $`\overline y_i(0)=0`$ and $`\tau(1,0)=1`$, and that equal exposures imply equal outcomes on $`\mathcal Z`$, as Condition 5.2 (SUTVA) requires. It asserts that at no assignment are both $`(1,D_2)`$ and $`(0,D_2)`$ exposure vectors of feasible assignments, and likewise for $`(D_1,1)`$ and $`(D_1,0)`$. It asserts further that, for each unit, the event that both of these vectors lie in $`\{0,1\}^2`$ has probability $`1/2`$. A second statement lists the exposure vectors of the four assignments, which are $`(0,0)`$, $`(2,1)`$, $`(1,2)`$ and $`(3,3)`$. A third statement, with six supporting statements, takes $`K\ge1`$ independent copies of the pair, so that $`N=2K`$. It asserts the same exposure probabilities, EPOs and AEED and the same stability of the outcomes. It asserts further that, for every unit, the two vectors obtained from $`\mathbf D`$ by setting the exposure of that unit to $`1`$ and to $`0`$ are not both exposure vectors of feasible assignments. In this statement the event that both vectors lie in $`\{0,1\}^N`$ has probability $`(1/2)(1/4)^{K-1}`$, a value that P061-N019 does not give.

### P061-T3

The comparison of the AEED with the “average direct effect” in Section 8, as corrected

**Kind:** `corrected_statement`

**Location:** p. 14 (PDF p. 14); the notation $`y_i(d;\mathbf z_{-i})`$ is that of footnote 8, p. 7 (PDF p. 7)

Two main Lean statements, with seven supporting statements on conditional sums, independence and feasible assignments, contain the two parts of the correction of P061-N019. In the first, $`\mathcal Z`$ is an arbitrary subset of $`\mathbb R^N`$ and $`g_i(\mathbf z)=z_i`$. Suppose that, for every $`i`$, $`\pi_i(d)\gt 0`$ and $`\pi_i(d')\gt 0`$, the assignment $`Z_i`$ is independent of $`\mathbf Z_{-i}`$, and $`\sum_{\mathbf z\in\mathrm{Supp}(\mathbf Z):g_i(\mathbf z)=e}|y_i(\mathbf z)|\Pr[\mathbf Z=\mathbf z]\lt \infty\qquad\text{for }e\in\{d,d'\}.`$ The statement asserts that, for every $`\mathbf w`$ such that $`\Pr[\mathbf Z_{-i}=\mathbf w]\gt 0`$, the assignments $`(d;\mathbf w)`$ and $`(d';\mathbf w)`$ lie in $`\mathcal Z`$ and have positive probability. It asserts further that $`\overline y_i(e)=\mathrm E[y_i(e;\mathbf Z_{-i})]`$ for $`e\in\{d,d'\}`$, where the expectation is finite, and that $`\tau(d,d')=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm E\bigl[y_i(d;\mathbf Z_{-i})-y_i(d';\mathbf Z_{-i})\bigr].`$ The second statement concerns a general exposure mapping on an arbitrary set of assignments. Suppose that $`D_i`$ is independent of $`\mathbf D_{-i}`$, that the same positivity and first-moment conditions hold, and that there exist functions $`H_i`$ of the exposure vector such that $`y_i(\mathbf z)=H_i(g_1(\mathbf z),\ldots,g_N(\mathbf z))`$ for every $`\mathbf z`$ in the support with $`g_i(\mathbf z)\in\{d,d'\}`$. The statement asserts that $`\overline y_i(e)=\mathrm E[H_i(e;\mathbf D_{-i})]`$ for $`e\in\{d,d'\}`$, and the display above with $`H_i`$ and $`\mathbf D_{-i}`$ in place of $`y_i`$ and $`\mathbf Z_{-i}`$. No assumption of SUTVA, of NURVA or of the absence of interference is made. The Lean statements require the first-moment condition, and the agreement of $`y_i`$ with $`H_i`$, at the two exposures only. The published correction assumes that $`y_i(\mathbf Z)`$ is integrable and that $`y_i`$ agrees with $`H_i`$ on the whole support, so the Lean statements are slightly more general. An outcome at an assignment outside $`\mathcal Z`$ is set to $`0`$, a convention without effect, since the first assertion places every relevant assignment in the support.

### P061-T4

A counterexample to the claim in Section 8 that inference on AEEDs is asymptotically equivalent to inference on the “average direct effect” when dependence and interference are local

**Kind:** `counterexample`

**Location:** p. 14 (PDF p. 14)

The Lean statements formalize the example of P061-N009. For each integer $`m\ge2`$, the population consists of $`m^8`$ pairs of units, so that $`N=2m^8`$, and the pairs are assigned independently. Within a pair, $`(0,0)`$ and $`(1,1)`$ each have probability $`(1+1/m)/4`$, and $`(0,1)`$ and $`(1,0)`$ each have probability $`(1-1/m)/4`$. The exposure mapping is $`g_i(\mathbf z)=z_i`$, and $`y_i(\mathbf z)=1+z_j/m`$, where $`j`$ is the other unit in the pair of $`i`$. Let $`\tau_N`$ be the AEED $`\tau(1,0)`$, let $`A_N`$ be the average direct effect, and let $`\widehat\tau_N`$ be the Horvitz–Thompson estimator of the AEED, with variance $`s_N^2`$. The main statement asserts, for every $`m\ge2`$, that every assignment has positive probability, that $`\pi_i(1)=\pi_i(0)=1/2`$, and that $`1\le y_i(\mathbf z)\le3/2`$. It asserts that the assignment of a unit is independent of the assignments of all other pairs, and that the covariance of the two assignments in a pair is $`1/(4m)`$. It asserts that $`A_N=0`$, that $`\overline y_i(1)=1+(1+1/m)/(2m)`$ and $`\overline y_i(0)=1+(1-1/m)/(2m)`$, that $`\tau_N=\mathrm E[\widehat\tau_N]=1/m^2`$, and that $`s_N^2=\frac{V_m}{4m^8},\qquad V_m=8+\frac{16}{m}+\frac{14}{m^2}+\frac{2}{m^3}-\frac{4}{m^4},\qquad 8\le V_m\lt 20,\qquad \frac{(\tau_N-A_N)^2}{s_N^2}=\frac{4m^4}{V_m}\ge\frac{m^4}{5}.`$ It asserts further that the interval $`\widehat\tau_N\pm5s_N`$ covers $`\tau_N`$ with probability at least $`24/25`$ and that, for $`m\ge5`$, it covers $`A_N`$ with probability at most $`V_m/m^4\le20/m^4`$. A second statement contains the limits as $`m\to\infty`$: $`\tau_N-A_N\to0`$, $`Ns_N^2\to4`$, $`(\tau_N-A_N)^2/s_N^2\to\infty`$, and the probability that $`\widehat\tau_N\pm5s_N`$ covers $`A_N`$ tends to $`0`$. Seventeen supporting statements give the probability, moment and interval calculations, for nineteen proved statements in total. The design is on all of $`\{0,1\}^N`$, and the limits are taken along the populations of size $`2m^8`$ only. The statements contain no definition of “sufficiently local”, which the paper does not give, and that each unit depends on one other unit only is expressed by the independence across pairs and by the form of $`y_i`$. In P061-N009, I also checked the conditions of the cited central limit theorem; the Lean statements do not cover that comparison.
